6.76	5. Here we run into a question. It is not about this person or that person, or about you or me. If I ask God for something and do not get it, anyone could easily say about me, “He does not have love.” That could easily be said about anyone living today. Let people think what they like—one person about another. No, the question is not really about us. It is about those people who, as everyone knows, were saints when they wrote, and who are now with God.
6.77	Who could have love, if Paul did not have it? He said, “We have spoken openly to you, Corinthians. Our heart is wide open. We are not holding back from you.” He also said, “I will gladly spend myself for your souls.” There was so much grace in him that it was clear he had love.
6.78	And yet we find that he asked for something and did not receive it.
6.79	What do we say to that, brothers and sisters?
6.80	It is a real question. Look carefully to God. This too is a big question.
6.81	Remember what was said about sin: “Anyone who is born of God does not sin.” We found that this sin meant breaking love. That was exactly what that passage meant. In the same way, we now ask what John means here.
6.82	If you look only at the words, the meaning seems clear. But if you think about real examples, it becomes hard to understand.
6.83	Nothing could be clearer than the words here.
6.84	“And we will receive from Him whatever we ask, because we keep His commands and do the things that please Him.”
6.85	“Whatever we ask,” he says, “we will receive from Him.”
6.86	He has put us in a very tight spot.
6.87	He would have put us in a tight spot in the other passage too, if he had meant every sin. But there we found a way to explain it. He meant one certain sin, not every sin. It was a sin that “anyone who is born of God does not commit.” And we found that this sin is nothing other than breaking love.
6.88	We also have a clear example from the Gospel. The Lord says, “If I had not come, they would not have had sin.”
6.89	What does that mean? Were the Jews innocent when He came to them, just because He talks like this?
6.90	If He had not come, would they have had no sin at all?
6.91	Did the Doctor’s visit make people sick instead of taking away their fever?
6.92	Who would be crazy enough to say that?
6.93	He came for one reason only: to cure and heal the sick.
6.94	So when He said, “If I had not come, they would not have had sin,” what did He want us to understand? He meant one particular sin.
6.95	There was one sin the Jews would not have had.
6.96	Which sin? That they did not believe in Him. When He came, they looked down on Him.
6.97	There He said “sin,” but that does not mean every sin, only a certain sin. It is the same here. John does not mean every sin. Otherwise it would go against the place where he says, “If we say that we have no sin, we fool ourselves, and the truth is not in us.” He means one particular sin: breaking love.
6.98	But in this passage he has tied us up even more tightly. He says, “If we ask”—“if our heart does not accuse us, but answers us before God that true love is in us”—then “we will receive from Him whatever we ask.”
6.99	6. Now then, my dear brothers and sisters, I have already told you: nobody should look to us.
6.100	After all, what are we? And what are you?
6.101	We are simply the Church of God, which everyone knows.
6.102	If it pleases God, we are in that Church. Those of us who stay in it through love should keep going there, if we want to show the love we have.
6.103	But what about the apostle Paul? What bad thing should we think about him?
6.104	Paul—not love his brothers and sisters? Impossible!
6.105	Paul—not have the witness of his conscience inside him before God? Impossible!
6.106	Paul—not have inside him that root of love from which all his good fruit grew? Impossible!
6.107	Who would be crazy enough to say that?
6.108	So where do we find that the apostle asked and did not receive?
6.109	He tells us himself: “So that I would not become too proud because of all the great things God showed me, I was given a thorn in my flesh. It was an angel of Satan sent to beat me.
6.110	I pleaded with the Lord three times to take it away from me.
6.111	And He said to me, My grace is enough for you, because strength is made complete in weakness.”
6.112	So you see, he prayed that the “angel of Satan” would be taken away from him, and that prayer was not answered.
6.113	Why not? Because it was not good for him.
6.114	So he was heard for his salvation, even though he did not get what he wanted.
6.115	My dear friends, understand this great mystery. We want you to think about it carefully so that you do not forget it when you are tempted.
6.116	God always hears the saints in a way that leads to their salvation. He always hears them in everything that has to do with their eternal salvation. That is what they really want, and in this their prayers are always answered.
6.117	7. But let us look at the different ways God answers prayer.
6.118	Some people do not get what they want, but they are heard for their salvation. And some people get what they want, but they are not heard for their salvation.
6.119	Notice this difference. Hold on to this example of a man who did not get what he wanted but was heard for his salvation.
6.120	Listen to the apostle Paul. God Himself showed him what it means to be heard for salvation. God said, “My grace is enough for you, because strength is made complete in weakness.”
6.121	You begged. You cried out. You cried out three times. I heard your cry once and for all. I did not turn My ears away from you. I know what I should do. You want Me to take away the thing that burns you, even though it is healing you. I know the weakness that weighs you down.
6.122	So here is a man who was heard for his salvation, even though he did not get what he wanted.
6.123	Where do we find people who got what they wanted but were not heard for their salvation?
6.124	Do you think we can find some wicked, ungodly person whom God gave what he wanted, but not in a way that led to his salvation?
6.125	If I gave you the example of some person, you might say to me, “You are the one calling him wicked. He was actually a good man. If he had not been good, God would not have answered his prayer.”
6.126	So the example I am going to give is someone whose wickedness and ungodliness nobody can doubt.
6.127	The devil himself. He asked for Job, and he got him.
6.128	Haven’t you also heard here about the devil, that “whoever commits sin is from the devil”?
6.129	That does not mean the devil created the sinner. It means the sinner copies the devil.
6.130	Isn’t it said about him, “He did not stand in the truth”?
6.131	Isn’t he also “that old serpent”? Through the woman he handed the first man a drink of poison.
6.132	And in Job’s case, didn’t he leave Job his wife, so that she would tempt her husband instead of comforting him?
6.133	The devil asked for a holy man so he could tempt him, and he got what he asked for. The apostle asked for the thorn in his flesh to be taken away, and he did not get it.
6.134	But the apostle was heard more than the devil.
6.135	The apostle was heard for his salvation, though not for what he wanted. The devil got what he wanted, but it led to his damnation.
6.136	Job was handed over to him to be tempted so that, when Job stood firm under the test, the devil would be tormented.
6.137	My brothers and sisters, we find this not only in the books of the Old Testament, but also in the Gospel.
6.138	When the Lord drove the demons out of a man, they begged Him to let them go into the pigs.
6.139	Didn’t the Lord have the power to tell them to stay away even from those animals?
6.140	If He had not wanted to allow it, they could never have rebelled against the King of heaven and earth.
6.141	But He let the demons go into the pigs because of a certain mystery, a deeper meaning. He wanted to show that the devil rules over people who live like pigs.
6.142	So the demons got what they asked for. Was the apostle not heard?
6.143	Or should we rather say—and this is more true—that the apostle was heard and the demons were not?
6.144	They got what they wanted. He got what was truly good for him, and it was made complete.
6.145	8. So we should understand this: even when God does not give us what we want, He gives us what we need for our salvation.
6.146	Suppose you ask for something that would hurt you, and the Doctor knows it would hurt you. What then?
6.147	We should not say that the doctor is not listening to you. Maybe you ask for cold water. If it is good for you, he gives it to you right away. If it is not good for you, he does not give it.
6.148	Was he refusing to listen to you? Or was he listening for your good, even when he said no to what you wanted?
6.149	So, my brothers and sisters, have love in you. Let love be in you, and then you can be at peace. Even when you do not get what you ask for, your prayer is answered. You just do not know it.
6.150	Many people have been left to have their own way, and it has hurt them. The apostle speaks about them: “God handed them over to the evil desires of their own hearts.”
6.151	Someone asks for a large amount of money. He gets it, and it hurts him.
6.152	When he did not have it, he had little to fear. But as soon as he had it, more powerful people went after him like hunters after prey.
6.153	Wasn’t that man’s prayer answered to his own harm? He insisted on having something that made robbers come looking for him. When he was poor, nobody came looking for him.
6.154	Learn to pray to God in a way that leaves it to the Doctor to do what He knows is best.
6.155	You admit that you are sick. Let Him choose the cure.
6.156	All you need to do is hold on to love.
6.157	He must cut, and He must burn. What if you cry out, and He does not spare you because of your crying while He cuts and burns and it hurts? Still, He knows how far the rot has spread.
6.158	You want Him to take His hands off you right now. But He is thinking only about how deep the wound is. He knows how far He needs to go.
6.159	He does not listen to you to give you what you want. But He does listen to you to make you well.
6.160	So, my brothers and sisters, be sure that what the apostle says is true: “We do not know what we ought to pray for as we should. But the Spirit Himself pleads for us with groans no one can put into words. He pleads for the saints.”
6.161	Why does it say, “The Spirit Himself pleads for the saints”? It means the love that the Spirit produces in you.
6.162	That is why the same apostle says, “The love of God is poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit, whom God gave to us.”
6.163	It is love that groans. It is love that prays. And the One who gave that love cannot close His ears to it.
6.164	So be at peace. Let love do the asking, and God’s ears are there listening.
6.165	What happens is not what you want, but what is good for you.
6.166	So John says, “Whatever we ask, we will receive from Him.” As I already said, if you take this to mean “for our salvation,” there is no problem. If you do not take it that way, there is a problem, and a big one. It is a problem that would make you accuse the apostle Paul.
6.167	“Whatever we ask, we receive from Him, because we keep His commands and do the things that please Him”—inside, where He sees.
6.168	9. And what are those commands?
6.169	“This,” he says, “is His command: that we believe in the name of His Son Jesus Christ and love one another.”
6.170	You see that this is the command. You see that anyone who goes against this command commits the sin from which “everyone who is born of God” is free.
6.171	“As He commanded us”: that we love one another.
6.172	“And whoever keeps His command”—you see that we are told to do nothing else but love one another—“whoever keeps His command will live in Him, and He in him.”
6.173	“And this is how we know that He lives in us: by the Spirit He has given us.
6.174	Isn’t it clear that this is what the Holy Spirit does in a person—He fills him with love and charity?
6.175	Isn’t it clear, as the apostle Paul says, that “the love of God is poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit, whom God gave to us”?
6.176	For [our apostle] was talking about love. He was saying that we should question our own heart before God.
6.177	“But if our heart does not think badly of us”—that is, if our heart admits that whatever good work we do comes from love for our brother.
6.178	Then, when he talks about the command, he also says this: “This is His command: that we believe in the name of His Son Jesus Christ and love one another, as He commanded us.”
6.179	“And whoever does what He commands lives in Him, and He in him.
6.180	This is how we know that He lives in us: by the Spirit He has given us.”
6.181	If you truly find that you have love, then you have the Spirit of God so that you can understand. And that is a very necessary thing.
6.182	10. In the very early days, “the Holy Spirit came down on those who believed, and they spoke in tongues”—languages they had never learned—“as the Spirit gave them the words.”
6.183	These were signs that fit that time.
6.184	There had to be this sign of the Holy Spirit in all languages. It showed that the Gospel of God would spread through every language over the whole earth.
6.185	That was done as a sign, and then it passed away.
6.186	Today, when we lay hands on people so that they may receive the Holy Spirit, do we expect them to speak in tongues?
6.187	When we laid our hands on these babies, did any of you watch to see if they would speak in tongues? And when you saw that they did not, did any of you get it so wrong as to say, “These babies have not received the Holy Spirit. If they had, they would speak in tongues, as people did back then”?
6.188	So if these miracles are no longer the proof that the Holy Spirit is present, what is the proof? How can someone know that he has received the Holy Spirit?
6.189	Let him ask his own heart.
6.190	If he loves his brother, the Spirit of God lives in him.
6.191	Let him look. Let him test himself before God’s eyes. Let him see if he loves peace and unity. Let him see if he loves the Church that is spread over the whole earth.
6.192	He should not be satisfied just with loving the brother he can see. We have many brothers and sisters we do not see, and we are joined to them in the unity of the Spirit.
6.193	Why be surprised that they are not here with us?
6.194	We are in one body. We have one Head, and He is in heaven.
6.195	Brothers and sisters, our two eyes cannot see each other. You could say they do not know each other.
6.196	But in the love that holds the body together, don’t they know each other?
6.197	Here is how you can tell that they know each other in the love that joins them: when both eyes are open, the right eye cannot rest on anything unless the left eye rests on it too.
6.198	Try to aim your right eye at something without the left, if you can.
6.199	They meet together on one thing. They point together at one thing. They have one aim, even though they are in different places.
6.200	So if everyone who loves God along with you has the same aim as you, don’t worry that your bodies are in different places. The eyes of your hearts are fixed together on the same light of truth.
6.201	So if you want to know whether you have received the Spirit, ask your heart. You might have the sacrament but not have the power of the sacrament.
6.202	Ask your heart. If love for your brothers and sisters is there, you can be at peace.
6.203	There cannot be love without the Spirit of God. Paul cries out, “The love of God is poured out in your hearts by the Holy Spirit, whom God gave to us.”
6.204	11. “Beloved friends, do not believe every spirit.”
6.205	He said this because he had just said, “This is how we know that He lives in us: by the Spirit He has given us.”
6.206	But how can we know this same Spirit? Listen: “Beloved friends, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they come from God.”
6.207	And who is able to test the spirits?
6.208	He has given us a hard task, my brothers and sisters!
6.209	It is good for us that he tells us himself how to tell them apart.
6.210	He is about to tell us, so do not be afraid. But first, look. Notice that this passage speaks of the very thing that empty-headed heretics mock us about.
6.211	Look closely at what he says: “Beloved friends, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they come from God.”
6.212	In the Gospel the Holy Spirit is called water. The Lord “cried out and said, If anyone is thirsty, let him come to Me and drink.
6.213	Whoever believes in Me, rivers of living water will flow from deep inside him.”
6.214	But the Gospel writer explains what He was talking about. He goes on to say, “He said this about the Spirit, whom those who believed in Him would receive.”
6.215	Why didn’t the Lord baptize many people?
6.216	But what does the Gospel writer say?
6.217	“The Holy Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified.”
6.218	So those people had been baptized, but they had not yet received the Holy Spirit. The Lord sent the Spirit from heaven on the day of Pentecost. First they had to wait for the Lord to be glorified, and then the Spirit could be given.
6.219	Even before He was glorified and before He sent the Spirit, He still invited people to get ready to receive the water He spoke about: “Whoever is thirsty, let him come and drink.” And, “Whoever believes in Me, rivers of living water will flow from deep inside him.”
6.220	What does “rivers of living water” mean?
6.221	What is that water?
6.222	Don’t ask me. Ask the Gospel.
6.223	“He said this,” it says, “about the Spirit, whom those who believed in Him would receive.”
6.224	So the water of the sacrament is one thing. The water that stands for the Spirit of God is another.
6.225	The water of the sacrament can be seen. The water of the Spirit cannot be seen.
6.226	The first washes the body, and it is a sign of what happens in the soul.
6.227	By this Spirit the soul itself is made clean and fed.
6.228	This is the Spirit of God. Heretics and all who cut themselves off from the Church cannot have Him.
6.229	Some people do not openly cut themselves off, but their wickedness cuts them off. They are inside, but they blow around like chaff and are not real grain. These people do not have this Spirit.
6.230	The Lord calls this Spirit by the name of water. We have heard this letter say, “Do not believe every spirit.” And the words of Solomon agree: “Stay far away from strange water.”
6.231	What does “water” mean here? The Spirit.
6.232	Does water always mean spirit?
6.233	Not always. In some places it means the Spirit. In some places it means baptism. In some places it means peoples. In some places it means wise advice. That is why you find it said in one place, “Wise advice is a fountain of life to those who have it.”
6.234	So in different places in the Scriptures, the word “water” means different things.
6.235	But here you have heard the word water used for the Holy Spirit. That is not our own explanation. The Gospel itself says so: “He said this about the Spirit, whom those who believed in Him would receive.”
6.236	So water stands for the Holy Spirit. And this letter tells us, “Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they come from God.” So let us understand that this is what the saying is about: “Stay far away from strange water, and do not drink from a strange fountain.”
6.237	What does “Do not drink from a strange fountain” mean?
6.238	Do not believe a strange spirit.
6.239	12. So what is left is the test that proves it is the Spirit of God.
6.240	He has given us a sign, and it may be hard. But let us see.
6.241	We have to go back to that love. Love is what teaches us, because love is the anointing.
6.242	But what does he say here?
6.243	“Test the spirits to see whether they come from God, because many false prophets have gone out into this world.”
6.244	That includes all the heretics and all the people who split the Church.
6.245	So how am I supposed to test the spirit?
6.246	He goes on: “This is how the Spirit of God is known.”
6.247	Wake up the ears of your heart!
6.248	We did not know what to do. We were saying, “Who knows? Who can tell?”
6.249	Look, he is about to tell us the sign.
6.250	“This is how the Spirit of God is known: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God. And every spirit that does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not from God. This is the antichrist. You have heard that he was coming, and he is already in the world right now.”
6.251	Our ears, you might say, are wide awake to learn how to tell the spirits apart. But what we have been told does not help us tell them apart one bit.
6.252	What does he say?
6.253	“Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ came in the flesh is from God.”
6.254	Then is the spirit among the heretics from God? After all, they “confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.”
6.255	Yes, here they may rise up against us and say, “You do not have the Spirit from God. But we confess ‘that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.’ And the apostle said here that the people who do not have the Spirit of God are those who do not confess ‘that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.’”
6.256	Ask the Arians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.” Ask the Eunomians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.” Ask the Macedonians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.” Ask the Cataphryges: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.” Ask the Novatians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.”
6.257	So do all these heresies have the Spirit of God?
6.258	Are they not false prophets, then?
6.259	Is there no lying there, no leading people astray?
6.260	Of course they are antichrists! For “they went out from us, but they were not truly part of us.”
6.261	13. So what should we do?
6.262	How can we tell them apart?
6.263	Pay close attention. Let us go together in our hearts and knock.
6.264	Love herself is keeping watch. She is the one who will knock, and she is the one who will open. Soon you will understand, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
6.265	You have already heard what was said earlier: “Whoever denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is an antichrist.”
6.266	There, too, we asked, “Who denies it?” We do not deny it, and they do not deny it either.
6.267	And we found that some people deny it by what they do. We gave proof from the apostle, who says, “They claim to know God, but by what they do they deny Him.”
6.268	So now, too, let us look for the answer in people’s actions, not in their words.
6.269	What is the spirit that is not from God?
6.270	The one “that denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.”
6.271	And what is the spirit that is from God?
6.272	The one “that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.”
6.273	Who is the one who confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh?
6.274	Now, brothers and sisters, let’s get to the point! Let us look at what people do, and not stop at the noise their tongues make.
6.275	Let us ask why Christ came in the flesh. Then we will find out who the people are who deny that He has come in the flesh.
6.276	If you only listen to what people say, you will hear many heresies confess that Christ has come in the flesh. But the truth proves them wrong.
6.277	Why did Christ come in the flesh?
6.278	Wasn’t He God?
6.279	Isn’t it written about Him, “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God”?
6.280	Wasn’t He the one who fed the angels? Isn’t He the one who still feeds the angels?
6.281	When He came here, didn’t He come without leaving heaven?
6.282	When He went back up, didn’t He go without leaving us?
6.283	So why did He come in the flesh?
6.284	Because we needed to be shown the hope of the resurrection.
6.285	He was God, and He came in a human body. God could not die, but a body could die. So He came in the flesh so that He could die for us.
6.286	But how did He die for us?
6.287	“No one has more love than this, that a person gives up his life for his friends.”
6.288	So it was love that brought Him into the flesh.
6.289	So whoever does not have love denies that Christ has come in the flesh.
6.290	Now you can question all the heretics.
6.291	Did Christ come in the flesh?
6.292	“Yes, He came. I believe this. I confess this.”
6.293	No, you deny it.
6.294	“How do I deny it?
6.295	You can hear me saying it!”
6.296	No, I can prove that you deny it.
6.297	You say it with your voice, but you deny it with your heart. You say it with words, but you deny it with your actions.
6.298	“How,” you ask, “do I deny it with my actions?”
6.299	Because the reason Christ came in the flesh was so that He could die for us.
6.300	He died for us because in doing that He taught us great love.
6.301	“No one has more love than this, that a person gives up his life for his friends.”
6.302	You do not have love, because you break up unity for the sake of your own honor.
6.303	So this is how you can tell the spirit that comes from God.
6.304	Tap the clay pots. Test them. See if they are cracked and make a dull sound. See if they ring out full and clear. See if love is there.
6.305	You pull yourself away from the unity of the whole world. You split the Church into groups. You tear apart the Body of Christ.
6.306	He came in the flesh to gather people into one. You shout and make trouble to scatter them.
6.307	So this is the Spirit of God: the one who says that Jesus has come in the flesh. He says it not with the tongue but with actions. He says it not by making noise but by loving.
6.308	And the spirit that denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not from God. Here too, it denies it not with the tongue but with its life, not with words but with actions.
6.309	So it is clear how we can recognize the brothers and sisters.
6.310	Many people who are inside the Church are only inside in a way. But nobody is outside unless he really is outside.
6.311	14. And so that you know he is talking about actions, he says, “And every spirit, qui solvit Christum, that undoes Christ, that He came in the flesh, is not from God.”
6.312	He means undoing Christ by what we do.
6.313	What has he shown you?
6.314	He shows what “denies” means when he says “undoes” (or “unmakes”).
6.315	He came to gather into one. You come to pull apart.
6.316	You want to tear the parts of Christ’s body away from each other.
6.317	You tear apart the Church of God that He gathered together. How can anyone say that you do not deny that Christ has come in the flesh?
6.318	So you are working against Christ. You are an antichrist.
6.319	Whether you are inside or outside, you are an antichrist. The only difference is this: when you are inside, you are hidden. When you are outside, everyone can see it.
6.320	You undo Jesus and deny that He came in the flesh. You are not from God.
6.321	That is why He says in the Gospel: “Whoever breaks one of these least commands, and teaches people to do so, will be called least in the kingdom of heaven.”
6.322	What is this breaking?
6.323	What is this teaching? It is breaking the command by what you do, and teaching, so to speak, by what you say.
6.324	“You preach that people should not steal. Do you steal?”
6.325	So the person who steals breaks or undoes the command by what he does, and in a way he teaches people to do so. “He will be called least in the kingdom of heaven”—that is, in the Church of this present time.
6.326	About such people it is said, “Do what they say, but do not do what they do.
6.327	But whoever does the commands, and teaches people to do so, will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.”
6.328	Notice that here He said fecerit, “will do.” In contrast, there He said solverit, which means non fecerit, “will not do, and will teach so.” So to break a command means not to do it. What is He teaching us? That we should look closely at what people do, and not just trust their words.
6.329	These things are hard to understand, and that forces us to speak for a long time. We do this mainly so that what the Lord is kind enough to reveal can reach even the brothers and sisters who understand more slowly. After all, all of them were bought with the blood of Christ.
6.330	And I am afraid we will not finish the letter itself during these days, as I promised. But as the Lord wills. It is better to save the rest for later than to overload your hearts with too much food.
T:7	Homily 7 (1 John 4:4–12)
7.1	“Now, little children, you come from God, and you have defeated him. For the One who lives in you is greater than the one who is in this world.
7.2	They come from the world. So they talk about the world, and the world listens to them.
7.3	We come from God. Whoever knows God listens to us. Whoever does not come from God does not listen to us.
7.4	This is how we can tell the spirit of truth from [the spirit] of error.
7.5	My dearly loved friends, let us love one another, because love comes from God. Everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God.
7.6	Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love.
7.7	This is how God’s love was shown in us: God sent His only Son into this world so that we might live through Him.
7.8	This is what love is: not that we loved, but that He loved us and sent His Son to be the One who makes up for our sins.
7.9	My dearly loved friends, if God loved us so much, then we too must love one another.
7.10	Nobody has ever seen God.”
7.11	1. For all believers who are looking for their true homeland, this world is like the desert was for the people of Israel.
7.12	They were still wandering and looking for their homeland. But with God as their guide, they could not get lost.
7.13	Their road was God’s command.
7.14	They wandered around for forty years, yet the journey itself has only a few stops, and everyone knows it.
7.15	They were slowed down because they were being trained, not because God had left them.
7.16	What God promises us is a sweetness and a goodness too great for words. The Scripture says so, and you have often heard us repeat it: it is what “the eye has not seen, the ear has not heard, and has never come into anyone’s heart.”
7.17	But for now we are put to work by the hard labors of this life, and we are trained by the temptations of this present life.
7.18	So if you don’t want to die of thirst in this desert, drink love.
7.19	Love is the fountain God was pleased to put here so that we do not faint on the way. And we will drink from it even more when we reach our own land.
7.20	The Gospel has just been read. Think about the very words at the end of that reading. What did you hear about, except love?
7.21	In our prayer we have made an agreement with our God. If we want Him to forgive our sins, then we must also forgive the sins others have done against us.
7.22	And the thing that forgives is nothing other than love.
7.23	Take love out of the heart, and hatred takes over. Then the heart does not know how to forgive.
7.24	Let love be there, and she forgives without fear, because she is not cramped or narrow.
7.25	And look at this whole letter we have started to explain to you. See if it praises anything else except this one thing: love.
7.26	We do not need to be afraid that if we talk about love a lot, people will start to hate it.
7.27	If love becomes hateful, what is left to love?
7.28	It is through love that we learn to love other things in the right way. So how much we must love love itself!
7.29	So what should never leave our hearts should not leave our tongues either.
7.30	2. “Now,” he says, “you come from God, little children, and you have defeated him.” Defeated whom? Who else but the Antichrist?
7.31	Earlier he had said, “Whoever undoes Jesus Christ and denies that He has come in the flesh is not from God.”
7.32	If you remember, we explained that everyone who breaks love denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.
7.33	Jesus had no reason to come except love. The love we are praising is the same love the Lord Himself praises in the Gospel: “No one can have more love than this, that a person gives up his life for his friends.”
7.34	How could the Son of God give up His life for us without putting on a body in which He could die?
7.35	So whoever breaks love—no matter what he says with his tongue—his life denies that Christ has come in the flesh. He is an antichrist, wherever he may be, however he has gotten in.
7.36	But what does the apostle say to those who are citizens of that homeland we long for?
7.37	“You have defeated him.”
7.38	And how did they defeat him?
7.39	“Because the One who lives in you is greater than the one who is in this world.”
7.40	He did not want them to think they had won by their own strength. Then arrogant pride would defeat them—for the devil defeats everyone he makes proud. He wanted them to stay humble. So what does he say?
7.41	“You have defeated him.”
7.42	Now when anyone hears the words “You have defeated,” he lifts up his head, sticks out his neck, and wants to be praised.
7.43	Don’t puff yourself up. See who it is that won the victory in you.
7.44	Why did you win?
7.45	“Because the One who lives in you is greater than the one who is in the world.”
7.46	Be humble. Carry your Lord. Be the animal He rides on.
7.47	It is good for you that He is in control and that He guides you.
7.48	If you do not have Him riding on you, you may lift up your neck and kick up your heels. But how terrible for you if no one is in charge of you! That kind of freedom sends you out among the wild beasts to be eaten!
7.49	3. “These people come from the world.”
7.50	Who? The antichrists. You have already heard who they are.
7.51	If you are not like them, you can recognize them. But anyone who is like them cannot.
7.52	“These people come from the world. So they talk about the world, and the world listens to them.”
7.53	Who are the people who “talk about the world”?
7.54	Notice who is against love.
7.55	Look, you have heard the Lord say, “If you forgive people when they wrong you, your Father in heaven will forgive your wrongs too.
7.56	But if you do not forgive people when they wrong you, your Father will not forgive your wrongs either.”
7.57	This is what the Truth has said. If it is not the Truth speaking, then argue against it.
7.58	If you are a Christian and believe Christ, remember that He said, “I am the truth.”
7.59	This saying is true. It stands firm.
7.60	Now listen to people who “talk about the world.”
7.61	“Aren’t you going to get back at him?
7.62	Are you going to let him brag that he did this to you?
7.63	No! Let him find out that he is dealing with a real man.”
7.64	People say things like this every day. Those who say such things “talk about the world, and the world listens to them.”
7.65	Only people who love the world say such things. And only people who love the world listen to them.
7.66	And you have heard that loving the world and ignoring love is the same as denying that Jesus came in the flesh.
7.67	Tell me: did the Lord Himself do that when He was in the flesh? When He was beaten, did He want revenge? When He was hanging on the cross, didn’t He say, “Father, forgive them, because they do not know what they are doing”?
7.68	He had all the power, yet He made no threats. So why do you threaten? Why do you swell up with anger, when you are under someone else’s power?
7.69	He died because He chose to die, and still He made no threats. You do not even know when you will die—and you make threats?
