0.1	1. The blessed Apostle wanted to show that the Prophets had foretold a time when all the nations would believe in Christ. So he quoted this passage, where it is written, “And it will happen that everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.”
0.2	In earlier times, only the Israelites called on the name of the Lord who made heaven and earth. The other nations called on idols that could not speak or hear, and those idols did not hear them. Or they called on demons, and the demons did hear them—but only to harm them.
0.3	“But when the right time had fully come,” what had been foretold came true: “And it will happen that everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.”
0.4	Some Jews, even some who believed in Christ, did not want the Gentiles to have the Gospel. They said the Gospel should not be preached to people who were not circumcised. So the Apostle Paul used this passage against them: “And it will happen that everyone who calls on the Name of the Lord will be saved.” Then, to convince the people who did not want the Gospel preached to the Gentiles, he went straight on to say, “But how can they call on Him if they have not believed in Him? And how can they believe in Him if they have not heard of Him? And how can they hear without someone preaching? And how can anyone preach unless they are sent?”
0.5	He said, “How can they call on Him if they have not believed in Him?” That is why you did not learn the Lord’s Prayer first and the Creed afterward. You learned the Creed first, so that you would know what to believe. Then you learned the Prayer, so that you would know whom to call on.
0.6	So the Creed is about faith, and the Lord’s Prayer is about prayer. That is because it is the person who believes who is heard when he calls.
0.7	2. But many people ask for things they should not ask for, because they do not know what is good for them.
0.8	So a person who prays must watch out for two things. He must not ask for what he should not ask for. And he must not ask it from someone he should not ask.
0.9	Nothing must be asked from the devil, from idols, or from evil spirits.
0.10	Whatever we need to ask, we must ask it from Him: from the Lord our God Jesus Christ, from God the Father of the Prophets and Apostles and Martyrs, from the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from God who made heaven and earth, the sea, and everything in them.
0.11	But we must be careful not to ask Him for things we should not ask for.
0.12	We ought to ask for life. But if you ask for it from idols that cannot speak or hear, what good does it do you?
0.13	In the same way, if you ask God the Father in heaven for your enemies to die, what good does it do you?
0.14	Have you not heard or read the Psalm that foretells the terrible, damned end of Judas the traitor? Have you not heard how the prophecy said about him, “Let his prayer be turned into sin”?
0.15	So if you get up and pray for bad things to happen to your enemies, your “prayer will be turned into sin.”
0.16	3. You have read in the Holy Psalms how the one who speaks there seems to call down many curses on his enemies.
0.17	Surely, someone might say, the one who speaks in the Psalms is a righteous man. So why does he wish evil on his enemies like that?
0.18	He does not wish it. He sees it coming. It is a prophecy from someone telling what will happen in the future, not a curse he wants to come true. The prophets knew by the Spirit who was going to suffer evil and who was going to receive good. And when they prophesied, they spoke as if they wanted what they saw coming.
0.19	But how can you know? The person you are asking evil for today might become a better person than you tomorrow.
0.20	But you will say, “I know he is a wicked man.”
0.21	Fine. Then you must know that you are wicked too.
0.22	Maybe you set yourself up to judge another person’s heart, which you do not really know. But you do know one thing about yourself: you are wicked.
0.23	Do you not hear the Apostle say, “I used to be a blasphemer, a persecutor, and a violent man. But I was shown mercy, because I did it in ignorance and unbelief”?
0.24	When the Apostle Paul was persecuting the Christians, he tied them up wherever he found them. He dragged them to the Chief Priests to be questioned and punished. So what do you think, brothers and sisters? Did the Church pray against him, or for him?
0.25	The Church of God had learned from her Lord. As He hung on the Cross, He said, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.” So surely the Church prayed for Paul (or rather Saul, as he was still called then). She prayed that what later happened in him would happen.
0.26	For he says, “But the churches of Judea that are in Christ did not know me by sight. They had only heard that the man who used to persecute us is now preaching the faith he once tried to destroy. And they praised God because of me.” Why did they praise God? Only because they had asked God for this before it happened.
0.27	4. So first of all our Lord cut out “much speaking.” He did not want you to bring a flood of words to God, as if your many words could teach Him something.
0.28	So when you pray, you need devotion, not lots of words.
0.29	“For your Father knows what you need before you ask Him.”
0.30	So do not be eager to use many words, because He knows what you need.
0.31	But someone might say here: If He knows what we need, why should we use even a few words? Why should we pray at all?
0.32	He already knows. So let Him just give us what He knows we need.
0.33	Yes, but He wants you to pray. He wants to give to your longing, so that you will not take His gifts lightly. After all, He is the one who put this deep longing in us.
0.34	So the words our Lord Jesus Christ taught us in His prayer are the rule and measure for our desires.
0.35	You must not ask for anything except what is written there.
0.36	5. “So this is what you should say,” He says: “Our Father, who is in heaven.”
0.37	Here you see that you have started to have God as your Father.
0.38	You will have Him as your Father when you are born again.
0.39	Even now, before you are born, you have been conceived from His seed. You are about to be brought to birth in the baptismal font, which is like the Church’s womb.
0.40	“Our Father, who is in heaven.”
0.41	So remember that you have a Father in heaven.
0.42	Remember that you were born from your father Adam to die. And remember that you are going to be born again from God the Father to live.
0.43	And whatever you say, say it in your hearts.
0.44	Only let your prayer come from deep, sincere longing, and the One who hears prayer will answer it with power.
0.45	“May Your name be kept holy.”
0.46	Why do you ask for God’s name to be kept holy?
0.47	It is already holy. So why do you ask for something that is already holy?
0.48	And when you ask for His name to be kept holy, aren’t you praying to Him for His sake, and not for your own?
0.49	No. If you understand it correctly, you are asking for yourself.
0.50	What you are asking is this: that His name, which is always holy in itself, will be kept holy in you.
0.51	What does “be kept holy” mean?
0.52	It means “be treated as holy,” and not be looked down on.
0.53	So you see, the good you wish for, you are wishing for yourself.
0.54	For if you look down on God’s name, it will be bad for you, not for God.
0.55	6. “May Your kingdom come.”
0.56	Who are we talking to? Won’t God’s kingdom come even if we do not ask for it?
0.57	We are speaking about the kingdom that will come after the end of the world.
0.58	For God always has a kingdom. The whole creation serves Him, so He is never without a kingdom.
0.59	So what kingdom are you wishing for?
0.60	The one written about in the Gospel: “Come, you who are blessed by My Father. Receive the kingdom that has been prepared for you since the world began.”
0.61	That is the kingdom we mean when we say, “May Your kingdom come.”
0.62	We pray that it will come in us. We pray that we will be found in it.
0.63	It will certainly come. But what good will it do you if it finds you standing on the left side?
0.64	So here again you are wishing good for yourself. You are praying for yourself.
0.65	This is what you long for. This is the desire in your prayer: that you will live in such a way that you will have a share in God’s kingdom, which will be given to all the saints.
0.66	So when you say, “May Your kingdom come,” you are praying for yourself, that you will live a good life.
0.67	Let us have a share in Your kingdom. Let what is coming to Your saints and righteous people come to us too.
0.68	7. “May Your will be done.”
0.69	What? If you do not say this, won’t God do His will anyway?
0.70	Remember what you said in the Creed: “I believe in God the Father Almighty.”
0.71	If He is Almighty, why do you pray for His will to be done?
0.72	So what does “May Your will be done” mean?
0.73	It means: may it be done in me, so that I do not fight against Your will.
0.74	So here again you are praying for yourself, not for God.
0.75	For God’s will is going to be done in you, even if it is not done by you.
0.76	He will say to some, “Come, you who are blessed by My Father. Receive the kingdom prepared for you since the world began.” In them God’s will will be done: the saints and righteous will receive the kingdom. And He will say to others, “Go away into the everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his angels.” In them too God’s will will be done: the wicked will be condemned to everlasting fire.
0.77	But for His will to be done by you is something different.
0.78	So there is a good reason why you pray for His will to be done in you. It is so that things will go well for you.
0.79	Whether things go well or badly for you, His will is still going to be done in you. But oh, how I wish it would be done by you as well!
0.80	So why do I say, “May Your will be done in heaven and on earth,” and not, “May Your will be done by heaven and earth”?
0.81	Because what is done by you is really done in you by Him.
0.82	Nothing is ever done by you unless He Himself does it in you.
0.83	Sometimes He does things in you that are not done by you. But nothing is ever done by you unless He does it in you.
0.84	8. But what does “in heaven and on earth” mean, or “as in heaven, so on earth”?
0.85	The Angels do Your will. May we do it too.
0.86	“May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth.”
0.87	The mind is heaven, and the flesh is earth.
0.88	Maybe you say with the Apostle (if you really do say it), “With my mind I serve God’s law, but with my flesh I serve the law of sin.” Then God’s will is being done in heaven, but not yet on earth.
0.89	But a day is coming when the flesh will agree with the mind, and “death will be swallowed up in victory.” Then no bodily cravings will be left for the mind to fight against. The struggle on earth will be gone, and the war in the heart will be over. And this will be gone too, which Scripture describes: “The flesh fights against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh. They are opposed to each other, so that you cannot do the things you want to do.” When this war is over, I say, and all sinful desire has been changed into love, nothing will be left in the body to oppose the spirit. There will be nothing to tame, nothing to hold back with a bridle, nothing to stamp down. Everything will move together in harmony toward righteousness, and God’s will will be done in heaven and on earth.
0.90	“May Your will be done in heaven and on earth.”
0.91	When we pray for this, we are wishing for perfection.
0.92	“May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth.”
0.93	In the Church, the spiritual people are heaven, and the worldly people are earth.
0.94	So “May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth” means this: as the spiritual people serve You, may the worldly people also be changed and serve You too.
0.95	“May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth.”
0.96	There is also another, very spiritual meaning.
0.97	For we are told to pray for our enemies.
0.98	The Church is heaven, and the enemies of the Church are earth.
0.99	So what does “May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth” mean?
0.100	May our enemies believe in You, just as we believe! May they become our friends and stop being our enemies!
0.101	They are earth, and that is why they are against us. May they become heaven, and then they will be with us.
0.102	9. “Give us today our daily bread.”
0.103	Here it is clear that we are praying for ourselves.
0.104	When you say, “May Your name be kept holy,” it takes some explaining to show that you are praying for yourself and not for God.
0.105	When you say, “May Your will be done,” this too needs explaining. Otherwise you might think you are wishing something good for God in this prayer, that His will would be done, instead of praying for yourself.
0.106	When you say, “May Your kingdom come,” this also must be explained. Otherwise you might think you are wishing something good for God in this prayer, that He would reign.
0.107	But from this point to the end of the Prayer, it is plain that we are praying to God for ourselves.
0.108	When you say, “Give us today our daily bread,” you admit that you are God’s beggar.
0.109	But do not be ashamed of this. No matter how rich someone is on earth, he is still God’s beggar.
0.110	The beggar stands in front of the rich man’s house. But the rich man himself stands at the door of the great Rich One.
0.111	People come to the rich man with requests, and he goes to God with his own requests.
0.112	If he were not in need, he would not knock on God’s ears in prayer.
0.113	And what does the rich man need?
0.114	I dare to say it: the rich man needs even his daily bread.
0.115	How is it that he has plenty of everything? Where did it come from, except that God gave it to him?
0.116	What would he have if God took His hand away?
0.117	Haven’t many people gone to sleep rich and woken up as beggars?
0.118	And if he does not go without, that is because of God’s mercy, not his own power.
0.119	10. Dear friends, think about this bread that fills our bodies and gives our flesh new strength each day. God gives this bread not only to people who praise Him, but also to people who insult Him. He is the one “who makes His sun rise on bad people and good people, and sends rain on those who do right and those who do wrong.”
0.120	You praise Him, and He feeds you. You insult Him, and He still feeds you.
0.121	He is waiting for you to turn from your sin. But if you refuse to change, He will condemn you.
0.122	So both good and bad people receive this bread from God. Do you think, then, that there is no other bread that the children ask for? The Lord spoke of it in the Gospel when He said, “It is not fitting to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”
0.123	Yes, there certainly is.
0.124	So what is that bread? And why is it called daily?
0.125	Because it is just as necessary as the other bread. We cannot live without it, just as we cannot live without bread.
0.126	It is shameless to ask God for riches. It is not shameless to ask for daily bread.
0.127	What feeds pride is one thing. What keeps us alive is something else.
0.128	This bread that can be seen and touched is given to both good and bad people. But there is also a daily bread that the children pray for.
0.129	That bread is the word of God, which is given out to us day by day.
0.130	Our bread is daily bread. It keeps alive not our bodies, but our souls.
0.131	We need it, because even now we are workers in the vineyard. It is our food, not our pay.
0.132	The man who hires a worker for his vineyard owes him two things: food, so that he does not faint, and his pay, so that he can be glad.
0.133	So our daily food here on earth is the word of God, which is always being given out in the churches. Our pay after the work is done is called eternal life.
0.134	Or again, by our daily bread you may understand what the faithful receive—what you will receive after you are baptized. Then it makes good sense for us to ask, “Give us today our daily bread.” We are asking to live in such a way that we are never cut off from the Holy Altar.
0.135	11. “And forgive us our debts, as we forgive those who owe us.”
0.136	Here again we do not need to explain that we are praying for ourselves.
0.137	We are begging for our debts to be forgiven.
0.138	For we are in debt—not in money, but in sins.
0.139	Maybe you are thinking right now, “And you too?”
0.140	We answer, “Yes, we too.”
0.141	“What? Holy Bishops, are you in debt too?”
0.142	“Yes, we are in debt too.”
0.143	“Surely not you, my lord!”
0.144	“Don’t say such a thing! Don’t be so unfair to yourself.”
0.145	I am not being unfair to myself. I am telling the truth. We are in debt: “If we say we have no sin, we are fooling ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
0.146	We have been baptized, and yet we are still in debt.
0.147	It is not that something was left over that was not forgiven us in Baptism. It is that as we live, we keep picking up things that need to be forgiven every day.
0.148	People who are baptized and then die right away come up out of the font with no debt. They leave the world without any debt.
0.149	But people who are baptized and go on living pick up stains of sin, because they are weak and mortal. It is like a leaking ship. These sins do not sink the ship, but it still needs to be pumped out.
0.150	Otherwise, water will leak in little by little until the whole ship sinks.
0.151	Praying this prayer is like using the pump.
0.152	But we should not only pray. We should also give to the poor. When the pump is used to keep a ship from sinking, both voices and hands are at work.
0.153	We work with our voices when we say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.154	And we work with our hands when we do this: “Share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house.
0.155	Hide your gift to the poor in a poor man’s heart, and it will plead for you before the Lord.”
0.156	12. All our sins were forgiven in the “washing of new birth.” Even so, we would be in serious trouble if we were not also given the daily cleansing of the Holy Prayer.
0.157	Giving to the poor and praying wash sins away. Only we must not commit the kind of sins that would force us to be cut off from our daily Bread. Let us all avoid debts like that, which bring a severe and certain condemnation.
0.158	Do not call yourselves righteous, as if you had no reason to say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.159	Maybe you stay away from idol worship, from the comforts astrologers offer, and from the cures of magicians. Maybe you stay away from the lies of false teachers and the splits caused by people who break away from the Church. Maybe you stay away from murder, adultery and sexual immorality, from stealing and robbery, from lying in court, and from all the other sins I do not name. Those sins have ruinous results. Anyone who commits them must be cut off from the altar. He is bound on earth in such a way that he is also bound in heaven, which puts him in great and deadly danger—unless he is set free again on earth in such a way that he is also set free in heaven. But even after we set all these sins aside, there are still plenty of ways a person can sin.
0.160	A person sins by enjoying looking at something he should not look at.
0.161	But who can control how quickly the eye moves?
0.162	In fact, people say the eye got its very name from its quickness.
0.163	Who can hold back the ear or the eye?
0.164	You can shut your eyes whenever you want, and they close in a moment. But you can only close your ears with some effort. You have to lift your hand and reach them. And if someone holds your hand, your ears stay open. Then you cannot shut them against insults, dirty talk, flattery, or tempting words.
0.165	And when you hear things you should not hear, even if you do not do them, don’t you sin with your ear? Don’t you enjoy hearing something bad?
0.166	How many great sins the deadly tongue commits!
0.167	Yes, sometimes they are sins so serious that a person is cut off from the altar because of them.
0.168	All speaking against God comes from the tongue. And many careless words are spoken that are not right.
0.169	But suppose your hand does nothing wrong, your feet do not run to anything evil, and your eyes do not look at anything immodest. Suppose your ears do not listen with pleasure to filthy talk, and your tongue does not speak indecent words. Even so, tell me: who can control his thoughts?
0.170	How often we pray, my brothers and sisters, while our thoughts are somewhere else! It is as if we forget who we are standing before, or who we are bowing down before!
0.171	If all these things are gathered together against us, will they fail to crush us just because they are small faults?
0.172	What does it matter whether we are crushed by lead or by sand?
0.173	Lead is one solid lump. Sand is tiny grains. But there are so many grains that they crush you.
0.174	In the same way, your sins are small.
0.175	Don’t you see how rivers fill up and land is flooded and ruined by small drops?
0.176	The drops are small, but there are many of them.
0.177	13. So let us say it every day, and say it sincerely from the heart, and do what we say: “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.178	It is a promise, a covenant, an agreement that we make with God.
0.179	The Lord your God says to you, “Forgive, and I will forgive.”
0.180	You have not forgiven. So it is you, not I, who keeps your sins held against you. My dearly loved children, I beg you, listen to me. I know what is good for you in the Lord’s Prayer, and most of all in this line of it: “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.181	You are about to be baptized. Forgive everything. Whatever anyone has in his heart against anyone else, let him forgive it from his heart.
0.182	Come in like that, and be sure of this: all your sins are forgiven. That includes the original sin you received when you were born from your parents, going back to Adam—the sin that is the reason you run with babies to the Savior’s grace. It also includes all the sins you have committed later in your lives, by word, deed, or thought. All of them are forgiven. And you will come out of the water as if from standing before your Lord, with every debt surely cancelled.
0.183	14. Now, because of those daily sins I have talked about, you need to say in that daily prayer that cleanses us, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.” So what will you do?
0.184	You have enemies. For who can live on this earth without them?
0.185	Be careful: love them.
0.186	Your enemy can never hurt you by his violence as much as you hurt yourself if you do not love him.
0.187	He may damage your property, or your flocks, or your house, or your male servant, or your female servant, or your son, or your wife. At the most, if he is given that much power, he may harm your body.
0.188	But can he harm your soul the way you can harm it yourself?
0.189	Dear friends, I beg you, reach forward to this perfection.
0.190	But am I the one who has given you the power to do this?
0.191	No, He alone has given it—the One to whom you say, “May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth.”
0.192	But do not think it is impossible.
0.193	I know—I have seen it for myself—that there are Christians who really do love their enemies.
0.194	If you think it is impossible, you will not do it.
0.195	So first believe that it can be done. Then pray for God’s will to be done in you.
0.196	After all, what good does your neighbor’s evil do you?
0.197	If there were no evil in him, he would not even be your enemy.
0.198	So wish him well. Wish that he would stop his evil, and then he will not be your enemy anymore.
0.199	It is not the human being in him that is your enemy. It is his sin.
0.200	Is he your enemy because he has a soul and a body?
0.201	In that way he is just like you. You have a soul, and so does he. You have a body, and so does he.
0.202	He is made of the same stuff as you. You were both made from the same earth and given life by the same Lord.
0.203	In all this he is just like you. So see him as your brother.
0.204	The first couple, Adam and Eve, were our parents—one our father, the other our mother. So we are brothers and sisters.
0.205	But let us leave aside our first beginnings.
0.206	God is our Father, and the Church is our Mother. So we are brothers and sisters.
0.207	But you will say, “My enemy is a pagan, a Jew, or a heretic.” I spoke about them a little while ago, when I explained the words, “May Your will be done as in heaven, so on earth.”
0.208	O Church, your enemy is the pagan, the Jew, the heretic. He is the earth.
0.209	If you are heaven, call on your Father in heaven and pray for your enemies. Saul was once an enemy of the Church like that. People prayed for him, and he became her friend.
0.210	He did not just stop persecuting her. He worked hard to help her.
0.211	And yet, to be honest, people did pray against him. But they prayed against his wickedness, not against his human nature.
0.212	So let your prayer be against your enemy’s wickedness. Pray that his wickedness will die and that he will live.
0.213	If your enemy died, it might seem that you had lost an enemy. But you would not have gained a friend.
0.214	But if his wickedness dies, you lose an enemy and gain a friend at the same time.
0.215	15. But you are still saying, “Who can do this? Who has ever done it?”
0.216	May God make it happen in your hearts!
0.217	I know as well as you do that only a few people do it. Those who do are great and spiritual people.
0.218	All the faithful in the Church who come to the altar and receive the Body and Blood of Christ—are they all like that?
0.219	And yet they all say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.220	What if God answered them, “Why do you ask Me to do what I have promised, when you do not do what I have commanded?”
0.221	What have I promised?
0.222	“To forgive your debts.”
0.223	What have I commanded?
0.224	“That you also forgive those who owe you.”
0.225	How can you do this if you do not love your enemies?
0.226	So what should we do, brothers and sisters?
0.227	Has Christ’s flock shrunk down to so few?
0.228	Suppose only those who love their enemies should say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.” Then I do not know what to do. I do not know what to say.
0.229	Should I tell you, “If you do not love your enemies, do not pray”? I do not dare say that. No—pray instead that you will love them.

0.232	If you do not say them, your debts are not forgiven. And if you do say them but do not live by them, your debts are not forgiven either.
0.233	So for them to be forgiven, you must both say the prayer and live by it.
0.234	16. I see a reason to comfort not just a few people, but the whole crowd of Christians. And I know you are eager to hear it.
0.235	Christ has said, “Forgive, so that you will be forgiven.”
0.236	And what do you say in the Prayer we have been talking about?
0.237	“Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.238	In other words: Lord, forgive us in the same way that we forgive.
0.239	This is what you are saying: “O Father, who is in heaven, forgive our debts in the same way that we also forgive those who owe us.”
0.240	This is what you must do. If you do not do it, you will be lost.
0.241	When your enemy asks you to forgive him, forgive him right away.
0.242	Is that too much for you to do?
0.243	It may be a lot to love your enemy while he is attacking you. But is it a lot to love someone who is humbly begging you for mercy?
0.244	What do you have to say?
0.245	Before, he was violent, and so you hated him.
0.246	I wish you had not hated him even then. I wish that while you were suffering his violence, you had remembered the Lord saying, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.”
0.247	I would have wished very much that even when your enemy was attacking you, you had paid attention to the Lord your God saying this.
0.248	But maybe you will say: He did it, but He did it because He is the Lord, the Christ, the Son of God, the Only Son, the Word who became flesh.
0.249	But what can I do? I am a weak and sinful person.
0.250	If your Lord is too high an example for you, then think about your fellow servant.
0.251	Holy Stephen was being stoned. While they were throwing stones at him, he knelt down and prayed for his enemies. He said, “Lord, do not put this sin on their account.”
