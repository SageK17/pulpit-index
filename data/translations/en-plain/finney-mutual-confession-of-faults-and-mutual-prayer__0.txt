0.1	“Tell one another the wrong things you have done, and pray for one another, so that you can be healed.” — James 5:16
0.2	In this message we need to look at the following points.
0.3	I. What does the command “Confess your faults to one another” mean?
0.4	II. What does it take to obey it?
0.5	III. Why were we given this command?
0.6	IV. What does the command “Pray for one another” mean?
H:0.6	I. What does the command “Confess your faults to one another” mean?
0.7	1. There is no reason to think that this verse supports the Roman Catholic teaching that people must confess their sins to priests.
0.8	You know the teaching that has been common in that church for so long. It is called “auricular confession,” because it was spoken privately into the priest’s ear.
0.9	This teaching says that everyone must privately confess every action and every thought that troubles their conscience at all.
0.10	So this system turns the priest into a storehouse for all the sins of the church. He holds all the most private sins of every member, man or woman. And they must confess them, or else face damnation.
0.11	Anyone who knows what this system has led to knows that it has naturally done great harm to the purity and morals of both the priests and the people.
0.12	It is very clear that the apostle did not have such a system in mind and could never approve of it.
0.13	Confessing faults to one another is not the same as confessing all your faults to the priest alone.
0.14	2. The verse does not say exactly which sins it means. But we can be sure that the apostle is mainly talking about wrongs done against other people. He is telling us to confess our wrongs to the people we have hurt.
0.15	The Bible teaches this kind of confession, and takes it for granted, in many places. So we can take it that this is what is meant here.
0.16	But the idea behind this may reach a bit further. (1) It may include telling others about the sins we keep falling into, and about our own special temptations and weaknesses.
0.17	Clearly, if we let trusted Christian friends know about these temptations and struggles, we can win their care and their prayers for us. That way we get real help to resist and overcome these temptations.
0.18	(2) The idea in this verse may also include sins that are not really done against one certain person, but that are done in front of other people. Because of that, they bring shame on our faith and make the people around us stumble.
0.19	Take a short temper, for example. It often makes others stumble, and it can become a sin we keep falling into. Or take the habit of speaking harshly about others. This often deeply hurts not only the people we speak badly about, but also every kind-hearted person who hears us.
0.20	Clearly, these sins and others like them should be confessed.
0.21	Please notice what I am not saying. I am not saying that people must reveal everything they know—every sin they think they are guilty of—even sins that no one knows about except themselves and God.
0.22	The Bible does not seem to make confessing to people a duty that covers everything like that.
H:0.12	II. What does it take to obey this command?
0.23	1. First, we must be fully convinced of our sin. We must admit not only that we did it, but also how wicked it was, as a sin against God and against our neighbor.
0.24	It is one thing for a person to confess a fault as a real fault and a sin. It is something quite different to admit what happened without seeing how terribly guilty it was.
0.25	So our verse must mean that we truly understand the real guilt of our sin and honestly confess it.
0.26	2. We must be sorry for our sin, turn away from it, and give it up.
0.27	If confession does not come with repentance and giving up the sin we confess, it is nothing but an insult to God and to people.
0.28	3. Obeying this command also takes humility.
0.29	By humility I mean an attitude that is happy to take its proper place, even if it is a very low place. It does not try to make excuses. Instead, it honestly takes off its own mask and naturally looks for the place that really belongs to us.
0.30	Usually the big thing that makes confessing hard is that people are too proud.
0.31	They do not like to lower themselves in other people’s eyes as much as they should.
0.32	Humility is the exact opposite of this pride. It means being willing to come down to your own low place.
0.33	The verse is not talking about a confession that is forced or dragged out of someone.
0.34	Sometimes a man confesses only because his conscience stings him like a scorpion. Or he confesses because things have come out in public, and shocking facts have left him cornered, so he has no other way to keep up any appearance of being a Christian. A proud man may have a huge struggle to make that kind of confession. Even so, it is worth very little.
0.35	4. True confession means being ready to make up for the wrong we have done.
0.36	In fact, confession is in one way a piece of making things right. For example, if you have harmed your neighbor’s property or his good name, admitting what you did is one step in making it right.
0.37	But it is far from the whole thing.
0.38	If it is about property, you must pay back what was lost. If it is about someone’s good name, you must repair it as much as you can.
0.39	This is really part of the spirit of this command.
0.40	No one in his right mind would think that if he had stolen something, his confession would be worth anything at all unless he paid it back in full.
0.41	And surely the same rule should apply whenever it is possible to make things right.
0.42	5. The confession must be full and freely given, not skimpy or forced.
0.43	Make it with a free, complete, and honest heart. Make it with an attitude and in a way that will satisfy what the hurt person can fairly expect.
0.44	How can we think God will be happy with anything less?
0.45	Imagine you have wronged a man, and he knows it and feels it.
0.46	You come to him and sort of confess. But instead of making a full confession that settles things, you only hint at the wrong things you did.
0.47	He knows you are not an honest man. He knows you have not even done what your own conscience demands.
0.48	A confession like that cannot do you or your neighbor any good.
0.49	So confession should pour out freely like water, full and complete.
0.50	6. Real confession means throwing yourself on someone’s mercy.
0.51	When people fully confess their sins to God, they stop defending themselves before Him. They throw themselves completely on His mercy.
0.52	They rely on His kindness alone and leave themselves entirely in God’s hands.
0.53	In the same way, when you confess to a person, you throw yourself on his kindness. You admit your wrong and give up every excuse.
0.54	7. When people confess the sins they keep falling into, it shows that they truly want to be holy. It shows they have honestly decided to give up sin and be free of it forever.
0.55	I have often been struck by how differently people react when they hear the teaching about being made holy.
0.56	Tell a group of people who say they are Christians that, by God’s grace, they can be set free from all sin in this life. The way they react will often say a lot about what they are really like.
0.57	It shows exactly how they feel about sin.
0.58	For example, I once preached on this subject in Rochester. As soon as the crowd had left, one man came up to me and said very seriously, “Mr. Finney, that news is too good to be true.”
0.59	But there was a minister in that crowd who did not think it was good news at all.
0.60	He did not act as if he had any wish for the teaching to be true.
0.61	Who has not noticed this very difference?
0.62	Preach about being saved from sin right now to a man who is really longing to be set free from sin. He will greet it with deep interest, and maybe even welcome it with an open heart right away.
0.63	He will accept it very gladly if he thinks it might be true.
0.64	He will long for it to turn out true. His heart will not put up any roadblocks when he honestly looks into the evidence. And he will warmly accept everything the evidence can be shown to support.
0.65	But if he does not want it to be true, he will bitterly pick at it and find fault with it. He will stubbornly push away the evidence. And of course he will likely show, without realizing it, that he is an enemy of all that is right.
0.66	He will show that he has no interest in being set free from sin now—and no real interest in ever being free from sin.
0.67	These things show what a person is really like inside much better than anything else can.
0.68	No one who truly longed in his heart to become holy has ever picked at this teaching and found fault with it.
0.69	Even if he does not believe the teaching is true, and so feels he has to oppose it, he will not have a fault-finding spirit.
0.70	When you see people nitpicking at any teaching, you can be sure they do not want it to be true.
0.71	Their hearts do not like it.
0.72	Lately the teaching that we can be made completely holy has faced a lot of opposition. People claimed they were only being zealous for the truth and fighting against error. But in the end, before God and the whole world, it has been nothing more or less than a fault-finding spirit. It has plainly been opposition to the truth and an unwillingness to see that teaching proved true. Who does not know this?
0.73	It has been sad and shocking to see how much real opposition to holiness has shown up in many places over the last few years.
0.74	I am sure I am not saying this in a harsh, judging spirit.
0.75	Everyone who has kept his eyes open knows it is true. They have seen the real attacks on the teaching of holiness in this life—from pulpits and in the press, through church organizations, and in many other ways.
0.76	But back to the point. When Christians tell their fellow believers about their deep sin, it always shows that they are truly longing for holiness. It shows that they hate the sins they confess. And it shows they are ready to do anything, no matter how humbling, to put to death these horrible sins they hate so much.
H:0.28	III. Why are we told, “Confess your faults to one another”?
0.77	When it comes to harm we have done to others, this command is based on justice.
0.78	Confession is a necessary part of making things right.
0.79	We cannot be fair to the brother we have hurt without it.
0.80	Also, confession is necessary for peace with God.
0.81	It is simply impossible for us to have peace with God if we do not confess the wrongs we know we have done to our neighbor.
0.82	Who needs to be told that God can have nothing to do with injustice—and of course nothing to do with people who act unjustly!
0.83	Doesn’t He see every sin? The neighbor you hurt could come to the Lord and say, “Lord, he has wronged me. You know it. Will You still be close to him now?”
0.84	Confession is just as necessary for peace of mind as it is for peace with God.
0.85	Suppose the whole world were at peace with us. And suppose, if it were possible, that God were at peace with us too. We still could not convince ourselves that we are in the right.
0.86	Our conscience will keep blaming us forever, until we confess and do everything we possibly can in our situation to make up for the wrong things we have done.
0.87	Confession is necessary for peace with the people we have hurt.
0.88	The person we hurt may hold no grudge against us. He may have no attitude that goes against the gospel and no wish to get back at us. Even so, he cannot feel Christian closeness and harmony with us as long as he knows we are not sorry and will not confess the wrongs we know about.
0.89	This is a problem—a wall between us. It cannot be taken down until he is sure that I am honest. And he cannot be sure of that until I confess my fault.
0.90	Confession is necessary for peace and closeness with all people who are fair and good.
0.91	It is true that neither God nor the person who was hurt can be at peace with the wrongdoer until he confesses. The same is just as true of all holy beings.
0.92	Their minds cannot be at peace and in harmony with me while they know I have done wrong and will not confess.
0.93	As long as they know I am in this condition, they have to see me as a lawbreaker. And that has to put a wall between me and them that will never go away.
0.94	They may have no wish to get back at me or hurt me. In fact, they may be praying very earnestly for me, asking God to humble me and break down my pride.
0.95	Their attitude toward me may be nothing but true kindness. Still, until I confess, they cannot share Christian closeness and friendship with me.
0.96	It is simply impossible.
0.97	The same is true even of wicked people.
0.98	They cannot be at peace with me until I confess my wrongs.
0.99	It is amazing, but a wicked man condemns wrongdoing just as truly as a good man does—even though he may do the very same thing himself.
0.100	His judgments about right and wrong can be fair and true, even though his own character is bad.
0.101	Give him a case where his own selfishness does not bribe his conscience and blind his thinking. Then he will decide that wrong is wrong, and that it ought to be confessed and put away.
0.102	So you cannot have the respect of even wicked people unless you confess the wrongs you know about.
0.103	Even the most wicked people, or the devils in hell, cannot be satisfied that what you are doing is right until you confess.
0.104	They might not love you if you became holy. But they certainly can never respect you until you do—never until you confess and give up all the wrong things you know you have done.
0.105	They can never excuse and approve of your sins.
0.106	Confessing your wrongs is necessary for self-respect.
0.107	It is simply impossible for you to respect yourself while you hold back from properly confessing your sins.
0.108	The very laws of your moral nature make this impossible.
0.109	Who does not know this?
0.110	If you do not know it, you certainly can know it. If you pay even a little careful attention to the warnings of your own conscience, you certainly can know it.
0.111	Surely fighting against the demands of a conscience that knows the truth will do you no good. Even if the whole universe left you alone in your sin, your conscience would not and could not leave you alone.
0.112	Its voice would still ring in your ears, and you could not silence its scolding.
0.113	Haven’t you sometimes been ashamed of yourself because you were too proud to confess?
0.114	This very shame about confessing has filled your soul with bitter pain and the sharpest self-blame. Sometimes you have felt that it is a bigger shame and a deeper guilt than the first wrong itself.
0.115	Imagine you happen to meet the very man you have wronged.
0.116	You have the perfect chance to make the confession you know you owe him—but shame and pride keep your mouth shut.
0.117	You do not whisper a single word of confession.
0.118	You walk away full of regret and guilty shame. You can hardly help feeling that this last sin is worse than the first one.
0.119	A doctor in this kind of situation once cried out, “Oh, how full of hell I am!” And perhaps you sometimes feel you have to say the same thing.
0.120	You know that what you feel in your soul is a foretaste of hell. For you are all too sure that you deserve the deepest, darkest place in the home of the damned.
0.121	No one else can trust you or respect you as long as people know you will not confess the wrongs you know about.
0.122	You may work hard to win back their trust—but all your work will be wasted.
0.123	You may hope to live it down. You may try to wash away, with good deeds, the ugly stain that sticks to you because of bad deeds you have not confessed. But you will surely work for nothing.
0.124	Who can think you are an honest man?
0.125	The truth is, they know better. You have given them the clearest possible proof that you are extremely proud and selfish.
0.126	Act as saintly as you like. You still cannot make them trust you as an honest Christian man until you confess your sins wherever the situation calls for confession.
0.127	Think about this too: you can never win back the trust of wise, observant people until you are willing to go further in confessing than you are absolutely forced to go.
0.128	People often go as far as they have to, but not one step further.
0.129	They make it very clear that they only keep going on the path of confession because they are pushed so hard they cannot avoid it.
0.130	Step by step—one step at a time, only as fast as they have to and no faster—they move along.
0.131	I have often stood and watched this strange process in amazement.
0.132	The guilty man finds that one thing has come out too clearly to deny any longer. So he confesses that—but nothing more.
0.133	“No,” he says, “that’s all.”
0.134	Soon another part of the same business comes out, and it is even darker than the first.
0.135	“Sir, you are guilty of this too!”
0.136	“No, I’m not.
0.137	You can’t prove it.”
0.138	“Yes, my friend, it has been proved beyond any doubt.”
0.139	“Oh, well then, I confess that. I am very sorry. But that’s all.
0.140	There is nothing more.”
0.141	Then by the next day some new fact comes up, and he goes down the same road all over again.
0.142	First he denies it, then it is proved, then he confesses. And then, to save what is left of his good name, he begs you to accept his solemn promise that there is nothing more like it to come out.
0.143	A man like that often uses up his good name faster than he realizes.
0.144	He hardly stops to think how worthless his forced confessions become. Nor does he see how little anyone trusts his most solemn promises that he has confessed everything.
0.145	It is much better to go all the way to the bottom right at the start.
0.146	Yes, go to the bottom—pour it all out—all of it. Do it so completely that neither people nor God can find anything more to do with that matter.
0.147	Do this, and there is hope for you.
0.148	People will say about you, “Now we know his heart is honest. Even though he has done wrong, he opens his heart completely, and we can trust him again.”
0.149	It is not in human nature to resist the pull that sincere, honest-hearted confession has on the human heart.
0.150	Also, confessing the wrongs we have done to others is very helpful to both people involved.
0.151	It brings great relief to the guilty person. It rolls a weight as heavy as a mountain off his soul, and it brings back sweet peace and joy.
0.152	On the other side, the person who was wronged often seems to need it.
0.153	You may remember a story I told not long ago about Dr. Hopkins. His deep and wholehearted confessions led to the conversion of his wicked brother-in-law.
0.154	Confessions will often do what nothing else can do.
0.155	They often show wicked people that Christians are completely different from them in spirit.
0.156	Wicked people know they do not confess their faults like that. They know nothing about that kind of broken spirit.
0.157	That is why the confessions of a humble Christian reach their conscience like nothing else can.
0.158	I have often seen people spread conviction of sin like fire through a whole congregation just by simply confessing their own sins.
0.159	I remember right now the story of a minister whose confession to his church started a wonderful revival.
0.160	He had been to Rome during the great revival there. There he became deeply convinced of how guilty he was for caring so little about whether his people were saved.
0.161	He went back home and wrote a sermon for his church. But when he stood up in the pulpit the next Sunday, he could not preach it.
0.162	His heart was so full it felt ready to burst, and he told the people he could not preach a single word.
0.163	“Oh,” he said, “I have been with you for so many years, and how little I have cared about your souls!
0.164	I have tried to please you. I have done my regular duties, but with far too little heart. Oh, how guilty I am for ignoring whether your dying souls were saved!”
0.165	He had hardly started confessing like this when the deacon wanted to confess too, and then other church members. That church building became another Bochim.
0.166	The Lord was there, and His work moved forward with power.
0.167	You can easily see what effect that minister’s confession had.
0.168	Everyone said, “If our minister has sins to confess, so do we!
0.169	If he has reason to confess with such sorrow and bitter tears, we have so much more reason!”
0.170	So his confessions did what none of his sermons had ever done.
0.171	Also, honest confessions bring great honor to the person who makes them.
0.172	There may be no other way that people show more strongly how sincerely they love what is right.
0.173	It surely says a lot about a man’s honesty when he shows he loves what is right so fully and strongly that he does not hold back for a moment from condemning himself for any wrong he knows he has done.
0.174	His love of what is right wins out over his pride in his reputation.
0.175	This man’s confessions are a noble proof that his heart is deeply sincere.
0.176	How strange that people should be ashamed to confess their sins!
0.177	They could not make this big mistake unless they were “treating darkness as light and light as darkness.”
0.178	Isn’t it very honorable for a man who has done wrong to make it right—for a man who has sinned to make a full and free confession?
0.179	How strange that people should be afraid to confess!
0.180	They might as well be ashamed to show that they are honest. They might as well be ashamed to let the world see that they care more about what is right than about a reputation they do not deserve—a reputation for being something they are not.
0.181	Confessions help take the weight off the mind of the person who was hurt, and they lead him to forgive.
0.182	Wrongs done to people, especially by those who say they are Christians, often become big traps and temptations for them.
0.183	Many people are overcome by them. They fall into a bad state of mind, where God’s truth and grace seem to do their hearts no good.
0.184	Maybe they know it is wrong to hold on to such feelings. But, they say, they have been treated very badly—and by someone who claims to be a Christian, too! And they find it hard to get what happened out of their minds.
0.185	In cases like this, an honest confession by the one who did the wrong is the natural cure.
0.186	It usually does more than anything else—more than everything else put together—to remove that terrible stumbling block.
0.187	Christian reader, is any sinner stumbling like this because of some sin of yours?
0.188	Be careful that you do not trap his soul and bring him to ruin.
0.189	Be careful that the blood of his murdered soul is not found on your clothes!
0.190	I have sometimes seen one man keep provoking another until he made him angry, and then scold him for that very anger, instead of blaming himself for causing it.
0.191	This is cruel and wicked.
0.192	The man who has made another person angry like this ought to humble himself and confess completely. He should say, “I have acted in a very low and mean way. I tempted you to sin, and then I scolded you for giving in to my temptation—when I should have scolded myself most of all.
0.193	I am sorry. I am grieved by my own wickedness, and I condemn it.”
0.194	You may remember a story that is told about a man who was against Wesley’s teaching about being made holy. He wanted to prove that a certain person who believed that teaching was not completely holy. So he took a washbowl full of dirty water and threw it in that person’s face.
0.195	Now let’s suppose the man who was wronged got offended and angry. Seeing this, the one who did the wrong gloats and says, “Some holiness this is—getting angry over such a small thing!”
0.196	But notice how unbelievably low and wicked it is for this man to tempt someone to sin like that.
0.197	He should break down and confess his own sin with shame.
0.198	He should say, “I am the guilty wretch in all this!”
0.199	If he did this, repentance would pour freely from both of their hearts.
0.200	Also, confessing is a strong way to win the prayers of the people you have sinned against and to whom you confess.
0.201	Nothing works better to make them want to pray for you right away.
0.202	How naturally they cry out, “O Lord, forgive this man, because he admits his wrong. Forgive all his sins and show him the light of Your face.”
0.203	It is also very helpful to confess the sins we keep falling into to people we have not hurt, so that we can get help to overcome those sins.
0.204	It usually makes our fellow Christians care a great deal about us.
0.205	Who does not know how strongly the heart is moved to pray for people who confess the sins they keep falling into like this?
0.206	Haven’t you experienced this yourself?
0.207	IV. In this setting, it is easy to understand what it means to pray for one another.
0.208	It means that we care deeply about the spiritual well-being of those who confess their faults. It means we use our influence with God to win their pardon.
0.209	It is like signing a petition asking that they be set free from the punishment for breaking the law.
0.210	Imagine that your neighbor has broken the law of the state. He is found guilty and sent to the state prison for life. But he is deeply sorry and shows that he is a changed man. Now someone brings you a petition asking for his pardon, and they ask you to sign it.
0.211	Will you join in the request?
0.212	Will you say, “Does he confess his sin?
0.213	If he does, I can pray for his pardon.”
0.214	In the same way, when people confess their sins to God and to others, you should pray for God to forgive them.
0.215	God commands you to do it.
0.216	V. Obeying this command means having a real spirit of forgiveness. It means wanting God to forgive, and showing that wish through real prayer to God.
0.217	This is too obvious to need an example.
0.218	When people confess the sins they keep falling into, it means we should care about their becoming holy and pray hard to God for this great result.
H:0.52	Remarks.
0.219	1. Wrongs done against the public should be confessed in public.
0.220	No one can have any doubt about this.
0.221	If a confession should be made at all, it should be made to the people who were sinned against.
0.222	2. Wrongs against individuals may be done either in public or in private. Either way, the confession should match how the wrong was done and how public it was.
0.223	Suppose a man hurts another man’s reputation in public by writing lies about him or preaching lies about him. Then the sin is public. Clearly the confession should reach as far as the wrong did.
0.224	If you can, you must take back your words just as widely as you spread the wrong.
0.225	3. You can see why many people never overcome the sins they keep falling into.
0.226	They are never humble and honest enough to confess them. They never pour out their hearts to their fellow believers so as to win their care and prayers for themselves.
0.227	They never use the great moral lever that confession gives. Confession slides under a person’s giant sins like a lever, to dig them up and roll them out and away forever.
0.228	4. You can see how wrong it is to think that confessing to God is enough, and that confessing to people is useless and not required.
0.229	The Catholic teaching about confession has pushed many Protestants to the opposite extreme. So many churches are against having anyone confess anything at all in front of others.
0.230	But just because Catholics have misused the teaching about confession, it does not at all mean we should throw it all away.
0.231	It does not mean we have no duty to confess to the people we have hurt.
0.232	Not at all! If that way of thinking were right, we would soon lose all our Bibles.
0.233	5. I have often seen people suffer for a long time under a load of sins that no one else knew about. These were sins they might not have needed to confess at all, except for their great pride.
0.234	But God saw that their pride had to be humbled, and He could not give their souls peace until it was.
0.235	So let a man like that go to the people whose good opinion he cares about most and confess his sins. Let him really take up his cross in this, and not go easy on his proud heart at all. He will find it is an excellent cure.
0.236	I have often seen this done.
0.237	A man goes to someone he respects very much and says, “My pride has trapped me. It is dragging me away from God and down to hell, and I am determined to defeat it.”
0.238	Then he pours out his confession of sin.
0.239	This is like grabbing a big club to crush the snake’s head—the head of wicked pride. And it usually works.
0.240	People in this situation will often find that confession is absolutely necessary.
0.241	6. When both people are to blame for a personal fault, neither one should ever wait for the other to confess first.
0.242	Let each one hurry to confess as warmly and fully as he can. Then the right spirit will win out, and each will encourage the other to do good.
0.243	Often one person holds back because he is afraid the other will use his confession against him.
0.244	It does not matter if he does.
0.245	I mean that, compared with everything else, it does not matter enough to think about for a moment.
0.246	It is far, far more important that you do what is right before God, the world, and the man you have hurt, than that you hold back your confession to keep him from misusing it.
0.247	Besides, there is not much danger that he will.
0.248	And if he does, he alone is responsible.
0.249	I once knew a man in a situation like this who said, “I will confess my wrong. If my neighbor does not want to confess his, or if he chooses to misuse what I confess, he alone must carry the blame.”
0.250	He poured out his confessions like water and found sweet relief and peace in his soul. Soon after that he died. That neighbor, and every enemy he had, grieved bitterly over his grave. They admitted that at least one good man had lived.
0.251	I once knew a church that was in serious trouble. Its members were set against each other, with strong prejudices, and they had taken sides. But in the end, the spirit of confession began to win out. Each man felt he was more to blame than his neighbor. Now the only contest was to see who could confess first and confess the most.
0.252	The argument now was not about each one proving that his neighbor was at fault. Instead, each one argued that he himself had been the worst sinner, and the main cause of both his own sins and his neighbor’s.
0.253	Every man was ready to take his hand off his neighbor and lay it on himself.
0.254	This was good. If you could have seen those humbled Christians confessing their sins, you would have said, “It is good to come down low and get the spirit of confession.”
