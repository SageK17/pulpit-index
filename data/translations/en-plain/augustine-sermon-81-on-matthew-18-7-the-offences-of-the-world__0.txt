You will glow with love, and you will be made strong against stumbling blocks.

0.21	For example, a man may face some hard necessity, and he is weighed down by the pressure of trouble.
0.22	Being weighed down by the pressure of trouble is not a stumbling block.
0.23	Even the Martyrs were pressed by this kind of pressure, but they were not crushed.
0.24	Watch out for a stumbling block. But do not worry so much about the pressure of trouble.
0.25	Pressure presses on you. A stumbling block crushes you.
0.26	So what is the difference between the two?

0.29	In short, if you give praise to God while this trouble presses on you, that press will be so useful to you! It squeezes such good oil out of you!
0.30	The Apostles sat in chains under a press like this. And in that press they sang a hymn to God.
0.31	What precious oil was squeezed and pressed out of them!

0.33	So where was the “stumbling block”?
0.34	It came when his wife came to him and said, “Say something against God, and die.”
0.35	Everything had been taken from him by the devil. But an Eve was left to this man who was being tested. She was not there to comfort her husband, but to tempt him.
0.36	So you can see where the stumbling block was.
0.37	She made his miseries seem even worse, and her own miseries along with his. And she began to persuade him to speak evil against God.
0.38	But he was “meek,” because “God had taught him from His law and given him rest from the days of trouble.” He had “great peace” in his heart, because he “loved the law of God, and nothing made him stumble.”
0.39	She was a stumbling block, but not to him.
0.40	In short, look at the meek man. Look at a man taught in the law of God. I mean the eternal law of God.
0.41	In Job’s time, the law written on tablets had not yet been given to the Jews. But the eternal law still remained in the hearts of godly people. The law given to the people was copied from that eternal law.
0.42	By the law of God he had “been given rest from the days of trouble,” and he “had great peace because he loved the law of God.” So see how “meek” he is, and what he answers.

0.44	He says, “You are talking the way one of the foolish women talks.
0.45	If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, should we not bear the bad?”

0.49	What good is it that we plant and water, unless God makes it grow?
0.50	“The one who plants is nothing, and the one who waters is nothing. It is God who makes it grow.”
0.51	Listen, whoever you are who wants to be “meek.” You want “rest from the days of trouble.” You “love the law of God.” You want there to be “no stumbling block for you.” You want to “have great peace.” You want to “possess the earth and delight in plenty of peace.” Listen, whoever you are who wants to be “meek.”
0.52	Whatever good you do, do not be pleased with yourself.
0.53	“For God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”
0.54	So whatever good you do, let nothing but God please you. Whatever evil you suffer, do not let God displease you.
0.55	What more do you need?
0.56	Do this, and you will live.
0.57	The days of trouble will not overwhelm you. You will escape what is said: “Woe to the world because of stumbling blocks.”
0.58	Which world has woe because of stumbling blocks? The world of which it is said, “And the world did not know Him.”
0.59	Not the world of which it is said, “God was in Christ, reconciling the world to Himself.”
0.60	There is an evil world, and there is a good world. The evil world is all the evil people in this world. The good world is all the good people in this world.
0.61	We often see this with a field.
0.62	This field is full. Full of what?
0.63	Of wheat. Yet we also say, and say truly, “This field is full of chaff.”
0.64	It is the same with a tree. It is full of fruit.
0.65	Someone else says it is full of leaves.
0.66	The one who says it is full of fruit is right. And the one who says it is full of leaves is right too.
0.67	All the leaves have not taken away the room for the fruit. And all the fruit has not pushed out the many leaves.
0.68	It is full of both. But the wind searches out the one, and the farmer gathers in the other.
0.69	So when you hear, “Woe to the world because of stumbling blocks,” do not be afraid. “Love the law of God, and nothing will make you stumble.”
0.70	4. But suppose your wife comes to you and advises you to do something evil.
0.71	You love her as a wife should be loved. She is one of your members.
0.72	You have just heard the Gospel: “But if your eye makes you stumble, if your hand makes you stumble, if your foot makes you stumble, cut them off and throw them away from you.”
0.73	Someone may be dear to you. Someone may be highly respected by you. Let him stay highly respected by you, and stay your beloved member, only as long as he does not begin to make you stumble. That means: as long as he does not advise you to do something evil.
0.74	Now listen, and see that this is what “stumbling block” means.
0.75	I have given the example of Job and his wife. But the word “stumbling block” was not used there.
0.76	Listen to the Gospel. When the Lord foretold His suffering, Peter began to persuade Him not to suffer.
0.77	“Get behind Me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to Me.”
0.78	Here the Lord, who has given you an example of how to live, has clearly taught you two things: what a “stumbling block” is, and how to avoid one.
0.79	A little while before, He had said to Peter, “Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah.” So He had shown that Peter was His member.
0.80	But when Peter begins to be a stumbling block, He cuts off the member. Only, He restored the member and put it back in its place.
0.81	So the person who begins to persuade you to do something evil will be a “stumbling block” to you.
0.82	And here, dear friends, be careful. Most of the time this does not happen through an evil will. It happens through a good will that is mistaken.
0.83	Your friend who loves you, and whom you love in return—your father, your brother, your child, your wife—sees you in a bad situation. And he wants you to do something evil.
0.84	What do I mean by “sees you in a bad situation”?
0.85	He sees you under some pressure of trouble.
0.86	Maybe you are suffering this pressure because you are doing what is right. You are suffering it because you will not give false testimony.
0.87	I am only speaking as an example.
0.88	There are plenty of examples. For “woe to the world because of stumbling blocks.”
0.89	For example, some powerful person wants to cover up his robbery and plunder. So he asks you to serve as a false witness.
0.90	You refuse. You refuse to swear falsely, so that you do not deny Him who is true.
0.91	To keep this short: he is angry, he is powerful, and he oppresses you. Then a friend comes who does not want you in this pressure of trouble, in this bad situation. He says, “Please, do what you are told. What’s the big deal?”
0.92	And then perhaps he speaks as Satan spoke to the Lord: “It is written about You, ‘He will give His angels orders concerning You, so that You do not strike Your foot against a stone.’”
0.93	Or perhaps this friend of yours sees that you are a Christian. So he wants to use the Law to persuade you to do what he thinks you should do.
0.94	“Do what the other man tells you.”
0.95	“What? Do what the other man wants.”
0.96	“But it is a lie. It is false.”
0.97	“Well, haven’t you read, ‘All men are liars’?”

0.99	He is a friend. What will you do?
0.100	He is an eye, he is a hand: “Cut it off and throw it away from you.”

0.104	Where there is disagreement, there is disease, or a sore.

0.106	But he is a stumbling block to you. “Cut him off and throw him away from you.”
0.107	Do not agree with him. Drive him away from your ears. Maybe he will come back a better man.
