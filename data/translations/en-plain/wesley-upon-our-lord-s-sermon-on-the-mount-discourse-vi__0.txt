0.1	“Be careful not to do your good deeds for the poor in front of people just so they will see you. If you do, you will get no reward from your Father in heaven.
0.2	So when you give to the poor, don’t blow a trumpet to announce it, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets. They do it so that people will praise them.
0.3	I tell you the truth, they already have their reward.
0.4	But when you give to the poor, don’t let your left hand know what your right hand is doing. Then your giving will be in secret. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will himself reward you openly.
0.5	And when you pray, don’t be like the hypocrites. They love to stand and pray in the synagogues and on the street corners, so that people will see them.
0.6	I tell you the truth, they already have their reward.
0.7	But when you pray, go into your own room and shut the door. Pray to your Father, who is in secret. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you openly.
0.8	And when you pray, don’t keep repeating empty words, as the pagans do. They think they will be heard because they talk so much.
0.9	So don’t be like them. Your Father knows what you need before you ask him.
0.10	This, then, is how you should pray: Our Father in heaven, may your name be honoured as holy.
0.11	Let your kingdom come. Let what you want be done on earth, just as it is done in heaven.
0.12	Give us today our bread for this day.
0.13	And forgive us our debts, just as we forgive those who owe us.
0.14	And don’t lead us into temptation, but rescue us from evil. For the kingdom, the power and the glory belong to you forever.
0.15	Amen. If you forgive people when they wrong you, your Father in heaven will forgive you too. But if you don’t forgive people when they wrong you, your Father won’t forgive the wrongs you do.” Matthew 6:1–15.
0.16	1. In the chapter before this, our Lord described the religion of the heart, in all its different parts.
0.17	He showed us the attitudes of the soul that make someone a real Christian. These are the inner qualities that make up the “holiness without which no one will see the Lord.” They are feelings and desires that come from the right source—a living faith in God through Christ Jesus. When they come from there, they are good in themselves, deep down, and God is pleased with them.
0.18	In this chapter he goes on to show how all our actions can also be made holy and good and pleasing to God. This is true even of actions that are neither good nor bad in themselves. What makes them holy is a pure and holy purpose.
0.19	He explains at length that anything done without this purpose is worth nothing to God.
0.20	But any outward work that is given to God in this way is very precious in his eyes.
0.21	2. He shows why this pure purpose is needed. First, he looks at the actions people usually call religious. And they really are religious when they are done with the right purpose.
0.22	Some of these are usually called works of devotion to God. The rest are called works of kindness or mercy.
0.23	Of the works of kindness, he names giving to the poor in particular. Of the works of devotion, he names prayer and fasting.
0.24	But the instructions he gives for these apply just as much to every work of kindness or mercy.
0.25	I. 1. First, let’s look at works of mercy.
0.26	He says, “Be careful not to do your good deeds for the poor in front of people just so they will see you. If you do, you will get no reward from your Father in heaven.”
0.27	“Not to do your good deeds for the poor”: only this one kind of good deed is named, but every act of kindness is included. That means everything we give, or say, or do, that helps our neighbour. It means anything by which another person may be helped in any way, in body or in soul.
0.28	It includes feeding the hungry and clothing those who have nothing to wear. It includes welcoming or helping strangers and visiting people who are sick or in prison. It includes comforting those who are suffering, teaching those who don’t know, warning those who do wrong, and cheering on and encouraging those who do right. And if there is any other work of mercy, this instruction covers it just the same.
0.29	2. “Be careful not to do your good deeds for the poor in front of people just so they will see you.” What is forbidden here is not simply doing good where people can see it. The fact that others see what we do does not make the action any worse or any better. What is forbidden is doing it in front of people “so they will see you”—when that is the aim, and the only aim.
0.30	I say the only aim, because in some cases this may be part of our aim. We may want some of our actions to be seen, and God may still be pleased with them.
0.31	We may want our light to shine in front of people. That is fine when our conscience, through the Holy Spirit, tells us that our final goal in wanting them to see our good works is “that they may give glory to our Father in heaven.”
0.32	But be careful not to do even the smallest thing to get glory for yourself. Be careful that wanting people’s praise has no place at all in your works of mercy.
0.33	If you are after your own glory, if you are trying in any way to win honour from people, then whatever you do with that aim is worth nothing. It is not done for the Lord. He does not accept it. For this “you get no reward from our Father in heaven.”
0.34	3. “So when you give to the poor, don’t blow a trumpet to announce it, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, so that people will praise them.” Here the word synagogue does not mean a place of worship. It means any public place where people gather, such as the market square or the place where merchants meet to trade.
0.35	Among the Jews, rich men often did this, especially the Pharisees. When they were about to give a large gift to the poor, they had a trumpet blown in front of them in the busiest parts of the city.
0.36	They said it was to call the poor together to receive the gift. But the real reason was so that people would praise them.
0.37	But don’t be like them.
0.38	Don’t have a trumpet blown in front of you.
0.39	Don’t show off when you do good.
0.40	Aim only for the honour that comes from God.
0.41	Those who look for people’s praise already have their reward. They will get no praise from God.
0.42	4. “But when you give to the poor, don’t let your left hand know what your right hand is doing.” This is a common saying, and it means: do it as secretly as you can. Keep it as secret as you can while still doing it at all—for you must not leave it undone. Don’t miss any chance to do good, whether in secret or in the open. And keep it as secret as you can while still doing it in the most helpful way.
0.43	Here too there is an exception. Suppose you are fully convinced in your own mind that if you don’t hide the good you do, it will help you do more good, or it will stir others to do more good. Then you should not hide it. Then let your light be seen, and let it “shine on everyone in the house.”
0.44	But unless God’s glory and the good of people require something else, act as privately and quietly as the thing allows. Do this “so that your giving will be in secret. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you openly.” Perhaps he will do it in this present world—there are many examples of this written down from every age. But he will certainly do it in the world to come, before the great gathering of all people and angels.
0.45	II. 1. From works of kindness or mercy our Lord moves on to what are called works of devotion to God.
0.46	He says, “And when you pray, don’t be like the hypocrites. They love to stand and pray in the synagogues and on the street corners, so that people will see them.” “Don’t be like the hypocrites.”
0.47	So hypocrisy, or being fake, is the first thing we must guard against when we pray.
0.48	Be careful not to say what you don’t mean.
0.49	Prayer is lifting up the heart to God. Without this, all the words of a prayer are nothing but pretending.
0.50	So whenever you try to pray, make sure your one aim is to talk with God, to lift up your heart to him, to pour out your soul to him. Don’t be like the hypocrites. They love to, or they are used to, “stand and pray in the synagogues”—the trading places or market squares—“and on the street corners,” wherever the most people are, “so that people will see them.” That was the only aim, the only reason and goal, of the prayers they said there.
0.51	“I tell you the truth, they already have their reward.” They should not expect any reward from your Father in heaven.
0.52	2. But wanting people’s praise is not the only thing that cuts us off from any reward in heaven. It is not the only thing that leaves us no reason to expect God’s blessing on our works, whether works of devotion or of mercy.
0.53	A pure purpose is ruined just as much by aiming at any earthly reward at all.
0.54	We may say our prayers, go to church to worship God, or help the poor. But if we do it to gain money or some advantage, God is not one bit more pleased with it than if we did it to be praised.
0.55	Any earthly aim makes every action disgusting to the Lord, however good it may look to people. That includes any reason at all on this side of eternity—any aim except bringing glory to God and happiness to people for God’s sake.
0.56	3. “But when you pray, go into your own room and shut the door. Pray to your Father, who is in secret.” There is a time when you should give glory to God in public, praying to him and praising him in the large gathering of his people.
0.57	But sometimes you want to tell God your requests more fully and in more detail. It may be in the evening, or in the morning, or at noon. Then “go into your own room and shut the door.”
0.58	Be as private as you can.
0.59	(Only don’t skip prayer, whether you have a room and some privacy or not. If you can, pray to God where no one sees you but him. But if you can’t, pray to God anyway.)
0.60	In this way “pray to your Father, who is in secret.” Pour out your heart to him. “And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you openly.”
0.61	4. “And when you pray,” even in secret, “don’t keep repeating empty words, as the pagans do”—mē battalogēsēte.
0.62	Don’t use lots of words that mean nothing.
0.63	Don’t say the same thing over and over again. Don’t think your prayers will work better because they are long, as the pagans do. They think they will be heard because they talk so much.
0.64	What is corrected here is not simply long prayers, any more than short ones. First, it is length without meaning—saying a lot and meaning little or nothing. It is using empty repetitions. (Not all repetitions are wrong. Our Lord himself prayed three times, repeating the same words.) It means empty repetitions like those of the pagans, who recited the names of their gods over and over. People do the same among those who are called Christians—and not only among the Roman Catholics. They say the same string of prayers over and over without ever feeling what they are saying. Second, it is the idea that we will be heard because we say a lot. It is imagining that God measures prayers by how long they are, and that he likes best the ones with the most words, which sound longest in his ears.
0.65	These are examples of superstition and foolishness. Everyone who is called by the name of Christ should leave them to the pagans—to those on whom the glorious light of the gospel has never shone.
0.66	5. So don’t be like them. You have tasted God’s grace in Christ Jesus. You are fully convinced that your Father knows what you need before you ask him.
0.67	So the point of praying is not to tell God something, as if he didn’t already know what you need. It is more to remind yourselves. It fixes the feeling of those needs more deeply in your hearts. It also fixes the sense that you always depend on him, the only one who can supply everything you need.
0.68	Prayer is not so much to move God. He is always more ready to give than you are to ask. It is more to move you, so that you will be willing and ready to receive the good things he has prepared for you.
0.69	III. 1. Our Lord has taught what prayer really is and what it is for. Next he adds an example of it: that divine prayer which here seems mainly to be given as a pattern—the model and standard for all our prayers. “This, then, is how you should pray.”
0.70	But in another place he tells us to use these very words: “He said to them, When you pray, say...” (Luke 11:2.)
0.71	2. Speaking generally about this divine prayer, we can notice several things. First, it contains everything we can sensibly or rightly pray for.
0.72	There is nothing we need to ask God for, and nothing we can ask without offending him, that is not included in this prayer. It covers it all, either directly or indirectly.
0.73	Second, it contains everything we can sensibly or rightly want. It includes whatever brings glory to God, and whatever is needed or useful—not only for ourselves, but for every creature in heaven and on earth.
0.74	In fact, our prayers are the proper test of our desires. Nothing belongs in our desires that does not belong in our prayers. What we may not pray for, we should not want either.
0.75	Third, it contains all our duty to God and to people. Whatever is pure and holy, whatever God asks of human beings, whatever pleases him, whatever helps our neighbour—all of it is stated or hinted at in this prayer.
0.76	3. It has three parts: the opening, the requests, and the closing words of praise, called the doxology.
0.77	The opening, “Our Father in heaven,” lays a general foundation for prayer. It sums up what we must first know about God before we can pray, confident that he will hear us.
0.78	It also shows us all the attitudes of heart we need when we come to God. These are most necessary if we want God to accept our prayers or our lives.
0.79	4. “Our Father.” If he is a Father, then he is good. Then he loves his children.
0.80	And this is the first and greatest reason to pray.
0.81	God wants to bless us, so let’s ask for a blessing. “Our Father”—our Creator, the one who gave us our being. He raised us from the dust of the earth. He breathed the breath of life into us, and we became living souls.
0.82	And if he made us, let’s ask him. He will not hold back any good thing from what his own hands have made. “Our Father”—the one who keeps us alive. Day after day he keeps up the life he gave us. Because of his continuing love we receive life and breath and everything else, now and every moment.
0.83	So let’s come to him all the more boldly, and we will “receive mercy, and grace to help us when we need it.”
0.84	Above all, he is the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, and of everyone who believes in him. He makes us right with himself “freely by his grace, through Jesus, who paid to set us free.” He has “wiped out all our sins, and healed all our weaknesses.” He has taken us in as his own children, by adoption and grace. And “because” we “are sons, he has sent the Spirit of his Son into” our “hearts, crying, Abba, Father.” He “has given us new birth from a seed that cannot decay,” and “created us new in Christ Jesus.”
0.85	That is why we know he always hears us. That is why we pray to him without stopping.
0.86	We pray because we love. And “we love him because he loved us first.”
0.87	5. “Our Father.” Not mine only, as I cry out to him now, but ours in the widest possible sense.
0.88	He is the God and “Father of the spirits of all people,” the Father of angels and humans. Even the pagans admitted that he was this: Pater te theōn te.
0.89	He is the Father of the whole universe, of all the families in heaven and on earth.
0.90	So he does not play favourites.
0.91	He loves everything he has made.
0.92	“He is loving to every person, and his mercy covers everything he has made.”
0.93	And the Lord delights in those who fear him and trust in his mercy. He delights in those who trust in him through the Son he loves, knowing that they are “accepted in his dearly loved Son.”
0.94	But “if God loved us so much, we should also love one another”—yes, all people. For “God loved the world so much that he gave his only Son,” even to die, so that they “would not perish but have everlasting life.”
0.95	6. “In heaven.” He is high and lifted up, God over all, blessed forever. He sits above the circle of the heavens and sees everything in heaven and on earth. His eye sees through the whole world of created things—yes, and even through the night that was never created. To him “all his works are known,” and all the works of every creature. They are known not only “from the beginning of the world”—a poor, weak translation—but ap’ aiōnos, from all eternity, from everlasting to everlasting. He makes the armies of heaven, as well as human beings, cry out in wonder and amazement: O the depth! How deep are the riches of both the wisdom and the knowledge of God!
0.96	In heaven: he is the Lord and Ruler of all, who watches over all things and arranges them. You are the King of kings and Lord of lords, the blessed and only Ruler. You are strong and wrapped in power, doing whatever you please. You are the Almighty, for whenever you want to do something, you are able to do it.
0.97	In heaven: he is there in a special way. Heaven is your throne, “the place where your glory” especially “lives.”
0.98	But you are not there only. You fill heaven and earth, the whole of space. “Heaven and earth are full of your glory.
0.99	Glory to you, O Lord Most High!”
0.100	That is why we should “serve the Lord with fear, and rejoice before him with awe.”
0.101	That is why we should think, speak, and act as if we are always under the eye of the Lord, the King, right in his presence.
0.102	7. “May your name be honoured as holy.” This is the first of the six requests that make up the prayer itself.
0.103	The name of God is God himself—God’s nature, as far as people can know it.
0.104	So it means his existence, together with all his qualities or perfections. It means his eternity. This is shown especially by his great name that belongs to no one else, JEHOVAH. The apostle John translates it as to A kai to Ō, archē kai telos, ho ōn kai ho ēn kai ho erchomenos—“the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end; the one who is, who was, and who is to come.” It means his fullness of being, shown by his other great name, I AM THAT I AM! It means that he is present everywhere. It means that he is all-powerful. He is in fact the only one who acts in the material world. All matter is dull and lifeless by nature. It moves only when the finger of God moves it. And he is the source of action in every creature, seen and unseen. Without the constant flow and working of his almighty power, no creature could act or even exist. It means his wisdom, which we clearly see from the things that are seen, from the beautiful order of the universe. It means his Trinity in Unity, and Unity in Trinity. This is shown to us in the very first line of his written word: bara Elohim, which literally means “the Gods created.” There a plural noun is joined to a singular verb. It is also shown in every later part of what he revealed through all his holy prophets and apostles. It means his perfect purity and holiness. And above all it means his love, which is the very brightness of his glory.
0.105	When we pray that God, or his name, may “be honoured as holy” or glorified, we are praying that he may be known as he really is. We pray that he may be known by all who are able to know him—by all beings who can think—and with feelings that match that knowledge. We pray that he may be properly honoured, feared, and loved by all in heaven above and on the earth below. We pray this for all angels and humans, whom he made able to know and love him forever for this very purpose.
0.106	8. “Let your kingdom come.” This is closely connected to the request before it.
0.107	We pray that his kingdom, the kingdom of Christ, may come, so that God’s name may be honoured as holy.
0.108	This kingdom comes to a person when he “turns from his sins and believes the gospel.” It comes when God teaches him to know himself, and also to know Jesus Christ and him crucified.
0.109	“This is eternal life: to know the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom he has sent.” In the same way, this is the kingdom of God begun here below, set up in the believer’s heart. “The Lord God Almighty” then “reigns,” when he is known through Christ Jesus.
0.110	He takes up his mighty power so that he can bring everything under his rule.
0.111	He keeps going in the soul, conquering and still conquering, until he has put everything under his feet. He goes on until “every thought is taken captive to obey Christ.”
0.112	One day God will “give his Son the nations as his inheritance, and the ends of the earth as his possession.” “All kingdoms will bow down before him, and all nations will serve him.” “The mountain of the Lord’s house”—the Church of Christ—“will be set up on the top of the mountains.” “The full number of the Gentiles will come in, and all Israel will be saved.” When that happens, everyone will see that “the Lord is King, and has clothed himself in glory.” He will appear to every human soul as King of kings and Lord of lords.
0.113	And it is right for all who love his appearing to pray that he will hurry that time along. They pray that his kingdom, the kingdom of grace, may come quickly and swallow up all the kingdoms of the earth. They pray that all people will receive him as their King and truly believe in his name. They pray that all will be filled with righteousness, peace, and joy, with holiness and happiness—until they are taken from here into his heavenly kingdom, to reign with him there forever and ever.
0.114	We also pray for this in the words “Let your kingdom come.” We pray for the coming of his everlasting kingdom, the kingdom of glory in heaven. That kingdom is the continuation and completion of the kingdom of grace on earth.
0.115	So this request, like the one before it, is offered for the whole creation of thinking beings. All of them have a stake in this great event: the final making new of all things. Then God will put an end to misery and sin, to weakness and death. He will take all things into his own hands and set up the kingdom that lasts through all ages.
0.116	The solemn words in the prayer at the burial of the dead match this exactly: “We ask you, if it pleases you, in your gracious goodness, soon to complete the number of your chosen ones, and to hurry the coming of your kingdom. Then we, with all those who have died in the true faith of your holy name, may have our perfect completion and joy, in both body and soul, in your everlasting glory.”
0.117	9. “Let what you want be done on earth, as it is in heaven.” This follows necessarily and immediately wherever the kingdom of God has come—wherever God lives in the soul by faith, and Christ reigns in the heart by love.
0.118	Many people, perhaps most, when they first look at these words, probably imagine they only express, or ask for, resignation. They think it is a prayer to be ready to accept God’s will, whatever it may be for us.
0.119	And this is without doubt a godly and excellent attitude, a most precious gift from God.
0.120	But this is not what we are praying for in this request—at least, not in its main and first meaning.
0.121	When we say, “Let what you want be done on earth, as it is in heaven,” we are not praying so much to accept God’s will passively. We are praying to do God’s will actively.
0.122	How do the angels of God in heaven do it—those who now circle his throne with joy?
0.123	They do it willingly. They love his commands, and they gladly listen to his words.
0.124	Doing his will is their food and drink. It is their highest glory and joy.
0.125	They do it all the time. Their willing service never stops.
0.126	They don’t rest day or night. They use every hour to carry out his commands, to accomplish his plans, and to do what he has decided. (I am speaking in human terms. Our ways of measuring time—days and nights and hours—have no place in eternity.)
0.127	And they do it perfectly.
0.128	Angels’ minds have no sin and no fault.
0.129	It is true that “the stars are not pure in his sight”—even the morning stars that sing together before him.
0.130	“In his sight” means compared with him. Compared with God, even the angels are not pure.
0.131	But this does not mean that they are not pure in themselves.
0.132	Of course they are. They are without spot and without blame.
0.133	They are completely devoted to his will, and they obey him perfectly in everything.
0.134	If we look at this from another angle, we can see that the angels of God in heaven do all of God’s will.
0.135	And they do nothing else—nothing except what they are completely sure is his will.
0.136	Also, they do all of God’s will in the way he wants it done, the way that pleases him, and no other way.
0.137	Yes, and they do it only because it is his will. That is their purpose, and they have no other reason.
0.138	10. So when we pray that God’s will may “be done on earth as it is in heaven,” this is what we mean. We pray that everyone who lives on earth, the whole human race, may do the will of their Father in heaven as willingly as the holy angels do. We pray that people may do it all the time, just as the angels do, with no break in their willing service. Yes, and we pray that they may do it perfectly—that “the God of peace, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, may make them perfect in every good work to do his will, and work in them all that pleases him.”
0.139	In other words, we pray that we and all people may do the whole will of God in everything. We pray that we may do nothing else—not the smallest thing—except what is God’s holy and pleasing will.
0.140	We pray that we may do the whole will of God in the way he wants, in the way that pleases him. And finally, we pray that we may do it because it is his will. We pray that this may be the only reason and basis, the whole and only motive, for everything we think, say, or do.
0.141	11. “Give us today our bread for this day.” In the first three requests we have been praying for all people.
0.142	Now we come more specifically to asking for what we ourselves need.
0.143	Even here, though, we are not told to keep our prayer only to ourselves. This request, and each of the ones after it, can be prayed for the whole Church of Christ on earth.
0.144	By “bread” we can understand everything we need, whether for our souls or our bodies—ta pros zōēn kai eusebeian, the things that belong to life and godliness. We don’t mean only ordinary bread, which our Lord calls the food that spoils. We mean much more the spiritual bread, the grace of God, the food that lasts to eternal life.
0.145	Many of the early church leaders believed that the bread of the Lord’s Supper is meant here too. In the beginning the whole Church of Christ received it every day. They valued it highly as the main channel through which the grace of his Spirit was brought to the souls of all God’s children—until the love of many grew cold.
0.146	“Our daily bread.” Different Bible scholars have explained the word we translate “daily” in different ways.
0.147	But its plainest and most natural meaning seems to be the one kept in almost all translations, old and new: what is enough for this day, and then for each day as it comes.
0.148	12. “Give us.” We don’t claim anything as our right. We ask only because of God’s free mercy.
0.149	We don’t deserve the air we breathe, the ground that holds us up, or the sun that shines on us.
0.150	We admit that all we deserve is hell. But God loves us freely. So we ask him to give us what we can’t get for ourselves, any more than we can earn it from him.
0.151	But God’s goodness and power are no reason for us to sit around doing nothing.
0.152	His will is that we work hard at everything and do our very best, as if our success came naturally from our own wisdom and strength. And then we are to depend on him as if we had done nothing, because he is the giver of every good and perfect gift.
0.153	“Today.” For we are not to worry about tomorrow.
0.154	This is the very reason our wise Creator divided life into these little pieces of time, so clearly separate from each other. He did it so we could see each day as a fresh gift from God, another life that we can give to his glory. And he did it so each evening could be like the end of life, with nothing beyond it for us to see but eternity.
0.155	13. “And forgive us the wrongs we have done, as we forgive those who have wronged us.” Only sin can keep God’s generous gifts from flowing out to every creature. So this request naturally comes after the one before it. Once every obstacle is taken away, we can trust the God of love more fully for every kind of good thing.
0.156	“The wrongs we have done.” The word really means our debts.
0.157	That is how Scripture often describes our sins. Every sin puts us in fresh debt to God. And we already owe him, so to speak, ten thousand talents.
0.158	So what can we answer when he says, “Pay me what you owe”?
0.159	We are completely bankrupt. We have nothing to pay with. We have wasted everything we had.
0.160	So if he deals with us by the strictness of his law, if he demands what he has every right to demand, he would have to order us to be “tied hand and foot, and handed over to the torturers.”
0.161	In fact, we are already tied hand and foot by the chains of our own sins.
0.162	Looked at from our side, these sins are chains of iron and shackles of brass.
0.163	They are wounds that the world, the flesh, and the devil have cut into us, until we are torn up all over.
0.164	They are diseases that drain our blood and our strength and bring us down to the grave.
0.165	But looked at as they are here, in relation to God, they are debts—huge and too many to count.
0.166	So, since we have nothing to pay with, we have every reason to cry out to him to “freely forgive” us all of it!
0.167	The word translated “forgive” can mean either to cancel a debt or to undo a chain.
0.168	And if we get the first, the second follows naturally. If our debts are cancelled, the chains fall off our hands.
0.169	Through the free grace of God in Christ, the moment we “receive forgiveness of sins,” we also receive “a place among those who are made holy by faith in him.”
0.170	Sin has lost its power. It has no control over those who “are under grace”—that is, those who have God’s favour.
0.171	“There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” In the same way, they are set free from sin as well as from guilt.
0.172	“What the law rightly demands is fulfilled in” them. They “live not by the flesh, but by the Spirit.”
