0.1	“Jehu left that place and met Jehonadab son of Rechab, who was coming to meet him. Jehu greeted him and said to him, Is your heart right with me, as my heart is with you?
0.2	Jehonadab answered: It is.
0.3	If it is, give me your hand.”
0.4	2 Kings 10:15.
0.5	1. Even people who do not pay this great debt of love agree that we owe love to all people. The royal law says, “Love your neighbour as yourself,” and everyone who hears it can see that it is true. But it does not mean what the religious zealots of long ago made it mean. They turned it into “Love your neighbour”—your relatives, the people you know, your friends—“and hate your enemy.” Not so! Our Lord said, “I tell you, love your enemies. Bless those who curse you. Do good to those who hate you. Pray for those who treat you badly and persecute you. Then you will be the children”—and everyone will see that you are—“of your Father in heaven. He makes his sun rise on evil people and on good people, and he sends rain on those who do right and on those who do wrong.”
0.6	2. But there is certainly a special love that we owe to those who love God.
0.7	David put it this way: “All my delight is in God’s holy people on the earth, and in those who stand out in goodness.”
0.8	And someone greater than David said: “I am giving you a new commandment: love one another. Just as I have loved you, you also must love one another.
0.9	If you love one another, everyone will know by this that you are My disciples” (John 13:34, 35).
0.10	This is the love that the apostle John talks about so often and so strongly. He says, “From the very beginning, this is the message you heard: that we should love one another” (1 John 3:11).
0.11	“This is how we see the love of God: he laid down his life for us. And we ought,” if love calls us to do it, “to lay down our lives for our fellow believers” (verse 16).
0.12	And again: “Dear ones, let us love each other, for love has its source in God.
0.13	If someone does not love, he does not know God. For God is love” (4:7, 8).
0.14	“Love is not that we loved God. It is that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the sacrifice that pays for our sins.
0.15	Dear ones, if God loved us like this, then we too must love one another” (verses 10, 11).
0.16	3. Everyone agrees with this. But does everyone actually do it?
0.17	Everyday life shows that they do not.
0.18	Where are the Christians, even, who “love one another as he has commanded us”? So many things stand in the way!
0.19	There are two big, general obstacles. First, people cannot all think the same way. Second, because of this, they cannot all live the same way. In several smaller matters, what they do will differ just as much as what they believe differs.
0.20	4. A difference in opinions or in ways of worship may keep us from being fully united on the outside. But does it have to keep us from being united in love?
0.21	Even if we cannot think alike, can’t we love alike?
0.22	Can’t we be one in heart, even if we are not one in opinion?
0.23	Without any doubt, we can.
0.24	All of God’s children can unite in this, even with these smaller differences.
0.25	The differences can stay just as they are, and they can still help each other grow in love and in doing good.
0.26	5. In this matter, Jehu himself is a good example. He was a mixed character, but every serious Christian should pay close attention to his example and follow it.
0.27	“Jehu left that place and met Jehonadab son of Rechab, who was coming to meet him. Jehu greeted him and said to him, Is your heart right with me, as my heart is with you?
0.28	Jehonadab answered, It is.
0.29	If it is, give me your hand.”
0.30	The text falls naturally into two parts. First, Jehu asks Jehonadab a question: “Is your heart right with me, as my heart is with you?”
0.31	Second, when Jehonadab answers, “It is,” Jehu makes an offer: “If it is, give me your hand.”
0.32	I. 1. First, let’s think about the question Jehu asked Jehonadab: “Is your heart right with me, as my heart is with you?”
0.33	The very first thing we notice in these words is that Jehu does not ask about Jehonadab’s opinions.
0.34	And yet Jehonadab certainly held some very unusual opinions. Some of them were his alone. Some had a big effect on the way he lived. He thought these were so important that he made them binding on his children’s children, all the way down to his very last descendants.
0.35	We can see this from what Jeremiah wrote many years after Jehonadab died: “I took Jaazaniah and his brothers and all his sons—the whole family of the Rechabites. . . . I set bowls full of wine and cups in front of them, and I said to them, Drink some wine.
0.36	But they said, We will not drink wine. For Jonadab,” that is, Jehonadab, “the son of Rechab, our father” (it would be clearer if the words were put this way: “Jehonadab our father, the son of Rechab.” Out of love and respect for Rechab, Jehonadab probably wanted his descendants to be called by Rechab’s name), “gave us this command: You must never drink wine, not you and not your sons, ever.
0.37	You must not build a house, or sow seed, or plant a vineyard, or own one. All your lives you must live in tents....
0.38	And we have obeyed. We have done everything that Jonadab our father commanded us” (Jeremiah 35:3–10).
0.39	2. It seems that Jehu’s way was to “drive furiously” at everything, both in worldly matters and in religion. And yet he does not bother himself at all about any of these things. He leaves Jehonadab free to hold his own views.
0.40	And neither of them seems to have troubled the other in the least about the opinions he held.
0.41	3. It is quite possible that many good people today also hold unusual opinions. Some of them may be just as odd in this as Jehonadab was.
0.42	And as long as we know only in part, it is certain that people will not all see everything the same way.
0.43	In this life our understanding is weak and limited. Because of that, people will always have different opinions about religion, just as they do about everyday life. This cannot be avoided.
0.44	It has been this way since the world began, and it will stay this way “until everything is restored.”
0.45	4. And there is more. Everyone has to believe that each opinion he holds is true. (To believe that an opinion is not true is the same as not holding it.) Yet no one can be sure that all his opinions, taken together, are true.
0.46	In fact, every thinking person is sure that they are not. For humanum est errare et nescire: “Not knowing many things, and being wrong about some, is simply part of being human.”
0.47	So he realizes that this is true of him too.
0.48	He knows that, in general, he is wrong about some things. But he does not know—and perhaps cannot know—which things they are.
0.49	5. I say “perhaps he cannot know.” For who can tell how far invincible ignorance may reach—ignorance that cannot be overcome? Or (which comes to the same thing) invincible prejudice? Such prejudice is often planted so firmly in young minds that later it is impossible to pull out something that has put down such deep roots.
0.50	And who can say how much blame any mistake deserves, unless he knows every detail around it? For guilt always means that the will has gone along with something. And only the one who searches the heart can judge that.
0.51	6. So every wise person will give others the same freedom of thought that he wants them to give him. He will no more insist that they accept his opinions than he would want them to insist that he accept theirs.
0.52	He is patient with those who disagree with him. When he wants to unite in love with someone, he asks him only one question: “Is your heart right with me, as my heart is with you?”
0.53	7. Second, we notice that Jehu does not ask about Jehonadab’s way of worship either. Yet it is very likely that the two men were very different in this too.
0.54	For we can well believe that Jehonadab and all his descendants worshipped God at Jerusalem. Jehu did not. He cared more about politics than about religion.
0.55	So although he killed the worshippers of Baal and “wiped Baal out of Israel,” he “did not turn away” from the convenient sin of Jeroboam—the worship of the “golden calves” (2 Kings 10:29).
0.56	8. But even among people with honest hearts—people who want to “keep a conscience free from offence”—there will be different ways of worshipping God as long as there are different opinions. It has to be this way, because different opinions always lead to different practices.
0.57	In every age, people have disagreed about nothing more than about their ideas of the Supreme Being. In the same way, they have disagreed about nothing more than about how to worship him.
0.58	If this happened only among the heathen, it would not surprise us at all. For we know that “through” their “wisdom” they “did not know God.” So they could not know how to worship him either.
0.59	But isn’t it strange that the same thing happens even among Christians? They all agree on the general truth: “God is a Spirit, and those who worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.” Yet the particular ways they worship God are almost as varied as among the heathen.
0.60	9. So how are we to choose among so many different ways?
0.61	No one can choose for another person or tell him what he must do.
0.62	Instead, everyone must follow his own conscience, simply and sincerely before God.
0.63	He must be fully convinced in his own mind, and then act by the best light he has.
0.64	And no one has the power to force another person to live by his rules.
0.65	God has not given anyone the right to boss around the consciences of his fellow believers. Each person must judge for himself, just as each person must give an account of himself to God.
0.66	10. Every follower of Christ must, by the very nature of Christianity, belong to some particular congregation—some Church, as people usually call it. (Belonging to a church means worshipping God in a particular way, because “two cannot walk together unless they agree.”) But no power on earth except his own conscience can force him to choose one congregation over another, or one way of worship over another.
0.67	I know people commonly think that the place where we are born decides which Church we should belong to. For example, they think someone born in England should be a member of what is called the Church of England, and should therefore worship God in the particular way that Church requires.
0.68	I once defended this idea very strongly. But I have found many reasons to cool down my zeal for it.
0.69	I am afraid it has problems that no reasonable person can get past.
0.70	One of the biggest is this: if people had followed this rule, there could never have been a Reformation that broke away from the Pope’s church. For this rule completely destroys each person’s right to judge for himself, and that right is the foundation of the whole Reformation.
0.71	11. So I dare not try to force my way of worship on anyone else.
0.72	I believe it is truly the way of the early church and the apostles. But what I believe is not a rule for anyone else.
0.73	So when I want to unite in love with someone, I do not ask him, Are you part of my church, my congregation?
0.74	Do you accept the same kind of church government as I do, and the same church leaders?
0.75	Do you use the same set prayers that I use to worship God?
0.76	I do not ask, Do you receive the Lord’s Supper in the same position and in the same way that I do? I do not ask whether you agree with me about baptism: whether there should be godparents for the one being baptized, how baptism should be done, or at what age people should be baptized.
0.77	In fact, I do not even ask you (though I am very clear in my own mind about it) whether you accept baptism and the Lord’s Supper at all.
0.78	Let all these things wait. If we need to, we will talk about them at a better time. For now, my only question is this: “Is your heart right with me, as my heart is with you?”
0.79	12. But what does this question really mean?
0.80	I don’t mean, What did Jehu mean by it?
0.81	I mean, What should a follower of Christ mean by it when he asks it of a fellow believer?
0.82	The first thing it means is this: Is your heart right with God?
0.83	Do you believe that God exists, and that he is perfect? Do you believe he is eternal, without limits, wise, and powerful? Do you believe he is just, merciful, and true?
0.84	Do you believe that he is now “holding all things together by his powerful word”? Do you believe he rules even the smallest things, and even the most harmful things, for his own glory and for the good of those who love him? Do you have proof from God himself—a certainty that comes from above—about the things of God?
0.85	Do you “live by faith, not by sight”? Do you look not at the things of this world that pass away, but at the things that last forever?
0.86	13. Do you believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, who is “God over all, blessed forever”?
0.87	Has he shown himself to your soul?
0.88	Do you know Jesus Christ, and him crucified?
0.89	Does he live in you, and you in him?
0.90	Is he formed in your heart by faith? Have you completely given up trusting in your own works and your own goodness? Have you “submitted yourself to the righteousness of God that comes by faith in Christ Jesus”?
0.91	Are you “found in him, not having your own righteousness, but the righteousness that comes by faith”?
0.92	And through him, are you “fighting the good fight of faith and taking hold of eternal life”?
0.93	14. Is your faith energoumene di agapes—filled with the energy of love?
0.94	Do you love God “with all your heart, and with all your mind, and with all your soul, and with all your strength”? (I do not say “above all things,” because that phrase is not found in Scripture and it is unclear.)
0.95	Do you look for all your happiness in him alone?
0.96	And do you find what you are looking for?
0.97	Does your soul always “praise the greatness of the Lord, and your spirit rejoice in God your Saviour”? Have you learned “to give thanks in everything”? And do you find that “it is a joyful and pleasant thing to be thankful”?
0.98	Is God the centre of your soul, the sum of everything you long for?
0.99	Because of this, are you storing up your treasure in heaven, and counting everything else as garbage and worthless trash? Has the love of God driven the love of the world out of your soul?
0.100	If so, you are “crucified to the world.” You are dead to everything down here. And your “life is hidden with Christ in God.”
0.101	15. Are you busy doing “not your own will, but the will of the one who sent you”? He sent you down here to stay for a little while—to spend a few days in a foreign land—until you have finished the work he gave you to do and you go back to your Father’s house.
0.102	Is it your food and drink “to do the will of your Father in heaven”?
0.103	Is your eye focused on one thing in everything you do? Is it always fixed on him? Are you always looking to Jesus?
0.104	Do you aim at him in whatever you do—in all your work, your business, your conduct? Are you aiming only at God’s glory in everything? Are you, “whatever you do, in word or deed, doing it all in the name of the Lord Jesus, and giving thanks to God the Father through him”?
0.105	16. Does the love of God move you to serve him with fear, to “be glad before him with deep respect”?
0.106	Are you more afraid of displeasing God than of death, or even of hell?
0.107	Is there nothing as terrible to you as the thought of offending God’s glorious eyes?
0.108	Because of this, do you “hate every evil way”—every breaking of his holy and perfect law? And do you work hard at this, “training yourself to keep a conscience free from offence toward God and toward people”?
0.109	17. Is your heart right toward your neighbour?
0.110	Do you love all people as you love yourself, with no exceptions?
0.111	“If you love only those who love you, why should anyone thank you for that?”
0.112	Do you “love your enemies”?
0.113	Is your soul full of kindness and tender love toward them?
0.114	Do you love even God’s enemies—the ungrateful and the ungodly?
0.115	Does your heart ache for them?
0.116	Could you even “wish yourself” “cursed” in this earthly life for their sake?
0.117	And do you show this by “blessing those who curse you, and praying for those who treat you badly and persecute you”?
0.118	18. Do you show your love by what you do?
0.119	While you have time, and whenever you have the chance, do you really “do good to everyone”—neighbours or strangers, friends or enemies, good or bad?
0.120	Do you do them all the good you can? Do you try to meet all their needs, and help them in body and soul as much as you possibly can? If this is how you think, every Christian may say to you: Yes, even if you only sincerely want this, and keep pressing on until you reach it, then “your heart is right with me, as my heart is with you.”
0.121	II. 1. “If it is, give me your hand.”
0.122	I don’t mean, “Agree with my opinions.”
0.123	You don’t have to. I don’t expect it, and I don’t want it.
0.124	And I don’t mean, “I will agree with your opinions.”
0.125	I can’t. It is not up to me. I can no more think whatever I choose than I can see or hear whatever I choose.
0.126	You keep your opinion, and I will keep mine—just as firmly as ever.
0.127	You don’t even need to try to come over to my side, or to bring me over to yours.
0.128	I don’t want you to argue about those points, or even to hear or say one word about them.
0.129	Let’s leave all opinions alone, on both sides. Just “give me your hand.”
0.130	2. I don’t mean, “Take up my ways of worship,” or, “I will take up yours.”
0.131	This is also something that is not up to you or to me.
0.132	Each of us must act as he is fully convinced in his own mind.
0.133	You hold on tightly to what you believe pleases God most, and I will do the same.
0.134	I believe that the Episcopal way of governing the church agrees with Scripture and goes back to the apostles.
0.135	If you think the Presbyterian or Independent way is better, go on thinking so, and act on it.
0.136	I believe babies should be baptized, and that this can be done either by dipping them or by sprinkling.
0.137	If you believe differently, go on believing it, and follow what you are convinced of.
0.138	I think written prayers are very useful, especially when a large congregation meets together.
0.139	If you think prayers made up on the spot are more useful, act on what you think.
0.140	I believe that I should not refuse water for people to be baptized, and that I should eat bread and drink wine in memory of my Master who died. But if you are not convinced of this, act according to the light you have.
0.141	I don’t want to argue with you for even a moment about any of these points.
0.142	Let’s set all these smaller matters aside.
0.143	Let’s never even bring them up. “If your heart is like my heart”—if you love God and all people—I ask nothing more. “Give me your hand.”
0.144	3. First, I mean: love me. And don’t love me only the way you love all people, or the way you love your enemies or God’s enemies—those who hate you, who “treat you badly and persecute you.” Don’t love me only as a stranger, someone you know nothing good or bad about. That is not enough for me. No! “If your heart is right with me, as mine is with you,” then love me with a very tender love. Love me as a friend who is closer than a brother. Love me as a brother in Christ, a fellow citizen of the New Jerusalem, a fellow soldier fighting in the same war under the same Captain of our salvation.
0.145	Love me as a companion in the kingdom of Jesus and in his patient endurance, and as someone who will share in his glory with you.
0.146	4. Love me more than you love most people. Love me with a love that is patient and kind. Be patient with me if I don’t know something or if I go the wrong way. Help carry my load instead of making it heavier. Keep on being tender, gentle, and full of compassion toward me. Love me with a love that is not jealous if God sometimes gives me even more success in his work than he gives you.
0.147	Love me with a love that does not get angry at my foolish mistakes or my weaknesses—or even when it looks to you (if it sometimes seems that way) as if I am not acting according to God’s will.
0.148	Love me in such a way that you don’t think evil of me. Put away all jealousy and all suspicion.
0.149	Love me with the love that covers all things—that never tells others about my faults or weaknesses. Love me with the love that believes all things—that is always ready to think the best, and to take everything I say and do in the kindest way. Love me with the love that hopes all things. Hope that the thing someone told you about me never happened. Or hope that it did not happen the way they said. Or at least hope that I meant well, or that I did it when a strong temptation suddenly came over me.
0.150	And keep hoping to the end that whatever is wrong in me will be corrected by God’s grace, and that whatever is missing will be supplied through the riches of his mercy in Christ Jesus.
0.151	5. Second, I mean: bring me before God in all your prayers. Wrestle with God for me. Ask him to quickly correct whatever he sees wrong in me, and to supply whatever is missing in me.
0.152	When you come closest to the throne of grace, ask him—for he is very near you then—to make my heart more like yours, more right toward God and toward people. Ask that I may be more deeply convinced of things that cannot be seen, and see the love of God in Christ Jesus more clearly. Ask that I may live by faith, not by sight, more steadily, and take hold of eternal life more eagerly.
0.153	Pray that the love of God and of all people will be poured into my heart more fully. Pray that I will be more eager and active in doing the will of my Father in heaven, more zealous to do good, and more careful to stay away from anything that even looks evil.
0.154	6. Third, I mean: stir me up to love and to good works.
0.155	Back up your prayers, whenever you have the chance, by speaking to me in love about whatever you think is good for my soul.
0.156	Urge me on in the work God has given me to do, and teach me how to do it better.
0.157	Yes, “correct me as a friend, and rebuke me,” whenever it looks to you as though I am doing my own will instead of the will of the one who sent me.
0.158	Oh, speak up and don’t hold back! Tell me whatever you think may help me: to fix my faults, to strengthen my weak spots, to build me up in love, or to make me more ready in any way for the Master to use.
0.159	7. Finally, I mean: love me not just in words, but in actions and in truth.
0.160	As far as your conscience allows (while you keep your own opinions and your own way of worshipping God), join with me in God’s work. Let’s go forward hand in hand.
0.161	And you can certainly go this far.
0.162	Wherever you are, speak with respect about the work of God, no matter whom he is working through. And speak kindly about his messengers.
0.163	And if you can, don’t just feel sorry for them when they are in trouble or distress. Give them cheerful and real help, so that they may praise God because of you.
0.164	8. Two things should be noticed about what I have said under this last point. First, whatever love I ask from the person whose heart is right with mine—whatever acts of love, whatever help for the soul or for daily life—I am ready to give him the same, by God’s grace, as far as I am able. Second, I have not asked this only for myself. I ask it for all whose hearts are right toward God and people, so that we may all love one another as Christ has loved us.
0.165	III. 1. From all this, we can draw one conclusion.
0.166	From this we can learn what a catholic (universal) spirit is.
0.167	Hardly any phrase has been so badly misunderstood, or so dangerously misused, as this one. But anyone who calmly thinks about what I have said can easily correct these misunderstandings and keep it from being misused.
0.168	First, we learn from this that a catholic spirit is not a loose, anything-goes attitude toward beliefs.
0.169	It is not being indifferent to all opinions. That attitude comes from hell, not from heaven.
0.170	This unsettled thinking—being “carried back and forth and thrown around by every wind of doctrine”—is a great curse, not a blessing. It is a bitter enemy, not a friend, of a truly catholic spirit.
0.171	A person with a truly catholic spirit is not still searching for his religion.
0.172	He is as fixed as the sun in his judgement about the main teachings of the Christian faith.
0.173	It is true that he is always ready to listen to and think about anything that can be said against what he believes. But this does not show that his mind is wavering, and it does not make it waver either.
0.174	He does not go back and forth between two opinions, or foolishly try to mix them into one.
0.175	Take note of this, you who don’t know what spirit you have! You call yourselves people with a catholic spirit only because your thinking is muddy, because your mind is all foggy, and because you have no settled, consistent beliefs, but want to mix all opinions together.
0.176	Be sure of this: you have completely lost your way. You don’t know where you are.
0.177	You think you have found the very spirit of Christ. In truth, you are closer to the spirit of Antichrist.
0.178	First go and learn the basics of the gospel of Christ. Then you will learn to have a truly catholic spirit.
0.179	2. Second, we learn from what has been said that a catholic spirit is not a loose, anything-goes attitude toward how we worship.
0.180	It is not being indifferent about public worship, or about the outward way it is done.
0.181	This, too, would be a curse and not a blessing.
0.182	Far from helping us worship God in spirit and in truth, it would, as long as it lasted, get in the way more than words can say.
0.183	But a person with a truly catholic spirit has weighed everything on God’s own scales. So he has no doubt and no hesitation at all about the particular way of worship he takes part in.
0.184	He is clearly convinced that this way of worshipping God agrees with Scripture and makes good sense.
0.185	He knows of no other way in the world that agrees more with Scripture, or makes more sense.
0.186	So he doesn’t wander here and there. He sticks closely to it, and he praises God for the chance to do so.
0.187	3. Third, we learn from this that a catholic spirit does not mean not caring which congregation you belong to.
0.188	This is another kind of looseness, and it is just as foolish and unscriptural as the one before.
0.189	A person with a truly catholic spirit is far from it.
0.190	He is settled in his congregation, just as he is settled in his beliefs.
0.191	He is joined to one congregation, not only in spirit but by all the outward ties of Christian fellowship.
0.192	There he takes part in all the practices God has given to his people.
0.193	There he receives the Lord’s Supper.
0.194	There he pours out his soul in prayer with others, and joins with them in praising and thanking God.
0.195	There he loves to hear the message of reconciliation, the good news of God’s grace.
0.196	On special occasions he seeks God by fasting together with these people, his closest and most dearly loved brothers.
0.197	He watches over these people in love in a special way, just as they watch over his soul. They warn, encourage, comfort, and correct one another, and in every way build each other up in the faith.
0.198	He sees them as his own family. So, as far as God has given him the ability, he naturally takes care of them and makes sure they have everything they need for life and godliness.
0.199	4. He stays firmly fixed in his beliefs, in what he believes is the truth as it is in Jesus. He holds tightly to the kind of worship he believes God accepts most. He is joined to one particular congregation by the tenderest and closest ties. But while all this is true, his heart is wide open toward all people, those he knows and those he doesn’t. He embraces with strong and warm love neighbours and strangers, friends and enemies.
0.200	This is catholic, or universal, love.
0.201	And whoever has this love has a catholic spirit.
0.202	For only love gives a person the right to this name: catholic love is a catholic spirit.
0.203	5. So, if we use this word in its strictest sense, a person with a catholic spirit is someone who, in the way I have described, gives his hand to everyone whose heart is right with his heart. He knows how to value all the blessings he has, and to praise God for them: his knowledge of the things of God, the true scriptural way of worshipping him, and above all his union with a congregation that fears God and does what is right. He guards these blessings with the greatest care, and keeps them as the apple of his eye. And at the same time he loves all who believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, whatever their opinions, their way of worship, or their congregation. He loves all who love God and people, who are glad to please God and afraid to offend him, and so are careful to stay away from evil and eager to do good. He loves them as friends, as brothers in the Lord, as members of Christ and children of God, as people who share with him now in God’s present kingdom, and who will be heirs with him of God’s eternal kingdom.
0.204	The person with a truly catholic spirit carries all these people on his heart all the time. He has a tenderness for them that words cannot express, and he longs for their good. So he never stops bringing them before God in prayer, and he also speaks up for them before people. He speaks comforting words to them, and he works hard, with everything he says, to strengthen their hands in God.
0.205	He helps them as much as he possibly can in everything, both spiritual and practical.
0.206	He is ready “to spend and be spent for them”—yes, even to give his life for them.
0.207	6. You, O man of God, think about these things!

0.216	These are my brothers, friends, and family: / Those who do my heavenly Father’s will, / Who aim at perfect holiness, / And want to carry out all your plans, / Who long to be all that you are, / And love their God with all their heart.
0.217	For these, though we are apart in body, / Wherever they are scattered across the earth, / I find a sincere love that has no limits, / As constant as the life of God; / It sprang from him, the Fountain of life, / As pure, as steady, and as strong.
0.218	Joined to the hidden church the world does not know, / In this sure bond that makes us perfect, / Safe though unseen, I live alone / And glory in the grace that unites us, / Given to me and to every believer, / To all your saints on earth and in heaven.
