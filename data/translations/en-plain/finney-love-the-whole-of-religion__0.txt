0.1	Text: “Love never harms its neighbor. That is why love fully keeps the law.” — Romans 13:10
0.2	As I speak on these words, I plan:
0.3	I. To say some things about what love is.
0.4	II. To show that love is all there is to true religion.
0.5	III. To name some things that perfect love does not require.
0.6	IV. To name some things that it does require.
0.7	V. To name some of the things that perfect love produces.
H:0.7	I. First, some things about what love is.
0.8	1. My first point is that love can take different forms.
0.9	When it comes to religion, the two main forms are goodwill and delight.
0.10	Goodwill is a settled attitude of the mind. In other words, it is a choice made by the will.
0.11	It means wanting good for someone. It is a desire to make that person happy.
0.12	Delight means respecting someone and approving of their character.
0.13	We should show goodwill to every being, whether they are good or bad.
0.14	But delight belongs only to those who are good and holy.
0.15	2. Love can exist in two ways: as an affection or as an emotion.
0.16	When love is an affection, it is something we choose. It is an act of the will.
0.17	When love is an emotion, we do not choose it directly.
0.18	What we call feelings, or emotions, are not directly chosen.
0.19	They do not depend directly on the will. We cannot control them just by deciding to.
0.20	The goodness, or virtue, of love is found mostly in love as a choice.
0.21	The happiness of love is found mostly in love as a feeling.
0.22	If the choice to love is very strong, it brings a lot of happiness. But the feeling of holy love is happiness itself.
0.23	I said that the feeling of love is not directly chosen.
0.24	I do not mean that the will has nothing to do with it. I mean that it does not come just from a direct decision of the will.
0.25	Nobody can feel love just by deciding to feel it.
0.26	And sometimes the feeling is there even when the will is against it.
0.27	People often feel emotions rising in their minds that they know are wrong. They try hard, by pure willpower, to push them out. When they find they cannot do it, they decide that they have no control over these emotions.
0.28	But the will can always control them in an indirect way.
0.29	The mind can bring up any kind of emotion it wants by paying enough attention to the right thing.
0.30	The more closely you fix your attention, the more surely those feelings will come—as long as your will is right toward the thing you are paying attention to.
0.31	It works the same way with emotions that are wrong or unpleasant. The mind can get rid of them by turning its attention completely away from the thing that causes them, and not letting the thoughts stay on it.
0.32	3. Usually, we feel love for God when we are choosing to love him.
0.33	But this is not always true.
0.34	We can have goodwill toward someone and still, at times, feel no love at all in our emotions.
0.35	We cannot be sure that even the Lord Jesus Christ felt love for God as an emotion at every moment.
0.36	From what we know about how the mind works, a person can have a steady affection and be guided and ruled by it all the time, in everything he does. And yet at that moment he may not feel any emotion of love for the one he loves.
0.37	For example, a husband and father may be working hard for the good of his family. His whole life is ruled by his love for them. Yet his thoughts may not be on them at that moment, so he does not feel any strong feelings of love for them right then.
0.38	The work he is doing may fill his mind so much that he hardly thinks about them. So he may feel no emotion toward them. And yet the whole time, his love for them is guiding and ruling him.
0.39	Notice that I am using the word “affection” the way President Edwards used it. He explained it in his famous Treatise on the Will.
0.40	In his book, an affection is an act of the will—a choice.
0.41	4. Loving our neighbor naturally means that we also love God. And loving God naturally means that we love our neighbor.
0.42	The same thing is said in verse 8: “Do not owe anyone anything, except to love one another. Whoever loves another person has done all the law asks.
0.43	Commands like ‘Do not commit adultery,’ ‘Do not kill,’ ‘Do not steal,’ ‘Do not tell lies about others,’ and ‘Do not crave what belongs to others’—and any other command there is—are all wrapped up in this one saying: ‘Love your neighbor as yourself.’”
0.44	This verse takes it for granted that loving our neighbor means we also love God. Otherwise it could not say that “whoever loves another person has done all the law asks.”
0.45	The apostle James sees the same principle. He says, “If you keep the royal law found in the Scriptures, ‘Love your neighbor as yourself,’ you are doing well.”
0.46	Here, loving our neighbor is described as obeying the whole law.
0.47	Goodwill toward our neighbor naturally includes love for God.
0.48	It is a love for the happiness of all beings.
0.49	In the same way, delighting in holy beings naturally includes love for God, because he is infinitely holy.
H:0.17	II. Next, I will show that love is all there is to true religion.
0.50	In other words, everything God asks of people comes down to love, in its many different forms and results.
0.51	Love is the total of it all.
0.52	1. My first proof is that this idea is taught in our text and in many other Bible passages.
0.53	The Bible clearly teaches that love is the total of everything required, both in the law and in the gospel.
0.54	Our Savior says that the great command is this: love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind and strength, and love your neighbor as yourself. He says this is the total of all the law and the prophets. In other words, it contains everything the whole Bible—both the law and the gospel—asks of us.
0.55	2. God is love. So to love is to be like God. And to be perfect in love is to be perfect as God is perfect.
0.56	All of God’s moral qualities are really love, acting in certain situations and for certain purposes.
0.57	When God punishes the wicked in his justice, or is angry at sin, and so on, these are simply his love at work for the happiness of his whole kingdom.
0.58	It is the same with people.
0.59	Everything good in a person is some form of love.
0.60	Hating sin is simply love for goodness in action, fighting against whatever fights against goodness.
0.61	In the same way, true faith includes love. Faith that has no love in it, or that does not work through love, is no part of religion.
0.62	The faith that is part of religion is a loving trust in God.
0.63	There is a kind of faith in God that has no love in it.
0.64	The devil has this kind of faith.
0.65	So does the sinner who has been made to feel his guilt.
0.66	But there is nothing of religion in it.
0.67	Faith could even become strong enough to do miracles. But if it has no love in it, it is worth nothing.
0.68	The apostle Paul says in 1 Corinthians chapter 13, “I might be able to prophesy. I might understand every secret and know everything. I might have faith so great that I could move mountains. But if I do not have love, I am nothing.”
0.69	Repentance is the same.
0.70	If repentance does not include love, it is not true “repentance toward God.”
0.71	True repentance means obeying the law of love, and as a result, standing against sin.
H:0.24	III. Now I will mention some things that perfect love does not require.
0.72	1. Perfect love does not require the strongest possible feelings.
0.73	It is clear that the Lord Jesus Christ very rarely had the strongest feelings of love. Yet he always had perfect love.
0.74	He usually showed very little emotion or excitement.
0.75	Excitement always matches how strong the emotions are, because excitement is made up of emotions.
0.76	The Savior usually seemed very calm.
0.77	Sometimes he was strongly angry at wrong, or deeply grieved at how hard people’s hearts were. And sometimes we read that he was full of joy in his spirit.
0.78	But most of the time he was calm and did not show strong emotion.
0.79	So it is clear that perfect love does not require the feeling of love to be very strong.
0.80	2. Perfect love does not mean that love cannot grow. It does not rule out growing in grace.
0.81	I believe that because the mind will keep growing in knowledge forever, it will naturally keep growing in love forever too.
0.82	The Lord Jesus Christ, as a human being, grew taller and grew in favor with God and people.
0.83	No doubt, as a child he grew in knowledge. And as he grew in knowledge, he grew in love for God, as well as in God’s favor.
0.84	His love was perfect when he was a child, but it was greater when he grew up.
0.85	As a human being, he probably kept growing in his love for God for as long as he lived.
0.86	From the way the mind works, we can see that it may be the same for all the saints in heaven. Their love will keep growing forever, and yet it will always be perfect love.
0.87	3. Perfect love does not require us to love every person in exactly the same way at all times.
0.88	We cannot think of everyone at the same time.
0.89	You cannot even think of every person you know all at once.
0.90	How much love we feel toward someone depends on whether that person is in our thoughts.
0.91	4. Perfect love does not require us to have the same spirit of prayer—the same deep urge to pray—for every person, or for the same person at all times.
0.92	Pure and perfect love does not always come with the spirit of prayer.
0.93	The saints in heaven have pure and perfect love for all beings. Yet we do not know that they feel led to pray for anyone.
0.94	You may love someone very strongly and still not have the spirit of prayer for that person.
0.95	That is, God’s Spirit may not lead you to pray for that person to be saved.
0.96	You do not pray for the wicked who are in hell.
0.97	The spirit of prayer depends on the Holy Spirit working in us. He leads our minds to pray for things that match God’s will.
0.98	You cannot pray in the Spirit with the same passion and faith for every person in the world.
0.99	Jesus Christ said plainly that he did not pray for everyone: “It is not the world I am praying for.”
0.100	People have made a big mistake here about the spirit of prayer.
0.101	Some people think that Christians have not done all their duty unless they have prayed in faith for every single person, as long as there is still one sinner on earth.
0.102	If that were true, Jesus Christ never did all his duty, because he never did this.
0.103	God has never told us that he will save every person. He has never given us any reason to believe he will.
0.104	So how can we pray in faith for everyone to be saved?
0.105	What would that faith be based on?
0.106	5. You can have perfect love even when you feel weak and worn out because you are exhausted or sick.
0.107	We are made in such a way that strong excitement naturally wears out our strength.
0.108	But love can still be perfect.
0.109	A person may feel more like lying down to sleep than like praying, and still his love may be perfect.
0.110	The Lord Jesus Christ often felt this tiredness and exhaustion. His spirit was still willing, but his body was weak.
H:0.35	IV. What perfect love does require.
0.111	1. It means there is nothing in the mind that goes against love.
0.112	There is no hatred, spite, rage, jealousy, or any other evil feeling that goes against pure and perfect love.
0.113	2. It means there is nothing in the way we live that goes against love.
0.114	All our actions, words, and thoughts are always fully and perfectly controlled by love.
0.115	3. It means that our love for God comes first, above everything.
0.116	Love for God is completely in first place. It is so far above everything else that nothing else is loved compared to God.
0.117	4. It means that we love God without selfish motives.
0.118	We love God for who he is. We love him not because of his relationship to us, but because his character is so excellent.
0.119	5. It means that we love our neighbor equally with ourselves. His interests and happiness should matter to us as much as our own, and we should treat him and his interests that way.
H:0.45	V. Now I will mention some of the things perfect love produces.
0.120	1. One sure result of perfect love for God and people is joy in giving things up to help God’s kingdom and to see sinners saved.
0.121	Look at loving parents. See how happy they are to give things up so that their children can be happy.
0.122	Look at that father. He wears himself out with hard work day after day and year after year, for his whole long life. He gets up early and lives with constant worry, all to take care of his family.
0.123	And he does not see all this sacrifice and hard work as sad or heavy. He sees it as a joy, because he loves his family.
0.124	Look at that mother. She wants to send her son to college.
0.125	Now, instead of finding it hard, she is glad to stay up late and work without stopping to help him.
0.126	It is because she really loves her son.
0.127	Parents like this are happier giving good things to their children than they would be enjoying those things themselves.
0.128	What parent does not enjoy a piece of fruit more by giving it to their little child than by eating it?
0.129	The Lord Jesus Christ found more deep satisfaction in winning salvation for people than any of his saints can ever find in receiving his gifts.
0.130	He said that giving brings more blessing than receiving.
0.131	This was the joy that lay ahead of him. For the sake of that joy he put up with the cross and thought nothing of the shame.
0.132	His love for people was so great that it drove him to take on this work. And it carried him in victory all the way through it.
0.133	The apostle Paul did not think it was sad or hard to be chased from place to place, put in prison, whipped, stoned, and treated like garbage, all to spread the gospel and save souls.
0.134	It was his joy.
0.135	The love of Christ drove him so strongly, and he wanted so much to do good, that his greatest joy was to offer himself on that altar as a sacrifice for the cause.
0.136	Other people have had the same attitude as the apostle.
0.137	Some people have been willing to live a thousand years, or until the end of time, if they could spend it doing good, building God’s kingdom, and saving people’s souls. They were even willing to go without sleep and food to help those they loved so much.
0.138	2. It frees the soul from the power of “legal” motives, the motives of mere law-keeping.
0.139	Perfect love makes a person obey God, not because he is afraid of God’s anger or hopes to get a reward for doing this or that. He obeys because he loves God and loves to do what God wants.
0.140	People go to two extremes on this topic.
0.141	One group says goodness means doing right just because it is right. They leave out God’s will and any love for God.
0.142	Another group says goodness means doing things because you enjoy doing them. They leave out God’s authority as Ruler and Lawgiver.
0.143	Both groups are wrong.
0.144	Doing something only because you think it is right, and not because you love God, is not true goodness.
0.145	And doing something because you like doing it, without caring about God’s will, is not true goodness either.
0.146	A wife might do certain things because she knows it will please her husband. But if she did the same thing only because she liked doing it, and without any thought of her husband, it would not be a virtue toward her husband.
0.147	If a person loves God, as soon as he knows what God wants, he will do it because God wants it.
0.148	Perfect love will lead to obeying in everything—doing God’s will in all things, because it is what God wants.
0.149	3. A person who has perfect love will be dead to the world.
0.150	By this I mean that worldly concerns will no longer have a hold on him.
0.151	Perfect love will wipe out selfishness so completely that he will want nothing except what God wants, and care about nothing except God’s glory.
0.152	He will not be moved by what the public thinks, or by what this or that person will say or think.
0.153	Look at what a woman will do out of natural love for her husband.
0.154	She is willing to break away from all her friends, as if she were dead to them. She will not pay the slightest attention to what they say. She will leave behind all the wealth, honor, and pleasures they can offer, to join the man she loves and live with him in poverty, in shame, and far from home.
0.155	Her love is so great that she does it with joy. She is ready to move from a palace to any cottage or cave on earth and be perfectly happy.
0.156	Nothing her friends say against the man she loves has any effect on her, except to make her hold on to him even more tightly.
0.157	This one ALL-CONSUMING love has actually killed every influence that used to work on her.
0.158	Trying to change her mind with such things is useless.
0.159	There is only one way to reach her mind. Only one kind of motive moves her, and it comes through the one she loves.
0.160	As far as the science of the mind goes, perfect love for God works in the same way.
0.161	When a mind is filled with perfect love, it cannot be turned away from God as long as that love keeps working.
0.162	Take away his things, his friends, his good name, and his children. Send him to prison, whip him, tie him to a stake, stuff his flesh with pine knots and set them on fire. Then leave him his God, and he is happy.
0.163	His strong love can make him feel nothing of anything else.
0.164	It is as if he were dead to the whole world except his God.
0.165	There have been martyrs who, while their bodies were burning at the stake, were so completely happy in God that they stopped feeling the pain.
0.166	Put a person like that in hell, in the lake of fire and burning sulfur. As long as he enjoys God and God’s love fills his soul, he is happy.
0.167	Who has not seen or heard of love almost as strong as what I have described? In such cases a person really is dead to everything else and lives only for the one they love.
0.168	How often do you see loving parents who live for their only child, and when that child dies, they wish they were dead too!
0.169	Sometimes a husband and wife love each other so completely that they live for nothing else. If the husband dies, the wife wastes away with grief and dies too.
0.170	The one who filled her whole soul, the one she lived for, is gone. So why should she go on living?
0.171	In the same way, when a person is filled with perfect love for God, he wants to live only to love and serve God. He is dead to the world and dead to his own reputation. He has no wish to live for any other reason—here, in heaven, or anywhere else in the universe—except to bring glory to God.
0.172	He is willing to live here or anywhere else, and to suffer and work for a thousand years, or forever, if it will bring glory to God.
0.173	I remember a friend who often said, “I don’t think I have a single thought of living even one moment for any purpose except to glorify God—any more than I would think of jumping straight into hell.”
0.174	He said this seriously and thoughtfully, and his whole life matched what he said.
0.175	He was intelligent, serious, and honest. I have no doubt that he was saying what he had fully believed for years.
0.176	What was this, if not perfect love?
0.177	Does any angel in heaven do more than this?
0.178	An angel’s love may be greater in amount, because the angel is stronger.
0.179	But even the highest angel could not love more perfectly than to be able to say honestly, “I would sooner think of jumping into hell than of living one moment for any other goal but to glorify God.”
0.180	What more could Jesus Christ himself say?

0.199	Look at that mother and how patient she is with her children for so long, because she loves them.
