7.70	4. “We are from God.”
7.71	Let’s see why. Let’s see if it is for any reason other than love.
7.72	“We are from God. Whoever knows God listens to us. Whoever is not from God does not listen to us.
7.73	This is how we know the spirit of truth and the spirit of error.” That is, whoever listens to us has the spirit of truth. Whoever does not listen to us has the spirit of error.
7.74	Let’s see what he advises us to do. And let’s choose to listen to him, since he gives his advice in the spirit of truth. Let’s not listen to antichrists, or to people who love the world, or to the world itself.
7.75	If we are born of God, “dear friends,” he goes on. Look back to see where this comes from: “We are from God. Whoever knows God listens to us. Whoever is not from God does not listen to us.
7.76	This is how we know the spirit of truth and the spirit of error.” Yes, now he makes us listen eagerly. He tells us that whoever knows God listens, but whoever does not know God does not listen. He tells us that this is how we tell the spirit of truth apart from the spirit of error. Well then, let’s see what he is about to advise. What is it that we must listen to him about? “Dear friends, let us love one another.”
7.77	Why? Just because a man gives this advice?
7.78	“Because love comes from God.”
7.79	He has praised love highly by saying that it “comes from God.” But he is going to say even more. Let’s listen eagerly.
7.80	So far he has said, “Love comes from God. Everyone who loves is born of God and knows God.
7.81	Whoever does not love does not know God.”
7.82	Why? “Because God is love” [Love is God].
7.83	What more could be said, brothers and sisters?
7.84	Suppose nothing else were said to praise love in all the pages of this letter. Suppose nothing at all were said about it in all the other pages of the Scriptures. Suppose this one thing was all that the voice of God’s Spirit told us: “For Love is God.” Then we would not need anything more.
7.85	5. Now see that acting against love is acting against God.
7.86	No one should say, “When I don’t love my brother, I sin against a human being (notice this!). And sin against a human being is no big deal. As long as I don’t sin against God.”
7.87	How can you not be sinning against God when you sin against love?
7.88	“Love is God.” Is this something “we” are saying?
7.89	If we said, “Love is God,” maybe one of you would be offended and say, What did he just say?
7.90	What does he mean, that “Love is God”?
7.91	God “gave” love. God handed out love as a gift.
7.92	“Love comes from God: Love IS God.”
7.93	Look, brothers and sisters, here you have God’s Scriptures. This letter is one of the Bible’s accepted books. It is read aloud in every nation. The whole world accepts its authority. It has built up the whole world.
7.94	Here God’s Spirit is telling you, “Love is God.”
7.95	Now go against God if you dare, and refuse to love your brother!
7.96	6. So what did it mean when it said a moment ago, “Love comes from God,” and now says, “Love IS God”?
7.97	God is Father and Son and Holy Spirit. The Son is God from God. The Holy Spirit is God from God. And these three are one God, not three Gods.
7.98	The Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God. And the person who loves is the one in whom the Holy Spirit lives. So “Love is God.” But love “IS God” because it is “from God.”
7.99	You find both in this letter: “Love comes from God,” and “Love is God.”
7.100	The Scripture never says about the Father alone that He is “from God.” So when you hear the phrase “from God,” it means either the Son or the Holy Spirit.
7.101	The apostle says, “God’s love is poured into our hearts by the Holy Spirit, who is given to us.” So let’s understand that the One who lives in love is the Holy Spirit.
7.102	This Holy Spirit is the One whom bad people cannot receive. He is the Fountain the Scripture talks about: “Let the fountain of your water be your very own, and don’t let any stranger share it with you.”
7.103	Everyone who does not love God is a stranger, an antichrist.
7.104	They may come to church, but they cannot be counted among God’s children. That Fountain of life does not belong to them.
7.105	Even a bad person can have baptism. Even a bad person can have the gift of prophecy.
7.106	We read that King Saul had the gift of prophecy. He was hunting down holy David, and yet he was filled with the spirit of prophecy and began to prophesy.
7.107	Even a bad person can receive the sacrament of the Lord’s body and blood. The Bible says about such people, “Whoever eats and drinks in an unworthy way eats and drinks judgment on himself.”
7.108	Even a bad person can carry the name of Christ. In other words, even a bad person can be called a Christian. It is like the people the Bible describes: “They made the name of their God dirty.”
7.109	So I say that even a bad person can have all these sacraments. But no one can have love and be a bad person at the same time.
7.110	So this is the special gift. This is the “Fountain” that is truly your “own.”
7.111	God’s Spirit urges you to drink from this. God’s Spirit urges you to drink from Himself.
7.112	7. “This is how God’s love was shown among us.”
7.113	Look, we are being urged to love God.
7.114	Could we love Him if He had not loved us first?
7.115	Even if we were slow to love first, let’s not be slow to love Him back.
7.116	He loved us first. And even then, we do not love.
7.117	He loved people who did wrong, but He took away their wrongdoing. He loved people who did wrong, but He did not bring them together to keep doing wrong. He loved the sick, but He came to them to make them well.
7.118	So “Love is God.”
7.119	“This is how God’s love was shown among us: God sent His one and only Son into the world, so that we could live through Him.”
7.120	The Lord Himself says, “No one can have a greater love than this: to give up his life for his friends.” That is where Christ’s love for us was proven, because He died for us. But how is the Father’s love for us proven?
7.121	It is proven because He “sent His only Son” to die for us. The apostle Paul says the same thing: “He did not spare His own Son but handed Him over for all of us. So how could He not also freely give us everything along with Him?”
7.122	Look, the Father handed Christ over. Judas handed Him over too. Doesn’t it seem like they did the same kind of thing?
7.123	Judas is called a “traditor,” someone who hands over [or, a traitor]. Is God the Father that?
7.124	“Never!” you say.
7.125	I am not the one saying it. The apostle says it: “He did not spare His own Son but ‘tradidit Eum,’ handed Him over, for all of us.”
7.126	The Father handed Him over, and He also handed Himself over.
7.127	The same apostle says, “He loved me and handed Himself over for me.”
7.128	If the Father handed over the Son, and the Son handed over Himself, what did Judas do?
7.129	There was a “traditio” (a handing over) by the Father. There was a “traditio” by the Son. There was a “traditio” by Judas. The action is the same. So what makes them different: the Father handing over the Son, the Son handing over Himself, and Judas the disciple handing over his Master?
7.130	Here is the difference: the Father and the Son did it out of love, but Judas did it as a treacherous betrayal.
7.131	So you see, what matters is not simply what a person does. What matters is the attitude and the will behind it.
7.132	We find God the Father doing the same deed as Judas. Yet we bless the Father, and we hate Judas.
7.133	Why do we bless the Father and hate Judas?
7.134	We bless love, and we hate wickedness.
7.135	What a great gift the human race received when Christ was handed over!
7.136	Was Judas thinking about this when he handed Him over?
7.137	God was thinking about our salvation, which bought us back. Judas was thinking about the money he would get for selling the Lord.
7.138	The Son Himself was thinking about the price He paid for us. Judas was thinking about the price he was paid for selling Him.
7.139	So the different intentions make the actions different.
7.140	The action is one and the same. But when we measure it by the different intentions, we find that one deserves love and the other deserves condemnation. One deserves glory, and the other deserves to be hated.
7.141	That is how powerful love is.
7.142	See how love alone tells actions apart. Love alone shows the difference between the things people do.
7.143	8. We have said this about cases where the actions look the same.
7.144	But sometimes the actions are different. Then we find a person made fierce by love, and another made charming and gentle by wickedness.
7.145	A father beats a boy, while a kidnapper hugs a boy.
7.146	If you just name the two things, blows and hugs, who would not choose the hugs and refuse the blows?
7.147	But if you look at the people doing them, it is love that gives the blows and wickedness that gives the hugs.
7.148	See what we are stressing here. People’s actions can only be judged by whether they grow from the root of love.
7.149	Many things people do look good, and yet they do not grow from the root of love.
7.150	After all, thorn bushes have flowers too. And some actions really do seem rough, even cruel. Yet they are done to train and correct, because love commands it.
7.151	So here, once and for all, is one short rule for you: Love, and do what you want. If you stay silent, stay silent out of love. If you cry out, cry out because of love. If you correct someone, correct them out of love. If you go easy on someone, go easy on them out of love. Let the root of love be inside you. Nothing but good can grow from this root.
7.152	9. “This is love—this is how God’s love for us was shown: God sent His one and only Son into this world, so that we could live through Him.—This is love: not that we loved God, but that He loved us.” We did not love Him first. He loved us for this purpose: so that we would love Him. “And He sent His Son to be the Atoner for our sins.” The Latin word is “litatorem,” which means one who offers a sacrifice.
7.153	He offered a sacrifice for our sins.
7.154	Where did He find something to sacrifice?
7.155	Where did He find a pure victim to offer?
7.156	He found no other. He offered Himself.
7.157	“Dear friends, since God loved us this much, we also should love one another.
7.158	Peter,” He says, “do you love me?”
7.159	And he said, “I love you.”
7.160	“Feed my sheep.”
7.161	10. “No one has ever seen God.” He is invisible. We must look for Him with our hearts, not with our eyes.
7.162	If we wanted to see the sun, we would clean our physical eyes. In the same way, if we want to see God, let’s clean the eye that can see God.
7.163	Where is this eye?
7.164	Listen to the Gospel: “Blessed are those whose hearts are pure, for they will see God.”
7.165	But no one should picture God according to what his eyes crave.
7.166	If he does that, he makes up a huge shape for himself. Or he imagines something so enormous it cannot be measured, spreading out in every direction like the light he sees with his physical eyes. He stretches it across field after field of space, making it as big as he possibly can. Or he pictures God as an old man with a dignified look.
7.167	Don’t imagine any of these things.
7.168	There is something you may picture, if you want to see God: “God is love.”
7.169	What kind of face does love have? What shape does it have? How tall is it? What feet does it have? What hands? No one can say.
7.170	And yet it does have feet, because they carry people to church. It has hands, because they reach out to the poor. It has eyes, because with them we notice people in need. As the Bible says, “Blessed is the person who pays attention to the needy and the poor.”
7.171	It has ears, the ones the Lord speaks of: “Whoever has ears to hear should listen.”
7.172	These are not body parts in separate places. The person who has love sees all of it at once with his mind.
7.173	Live in it, and it will live in you. Make your home there, and you will become a home.
7.174	What do you say, brothers and sisters? Who loves something he cannot see?
7.175	So why, when love is praised, do you raise your hands, cheer and applaud?
7.176	What have I shown you?
7.177	Did I bring out something shining with colors?
7.178	Did I put gold and silver in front of you?
7.179	Did I dig up jewels from some hidden treasure?
7.180	Have I shown your eyes anything like that?
7.181	Does my face change while I am speaking?
7.182	I am a human being in a body. I look the same as when I came out to you. You look the same as when you came here. Yet love is praised, and you cheer.
7.183	You certainly don’t see anything.
7.184	But just as you enjoy praising love, enjoy keeping it in your hearts too.
7.185	Pay close attention to what I say, brothers and sisters. With God’s help, I am urging all of you toward a great treasure.
7.186	Imagine you were shown a beautiful little vase. It has raised designs, it is inlaid with gold, and it is skillfully made. It delights your eyes and pulls at the eager desire of your heart. You admire the artist’s skill, the weight of the silver, and the shine of the metal. Wouldn’t each of you say, “Oh, if only I had that vase!”
7.187	But it would do you no good to say it, because you would have no way to get it.
7.188	Or if someone really wanted it, he might think about stealing it from someone else’s house.
7.189	Love is being praised to you. If you like it, take it and make it yours. You don’t need to rob anyone. You don’t need to think about buying it. You can have it free, at no cost.
7.190	Take it and hold it close. There is nothing sweeter.
7.191	If it is this wonderful when we only talk about it, what must it be like to actually have it?
7.192	11. Brothers and sisters, some of you may want to hold on to love. If so, above all, do not think of it as something weak and lazy. And don’t think love is kept by a kind of gentleness. No, that isn’t gentleness at all. It is just being tame and not caring.
7.193	That is not how love is kept.
7.194	Don’t think you love your servant just because you never beat him. Don’t think you love your son just because you never discipline him. Don’t think you love your neighbor just because you never correct him. That is not love. It is just weakness.
7.195	Let love be eager to correct and to improve. If someone’s behavior is good, enjoy it. If it is bad, let it be fixed and corrected.
7.196	Don’t love the wrong that is in a person. Love the person. God made the person, but the person made the wrong himself.
7.197	Love what God made. Don’t love what the person made himself.
7.198	When you love the person, you take away the wrong. When you value the person, you correct the wrong.
7.199	Even if you have to be strict sometimes, let it be because of love, to correct.
7.200	That is why love was pictured by the Dove that came down on the Lord.
7.201	The Holy Spirit came in the shape of a dove. He is the One through whom love would be poured into us. Why did He come like that?
7.202	The dove has no bitter gall. Yet she fights for her young with her beak and wings. Her fierceness has no bitterness in it.
7.203	A father is the same way. When he punishes his son, he does it to train him.
7.204	As I said, a kidnapper lures a child with bitter sweet-talk so that he can sell him. A father punishes without bitterness so that he can correct.
7.205	Be like that toward everyone.
7.206	Look, brothers and sisters, here is a great lesson, a great rule. Each of you has children or wants to have them. Or if someone has fully decided not to have children in the body, at least he wants to have spiritual children. What father does not correct his son? What son does not get disciplined by his father?
7.207	And yet the father seems fierce with him.
7.208	It is the fierceness of love, the fierceness of caring. It is a kind of fierceness without bitterness, like the dove’s and not like the raven’s.
7.209	That reminds me, brothers and sisters, to tell you this. The people who have broken love are the ones who caused the split in the church. Just as they hate love itself, they also hate the dove.
7.210	But the dove proves them wrong. It comes down from heaven, the heavens open, and it stays on the Lord’s head.
7.211	Why does this happen? So that John could hear, “This is the One who baptizes.”
7.212	Get away, you robbers! Get away, you invaders of Christ’s property!
7.213	On your own properties, where you insist on being the masters, you have dared to put up the ownership signs of the great Owner.
7.214	He recognizes His own signs. He claims His own property for Himself.
7.215	He does not tear down the signs. Instead He comes in and takes over.
7.216	In the same way, when someone comes into the Catholic Church, his baptism is not canceled, so that the commander’s sign is not canceled. But what happens in the Catholic Church?
7.217	The sign is recognized. The Owner comes in under His own signs, where the robber was coming in under signs that were not his.
T:8	Homily 8 (1 John 4:12–16)
8.1	“If we love one another, God lives in us, and His love is made complete in us.
8.2	This is how we know that we live in Him and He lives in us: He has given us some of His Spirit.
8.3	And we have seen and we are witnesses that the Father sent the Son to be the Savior of the world.
8.4	If anyone openly says that Jesus is the Son of God, God lives in him, and he lives in God.
8.5	And we have come to know and believe the love God has for us.
8.6	God is love. Whoever lives in love lives in God, and God lives in him.”
8.7	1. Love is a sweet word, but the deed is even sweeter.
8.8	We can’t talk about it all the time. We have lots of things to do, and all kinds of business pull us in different directions. So our tongues don’t have time to talk about love constantly, even though our tongues could do nothing better.
8.9	But even if we can’t always talk about it, we can always keep it.
8.10	Think of the Alleluia we sing during this season. Are we singing it all the time?
8.11	We don’t sing Alleluia for one hour—I’m not even talking about the whole hour. We sing it for barely a few moments of one hour, and then we move on to something else.
8.12	As you already know, Alleluia means “Praise the Lord.”
8.13	The person who praises God with his tongue can’t do it all the time. But the person who praises God with his life and behavior can do it always.
8.14	Acts of mercy, loving feelings, holy devotion, clean and pure living, humble self-control: we should practice these things all the time. We should do them in public and at home, in front of people and in our own rooms, while talking and while silent, while busy and while free. We must always keep them, because all these good qualities I have named are on the inside.
8.15	But who could name them all?
8.16	It is as if an emperor’s army were stationed inside your mind.
8.17	An emperor gets whatever he wants done through his army. In the same way, once the Lord Jesus Christ begins to live in our inner self (that is, in the mind, through faith), He uses these good qualities as His servants.
8.18	These good qualities can’t be seen with the eyes. Yet when we name them, people praise them. And they would not be praised unless they were loved. They would not be loved unless they were seen. So if they are only loved when seen, they must be seen with a different eye: the inner sight of the heart. Through these invisible qualities, the parts of the body are set moving where everyone can see. The feet walk, but where do they go? They go wherever the good will moves them, the will that serves the good emperor like a soldier. The hands work, but on what? On whatever love commands, the love that the Holy Spirit breathes into us within.
8.19	So the body parts can be seen when they move. But the One who gives them orders from within cannot be seen. Who He is, the One giving orders within, is known almost only to Him, the One who gives the orders, and to the person inside who receives them.
8.20	2. Brothers and sisters, you heard this just now when the Gospel was read—at least if you listened not only with the ears of your body but also with the ears of your heart.
8.21	What did it say? “Be careful not to do your good deeds in front of people so they will see you.”
8.22	Did He mean that we should hide every good thing we do from people’s eyes, and be afraid of being seen?
8.23	If you are afraid of people watching, you won’t have anyone copying your example. So you should be seen.
8.24	But you must not do it just so that people will see you.
8.25	That should not be where your joy ends. That should not be the goal of your happiness—thinking you have gotten the full reward for your good deed once people see you and praise you.
8.26	That is worth nothing. When you are praised, think little of yourself. Let the One who works through you be praised in you.
8.27	So don’t do good things to be praised yourself. Do them for the praise of the One who gives you the power to do good.
8.28	Doing wrong comes from you. Doing right comes from God.
8.29	But look at twisted people and how backward they are.
8.30	When they do something good, they insist on taking the credit. When they do something bad, they insist on blaming God.
8.31	Turn this twisted, backward way of thinking around. It turns things upside down, so to speak. It puts on the bottom what belongs on top, and puts on top what belongs on the bottom.
8.32	Do you want to put God on the bottom and yourself on top?
8.33	You won’t lift yourself up. You will fall flat on your face, because He is always above.
8.34	What then? You do well, and God does badly? No! If you want to speak more truthfully, say this instead: I do badly, He does well. When I do something good, the good comes from Him. Whatever I do on my own is bad.
8.35	Admitting this makes the heart strong and lays a firm foundation for love.
8.36	If we must hide our good works so people won’t see them, what about what the Lord said in the sermon He preached on the mountain?
8.37	In that same place, a little earlier, He also said, “Let your good works shine in front of people.”
8.38	And He did not stop there or end there. He added, “And give glory to your Father in heaven.”
8.39	And what does the apostle say?
8.40	“The churches of Judea that were in Christ did not know my face. They had only heard people say, The man who used to persecute us is now preaching the faith he once tried to destroy.
8.41	And they praised God because of me.”
8.42	See how he too, when he became so well known, did not use the good to praise himself. He used it to praise God.
8.43	And as for himself—that he once tore the Church apart and was a persecutor, jealous and cruel—he admits this himself. We are not the ones blaming him for it.
8.44	Paul is glad for us to talk about his sins, so that the One who healed such a terrible disease may be glorified.
8.45	For it was the Doctor’s hand that cut open that huge wound and healed it.
8.46	That voice from heaven knocked down the persecutor and raised up the preacher. It killed Saul and brought Paul to life.
8.47	Saul was the one who persecuted a holy man. That is where this man got his name, back when he was persecuting the Christians. Later, instead of Saul he became Paul.
8.48	What does the name Paulus mean?
8.49	It means “little.” So when he was Saul, he was proud and puffed up. When he was Paul, he was humble and little.
8.50	That’s why we say, I will see you “paulo post,” which means, after a little while.
8.51	Listen to how he was made little: “For I am the least of the apostles.” And in another place he says, “To me, the least of all God’s holy people.”
8.52	So among the apostles he was like the hem of a robe. But the Church of the non-Jewish nations touched it, like the woman who had the bleeding illness, and was made well.
8.53	3. So, brothers and sisters, this is what I want to say. This is what I am saying. If I could help it, I would never leave this unsaid: Do these good works at one time and those at another, depending on the time, the hour, and the day.
8.54	Should you always be talking? Always be silent? Always be eating to refresh your body? Always be fasting? Always be giving bread to the needy? Always be giving clothes to people who have none? Always be visiting the sick? Always be making peace between people who disagree? Always be burying the dead?
8.55	No. Now you do this, then you do that.
8.56	You start these things, and then they stop. But the thing that commands all the forces inside you like an emperor has no starting point and should never stop.
8.57	Let love inside you never take a break. But let the acts of love be done at the right times.
8.58	So, as the Bible says, let “brotherly love” go on. Let “brotherly love continue.”
8.59	4. But maybe some of you have been wondering all along, while we have been explaining this letter of blessed John to you. Why is it only “brotherly” love that he praises so strongly?
8.60	He says, “Whoever loves his brother.” And he says, “We have been given a command to love one another.”
8.61	Over and over he talks about brotherly love. But love for God, the love we should have for God, he does not mention nearly as often. Still, he does not leave it out completely.
8.62	But about loving an enemy, he says almost nothing in the whole letter.
8.63	He preaches love to us with great passion and praises it. Yet he does not tell us to love our enemies. He tells us to love our brothers and sisters.
8.64	But just now, when the Gospel was read, we heard, “If you love the people who love you, what reward will you get?
8.65	Don’t even the tax collectors do that?”
8.66	So how can this be? John the apostle praises brotherly love as something very important for us, to help us become complete in a certain way. But the Lord says it is not enough to love our brothers and sisters. He says we must stretch that love so far that it reaches even our enemies.
8.67	Whoever reaches as far as his enemies does not jump over his brothers and sisters.
8.68	Like fire, love has to catch hold of what is closest first, and then spread to what is farther away.
8.69	A brother is closer to you than some random person.
8.70	And a person you don’t know, who is not against you, has more claim on you than an enemy who is against you.
8.71	Stretch your love to the people closest to you. But don’t call that stretching. Loving the people close to you is almost the same as loving yourself.
8.72	Stretch it to strangers who have done you no harm.
8.73	Go past even them. Reach all the way to loving your enemies.
8.74	This, at least, is what the Lord commands.
8.75	So why has the apostle said nothing here about loving an enemy?
8.76	5. Think about every kind of love. There is the kind called fleshly love, which is usually called not “dilectio” but “amor.” (The word “dilectio” is usually used for better things and understood to mean better things.) Yet every kind of love, dear brothers and sisters, includes wishing good things for the ones who are loved.
8.77	For we should not love like this, and we are not able to love like this (whether we say “diligere” or “amare”—the Lord used the second word when He said, “Petra, amas me?”
8.78	“Peter, do you love me?”). We should not love people the way we hear greedy eaters say, I love thrushes.
8.79	Do you ask why he loves them?
8.80	So he can kill them and eat them.
8.81	He says he loves them. But he loves them so that they will stop existing. He loves them so that he can get rid of them.
8.82	And whatever we love as food, we love so that it will be eaten up and we will be refreshed.
8.83	Should people be loved like that, so that they get used up?
8.84	But there is a kind of friendly goodwill. It makes us want, at some point, to do good to the people we love.
8.85	What if there is nothing good we can do for them?
8.86	Then the goodwill, the wishing well, is enough by itself for the one who loves.
8.87	We should not want people to be miserable just so we can do acts of mercy.
8.88	You give bread to the hungry. But it would be better if no one were hungry and you had no one to give bread to.
8.89	You give clothes to people who have none. Oh, if only everyone had clothes and there were no such need!
8.90	You bury the dead. Oh, if only that life had finally come, where no one will ever die!
8.91	You make peace between people who are fighting. Oh, if only it were finally here, that everlasting peace of Jerusalem, where no one will disagree!
8.92	All these are services we do because people are in need.
8.93	Take away the miserable, and acts of mercy will come to an end.
8.94	Acts of mercy will end. But will the fire of love be put out?
8.95	You love a happy person with a truer kind of love, because there is no favor you can do for him. That love will be purer and much more genuine.
8.96	When you do a kindness for a miserable person, you might want to put yourself above him. You might want him to be under you, since you are the one who helped him.
8.97	He was in need, and you gave. And because you gave, you think of yourself as greater than the person who received.
8.98	Wish for him to be your equal, so that you can both be under the One Lord, to whom no one can give anything.
8.99	6. This is where the proud soul has gone past its limits and, in a way, become greedy.
8.100	For, “The root of all evils is greed.” And it is also said, “The beginning of all sin is pride.”
8.101	And maybe we ask how these two sayings fit together: “The root of all evils is greed,” and, “The beginning of all sin is pride.”
8.102	If pride is the beginning of all sin, then pride is the root of all evils.
8.103	Yet it is certainly true that “the root of all evils is greed.”
8.104	We find that pride also has greed (or grabbing) in it. For man has gone past his limits. And what is being greedy? It is going beyond what is enough.
8.105	Adam fell because of pride. “The beginning of all sin is pride,” it says. Did he fall because of greed?
8.106	Who could be greedier than someone for whom God was not enough?
8.107	In fact, brothers and sisters, we read that man was made in the image and likeness of God. And what did God say about him?
8.108	“Let him have power over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, and all the livestock that move on the earth.”
8.109	Did He say, Have power over people?
8.110	“Have power,” He says. He has given him a natural power. “Have power” over what? “Over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, and every creature that moves on the earth.”
8.111	Why is this power over these creatures a natural power?
8.112	Because man gets this power from the fact that he was made in God’s image.
8.113	And in what way was he made in God’s image?
8.114	In his intellect, in his mind, in his inner self. He can understand truth. He can tell right from wrong. He knows who made him. He is able to understand his Creator and to praise his Creator. The person who has wisdom has this kind of understanding.
8.115	But many people wore away God’s image in themselves through evil desires. By their twisted way of living, they put out the very flame of understanding, so to speak. So the Scripture cried out to them, “Don’t be like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding.”
8.116	In other words: I have put you above the horse and the mule. I made you in my image. I have given you power over these animals.
8.117	Why? Because they don’t have a reasoning mind. But you, with your reasoning mind, can grasp truth, and you understand what is above you. So place yourself under the One who is above you, and the things you were put in charge of will be under you.
8.118	But through sin, man abandoned the One he should be under. So now he has been put under the things he should be above.
8.119	7. Pay attention to what I say: God, man, animals. Above you is God. Below you are the animals.
8.120	Recognize the One above you, so that the creatures below you will recognize you.
8.121	That is why, since Daniel recognized God as above him, the lions recognized Daniel as above them.
8.122	But if you do not recognize the One above you, you look down on the One who is greater than you, and you end up under what is lower than you.
8.123	For example, how was the pride of the Egyptians crushed?
8.124	By frogs and flies.
8.125	God could have sent lions. But a great man might be scared of a lion.
8.126	The prouder they were, the more their wicked necks were broken by small, weak, worthless things.
8.127	But the lions recognized Daniel, because he was under God.
8.128	So what about the martyrs who were thrown to the wild beasts to fight them, and were torn apart by the teeth of savage animals? Were they not under God? Or were those three men servants of God, but the Maccabees not servants of God?
8.129	The fire recognized the three men as God’s servants. It did not burn them or even damage their clothes. So didn’t the fire recognize the Maccabees too?
8.130	Yes, it recognized the Maccabees. It did, brothers and sisters, recognize them too.
8.131	But by the Lord’s permission, a whipping was needed. He has said in Scripture, “He whips every son He accepts.”
8.132	Brothers and sisters, do you think the iron could have pierced deep into the Lord’s body unless He had allowed it? Or that He would have hung nailed to the cross unless He wanted to?
8.133	Didn’t His own creation recognize Him?
8.134	Or was He giving His faithful people an example of patience?
8.135	So you see, God rescued some people in a way everyone could see, and others He did not rescue in a visible way. Yet He rescued all of them spiritually. Spiritually, He abandoned no one.
8.136	To the eye, He seemed to abandon some, and He seemed to rescue others.
8.137	He rescued some so that you would not think He lacked the power to rescue.
8.138	He has proved that He has the power. So when He does not rescue, you should understand that there is a more hidden will at work. Don’t guess that it was too hard for Him.
8.139	But what then, brothers and sisters? One day we will escape all these traps of our mortal life. The times of temptation will be over. The river of this world will have flowed past. We will get back that “first robe,” the immortality we lost by sinning. “This body that decays will have put on what cannot decay,” that is, this flesh will be clothed with what cannot decay, “and this body that dies will have put on immortality.” Then we will be God’s children, now made complete, with no more need to be tempted or whipped, and all creatures will recognize us. All things will be placed under us, if here we place ourselves under God.
8.140	8. So a Christian should be the kind of person who does not brag that he is above other “people.”
8.141	God has given you a place over the animals, meaning that you are better than the animals.
8.142	You have this by nature. You will always be better than an animal.
8.143	If you want to be better than another person, you will resent him when you see that he is your equal.
8.144	You should want everyone to be your equal. And if you are wiser than someone, you should want him to become wise too.
8.145	As long as he is slow, he learns from you. As long as he is untaught, he needs you. You are seen as the teacher and he as the student. So you seem to be the greater one, because you are the teacher, and he seems to be the lesser one, because he is the student.
8.146	If you don’t want him to be your equal, then you want him to stay a student forever.
8.147	But if you want him to stay a student forever, you will be a jealous teacher.
8.148	And if you are a jealous teacher, how can you really be a teacher?
8.149	Please, don’t teach him your jealousy.
8.150	Listen to the apostle speaking from a heart full of love: “I wish everyone were just like me.” In what way did he want everyone to be his equal?
8.151	This is how he was greater than everyone: out of love, he wanted all people to be his equals.
8.152	So I say, man has gone past his limits. He wanted to grab more than was his. He wanted to be above other people—he who was made to be above the animals. And that is pride.
