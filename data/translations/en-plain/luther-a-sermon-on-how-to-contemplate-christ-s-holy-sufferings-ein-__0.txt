H:0.0	I. Wrong Ways of Looking at Christ's Sufferings
0.1	1. First, some people think about Christ's sufferings in a way that makes them angry at the Jews. They sing and grieve over poor Judas, and then they are satisfied. It is just like the way they are used to complaining about other people. They condemn them and spend their time with their enemies.
0.2	This kind of exercise could truly be called thinking about the wickedness of Judas and the Jews, not about the sufferings of Christ.
0.3	2. Second, others have pointed out the different benefits and fruits that come from thinking about Christ's Passion.
0.4	Here people are misled by a saying credited to Albertus. It says that thinking just once, on the surface, about Christ's sufferings is better than fasting for a whole year or praying through the Psalms every day, and so on.
0.5	So people blindly follow him and act against the true fruits of Christ's Passion. For they are looking for their own selfish gain in it.
0.6	That is why they decorate themselves with pictures and little books, with letters and crucifixes. Some even go so far as to imagine that these things protect them from the dangers of water, fire, and the sword, and from every other danger.
0.7	In this way they expect Christ's suffering to keep them from suffering. But that goes against its nature and character.
0.8	3. A third group feels so sorry for Christ that they weep and grieve for him because he was so innocent. They are like the women who followed Christ out of Jerusalem. He rebuked them and told them they should rather weep for themselves and for their children.
0.9	These are the people who run far away in the middle of the Passion season. They get great benefit from Christ's leaving Bethany and from the pain and sorrow of the Virgin Mary. But they never get any further.
0.10	So they put off the Passion for many hours. And only God knows whether this is planned more for sleeping than for staying awake.
0.11	Among these fanatics are also those who taught what great blessings come from the holy mass. In their simple way, they think it is enough just to attend mass.
0.12	Some teachers have led us to this with their sayings. They say that the mass works opere operati, non opere operantis, and is acceptable in itself, even without our merit and worthiness. They act as if that were enough.
0.13	But the mass was not set up for the sake of its own worthiness. It was given to test us, and especially so that we would think deeply about Christ's sufferings.
0.14	Where this does not happen, we turn the mass into a passing work that bears no fruit, however good it may be in itself.
0.15	What good is it to you that God is God, if he is not God to you?
0.16	What good is it that eating and drinking are healthy and good in themselves, if they are not healthy for you? And we should fear that our many masses never make us any better, if we do not look for the true fruit in them.
H:0.4	II. The Right Way of Looking at Christ's Sufferings
0.17	4. Fourth, the people who think about Christ's Passion in the right way are those who look at Christ so that their hearts are filled with terror at the sight. Their conscience sinks at once into despair.
0.18	This terror should rise up in you so that you see God's severe anger and his unchanging seriousness about sin and sinners. He was not willing for his only, dearly loved Son to set sinners free unless the Son paid the costly ransom for them. As Isaiah 53:8 says: "He was struck for the wrongdoing of my people."
0.19	What happens to the sinner, then, when the dear child is struck like this?
0.20	There must be a seriousness here that words cannot express and no one can bear. For a person so great beyond measure goes out to meet it, suffers, and dies for it. If you think very deeply about this, that God's Son, the eternal wisdom of the Father, suffers himself, you will be filled with terror. And the more you think about it, the deeper the impression will be.
0.21	5. Fifth, you must believe deeply and never doubt in the least that you are the one who put Christ through this torment.
0.22	For it was surely your sins that did it.
0.23	This is how St. Peter struck the Jews with terror, like a bolt of lightning, in Acts 2:36-37. He spoke to all of them together: "You crucified him." So three thousand people were filled with terror that same day. Trembling, they cried out to the apostles: "Dear brothers, what are we to do?"
0.24	So when you see the nails piercing his hands, firmly believe that this is your doing.
0.25	When you see his crown of thorns, believe that the thorns are your wicked thoughts, and so on.
0.26	6. Sixth, now see this: where one thorn pierces Christ, more than a thousand thorns should pierce you. Yes, they should pierce you like this forever, and even more painfully.
0.27	Where one nail is driven through his hands and feet, you should suffer nails like these forever, and even more painful ones. And this will indeed happen to those who let Christ's sufferings be lost and wasted on them.
0.28	For Christ, this serious mirror, will not lie or mock. Whatever he says must surely come true.
0.29	7. Seventh, St. Bernard was so terrified by Christ's sufferings that he said: I thought I was safe. I knew nothing of the eternal sentence passed on me in heaven. Then I saw that the eternal Son of God took pity on me. He stepped forward and offered himself for me in that same judgment.
0.30	Oh, it is not right for me to keep on playing and feeling safe when there is such seriousness behind those sufferings.
0.31	So he commanded the women: "Do not weep for me. Weep for yourselves and for your children."
0.32	Luke 23:28. And in verse 31 he gives the reason: "For if they do these things to the green tree, what will be done to the dry one?"
0.33	It is as if he were saying: Learn from my torment what you have deserved and how you should be repaid.
0.34	For here the saying is true: a little dog was killed to frighten a big one.
0.35	In the same way the prophet also said: "All generations will mourn and weep over themselves more than over him." It does not say they will mourn for him, but for themselves rather than for him.
0.36	In the same way, the apostles were also filled with terror in Acts 2:27, as mentioned before, so that they said to the apostles: "Oh, brothers, what are we to do?"
0.37	So the church also sings: I will think about it carefully, and so my soul within me will wear itself out.
0.38	8. Eighth, a person must practice this point with skill. For the benefit of Christ's sufferings depends almost completely on this: that a person comes to truly know himself, and is terrified and slain before himself.
0.39	Where a person does not come to this point, Christ's sufferings have been of no true benefit to him.
0.40	For the natural work that is typical of Christ's sufferings is to make all people equal and alike. Christ was horribly tortured in body and soul in our sins. So we too, like him, must be tortured in our consciences by our sins.
0.41	This does not happen through many words. It happens through deep thoughts and a deep awareness of our sins.
0.42	Here is an example. Suppose a criminal were sentenced because he had killed the child of a prince or king. And suppose you were safe, singing and playing as if you were completely innocent. Then someone seized you in a terrifying way and proved to you that you had made it possible for that wicked man to do the deed. You would be in the greatest distress, especially if your own conscience also turned against you.
0.43	You should be much more anxious than that when you think about Christ's sufferings.
0.44	For the evildoers, the Jews, have now judged and banished God. Even so, they were the servants of your sins. You are truly the one who strangled and crucified the Son of God through your sins, as has been said.
0.45	9. Ninth, a person may find that he is so hard and barren that Christ's sufferings do not terrify him or lead him to a knowledge of him. That person should fear and tremble.
0.46	It cannot be any other way. You must become like the picture and the sufferings of Christ, whether that happens in life or in hell. At the time of death, if not sooner, you must fall into terror. You must tremble and shake and go through everything Christ suffered on the cross.
0.47	It is truly terrible to have to deal with this on your deathbed. So you should pray that God will soften your heart and let you think about Christ's Passion in a way that bears fruit.
0.48	For we cannot think deeply about Christ's sufferings on our own, unless God sinks them into our hearts.
0.49	Also, neither this meditation nor any other teaching is given to you so that you would rush at it on your own to get it done. First you are to seek and long for God's grace, so that you may do it through God's grace and not by your own strength.
0.50	That is why the people mentioned above never deal with Christ's sufferings in the right way. They never call on God for this. Instead, they come up with their own way, based on their own ability. And they treat those sufferings in a purely human way that bears no fruit.
0.51	10. Tenth, suppose someone thinks about God's sufferings in this way for a day, an hour, or even a quarter of an hour. We want to say freely and openly that this is better than fasting a whole year and praying through the Psalms every day. Yes, it is better than hearing a hundred masses.
0.52	For this kind of meditation changes a person's character. He is born again, made new, almost as in baptism.
0.53	Then Christ's suffering does its true, natural, and noble work. It kills the old Adam. It drives out all the lust, pleasure, and security that a person may get from God's creatures, just as Christ was abandoned by everyone, even by God.
0.54	11. Eleventh, this work is not in our hands. So it sometimes happens that we pray for it and do not receive it at that time. Even so, we should not despair or stop praying.
0.55	Sometimes it comes when we are not praying for it, as God knows and wills. For it wants to be free and unbound. Then a person is troubled in his conscience and deeply unhappy with his own life. It may easily happen that he does not know that Christ's Passion is doing this very thing in him. Perhaps he was not aware of it. In the same way, the others thought about Christ's Passion so exclusively that, in their knowledge of themselves, they could not get free from that state of meditation.
0.56	With the first group, Christ's sufferings are quiet and true. With the others, they are a show and false. And by its nature, God often turns the page. So those who do not meditate on the Passion really do meditate on it. Those who hear mass do not hear it. And those who do not hear it do hear it.
H:0.13	III. The Comfort of Christ's Sufferings
0.57	12. Up to now we have been in Passion Week and have kept Good Friday in the right way. Now we come to Easter and Christ's resurrection.
0.58	When a person sees his sins in this light and is completely terrified in his conscience, he must be careful. His sins must not stay in his conscience like this, or nothing but pure doubt will come of it. Just as the sins flowed out of Christ and we became aware of them, so we should pour them back onto him and set our conscience free.
0.59	So be careful not to act like twisted people. They bite and eat themselves up in their hearts with their sins. They run here and there with their good works or with their own ways of making up for sin. Or they even work their way out of this condition with indulgences and get rid of their sins. But that is impossible. And sadly, this false refuge of making amends and going on pilgrimages has spread far and wide.
0.60	13. Thirteenth. Then throw your sins off yourself and onto Christ. Believe with a joyful spirit that your sins are his wounds and sufferings, that he carries them and pays for them. As Isaiah 53:6 says: "Jehovah has laid on him the wrongdoing of us all." And St. Peter says in his first letter, 2:24: "He himself carried our sins in his body on the tree" of the cross. And St. Paul says in 2 Corinthians 5:21: "He who knew no sin was made to be sin for us, so that we might become the righteousness of God in him."
0.61	You must lean on these passages and others like them with all your weight. And the harder your conscience torments you, the more you must do so.
0.62	If you do not do this, and you miss the chance to quiet your heart, you will never find peace. In the end you will still despair in doubt.
0.63	For suppose we deal with our sins in our own conscience. We let them stay inside us and nurse them in our hearts. Then they become much too strong for us to handle. And they will live forever.
0.64	But when we see that they are laid on Christ and that he has conquered them by his resurrection, and we believe this without fear, then they are dead. They have become nothing.
0.65	For they cannot stay on Christ. There they are swallowed up by his resurrection. And now you see no wound and no pain in him, that is, no sign of sin.
0.66	This is what St. Paul says in Romans 4:25: he was given up because of our wrongs and raised so that we would be declared righteous. That is, in his sufferings he made our sins known and also crucified them. But by his resurrection he makes us righteous and free from all sin, even if we believe this in different ways.
0.67	14. Fourteenth. Now if you cannot believe, then, as I said before, you should pray to God for faith.
0.68	For this is something that is entirely free and in God's hands. It is also given in the same way, sometimes knowingly and sometimes secretly, as was just said about suffering.
0.69	15. But now stir yourself toward this goal. First, stop looking at Christ's sufferings. They have already done their work and terrified you. Instead, push through every difficulty and look at his friendly heart. See how full of love it is toward you. That love moved him to carry the heavy load of your conscience and your sin.
0.70	Then your heart will become loving and sweet toward him, and the certainty of your faith will grow stronger.
0.71	Then climb higher, through the heart of Christ to the heart of God. See that Christ could not have loved you if God had not willed it in eternal love. Christ obeys that love in his love for you. There you will find the good, divine heart of a Father. And, as Christ says, you will be drawn to the Father through Christ in this way.
0.72	Then you will understand what Christ says in John 3:16: "God loved the world so much that he gave his only Son," and so on.
0.73	That is what it means to know God rightly: when we grasp him not by his power and wisdom, which terrify us, but by his goodness and love. Then our faith and trust can stand firm and unshaken. And in this way a person is truly born again in God.
0.74	16. Sixteenth. Your heart may now be established in Christ like this. You may be an enemy of sin out of love, and not out of fear of punishment. Then Christ's sufferings should also be an example for your whole life. You should think about them in a different way.
0.75	Up to now we have looked at Christ's Passion as a sacrament that works in us while we undergo it. Now we look at it so that we also do the work. It goes like this: if a day of sorrow or sickness weighs you down, think how small that is compared with the thorns and nails of Christ.
0.76	If you have to do something, or not do something, that you find unpleasant, think how Christ was led here and there, tied up and a prisoner.
0.77	Does pride attack you? Look at how your Lord was mocked and disgraced along with murderers.
0.78	Do sexual impurity and lust push against you? Think how bitter it was for Christ to have his tender flesh torn, pierced, and beaten again and again.
0.79	Do hatred and envy fight against you, or do you want revenge? Remember how Christ, with many tears and cries, prayed for you and for all his enemies. And he surely had more reason to want revenge.
0.80	If trouble or any hardship of body or soul hits you, strengthen your heart and say: Oh, why shouldn't I also suffer a little? My Lord sweat blood in the garden because of fear and grief.
0.81	A servant would be lazy and shameful if he wanted to lie in bed while his master had to fight the agony of death.
0.82	17. See, this is how we can find strength and comfort in Christ against every vice and bad habit.
0.83	That is the right way to keep Christ's Passion, and that is the fruit of his suffering. Whoever practices it in this way does better than by hearing the whole Passion or reading all the masses.
0.84	And the people who are called true Christians are those who build the life and name of Christ into their own lives. As St. Paul says in Galatians 5:24: "And those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and lusts."
0.85	For Christ's Passion must not be handled with words and outward show, but in our lives and in truth.
0.86	So St. Paul urges us in Hebrews 12:3: "Think about him who put up with such opposition from sinners against himself, so that you do not grow tired and lose heart in your souls." And St. Peter says in his first letter, 4:1: "As Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves too with the same mind."
0.87	But this kind of meditation has now fallen out of use and is very rare, even though the letters of St. Paul and St. Peter are full of it.
0.88	We have turned the real thing into a mere show. We have painted the meditation on Christ's sufferings only in letters and on walls.
