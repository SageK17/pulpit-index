5.34	2. Now pay attention to these words.
5.35	I am still pressing this on you: see what a tight spot we are in. So stretch your minds—that is, pray for us and for yourselves—so that God may make room for us and show us a way out. We do not want anyone to find in God's word a reason for his own ruin. That word was preached and written down only to heal and to save.
5.36	“Everyone who sins,” John says, “also does wickedness.”
5.37	In case you try to draw a line between the two: “Sin is wickedness.”
5.38	In case you say, “I am a sinner, but I do not do wickedness”: “Sin is wickedness.
5.39	And you know that He appeared for this reason: to take away sin. And there is no sin in Him.”
5.40	And how does it help us that He came without sin?
5.41	“Everyone who does not sin stays in Him. And everyone who sins has not seen Him and has not known Him.
5.42	Little children, do not let anyone lead you astray.
5.43	The person who does what is right is righteous, just as He is righteous.”
5.44	We have already said this: the word “as” is usually used to show a kind of likeness, not that two things are equal.
5.45	“The person who sins belongs to the devil, because the devil has been sinning from the beginning.”
5.46	We have also said this already: the devil did not create anyone, and he did not father anyone. But those who copy him are, in a way, born from him.
5.47	“The Son of God appeared for this reason: to undo the works of the devil.”
5.48	So the One who has no sin is the One who comes to undo (or set loose) sins.
5.49	Then comes this: “Everyone who is born of God does not sin. For his seed stays in him. He cannot sin, because he is born of God.” He has pulled the rope tight! Perhaps he means one particular sin when he says, “does not sin,” and not every sin. So when he says, “Whoever is born of God does not sin,” you can take it to mean one special sin that a person born of God cannot commit. And this sin is the kind that, if someone commits it, it locks all the other sins in place.
5.50	What is this sin?
5.51	Going against the commandment.
5.52	What is the commandment?
5.53	“I give you a new commandment: love one another.”
5.54	Pay close attention! This commandment of Christ is called “love.”
5.55	Through this love, sins are undone.
5.56	If this love is not kept, then failing to hold on to it is a serious sin. It is also the root of all other sins.
5.57	3. Pay close attention, brothers and sisters. We have put forward something that solves the question for those who understand well.
5.58	But should we only walk along the road with those who run faster?
5.59	Those who walk more slowly must not be left behind.
5.60	Let us turn the matter over from every side, using whatever words we can, so that everyone can reach it.
5.61	For I think, brothers and sisters, that a person cares about his own soul if he does not come to church for no reason. He does not look for things that pass away in the Church. He does not come here to do worldly business. He comes here to take hold of something eternal that has been promised to him, something he can reach. Such a person has to think about how to walk on the road. He does not want to be left behind, or turn back, or wander off, or limp and never arrive.
5.62	So whoever is serious about this—whether he is slow or fast—must not leave the road.
5.63	So this is what I have said. When John says, “Whoever is born of God does not sin,” he probably meant one particular sin. Otherwise it would go against this other passage: “If we say that we have no sin, we fool ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.64	So that is how the question can be solved.
5.65	There is a certain sin that a person born of God cannot commit. When this sin is not committed, other sins are undone. When it is committed, other sins are locked in place.
5.66	What is this sin?
5.67	Going against the commandment of Christ, against the New Testament.
5.68	What is the new commandment?
5.69	“I give you a new commandment: love one another.”
5.70	If someone goes against love, and against love for his brother, he should not dare to boast and say that he is born of God. But if someone lives in love for his brother, there are certain sins he cannot commit. Above all, he cannot hate his brother.
5.71	And what about his other sins? About those it is said, “If we say that we have no sin, we fool ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.72	Let him listen to another place in Scripture that will put his mind at rest: “Love covers a great many sins.”
5.73	4. So we praise love, and this letter praises love.
5.74	After the Lord rose from the dead, what question did He ask Peter? Only this: “Do you love Me?”
5.75	Asking once was not enough. The second time He asked no other question. The third time, no other question either.
5.76	When it came to the third time, Peter was hurt. He did not understand the point of it, and it seemed as if the Lord did not believe him. Still, the Lord asked this question a first time, a second time, and a third time.
5.77	Three times fear denied; three times love confessed.
5.78	Look, Peter loves the Lord.
5.79	What should he do for the Lord?
5.80	Do not think that the writer of the Psalm did not also feel unsure about what to do: “What can I give back to the Lord for all the good things He has done for me?”
5.81	The one who said this in the Psalm noticed what great things God had done for him. He looked for something to give back to God, and he could find nothing.
5.82	For whatever you want to give back, you first received from Him, so that you could give it.
5.83	So what did he find to offer in return?
5.84	As we said, my brothers and sisters, the only thing he found to offer back was what he had received from God.
5.85	“I will receive the cup of salvation, and call on the name of the Lord.”
5.86	For who had given him the cup of salvation? Only the One he wanted to give something back to.
5.87	Now, to receive the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord means to be filled with love. It means being so full of love that you will not only not hate your brother, but you will be ready to die for your brother.
5.88	This is perfect love: to be ready to die for your brother.
5.89	The Lord showed this in Himself. He died for all. He prayed for the very people who crucified Him, saying, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.”
5.90	But if He were the only one who did this, He would not be a Teacher, since He would have no disciples.
5.91	Disciples who came after Him have done this too.
5.92	People were throwing stones at Stephen. He knelt down and said, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.”
5.93	He loved the people who were killing him, because he was dying for them too.
5.94	Listen also to the apostle Paul: “And I myself,” he says, “will be used up for your souls.”
5.95	For Paul was one of the people Stephen asked God to forgive as he was dying at their hands.
5.96	So this is perfect love.
5.97	If someone has so much love that he is ready even to die for his brothers and sisters, love is perfect in that person.
5.98	But is love already completely perfect the moment it is born?
5.99	It is born so that it can become perfect. Once it is born, it is fed. Once it is fed, it grows strong. Once it is strong, it is made perfect. And when it has become perfect, what does it say?
5.100	“For me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain.
5.101	I wanted to leave this life and be with Christ, which is far better. But for your sake it is necessary that I stay in the body.”
5.102	He was willing to live for the sake of the same people he was ready to die for.
5.103	5. You should know that this perfect love is what a person born of God does not break and does not sin against. That is why the Lord says to Peter, “Peter, do you love Me?”
5.104	And he answers, “I love You.”
5.105	The Lord does not say, “If you love Me, be kind to Me.”
5.106	When the Lord lived in a mortal body, He got hungry and thirsty. At that time, when He was hungry and thirsty, people welcomed Him as a guest. Those who had money took care of Him with what they owned, as we read in the Gospel.
5.107	Zacchaeus welcomed Him as a guest. By welcoming the Doctor, he was healed of his disease.
5.108	What disease? The disease of greed.
5.109	For he was very rich, and he was the chief of the tax collectors.
5.110	Look at this man, healed from the disease of greed: “I give half of what I own to the poor. And if I have taken anything from anyone, I will pay him back four times as much.”
5.111	He kept the other half, not to enjoy it, but to pay his debts.
5.112	So at that time he welcomed the Doctor as his guest. He could do this because the Lord had a weak human body, and people could show Him this kindness. And the Lord allowed it because He wanted to give this very gift to those who served Him kindly. The good of it went to those who served, not to Him.
5.113	For how could He, whom angels served, need these people's kindness?
5.114	Even His servant Elijah did not need this. Once God sent him bread and meat by ravens. And yet, so that a godly widow might be blessed, God's servant was sent to her. The man whom God had fed in secret was fed by the widow.
5.115	Still, those who care for the needs of these servants of God do good to themselves. They gain the reward the Lord clearly described in the Gospel: “Whoever welcomes a righteous person because he is righteous will receive a righteous person's reward. Whoever welcomes a prophet because he is a prophet will receive a prophet's reward. And whoever gives even a cup of cold water to one of these little ones because he is a disciple—I tell you the truth, he will certainly not lose his reward.” So those who do this do it for their own good. But when the Lord was about to go up into heaven, this kind of service could no longer be done for Him.
5.116	So what could Peter, who loved Him, give back to Him?
5.117	Listen: “Feed My sheep.” In other words: do for your brothers and sisters what I have done for you.
5.118	I bought everyone back with My blood. Do not hold back from dying for confessing the truth, so that the others may follow your example.
5.119	6. But this, as we have said, brothers and sisters, is perfect love.
5.120	The person who is born of God has it.
5.121	Pay attention, my dear friends. See what I am saying.
5.122	Look, a person has received the sacrament of that birth by being baptized. He has the sacrament—a great sacrament, divine and holy, beyond words.
5.123	Think what a sacrament that is!
5.124	It makes him a new person by forgiving all his sins!
5.125	Even so, he should look closely at his heart. He should check whether what was done to his body has truly been done in his heart too. Let him see whether he has love. Then he can say, “I am born of God.”
5.126	But if he does not have love, he may carry the soldier's mark on him, but he is wandering around like a deserter.
5.127	Let him have love. Otherwise, he should not say that he is born of God.
5.128	But he says, “I have the sacrament.”
5.129	Listen to the apostle: “If I know all mysteries, and have all faith so that I can move mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing.”
5.130	7. If you remember, we told you this when we began to read this letter: nothing in it is praised as much as love.
5.131	Even when it seems to talk about other things, it always finds its way back to love. Whatever it says, it brings it all back to love.
5.132	Let us see whether it does that here.
5.133	Look: “Whoever is born of God does not sin.”
5.134	We ask, “What sin?” If you take it to mean every sin, it goes against this passage: “If we say that we have no sin, we fool ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.135	So let John tell us what sin he means. Let him teach us. Otherwise I may have been too quick to say that the sin here is breaking love. I said it because he said earlier, “Whoever hates his brother is in darkness. He walks in darkness and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.”
5.136	But maybe he says something in what comes next, and mentions love by name?
5.137	See how this long chain of words ends up here and leads out to this.
5.138	“Whoever is born of God does not sin, because His seed stays in him.”
5.139	The “seed” of God means the word of God. That is why the apostle says, “I became your father through the Gospel.
5.140	And he cannot sin, because he is born of God.”
5.141	Let him tell us this. Let us see in what way we cannot sin.
5.142	“This is how the children of God and the children of the devil are shown for what they are.
5.143	Whoever does not do what is right is not from God, and neither is the one who does not love his brother.”
5.144	Yes! Now it is clear what he is talking about: “And neither is the one who does not love his brother.”
5.145	So love alone makes the difference between the children of God and the children of the devil.
5.146	They may all make the sign of the cross of Christ on themselves. They may all answer, “Amen.” They may all sing “Alleluia.” They may all be baptized. They may all come to church. They may all build the walls of churches. But there is no way to tell the children of God from the children of the devil, except by love.
5.147	Those who have love are born of God. Those who do not have it are not born of God.
5.148	What a powerful sign! What a powerful difference!
5.149	Have whatever you want—if this one thing is missing, it does you no good. If you are missing other things, have this, and you have kept the whole law.
5.150	“For whoever loves another has kept the whole law,” says the apostle. And, “Love is the fulfilling of the law.”
5.151	I believe this is the pearl the merchant in the Gospel was looking for. He “found one pearl, sold everything he had, and bought it.”
5.152	This is the precious pearl: Love. Without it, whatever you have does you no good. And if you have only this, it is enough for you.
5.153	Now you see by faith. Later you will see with your own eyes.
5.154	For if we love now, when we cannot see, how tightly we will hold on to it when we do see!
5.155	But what should we practice on?
5.156	On love for our brothers and sisters. You might say to me, “I have not seen God.” But can you say to me, “I have not seen a human being”?
5.157	Love your brother. For if you love your brother whom you see, you will see God too at the same time. Because you will see Love itself, and God lives inside.
5.158	8. “Whoever does not do what is right is not from God, and neither is the one who does not love his brother.”
5.159	“For this is the message.” See how he backs it up: “For this is the message we heard from the beginning: that we should love one another.”
5.160	He has made it clear to us that this is what he is talking about. Whoever acts against this commandment is caught in that cursed sin. It is the sin that people fall into when they are not born of God.
5.161	“Do not be like Cain, who belonged to the evil one and killed his brother.
5.162	And why did he kill him?
5.163	Because his own works were evil, and his brother's were right.”
5.164	So where there is envy, there cannot be love for one's brother.
5.165	Pay attention, my dear friends. Whoever envies does not love.
5.166	The devil's sin is in that person, because the devil threw man down out of envy.
5.167	For the devil fell, and he envied the one who was still standing.
5.168	He did not want to throw man down so that he himself could stand. He only wanted not to fall alone.
5.169	From what John has added here, hold on to this in your mind: envy cannot live inside love.
5.170	You have it said plainly where love is praised: “Love does not envy.”
5.171	There was no love in Cain. And if there had been no love in Abel, God would not have accepted his sacrifice.
5.172	They both brought offerings. One brought crops from the ground, the other brought young animals from the flock. What do you think, brothers and sisters? Did God look down on the crops and love the young animals?
5.173	God did not look at the hands; He looked into the heart. When He saw someone offering with love, He paid attention to his sacrifice. When He saw someone offering with envy, He turned His eyes away from his sacrifice.
5.174	So by Abel's good works, John means only love. By Cain's evil works, he means only his hatred of his brother.
5.175	It was not enough for Cain to hate his brother and envy his good works. Because he would not copy him, he wanted to kill him.
5.176	This showed that Cain was a child of the devil. It also showed that Abel was God's righteous one.
5.177	So this is how people are told apart, my brothers and sisters.
5.178	Do not pay attention to what people say. Look at their deeds and their hearts.
5.179	If someone does not do good for his brothers and sisters, he shows what is inside him.
5.180	People are proven by their trials.
5.181	9. “Do not be surprised, brothers and sisters, if the world hates us.”
5.182	Do I have to keep telling you what “the world” means?
5.183	It does not mean the sky or the earth, or the things we can see that God made. It means people who love the world.
5.184	I know that by saying this so often, I am wearing some of you out. But I do not say it for no reason. Some people could be asked whether I ever said it, and they would not be able to answer.
5.185	So even if I have to push it on people, let something stick in the hearts of those who listen.
5.186	What is “the world”?
5.187	When “the world” is used in a bad sense, it means people who love the world. When the word is used in a good sense, it means heaven and earth, and the works of God that are in them. That is why it says, “And the world was made by Him.”
5.188	“The world” can also mean everyone all over the earth. As John himself said, “Not only for our sins does He make peace with God, but for (the sins) of the whole world.” By “the world” he means all the believers spread out across the whole earth.
5.189	But in the bad sense, the world means people who love the world.
5.190	Those who love the world cannot love their brother.
5.191	10. “If the world hates us, we know”—what do we know?—“that we have passed from death to life”—how do we know?
5.192	“Because we love our brothers and sisters.”
5.193	No one should ask another person. Each one should go back to his own heart. If he finds love for his brother there, he can put his mind at rest, because he has “passed from death to life.”
5.194	He is already on the right hand. He should not worry that, for now, his glory is hidden. When the Lord comes, then he will appear in glory.
5.195	For he has life in him, but it is still winter. The root is alive, but the branches look dry, so to speak. Inside is the living substance. Inside are the leaves of the trees. Inside are the fruits. But they are waiting for summer.
5.196	So then: “We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love our brothers and sisters.
5.197	Whoever does not love stays in death.”
5.198	Brothers and sisters, so that you do not think it is a small thing to hate, or not to love, listen to what comes next: “Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer.”
5.199	Well then—if someone thought hating his brother was no big deal, will he also, in his heart, think murder is no big deal?
5.200	He does not lift a hand to kill anyone. Yet God already counts him as a murderer. The other person is still alive, and yet this man is already judged as his killer!
5.201	“Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer. And you know that no murderer has eternal life living in him.”
5.202	11. “This is how we know love.” He means perfect love, the perfect love we have urged you to take to heart. “This is how we know love: He gave up His life for us. And we ought to give up our lives for our brothers and sisters.”
5.203	See, this is where those words came from: “Peter, do you love Me?
5.204	Feed My sheep.” He wanted Peter to feed His sheep in such a way that he would give up his life for the sheep. So that you may know this, He said to him right away: “When you were young, you tied your own belt and walked wherever you wanted. But when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and someone else will tie a belt around you and take you where you do not want to go.
5.205	He said this,” the Gospel writer says, “to show by what kind of death Peter would bring glory to God.” So the one He had told, “Feed My sheep,” was the same one He taught to give up his life for His sheep.
5.206	12. Where does love begin, brothers and sisters?
5.207	Listen for a moment. You have heard how far love goes when it is made perfect. Its very goal and its full measure is what the Lord has set before us in the Gospel: “No one has greater love,” He says, “than this: to give up his life for his friends.”
5.208	So He has shown us in the Gospel what perfect love is, and here too it is perfect love that is shown to us. But you ask yourselves and say to yourselves, “When will we ever be able to have ‘this’ love?”
5.209	Do not give up on yourself too quickly.
5.210	Maybe your love has been born but is not yet perfect. Feed it, so that it does not get choked.
5.211	But you will say to me, “And how can I know it is there?”
5.212	We have heard how far love goes when it is perfect. Now let us hear where it begins.
5.213	John goes on to say: “But if someone has this world's goods and sees his brother going hungry, and closes his heart against him, how can the love of God live in him?”
5.214	Look—this is where love begins!
5.215	If you are not yet up to dying for your brother, be up to giving your money to your brother right now.
5.216	Let love touch you deep inside right now. Do it not to show off, but from the very deepest part of mercy. Think of him, since he is in need now.
5.217	For if you cannot give your brother what you do not even need, how could you give up your life for your brother?
5.218	Your money sits there close to you, and thieves could take it from you. And even if thieves do not take it, you will leave it behind when you die—even if it does not leave you while you are alive. So what will you do with it?
5.219	Your brother is hungry. He is in need. Maybe he is anxious and being pressured by someone he owes money to. He is your brother. You were both bought in the same way. One price was paid for you both. You are both rescued by the blood of Christ. So see whether you show mercy, if you have this world's money.
5.220	Maybe you say, “What does that have to do with me?
5.221	Should I give my money so that he does not have trouble?”
5.222	If that is the answer your heart gives you, the love of the Father does not live in you.
5.223	If the love of the Father does not live in you, you are not born of God.
5.224	How can you boast that you are a Christian?
5.225	You have the name, but you do not have the deeds.
5.226	But if your actions match your name, let people call you a pagan if they like. Show by what you do that you are a Christian.
5.227	For if you do not show you are a Christian by what you do, everyone may still call you a Christian—but what good is the name to you if the real thing is not there?
5.228	“But if someone has this world's goods and sees his brother in need, and closes his heart against him, how can the love of God live in him?”
5.229	Then he continues: “My little children, let us not love with words or with our tongue, but with actions and in truth.”
5.230	13. I think the matter is now clear to you, my brothers and sisters: this great and deeply important secret and mystery.
5.231	All of Scripture shows how powerful love is. But I do not know if any other place shows it more fully than this letter.
5.232	We ask you and beg you in the Lord: remember what you have heard. And come eagerly to what is still to be said until we finish the letter, and listen to it eagerly.
5.233	But open your heart to the good seed. Pull out the thorns, so that what we are planting in you does not get choked. Instead, let the harvest grow. Then the Farmer will be glad and will get the barn ready for you, as for grain—not the fire, as for the useless husks.
T:6	Homily 6 (1 John 3:19–4:3)
6.1	“And this is how we know that we belong to the truth, and how we reassure our hearts before Him.
6.2	For if our heart thinks badly of us, God is greater than our heart, and He knows everything.
6.3	Dear friends, if our heart does not think badly of us, then we can be confident before God.
6.4	And whatever we ask for, we will receive from Him, because we keep His commandments and do what pleases Him in His sight.
6.5	And this is His commandment: that we believe in the name of His Son Jesus Christ, and love one another, just as He commanded us.
6.6	Whoever keeps His commandments will live in Him, and He in that person.
6.7	And this is how we know that He lives in us: by the Holy Spirit He has given us.
6.8	Dear friends, do not believe every spirit. Test the spirits to see whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into this world.
6.9	This is how the Spirit of God is recognized: every spirit that openly says that Jesus Christ has come in a human body is from God. And every spirit that does not say that Jesus Christ has come in a human body is not from God. This is the antichrist. You have heard that he would come, and even now he is already in this world.”
6.10	1. If you remember, brothers and sisters, yesterday we ended our sermon with this sentence. It surely should have stayed in your heart, and it still should, since it was the last thing you heard.
6.11	“My little children, let us not love only with words and with our tongue, but with actions and in truth.”
6.12	Then he continues: “And this is how we know that we belong to the truth, and how we reassure our hearts before Him.”
6.13	“For if our heart thinks badly of us, God is greater than our heart, and He knows everything.”
6.14	He had said, “Let us not love only with words and with our tongue, but with actions and in truth.” So we are asked: by what action, or by what truth, can we recognize someone who loves God, or loves his brother?
6.15	Earlier he had said how far love goes when it is made perfect. The Lord says in the Gospel, “No one has greater love than this: to give up his life for his friends.” The apostle had said the same thing: “Just as He gave up His life for us, we also ought to give up our lives for our brothers and sisters.”
6.16	This is perfect love, and you cannot find any greater love at all.
6.17	But it is not perfect in everyone. And the person in whom it is not yet perfect should not give up hope, if the love that can grow to perfection has already been born. Of course, if it has been born, it must be fed. It must be fed with its own kind of food, and so be brought to its full perfection. So we asked about the start of love—where it begins. And right away we found this: “But if someone has this world's goods and sees his brother in need, and closes his heart against him, how does the love of the Father live in him?”
6.18	So here, my brothers and sisters, is where love begins: giving what you have left over to someone who is in need or in any kind of trouble. It means using your extra earthly things to rescue your brother from earthly trouble.
6.19	This is where love first starts to rise.
6.20	Once it has started like this, feed it with the word of God and with the hope of the life to come. Then you will finally reach perfection: you will be ready to give up your life for your brothers and sisters.
6.21	2. But many such things are done by people who are after something else, and who do not love their brothers and sisters. So let us come back to the witness of our conscience.
6.22	How can we prove that many such things are done by people who do not love their brothers and sisters?
6.23	Think how many people in heresies and splinter groups call themselves martyrs!
6.24	They think they are giving up their lives for their brothers and sisters.
6.25	If they really gave up their lives for their brothers and sisters, they would not cut themselves off from the whole family of believers.
6.26	And again, how many people give away a lot, hand out a lot, just to show off! All they want from it is people's praise and popularity—which is full of hot air and does not last.
6.27	Since there are people like this, how can love for our brothers and sisters be proven?
6.28	John wanted it to be proven, and he warned us, “My little children, let us not love only with words and with our tongue, but with actions and in truth.” So we ask: by what action? By what truth?
6.29	Is there any action more obvious than giving to the poor?
6.30	Many people do this to show off, not out of love.
6.31	Is there any action greater than dying for your brothers and sisters?
6.32	Many people would like others to think they do this too. But they do it to show off and make a name for themselves, not from a heart full of love.
6.33	So what is left? The person who truly loves his brother is the one who reassures his own heart before God, where only God can see. He asks his own heart whether he really does this out of love for his brothers and sisters. And his witness is the eye that sees right into the heart, where no human can look.
6.34	Think of the apostle Paul. He was ready to die for his brothers and sisters, and said, “I myself will be used up for your souls.” But only God saw this in his heart, not the human beings he was speaking to. So he tells them, “To me it matters very little whether I am judged by you or by any human court.”
6.35	The same apostle also shows in one place that these things are often done from empty showing off, not on the solid ground of love. When he praises love, he says, “If I give away everything I own to the poor, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but do not have love, it does me no good.”
6.36	Can a person really do these things without love?
6.37	Yes. For those who have broken unity are people who do not have love.
6.38	Look there, and you will see many giving a lot to the poor. You will see others ready to welcome death, so much so that when no one is persecuting them, they hurl themselves to their death. These people are surely doing this without love.
6.39	So let us come back to conscience. The apostle says about it: “For this is what we boast about: the witness of our conscience.”
6.40	Let us come back to conscience. The same apostle says about it, “But each person should test his own work. Then he will have something to boast about in himself, and not in someone else.”
6.41	So let each of us “test his own work.” Let him see whether it flows from the vein of love. Let him see whether his good works grow like branches from love as their root.
6.42	“But each person should test his own work. Then he will have something to boast about in himself, and not in someone else.” That means not when someone else's tongue speaks well of him, but when his own conscience speaks for him.
6.43	3. So this is what John is pressing on us here.
6.44	“This is how we know that we belong to the truth: when we love with actions and in truth, not only with words and with our tongue. And we reassure our heart before Him.”
6.45	What does “before Him” mean?
6.46	It means where He sees. That is why the Lord Himself says in the Gospel: “Watch out that you do not do your good deeds in front of people, to be seen by them. Otherwise your Father who is in heaven will give you no reward.”
6.47	And what does this mean: “Do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing”? What else can it mean, except that the right hand stands for a pure conscience, and the left hand for the craving for worldly things?
6.48	Many people do many amazing things because they crave worldly things. Then the left hand is working, not the right.
6.49	The right hand should do the work, and without the left hand knowing. That way, the craving for worldly things will not even mix itself in when we do something good out of love.
6.50	And where do we find this out?
6.51	You are standing before God. Question your heart. Look at what you have done and what you were aiming for in it: your salvation, or the empty praise of people?
6.52	Look inside, because no person can judge someone he cannot see.
6.53	If “we reassure our heart,” let it be “before Him.”
6.54	Because “if our heart thinks badly of us”—that is, if it accuses us inside, telling us we did not do the thing with the attitude we should have—“God is greater than our heart, and He knows everything.”
6.55	You hide your heart from people. Hide it from God, if you can!
6.56	How will you hide it from Him? A sinner, full of fear and confessing, says to Him, “Where can I go to get away from Your Spirit? And where can I run from Your face?”
6.57	He looked for a way to run away and escape God's judgment, and he found none.
6.58	For where is God not?
6.59	“If I go up to heaven,” he says, “You are there. If I go down to hell, You are there.”
6.60	Where will you go? Where will you run?
6.61	Do you want some advice?
6.62	If you want to run from Him, run to Him.
6.63	Run to Him by confessing, not away from Him by hiding. You cannot hide, but you can confess.
6.64	Say to Him, “You are my place of safety.” And let love be fed in you, because love alone leads to life.
6.65	Let your conscience be your witness that your love comes from God.
6.66	If it comes from God, do not try to show it off to people. For people's praise does not lift you up to heaven, and their criticism does not pull you down from there.
6.67	Let Him see it—the One who gives you the crown. Let Him be your witness—the Judge who crowns you.
6.68	“God is greater than our heart, and He knows everything.”
6.69	4. “Dear friends, if our heart does not think badly of us, we can be confident before God.” What does “If our heart does not think badly of us” mean?
6.70	It means this: our heart tells us truthfully that we love, and that there is real love in us—not fake but sincere. It is a love that looks for a brother's salvation and expects to gain nothing from him except his salvation. Then “we can be confident before God, and whatever we ask for, we will receive from Him, because we keep His commandments.” So this is not in front of people, but where God Himself sees, in the heart. Then “we can be confident before God, and whatever we ask for, we will receive from Him”—but only because we keep His commandments.
6.71	What are “His commandments”?
6.72	Do we have to keep repeating it?
6.73	“I give you a new commandment: love one another.”
6.74	It is love itself that he is talking about. This is what he is pressing on us.
6.75	So suppose someone has love for his brothers and sisters, and has it before God, where God sees. And suppose his heart is questioned in a fair examination and gives him no other answer than this: the true root of love is there, and good fruit will come from it. That person can be confident before God. Whatever he asks for, he will receive from Him, because he keeps His commandments.
