0.1	Text: Colossians 3:12-17.
0.2	12 God has chosen you. You are holy, and he loves you. So put on a heart full of compassion, along with kindness, humility, gentleness and being slow to anger. 13 Put up with one another. If anyone has a complaint against someone, forgive each other. Forgive just as the Lord forgave you. 14 And above all these things, put on love. Love is the perfect bond.
0.3	15 Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts. You were called to this peace together, in one body. And be thankful.
0.4	16 Let the Word of Christ live in you richly. Use all wisdom as you teach and warn one another with psalms and hymns and spiritual songs. Sing to God with grace in your hearts.
0.5	17 Whatever you do, whether you speak or act, do it all in the name of the Lord Jesus. Give thanks to God the Father through him.
0.6	1. This text is also a letter of warning and encouragement. It teaches what kind of fruit should grow out of faith.
0.7	Paul treats the Colossians kindly.
0.8	He does not give orders, push or threaten them. Teachers of the Law have to do that with people who live under the Law.
0.9	Instead, he wins them over with loving words. He reminds them of the blessing and grace they have received from God, and he points them to Christ's own example.
0.10	Christians should act willingly and cheerfully. They should not be moved by fear of punishment or by wanting a reward. We have said this many times before.
0.11	This encouragement was repeated so often in the last epistle reading that I trust we now know what makes someone a Christian.
0.12	So we will only touch on the subject briefly.
0.13	“So put on.”
0.14	2. In the epistle for New Year's Day we explained well enough what “putting on” means. By faith we put on Christ, and he puts on us. In love we put on our neighbor, and our neighbor puts on us.
0.15	Christians wear two kinds of clothing: faith and love.
0.16	Christ wore two kinds of garments. One was whole, and it pictures faith. The other was divided, and it pictures love.
0.17	Here Paul is talking about the second garment, love.
0.18	He wants to teach us Christians what kind of jewelry and clothing we should wear in the world. He does not mean silk or precious gold.
0.19	Peter (1 Peter 3:3) and Paul (1 Timothy 2:9) forbid women to wear these things.
0.20	Love for our neighbor is a garment that suits us well. It is the kind of love that makes us care about our neighbor and his troubles.
0.21	This love is called the jewel of a Christian's character. It is a jewel that people can see.
0.22	3. Notice how tender and holy the apostle's way of warning is. This is how he usually speaks to us.
0.23	He does not drive us with laws. Instead, he wins us over by reminding us of God's grace, which is too great for words. He calls us “chosen by God,” “holy” and “loved.”
0.24	He wants to bring out the fruits of faith. He wants them to come from a willing, cheerful and happy spirit.
0.25	Suppose someone truly believes and trusts that God sees him as loved, holy and chosen. That person will think about how to live up to these honors and titles, and how to behave in a way that is worthy of them. Even more, he will love God so warmly that he can cheerfully do anything, give up anything, or suffer anything. And he will never feel that he has done enough.
0.26	But someone who doubts that God feels this way about him will not see how strong these words are.
0.27	He will not feel the power of the truth that God sees us as holy, loved and chosen.
0.28	4. So let us pay no attention to the “saints” who choose themselves and love themselves. They decorate themselves with the works of the Law. They keep fasts and strict rules. They care about clothing and rank. They do this because they refuse to be sinners before God.
0.29	Our jewels are not like theirs. They have nothing to do with such mockery.
0.30	Our jewels are honesty, sincerity, good works and serving our neighbor.
0.31	We are free from laws about food, clothing, special days and so on.
0.32	We are holy in God's eyes. No one can be holy before God until he sees that he is a sinner and gives up his own righteousness.
0.33	But the people I just mentioned are holy only in their own opinion. So they always stay wicked. In God's eyes they are sinners.
0.34	God loves us because we look down on ourselves. We judge and condemn ourselves, and we turn away from loving ourselves.
0.35	The others love themselves and think highly of themselves. So in God's eyes they are hateful and not acceptable.
0.36	Also, God has chosen us because we look down on ourselves as filth.
0.37	God chooses people like that. He has chosen them from eternity.
0.38	These would-be saints choose themselves, so God will reject them. In fact, he has rejected them from eternity.
0.39	This is what Paul means by these words:
0.40	“A heart of compassion.”
0.41	5. These words describe part of the Christian's jewelry. It is a beautiful, lovely Christian jewel. In God's eyes it looks better on us than pearls, precious stones, silk and gold look on us in the eyes of the world.
0.42	“A heart of compassion” is a sign of a true Christian.
0.43	Paul means: “Do not be merciful only in what you do on the outside, or only in how things look. Be merciful deep in your heart.”
0.44	He means the sincere, whole-hearted mercy of a father and mother who see their child in trouble. They would gladly risk their lives or give up everything they own for that child.
0.45	A Christian's mind and heart should always be set on acts of mercy. He should be so eager about this that he does not even notice he is doing good and kind things.
0.46	6. With this one phrase, Paul condemns the works and made-up rules of hypocritical saints. They are so harsh that they will not spend time with sinners.
0.47	Their strict laws must rule everything.
0.48	All they do is force and push.
0.49	They show no mercy. They only scold, criticize, condemn, blame and bluster, over and over.
0.50	They cannot put up with any imperfection.
0.51	But among Christians many are sinners, and many are weak.
0.52	In fact, Christians spend time only with people like this, not with “saints.”
0.53	Christians reject no one. They put up with everyone.
0.54	They truly care about sinners, just as much as they would care about themselves if they were the weak ones.
0.55	They pray for sinners. They teach, warn and persuade them. They do everything they can to win them back.
0.56	That is what a true Christian is like.
0.57	This is how God, in Christ, has treated us, and how he always treats us.
0.58	This is how Christ treated the woman caught in adultery (John 8:11). He rescued her from the people who tormented her. His kind words moved her to repent, and he let her go.
0.59	We read that St. Antony said Paphrutius knew how souls are saved. Paphrutius had rescued a man from his fellow brothers, who were attacking and bullying him because of his sin.
0.60	See the book “Lives of the Fathers.”
0.61	If God treated us as strictly as his laws demand, we would all be lost.
0.62	But in his mercy he sets the Law aside.
0.63	Isaiah says (9:4): “The yoke that weighed him down, the bar across his shoulder, the rod of the one who oppressed him—you have broken them.”
0.64	Now God only persuades.
0.65	7. Look how tangled up in the Law and in hypocrisy some people still are. They think of themselves as great saints, yet they cannot stand the weaknesses of Christians.
0.66	They expect perfect holiness—a miracle of purity—in people who have Christ and know the Gospel. If they do not find it, then nothing is right. To them it seems the sky is about to fall and the earth is about to be destroyed.
0.67	All they can do is judge, criticize and make fun. They say: “Oh, yes, he is a real evangelical! Sure, he is a wild dreamer!”
0.68	This shows how completely blind they are.
0.69	They always have a beam in their own eye, and so they show how little they know about Christ.
0.70	So when you meet someone who is quick to criticize and condemn, and who demands that Christians be perfect, know this: that person is only an enforcer of the Law. He is a low hypocrite and a merciless jailer. He does not truly know Christ.
0.71	Among Christians there is no law; everything is love. So there can be no judging, condemning or criticizing either.
0.72	And whoever calls someone else a wild dreamer is surely ten times more a wild dreamer himself.
0.73	He condemns himself for the very thing he judges and condemns in someone else.
0.74	He pays no attention to mercy and cares only about the Law. So he finds no mercy with God. In fact, he has never experienced or tasted God's mercy.
0.75	To him, both God and his neighbor taste as bitter as gall and wormwood.
0.76	8. But this tender mercy is to be shown only to Christians and only among Christians.
0.77	We must treat those who reject and persecute the Gospel differently.
0.78	My love should not be so loose that it puts up with false teaching.
0.79	When it comes to false faith and false teaching, there is no room for love or patience.
0.80	I must fight hard against these things and not give in even a hair's width.
0.81	But in other matters, when faith is not in danger, I must always be kind and merciful to everyone, no matter how weak their lives are.
0.82	I may not criticize, crush or push them. I must persuade, plead with and put up with them.
0.83	A flawed life does not destroy Christianity. It gives it exercise.
0.84	But flawed teaching—false belief—destroys everything good.
0.85	So when it comes to false teaching, there is no place for putting up with it or showing mercy. Only anger, resistance and death are fitting. But this must always be done according to the Word of God.
0.86	9. On the other hand, those who are treated with mercy must not think their beliefs and actions are right just because no one criticizes or forces them.
0.87	They must not take this mercy as permission to become lazy and careless and to keep on in their error.
0.88	Mercy is not shown to them for that reason.
0.89	The goal is to give them a chance to get their eagerness and strength back.
0.90	But if they want to stay as they are, fine. Leave them alone.
0.91	They will not stay that way for long. The devil will lead them further away, until in the end they fall away completely. They will even become enemies of the Gospel.
0.92	That is how they will end up if they let mercy be poured out on them for nothing.
0.93	We must not be lazy and sleepy about our false teachings. We must not count on the fact that people do not look down on us or force us.
0.94	We especially need to be active and hard-working, because the devil never sleeps or rests.
0.95	We need to be careful that he does not lead us to a place where we will never have God's mercy.
0.96	“Kindness, humility, gentleness, being slow to anger.”
0.97	10. These words name the other parts of Christian character.
0.98	You will find kindness explained in the second epistle reading for the early Christmas service.
0.99	Kindness shows in the way a person behaves when he is gentle and caring toward everyone. He does not push anyone away with an unfriendly face, harsh words or rude behavior.
0.100	We Germans would call such a person pleasant and friendly.
0.101	Kindness is not a virtue that shows up only in certain deeds. It shapes a person's whole life.
0.102	A kind person is helpful to everyone. He is not annoyed with anyone, and everyone is drawn to him.
0.103	The opposite are those odd people who enjoy nothing but their own ideas. They insist that others fit in with them and their ways, but they give way to no one.
0.104	People like that are called “rude.”
0.105	11. But the generosity of kindness must not reach as far as false teaching.
0.106	It is to be used only when it comes to how people live and what they do.
0.107	As I have often said before, love with all its works and fruits has no place when it comes to false teaching.
0.108	I must love my neighbor and be kind to him, no matter how imperfect his life is.
0.109	But if he refuses to believe or teach sound teaching, I cannot love him or be kind to him. I do not dare to.
0.110	Paul says (Galatians 1:8-9) that I must treat him as cut off and cursed, even if he were an angel from heaven.
0.111	This shows how very different faith and love are, and how separate.
0.112	Love will be kind, and must be kind, even to the worst enemy, as long as he does not attack faith and teaching.
0.113	But love will not and cannot put up with someone who does attack them, even if it is your father, your mother or your dearest friend.
0.114	Deuteronomy 13:6-8. So love must be practiced toward our neighbor's life and works, not toward his teaching and faith.
0.115	Faith is the opposite. It is concerned not with his works and life, but with his teaching and belief.
0.116	12. By now I think we must know what “humility” of mind means. It means thinking of yourself as the least and of others as greater.
0.117	Christ gives a picture of it: taking the lowest seat at the wedding, and doing it cheerfully.
0.118	We are to serve even when no one wants our service. We are to serve even our enemies.
0.119	That is how Christ humbled himself before Judas, who betrayed him, and before all of us.
0.120	He did not come to be served, but to serve.
0.121	It is no surprise that humility is rare, because every Christian virtue is rare.
0.122	These virtues are especially missing in people who claim to know the most about Christ, yet find something to criticize in every Christian.
0.123	Paul calls Christianity a mystery of God, and it will probably stay that way.
0.124	13. “Gentleness” is the opposite of anger.
0.125	A gentle person is not quick to get angry. He does not curse, hit, hate or wish harm on anyone, not even an enemy.
0.126	Holding back like this is an art. Hypocrites—in fact, the whole world—can be gentle toward friends and toward people who treat them well.
0.127	But true gentleness and humility will be found only among God's chosen and loved saints, as Paul hints here.
0.128	Even among them, many are missing all of the Christian virtues, or at least a large part of them.
0.129	So hypocrites can find something to criticize and be offended by in God's loved and chosen saints.
0.130	And the true saints get a chance to practice mercy, humility, gentleness and tolerance.
0.131	Paul calls them God's chosen and loved saints, even though they are a little lacking in humility, gentleness and tolerance. That does not make them unholy. They are not rejected or looked down on.
0.132	14. Paul makes a difference between being slow to anger and tolerance, as in Romans 2:4: “Do you look down on the riches of his goodness and tolerance and slowness to anger?”
0.133	The idea of being slow to anger is what God expresses here and there in the Psalms and in other places with the Hebrew words “arich apaim,” meaning “slow to anger.”
0.134	God patiently puts up with evil.
0.135	In fact, he holds back his punishment again and again. He seems more ready to forgive than to punish, even when he is greatly provoked and has good reason to punish.
0.136	Being slow to anger goes further than patience.
0.137	Patience puts up with evil and unfairness. But being slow to anger holds back punishment.
0.138	It does not plan to punish. It will not take quick revenge.
0.139	It is not like people who want revenge. It wishes no one harm.
0.140	We see many people who suffer a lot and are patient, but at the same time they are counting on getting even in the end.
0.141	But a Christian who is slow to anger is against revenge. He wants the sinner to change his ways.
0.142	“Put up with one another, and forgive each other if anyone has a complaint against someone. Forgive just as the Lord forgave you.”
0.143	15. With this verse, all law is done away with among Christians.
0.144	No one is allowed to go to court to get his property back.
0.145	He must forgive and give way.
0.146	Christ's example teaches this rule, because he has forgiven us.
0.147	And how far does his forgiveness go?
0.148	He forgives the sins we have already done, but that is not all. As John says (1 John 2:1-2), “If anyone sins, we have someone to speak for us before the Father: Jesus Christ, the righteous one. He is the sacrifice that turns away God's anger for our sins.”
0.149	16. Notice that Paul is describing true Christian saints. Yet he sees them as weak enough to offend one another and complain about one another.
0.150	This is not at all the way Christians and saints should behave.
0.151	That is why I say Christ's kingdom is a mystery. It is so hidden that our preaching and teaching can never fully explain it.
0.152	You cannot get unbelievers to do good works, but you cannot stop believers from doing them.
0.153	Some would not believe, and some would not love.
0.154	This is true of Christ's kingdom: his Christians are not perfectly holy.
0.155	They have started to be holy and are still growing.
0.156	Among them you can still find anger, wrong desires, unholy love, worry about worldly things and other sad weaknesses. These are what is left of the old Adam.
0.157	Paul calls these things burdens that we must carry for our neighbor (Galatians 6:2). In Romans 15:1 he tells us to “bear the weaknesses of the weak.”
0.158	In the same way, Christ loved his apostles very much and suffered much from them. And every day he still puts up with his own people.
0.159	17. Some people list the fruits of the Spirit named in Galatians 5:22-23. They say a Christian should be kind, gentle, slow to anger and pure. They see this passage as a law that commands these fruits.
0.160	So they refuse to accept anyone as a Christian who does not have these fruits perfectly.
0.161	People like this cannot believe that there is a Christ, even though it is certainly true.
0.162	They judge with spite and complain that there are no Christians.
0.163	They are offended at Christ because he is wiser than they are.
0.164	For Christ has given us authority in Scripture to recognize Christians by their fruits.
0.165	He says (Matthew 7:16), “By their fruits you will recognize them.”
0.166	They put great weight on this.
0.167	18. Can you see where such a person goes wrong?
0.168	He goes wrong because he understands nothing at all about Christ's kingdom.
0.169	He gets the passages about Christians wrong.
0.170	He hears that Christians should be kind and gentle, and he thinks it means they must never get angry. They must put up with anything and never be impatient with anyone. If they fail at this, he says, they cannot be Christians, because they do not have the fruits.
0.171	Dear man, what could lead him to think that except his own blindness?
0.172	He imagines that Christianity is a holy order of perfection with no weakness at all. He thinks it is perfect, like heaven among the angels.
0.173	But tell me, where does the Bible talk about Christians like that?
0.174	But someone who sees Christianity as something still growing, still at its beginning, will not be offended when a Christian is sometimes harsh, unkind or impatient. He remembers that Christians are commanded to carry one another's burdens and weaknesses.

0.176	He knows that the passage means Christians are to try hard to be kind. That is the goal they aim at.
0.177	Even though they have made a start and some progress in this virtue, they are often unkind. They produce fruits that are the exact opposite of the fruits of the Spirit.
0.178	It is true that the text says we should be kind. But it does not say we are kind.
0.179	We are moving toward it. We are growing. But while we grow, much of our old nature, which has not yet been changed, is still mixed in.

0.181	But if someone does oppose the Gospel, you can safely judge that he is without Christ and under the devil's control.
0.182	Pray for such a person and warn him, hoping that he will turn back to God.
0.183	But when someone supports and honors the Gospel, pay attention to what Paul says (Romans 14:4): “Who are you to judge someone else's servant? Whether he stands or falls is up to his own master.

0.186	Christ wants to be hidden and shown at the same time, found and not found.
0.187	He allows some weaknesses to be mixed in with the fruits of the Spirit. He does this to hide himself, and so that spiteful judges will be offended.
0.188	“And above all these things, put on love. Love is the perfect bond.”
0.189	20. The apostle sets love apart from being slow to anger and gentleness, and from the other jewels of spiritual beauty we have already heard about. Yet all of them are included in love.
0.190	Faith is the main part of Christian character. In the same way, love is the main fruit of the Spirit, the most beautiful jewel of all.
0.191	That is why Paul says, “Above all these things, put on love.”

0.195	It does not let anyone start his own special kind of teaching or faith.
0.196	Everyone who loves believes the same thing.
0.197	So the poor and the rich, rulers and the people they rule, the sick and the healthy, the high and the low, the honored and the disgraced all share the same purpose of heart.
0.198	A loving heart lets everyone share in the good it has. Even more, it shares in everyone's troubles and treats them as its own.
0.199	Where there is love, there is perfect unity and fellowship in everything that happens, good or bad.
0.200	It is the most perfect bond.
0.201	21. Where there is no love, hearts are united and share the same goals in only a few things. In most things people disagree.
0.202	For example, robbers have a bond with each other. But it is only a shared plan to rob and murder.
0.203	Worldly friends agree only when it comes to their own interests.
0.204	Monks are united only when it comes to their order and their honor.
0.205	Herod and Pilate agreed, but only about Christ.
0.206	Most of the time it is unusual for one monk, priest or ordinary church member to agree with another.
0.207	The bond that holds them together is weak. They are like chaff tied together with straw.
0.208	“And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts. You were called to this peace.”
0.209	22. Many things threaten to break the bond of love.
0.210	The devil never sleeps. He keeps stirring up fights and unrest.
0.211	Paul does not deny that the bond is under attack.
0.212	But he urges us to fight back, remembering that love has to be exercised against opposition.
0.213	He tells us to let the peace of Christ rule in our hearts.
0.214	The meaning of the verse is this: The peace of the world and the flesh does not last. You will have to watch forces that cause fighting and division. Even so, let your hearts have peace in Christ.

0.216	This is the peace the Gospel calls us to. It is not the peace of the world, the flesh or the devil. It is the peace that goes far beyond all our understanding, as Paul tells us.
0.217	We are to hold on to God's peace not only when everything is going well, but also when sin, death, the flesh, the world and every kind of disaster are raging.
0.218	“And be thankful.”

0.221	In another place (2 Timothy 3:2) the apostle says that in the last days, among other sins, people will be “unthankful” toward one another.
0.222	Each person can choose for himself which of the two meanings to take.
0.223	In my opinion, Paul means gratitude toward other people here. He talks about thankfulness to God later, and here he is dealing with love for our neighbor. So that is my opinion.
0.224	I think this is what he means.

0.232	It is true today, and it has always been true: preachers of God's Word usually have to find their own bread. They get ungratefulness as their reward for the wonderful blessings they bring.
0.233	If their job were to hold masses and offer indulgences, people would be grateful. They would give them great gifts and service to show their thanks.
0.234	But the Levites were treated just as ungratefully under the old Law, while the priests of idols and sacred groves were treated with favor.
0.235	“Let the Word of Christ live in you richly. Use all wisdom as you teach and warn one another with psalms and hymns and spiritual songs. Sing to God with grace in your hearts.”

0.237	Paul means: Be sure to honor your teachers and preachers. Be grateful that they work with the Word and can share it richly with you.

0.244	26. People who are full and lazy soon grow tired of their pastors and send them away to go wherever they want.
0.245	Then the pastors have to earn a living by doing other work. So God's Word is neglected. It becomes rare, like seed scattered thinly across the land.
0.246	Nehemiah (13:10) complains that the Levites were not supported. So they had to leave their worship and the temple and run off to the fields. Or they had to start false worship and made-up stories to lead the people astray.

Through it the preachers have become like lords.
0.249	Now that the Gospel has come back, this money problem is coming back too, and it will keep coming back.

0.251	Once again God punishes ungratefulness. Sometimes he lets his preachers leave the ministry completely and work to support themselves. Or he sends the people even greater lies than ever before, which cheat them out of their money and destroy body and soul.
0.252	This happens because they refuse to let the Word of God live among them richly.
0.253	Paul adds a phrase that explains it further:
0.254	“In all wisdom.”

0.257	He meant for us to understand it. He meant for it to be preached and sung in a way people could understand.
0.258	And those who serve the Word, who sing it and speak it, should be wise. They should understand everything that has to do with saving souls and honoring God.

0.262	28. We have often explained what it means to teach and to warn.
0.263	Here Paul makes teaching a duty for all Christians: “teaching and warning one another.”
