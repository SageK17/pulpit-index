0.1	“Let me die like a person who is right with God. Let the end of my life be like his!” Numbers 23:10.
0.2	1. “Let the end of my life be like his!”
0.3	How many of you share this wish?
0.4	Even in this huge crowd, there are probably very few of you who do not!
0.5	Oh, I pray that this wish will stay in your minds! I pray that it will not fade away until your souls are also safe in the place “where the wicked stop causing trouble, and where the tired are at rest!”
0.6	2. No one will expect me to explain this Bible text in detail today.
0.7	That would keep you too long from thinking about your beloved brother, friend, and pastor. That thought is sad, and yet it is also sweet. Yes, he was your father too. So many people here were brought to new life “in the Lord” through him!
0.8	So wouldn’t it suit your feelings, and this serious occasion, better if I spoke directly about this man of God? You have heard him speak in this very place so many times. And you know the outcome of his whole way of life: “Jesus Christ, the same yesterday, today, and forever.”
0.9	So shouldn’t we do these three things?
0.10	I. Look at a few facts about his life and death.
0.11	II. Take a look at his character. And,
0.12	III. Ask how we can gain something good from this solemn event, when God took him from us so suddenly.
0.13	1. First, let’s look at a few facts about his life and death.
0.14	He was born in Gloucester in December 1714. When he was about twelve years old, he was sent to a grammar school there.
0.15	When he was seventeen, he began to take his faith seriously. He served God as well as he knew how.
0.16	At about eighteen he went to university. He was accepted at Pembroke College in Oxford. About a year later he got to know the people called “Methodists.” From then on he loved them as much as his own soul.
0.17	2. They convinced him that we “must be born again.” Without that, religion on the outside does us no good at all.
0.18	He joined them in fasting on Wednesdays and Fridays. He visited the sick and the prisoners with them. Like them, he used even the smallest scraps of time, so that not one moment was wasted. He also changed what he studied. He read mostly books that went to the heart of faith, books that led straight to knowing Jesus Christ, and Him crucified, by personal experience.
0.19	3. Soon his faith was tested, as if by fire.
0.20	He lost his good name, and some of his closest friends turned their backs on him. But he also suffered troubles on the inside, and they were the hardest kind.
0.21	Many nights he lay awake in his bed. Many days he lay face down on the ground.
0.22	For several months he groaned under “the spirit of being a slave.” But then God kindly took the heavy load away. He gave him “the Spirit of adoption.” Through a living faith, God helped him take hold of “the Son of His love.”
0.23	4. His health had become very poor. So people thought he needed to go to the countryside to get better.
0.24	So he went to Gloucester. There God used him to wake up several young people to faith.
0.25	They soon formed a small group of their own. They were some of the first fruit of his work.
0.26	Soon after that, he began reading to some poor people in the town two or three times a week. Every day he also read to the prisoners in the county jail and prayed with them.
0.27	5. He was now about twenty-one years old. People urged him to become an ordained minister.
0.28	He was very afraid of this, because he felt deeply that he was not good enough for it.
0.29	But the Bishop himself sent for him. He told him, “I had planned not to ordain anyone under twenty-three. But I will ordain you whenever you come.” Several other things that God arranged also pointed the same way. So he gave in, and he was ordained on Trinity Sunday, 1736.
0.30	The next Sunday he preached to a packed church, in the same church where he had been baptized.
0.31	The week after that, he went back to Oxford and got his Bachelor’s degree. Now he was fully busy, because caring for the prisoners and the poor rested mostly on him.
0.32	6. But before long he was invited to London. A friend who was going away to the countryside needed someone to take over his church work.
0.33	He stayed there two months and lived in the Tower. Twice a week he read prayers in the chapel, and once a week he taught the catechism and preached. He also visited the soldiers in the barracks and in the hospital.
0.34	He also read prayers every evening at the Wapping chapel, and he preached at Ludgate prison every Tuesday.
0.35	While he was there, letters came from his friends in Georgia. They made him long to go and help them. But he did not yet see clearly that God was calling him. So at the planned time he went back to his small flock at Oxford. There several young men met in his room every day to build each other up in their most holy faith.
0.36	7. But he was soon called away again, this time to fill in for the minister at Dummer, in Hampshire.
0.37	There he read prayers twice a day: early in the morning, and in the evening after the people came home from work.
0.38	Every day he also taught the children the catechism and visited people from house to house.
0.39	He now split his day into three parts. He gave eight hours to sleep and meals, eight to study and quiet time alone, and eight to reading prayers, teaching the catechism, and visiting the people.
0.40	Is there any better way for a servant of Christ and His Church to live?
0.41	If not, who will “go and do the same”?
0.42	8. But he still kept thinking about going overseas. Now he was fully convinced that God was calling him to do it. So he put everything in order, and in January 1737 he went to Gloucester to say goodbye to his friends.
0.43	It was on this journey that God began to bless his preaching in an unusual way.
0.44	Wherever he preached, huge crowds came flocking to hear him—in Gloucester, in Stonehouse, in Bath, in Bristol. The churches got so hot that people could hardly stand it. And the effect on the minds of many people was just as extraordinary.
0.45	Then he returned to London. General Oglethorpe kept him waiting there week after week and month after month. During that time God blessed his preaching even more.
0.46	He never seemed to get tired of working. On Sundays he usually preached four times to very large crowds. On top of that, he read prayers two or three times, and he often walked ten or twelve miles back and forth.
0.47	9. He left London on December 28.
0.48	On the 29th he preached without notes for the first time.
0.49	On December 30 he boarded the ship. But it was more than a month before they got away from the coast.
0.50	The next April he wrote about one happy result of their very slow trip: “Praise God, we now live very happily together in the big cabin.
0.51	We talk about almost nothing except God and Christ. When we are together, you hardly hear a word that is not about how we fell in the first Adam and how we are born again in the Second Adam.”
0.52	It also seems to have been a special act of God that he spent a little time at Gibraltar. There citizens and soldiers, important people and ordinary people, young and old, all recognized that God had come to visit them.
0.53	10. From Sunday, May 7, 1738, until late in the following August, he “fully carried out his ministry” in Georgia, especially at Savannah. Twice a day he read prayers and explained the Bible, and every day he visited the sick.
0.54	On Sundays he explained the Bible at five in the morning. At ten he read prayers and preached, and he did the same again at three in the afternoon. At seven in the evening he explained the Church Catechism.
0.55	How much easier it is for our fellow ministers in England, Scotland, or Ireland to criticize a worker like this in our Lord’s vineyard than to follow in his footsteps!
0.56	11. At this time he saw how terrible life was for many children there. And God put into his heart the first idea of starting an orphanage. He decided to raise money for it in England, if God brought him back there safely.
0.57	The following December he did return to London. On Sunday, January 14, 1739, he was ordained a priest at Christ Church, Oxford.
0.58	The next day he came back to London. On Sunday the 21st he preached twice.
0.59	The churches were large and extremely crowded. Even so, many hundreds of people stood outside in the churchyard, and hundreds more went home.
0.60	This first gave him the idea of preaching outdoors.
0.61	But when he told some of his friends about it, they thought it was simply crazy. So he did not actually do it until after he had left London.
0.62	On Wednesday, February 21, he found that all the church doors in Bristol were shut. Besides, no church could hold even half the crowd. So at three in the afternoon he went to Kingswood and preached outdoors to nearly two thousand people.
0.63	On Friday he preached there to four or five thousand people. On Sunday he preached to what people thought was ten thousand!
0.64	The crowds kept growing the whole time he stayed at Bristol. A fire of holy love was lit that will not easily be put out.
0.65	Later the same fire was lit in many parts of Wales, Gloucestershire, and Worcestershire.
0.66	In fact, wherever he went, God strongly confirmed the message of the one He had sent.
0.67	12. On Sunday, April 29, he preached for the first time in Moorfields and on Kennington Common. Thousands of people listened, and they were as quiet as they would have been in a church.
0.68	Once again he was kept in England month after month. So he made short trips into several counties, and large crowds gladly gave money for an orphanage in Georgia.
0.69	At this time ships were banned from sailing. That gave him time for more journeys through different parts of England. Many people will thank God for those journeys forever.
0.70	Finally, on August 14, he set sail. But he did not land in Pennsylvania until October 30.
0.71	After that he traveled through Pennsylvania, the Jerseys, New York, Maryland, Virginia, and North and South Carolina. All along the way he preached to enormous crowds, with just as great an effect as in England.
0.72	On January 10, 1740, he arrived in Savannah.
0.73	13. On January 29 he took in three orphans who had no one to care for them. He already had nearly twenty in his house.
0.74	The next day he marked out the land for the orphanage building, about ten miles from Savannah.
0.75	On February 11 he took in four more orphans. Then he set out for Frederica to bring back the orphans who lived in the southern parts of the colony.
0.76	On the way back he started a school at Darien, for both children and adults, and he took four orphans from there.
0.77	On March 25 he laid the first stone of the orphanage. He gave it the very fitting name Bethesda. Children who are not even born yet will praise the Lord for this work.
0.78	He now had about forty orphans, so there were nearly a hundred mouths to feed every day.
0.79	But he did not “worry about anything.” He handed his worries over to the One who feeds the young ravens when they call to Him.
0.80	14. In April he made another trip through Pennsylvania, the Jerseys, and New York.
0.81	Unbelievably large crowds came to hear him, and among them were a great many Black people.
0.82	Everywhere, most of the listeners were moved to an amazing degree.
0.83	Many became deeply aware that they were lost. Many truly turned to God.
0.84	In some places thousands cried out loud. Many seemed to be in the pain of dying. Most were in floods of tears. Some turned as pale as death. Others were wringing their hands. Others were lying on the ground. Others were collapsing into their friends’ arms. Almost all of them were looking up and crying out for mercy.
0.85	15. He returned to Savannah on June 5.
0.86	The next evening, during the church service, the whole congregation, young and old, broke down in tears. After the service several members of the church, and his whole household, especially the little children, walked home crying along the street. Some could not help praying out loud.
0.87	The children kept groaning and crying all night and for much of the next day.
0.88	16. In August he set out again. He traveled through several colonies and came to Boston.
0.89	While he was there and in the nearby towns, his body was extremely weak. Yet the crowds were so huge, and the effects on them so astonishing, that even the oldest men in town had never seen anything like it.
0.90	The same power was present when he preached in New York, especially on Sunday, November 2. Almost as soon as he started, you could hear crying, weeping, and wailing on every side.
0.91	Many people sank to the ground, cut to the heart. And many were filled with comfort from God.
0.92	Near the end of this journey he wrote: “Today is the seventy-fifth day since I arrived at Rhode Island, very weak in body. Yet God has helped me preach in public one hundred and seventy-five times, besides often encouraging people in private!
0.93	God has never given me greater comfort. I have never made my journeys with less tiredness. And I have never seen God’s presence stay so constantly with the people I preached to.”
0.94	In December he went back to Savannah, and the next March he arrived in England.
0.95	17. You can easily tell that most of this account comes from his own journals. They are written so simply and honestly that they can match any writings of that kind.
0.96	And this is a true sample of his work in Europe and America during the next thirty years, all done for the honor of his beloved Master. It also shows the steady shower of blessings that God was pleased to pour on his work!
0.97	Isn’t it a great pity that something stopped him from continuing this account, at least until close to the time when his Lord called him to enjoy the fruit of his work?
0.98	If he has left any papers like this, and his friends think I am worthy of the honor, it would be my glory and joy to organize them, copy them out, and get them ready to be published.
0.99	18. A gentleman from Boston gives this detailed account of the last part of his life: —
0.100	“He spent about a month with us in Boston and the area around it, preaching every day. Then he went to Old York and preached there on Thursday, September 27. He went on to Portsmouth and preached there on Friday.
0.101	On Saturday morning he set out for Boston. He had promised to preach at Newbury the next morning. But before he got there, people begged him to preach along the way.
0.102	The house was not big enough for all the people, so he preached in an open field.
0.103	He had been sick for several weeks, and this wore him out so much that when he reached Newbury, he could not get out of the ferry boat without two men helping him.
0.104	But in the evening he felt better again and was as cheerful as usual.
0.105	At nine o’clock he went to his room. That was his fixed bedtime, and no company could ever keep him from it. He slept better than he had for several weeks.
0.106	He got up at four in the morning on September 30 and went into his small private room. His companion noticed that he stayed alone there unusually long.
0.107	He came out of the room, went back to his companion, threw himself on the bed, and lay there about ten minutes.
0.108	Then he fell on his knees and prayed very earnestly to God. He asked that, if it was God’s will, he might finish his Master’s work that very day.
0.109	Then he asked his servant to call Mr. Parsons, the minister whose house he was staying in. But within a minute, before Mr. Parsons could get to him, he died, without a sigh or a groan.
0.110	When the news of his death came, six gentlemen set out for Newbury to bring his body back here. But his body could not be moved, so his precious remains have to stay at Newbury.
0.111	Hundreds of people would have gone from this town to his funeral, if they had not expected him to be buried here....
0.112	May God use this blow for good in His Church everywhere, and especially in this colony!”
0.113	II. 1. Second, we will take a look at his character.
0.114	Soon after his death, a short description of it was printed in the Boston Gazette. Here is part of it: — [“There is little to say about him that every friend of living Christianity who has sat under his preaching would not confirm.”]
0.115	“For many years his public work amazed the world with its eloquence and devotion.
0.116	With what heavenly feeling he urged unrepentant sinners to take up a life of godliness and goodness!
0.117	[Filled with the spirit of grace, he] spoke from the heart. His zeal was so passionate that perhaps no one has equaled it since the days of the Apostles. [He decorated the truths he preached with the most graceful beauty of fine speaking.]
0.118	In the pulpit no one could match the way he held the attention of his always-packed audience.
0.119	He was just as pleasant and helpful to talk with in private. He was wonderfully easy to approach, glad to share what he knew, and eager to build people up.
0.120	May the next generation catch a spark of that flame! It shone so brightly in the spirit and life of this faithful servant of the Most High God!”
0.121	2. One of the English newspapers has printed a fuller description of his character, and it is just as fair.
0.122	You may like it if I add the main points of this one too: —
0.123	“Every friend of true, living religion must have the character of this truly godly man [deeply] printed on their heart.
0.124	His body was weak [and delicate]. Even so, right up to the last day of his life, he kept preaching so often and so passionately that it seemed to be more than even the strongest person could naturally do.
0.125	He was called to do the work of a minister at an age when most young men are only starting to train for it. So he did not have time to get very far in learning the ancient languages.
0.126	But he more than made up for this with a lively and creative mind, with burning zeal, and with a powerful and very persuasive way of speaking.
0.127	In the pulpit he often found he had to “persuade people” by speaking of “the terror of the Lord.” Yet there was nothing gloomy about him. He was unusually cheerful, and also generous and kindhearted.
0.128	He was just as ready to help with people’s physical needs as with their spiritual needs when they came to him.
0.129	It should also be noted that he always urged his listeners to do every moral duty. He especially urged them to work hard at their jobs and to obey those in charge of them.
0.130	He worked incredibly hard at preaching in many different places, even in open fields. He did this to wake up the poorer people from the deepest carelessness and ignorance, so that they would begin to take religion seriously.
0.131	Because of this, and his other work, the name of GEORGE WHITEFIELD will be remembered with respect and honor for a long time.”
0.132	3. Everyone will readily agree that both of these accounts are true and fair—as far as they go.
0.133	But they hardly go beyond the outside of his character.
0.134	They show you the preacher. But they do not show you the man, the Christian, the saint of God.
0.135	May I add a little about this? I knew him personally for nearly forty years.
0.136	I know very well how hard it is to speak about such a delicate subject. It takes great wisdom to avoid both extremes and to say neither too little nor too much!
0.137	In fact, I know it is impossible to say anything at all—whether less or more—without some people blaming me for saying too little, and others for saying too much.
0.138	Some will honestly think I have said too little, and others that I have said too much.
0.139	But I will not let that worry me. I will say just what I know, in the presence of the One to whom we all must give an account.
0.140	4. We have already mentioned his zeal, which no one could match. We have mentioned how he never tired of working, how tender he was toward people who were suffering, and how generous he was to the poor.
0.141	But shouldn’t we also mention how deeply thankful he was to everyone God had used to do him good? He never stopped speaking about them with the greatest respect, right up to the day he died.
0.142	Shouldn’t we mention that his heart was open to the most generous and tender friendship?
0.143	I have often thought that this, more than anything else, was what made his character stand out.
0.144	How few people we have known with such a kind nature, with such big and overflowing love!
0.145	Wasn’t this the main reason why other people’s hearts were so wonderfully drawn to him and joined to his?
0.146	Can anything except love produce love?
0.147	This love shone in his very face. It breathed through all his words all the time, whether in public or in private.
0.148	Wasn’t it this love that flew from heart to heart, as fast and piercing as lightning? Wasn’t it this that gave such life to his sermons, his conversations, and his letters?
0.149	You yourselves are witnesses!
0.150	5. But away with the ugly twisting of this by people with dirty minds, who know no love except the earthly, physical kind!
0.151	At the same time, remember that he had the purest and most spotless modesty.
0.152	His work as a minister meant that he often had long talks with women as well as men, of every age and situation in life.
0.153	But the way he acted toward them was a living example of St. Paul’s advice to Timothy: “Appeal to the older women as mothers, and to the younger women as sisters, in all purity.”
0.154	6. His friendly spirit also fit well with how open and honest he was when he talked with people! And yet he was never rude on the one hand, and never sneaky [or pretending] on the other.
0.155	Wasn’t this openness both a result of his courage and fearlessness, and a proof of it?
0.156	With these as his armor, he was not afraid of anyone. He “spoke very plainly” to people of every rank and situation, high and low, rich and poor. His only goal was, “by making the truth clear, to win a place in every person’s conscience in the sight of God.”
0.157	7. He was not afraid of hard work or pain, any more than he was afraid of “what people [could] do to him.” He was just as
0.158	Patient in putting up with trouble as in doing good.
0.159	You could see this in how steadily he kept at whatever he took on for his Master’s sake.
0.160	Take just one example: the orphanage in Georgia. He started it and finished it, even though everything seemed to be against him.
0.161	In things that only concerned himself, he was easygoing and flexible.
0.162	In those cases he was “easy to reason with.” It was easy to convince him or to persuade him.
0.163	But in the things of God, or wherever his conscience was involved, no one could move him.
0.164	No one could talk him into changing even the smallest point, and no one could scare him into it either. His integrity was part of his whole character, and it guided everything he said and did.
0.165	In this he truly did
0.166	Stand like a strong iron pillar, / And firm as a wall of bronze.
0.167	8. Someone may ask what the foundation was for this integrity, or for his sincerity, courage, patience, and all his other good and lovely qualities. The answer is easy.
0.168	It was not that he was born with a good nature. It was not his great intelligence. It was not the power of his education. No, and it was not the advice of his friends. It was nothing else but faith in a Lord who bled for him, “faith that God Himself works in us.”
0.169	It was “a living hope of an inheritance that will never rot, never be stained, and never fade.”
0.170	It was “the love of God spread through his heart by the Holy Spirit, who was given to him.” That love filled his soul with tender, unselfish love for every human being.
0.171	This was the source of that flood of eloquence that often swept away everything in its path. This was the source of that amazing power to persuade, which even the most hardened sinners could not resist.
0.172	This was what often made him weep as if his “head were full of water, and his eyes a fountain of tears.”
0.173	This was what helped him pour out his soul in prayer in his own special way. His prayers were full and flowing at the same time, with such strength and variety in both thoughts and words.
0.174	9. I will finish this point by showing the great honor God gave His faithful servant. God let him announce His everlasting good news in so many different countries, to so many people, and with such a great effect on so many of their precious souls!
0.175	Since the time of the Apostles, have we ever read or heard of anyone who spread the good news of God’s grace over such a wide area, through so much of the inhabited world?
0.176	Have we ever read or heard of anyone who called so many thousands, even tens of thousands, of sinners to repent?
0.177	Above all, have we read or heard of anyone God has used so wonderfully to bring so many sinners “from darkness to light, and from the power of Satan to God”?
0.178	It is true that if we talked this way to people who live for pleasure, they would think we were speaking a foreign language.
0.179	But you understand the language of the country you are traveling to, where our dear friend has gone a little ahead of us.
0.180	III. But how can we gain something good from this solemn event that God has sent?
0.181	This is the third thing we need to think about.
0.182	The answer to this important question is easy. (May God write it in all our hearts!)
0.183	We do it by holding tightly to the great teachings he preached, and by soaking up his spirit.
0.184	1. So first, let us hold tightly to the great Bible teachings that he preached everywhere.
0.185	There are many teachings that are less essential. On these, even sincere children of God have disagreed for many centuries, and they still do. That is how weak human understanding is right now.
0.186	On these points we can think for ourselves and let others think for themselves. We can “agree to disagree.”
0.187	But at the same time, let us hold on firmly to the essentials of “the faith that was once handed down to the saints.” This champion of God insisted so strongly on these, at all times and in all places!
0.188	2. His most basic point was: “Give God all the credit for whatever is good in people.” And: “When it comes to salvation, lift Christ up as high as possible, and put people as low as possible.”
0.189	He and his friends at Oxford, the first people called Methodists, started out with this point.
0.190	Their great principle was this: by nature people have no power of their own, and nothing in them earns God’s favor.
0.191	They insisted that all the power to think, speak, or act rightly is in the Spirit of Christ and comes from Him. And everything that earns God’s favor is found only in the blood of Christ—not in people, no matter how far they have grown in grace.
0.192	That is what he and they taught. People have no power, until it is given to them from above, to do one good deed, to speak one good word, or even to have one good desire.
0.193	It is not enough to say that everyone is sick with sin. No, we are all “dead in our wrongdoing and sins.”
0.194	So it follows that all human beings are “by nature children under God’s anger.”
0.195	We are all “guilty before God,” and we deserve to die, both in this life and forever.
0.196	3. And we are all helpless, both against the power of sin and against its guilt.
0.197	“For who can make something clean out of something unclean?”
0.198	No one but the Almighty.
0.199	Who can raise people who are dead—spiritually dead in sin?
0.200	No one but the One who raised us up from the dust of the earth.
0.201	But why would He do this?
0.202	“Not on account of any right things we have done.”
0.203	“The dead cannot praise You, O Lord.” And they cannot do anything to earn being brought back to life.
0.204	So whatever God does, He does only for the sake of His dearly loved Son. “He was wounded because of our rebellion. He was bruised because of our evil.”
0.205	He Himself “bore” all “our sins in His own body on the wooden cross.”
0.206	He “was given up for our wrongs, and was raised again so that we could be declared right with God.”
0.207	So here is the only thing that earns every blessing we have or ever can have. This is especially true of our forgiveness, of God accepting us, and of our being fully and freely made right with Him.
0.208	But how do we come to have a share in what Christ has done and suffered?
0.209	“Not by our own deeds, so that nobody can brag.” We receive it by faith alone.
0.210	“We conclude,” says the Apostle, “that a person is made right with God by faith, without doing the works the law requires.”
0.211	And “to all who” in this way “receive Him, He gives the power to become sons of God—to those who believe in His name. They are born, not because a human being wanted it, but because of God.”
0.212	4. And “unless a person is” born again in this way, “he cannot see the kingdom of God.”
0.213	But everyone who is “born of the Spirit” in this way has “the kingdom of God inside them.”
0.214	Christ sets up His kingdom in their hearts: “right living, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit.”
0.215	They have in them “the same mind that was in Christ Jesus.” This makes them able to “walk just as Christ walked.”
0.216	His Spirit, living in them, makes them holy in their hearts and also “holy in every part of their lives.”
0.217	But all of this is still a free gift, given because of Christ’s goodness and His blood. So there is always the same reason to remember: “Anyone who brags should brag about the Lord.”
0.218	5. You know very well that these are the basic teachings he insisted on everywhere.
0.219	And can’t they be summed up in two phrases: the new birth, and being made right with God by faith?
0.220	Let us insist on these teachings boldly, at all times and in all places. Those of us who are called to it should do so in public, and we should all do so in private whenever we get the chance.
0.221	Hold on tight to these good, old, unfashionable teachings, no matter how many people argue against them and insult them.
0.222	Keep going, my brothers and sisters, “in the name of the Lord, and in the power of His strength.”
0.223	Carefully and faithfully “keep safe what has been put in your care.” Know that “heaven and earth will pass away, but this truth will never pass away.”
0.224	6. But is it enough to hold tightly to his teachings, however pure they are?
0.225	Isn’t there something even more important than this? Isn’t it to soak up his spirit—to follow him in this, just as he followed Christ?
0.226	Without this, pure teaching would only make our judgment worse.
0.227	So this is the main thing: to copy his spirit.
0.228	We have to admit that in some ways we can only admire what we cannot copy. But in many other ways we can share in the same blessing, through the same free grace.
0.229	So remember how much you lack and how generous His love is. He “gives freely and does not scold.” Cry out to Him, the One who does everything in everyone, for some of the same precious faith, the same zeal and energy, the same tender heart, generosity, and deep, heartfelt mercy.
