0.1	“If your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault. Do it just between the two of you. If he listens to you, you have won your brother back.
0.2	But if he will not listen, take one or two others with you. Then every word can be confirmed by two or three witnesses.
0.3	If he will not listen to them, tell it to the church.
0.4	And if he will not listen to the church, let him be to you like a pagan and a tax collector.” Matthew 18:15–17
0.5	1. The great apostle says, “Speak evil of no one.” This is as plain a command as “You must not commit murder.”
0.6	But who, even among Christians, pays attention to this command?
0.7	Indeed, how few people even understand it?
0.8	What is evil-speaking? Some people think it is the same as lying or slander. But it is not.
0.9	Everything a person says may be as true as the Bible. And yet saying it is still evil-speaking.
0.10	For evil-speaking is simply this: speaking evil about someone who is not there. It means telling something bad that a person really did or said, when that person is not present.
0.11	Suppose I see a man drunk, or hear him curse and swear. If I tell others about it when he is not there, that is evil-speaking.
0.12	In our language it also has a very fitting name: backbiting.
0.13	And there is no real difference between this and what we usually call telling tales.
0.14	Sometimes the story is told in a soft, quiet way. Perhaps the speaker says he wishes the person well, and hopes things are not quite so bad. Then we call it whispering.
0.15	But however it is done, it is the same thing. It is the same in substance, even if the details are different.
0.16	It is still evil-speaking. We still trample on the command “Speak evil of no one” whenever we tell someone the fault of a third person who is not there to answer for himself.
0.17	2. And how very common this sin is among people of every rank and class!
0.18	High and low, rich and poor, wise and foolish, educated and uneducated: how constantly they all fall into it!
0.19	People who disagree about everything else still agree in this.
0.20	How few can say before God, “I am innocent in this matter. I have always set a guard in front of my mouth and kept the door of my lips!”
0.21	What long conversation do you ever hear that does not include some evil-speaking? And this happens even among people who generally have the fear of God before their eyes. They truly want a clear conscience before God and before people.
0.22	3. The very fact that this sin is so common makes it hard to avoid.
0.23	It is all around us. So if we are not deeply aware of the danger, and always on guard against it, the flood can easily sweep us away.
0.24	Here almost the whole human race is, so to speak, plotting against us.
0.25	Their example creeps up on us, and we do not know how. So we slowly slide into copying it without noticing.
0.26	Besides, it is urged on us from inside as well as from outside.
0.27	There is hardly any wrong attitude in the human mind that it cannot satisfy at times. And so each of these attitudes pulls us toward it.
0.28	It feeds our pride to tell about faults in others that we think we do not have.
0.29	Speaking against people who displease us feeds our anger, our resentment, and every unkind feeling. And often, by telling the sins of their neighbors, people feed their own foolish and harmful desires.
0.30	4. Evil-speaking is even harder to avoid because it often attacks us in disguise.
0.31	We speak this way out of a noble, generous (maybe we even say holy) anger against these terrible people!
0.32	We commit sin just because we hate sin!
0.33	We serve the devil out of pure zeal for God!
0.34	We fall into this wickedness only to punish the wicked!
0.35	As someone has said, “This is how the passions all justify themselves.” They pass off sin to us under a cover of holiness!
0.36	5. But is there no way to avoid this trap?
0.37	Certainly there is. In the words quoted above, our blessed Lord has shown His followers a plain way.
0.38	No one who walks carefully and steadily on this path will ever fall into evil-speaking.
0.39	This rule either surely prevents it or surely cures it.
0.40	In the verses before this, our Lord had said, “How terrible for the world because of offenses!” Great misery will come into the world from this deadly source. (Offenses are anything that turns a person away from God’s ways or holds him back in them.) “For offenses are bound to come.” That is the nature of things. That is how wicked, foolish, and weak people are. “But how terrible for the man through whom the offense comes!” That man is truly miserable.
0.41	“So if your hand, your foot, or your eye makes you stumble…” It may be your dearest pleasure, or the person you love most and find most useful. If it turns you away from the way, or holds you back in it, “pluck it out.” Cut them off and throw them away from you.
0.42	But how can we avoid offending some people, and being offended by others?
0.43	Especially if they are clearly in the wrong, and we see it with our own eyes?
0.44	Here our Lord teaches us how. He gives a sure way to avoid both offenses and evil-speaking.
0.45	“If your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault, just between the two of you. If he listens to you, you have won your brother back.
0.46	But if he will not listen to you, take one or two others with you. Then every word can be confirmed by two or three witnesses.
0.47	If he will not listen to them, tell it to the church. And if he will not listen to the church, let him be to you like a pagan and a tax collector.”
0.48	I. 1. First: “If your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault, just between the two of you.”
0.49	Where it is possible, the best way to follow this first rule is the most literal way. Suppose you see with your own eyes a brother, a fellow Christian, commit a sin that cannot be denied. Or you hear it with your own ears, so that you cannot doubt it. Then what you must do is plain. Take the very first chance to go to him. And if you can get to see him, “tell him his fault, just between the two of you.”
0.50	Of course, you must take great care to do this in the right spirit and in the right way.
0.51	Whether a correction works depends very much on the spirit in which it is given.
0.52	So pray earnestly to God that you may give it in a humble spirit. Be deeply convinced that God alone makes you any different. And if anything good comes from what you say, God Himself is the one who does it.
0.53	Pray that He will guard your heart, give light to your mind, and lead your tongue to words that He will be pleased to bless.
0.54	Make sure you speak gently as well as humbly, “for man’s anger does not bring about God’s righteousness.”
0.55	If he has been “caught in a fault,” the only way to restore him is “in a spirit of gentleness.”
0.56	If he opposes the truth, he can still only be brought to know it through gentleness.
0.57	Keep speaking in a spirit of tender love, “which many waters cannot put out.”
0.58	If love is not defeated, it defeats everything.
0.59	Who can describe the power of love?
0.60	Love can bend the stubborn neck, / And turn stone into flesh; / It can soften, melt, pierce, and break / A heart as hard as diamond.
0.61	So show him your love clearly. In this way you will “heap burning coals on his head.”
0.62	2. But also make sure that the way you speak agrees with the gospel of Christ.
0.63	Avoid anything in your look, gestures, words, or tone of voice that suggests pride or self-importance.
0.64	Carefully avoid anything bossy or dogmatic, anything that looks arrogant or presumptuous.
0.65	Stay far away from anything like disdain, domineering, or contempt.
0.66	Be just as careful to avoid any sign of anger. You may speak very plainly. But let there be no blame, no harsh accusations, no sign of any warmth except the warmth of love.
0.67	Above all, let there be no trace of hatred or ill will, and no bitter or sour words. Instead, use a sweet and gentle manner and gentle words, so that everything clearly comes from love in your heart.
0.68	Yet this sweetness does not have to stop you from speaking very seriously and solemnly. As far as possible, use the very words of God, for there are no words like them. And speak as one who is under the eye of Him who is coming to judge the living and the dead.
0.69	3. Perhaps you have no chance to speak to him in person, or you cannot get to see him. Then you may do it through a messenger: a friend you both know, whose good sense and honesty you can fully trust.
0.70	Such a person can speak for you, in the spirit and manner described above. This may serve the same purpose. It can largely make up for your not doing it yourself.
0.71	But be careful not to pretend you have no chance, just to avoid the cross. And do not simply assume you cannot see him, without ever trying.
0.72	Whenever you can speak to him yourself, that is far better.
0.73	But it is better to do it through someone else than not at all. This way is better than nothing.
0.74	4. But what if you cannot speak to him yourself, and cannot find a messenger you can trust?
0.75	If that is really the case, then all that is left is to write.
0.76	And in some situations this may be the wisest way to speak.
0.77	One such situation is when the person we are dealing with has a hot and hasty temper. Such a person does not take correction easily, especially from an equal or from someone below him.
0.78	But in writing, the correction can be introduced and softened so that it is much easier to accept.
0.79	Besides, many people will read the very same words that they could not stand to hear.
0.80	Reading does not shock their pride so badly, or hurt their sense of honor so much.
0.81	Even if it makes little impression at first, they may read it a second time. Then, after thinking it over, they may take to heart what they ignored before.
0.82	If you sign your name, this is almost the same as going to him and speaking in person.
0.83	And you should always do this, unless some very special reason makes it wrong to do so.
0.84	5. We should note this carefully. Our Lord does not only command us to take this step. He commands us to take this step first, before we try anything else.
0.85	No alternative is allowed. We have no choice of anything else. This is the way; walk in it.
0.86	It is true that He tells us to take two more steps if needed. But we must take them one after another, after this step, and neither of them before it. Much less may we take any other step, either before this one or alongside it.
0.87	So doing anything else, or not doing this, is equally inexcusable.
0.88	6. Do not try to excuse yourself for taking a completely different step by saying, “Well, I did not speak to anyone until I felt so burdened that I could not hold it in.”
0.89	You felt burdened! No wonder, unless your conscience had lost all feeling. For you were guilty of sin. You were disobeying a plain command of God!
0.90	You should have gone right away and told “your brother his fault, just between the two of you.”
0.91	If you did not, how could you not feel burdened (unless your heart was completely hard)? You were trampling on God’s command and “hating your brother in your heart.”
0.92	And what a way you found to relieve your burden!
0.93	God corrects you for a sin of omission: you did not tell your brother his fault. And you comfort yourself under His correction by a sin of commission: you tell your brother’s fault to someone else!
0.94	Relief bought with sin comes at a high price!
0.95	I trust in God that you will find no relief. I trust you will be burdened even more, until you “go to your brother and tell him,” and no one else.
0.96	7. I know of only one exception to this rule. There may be a special case where we must accuse a guilty person who is not present, in order to protect the innocent.
0.97	For example, you know that a man is planning something against his neighbor’s property or life.
0.98	The situation may be such that the only way to stop the plan is to tell it right away to the person it is aimed at.
0.99	In this case, then, this rule is set aside. So is the apostle’s rule, “Speak evil of no one.” It is right, and in fact it is our duty, to speak evil of someone who is absent. We do it to stop him from doing evil to others and to himself at the same time.
0.100	But remember that all evil-speaking is, by its very nature, deadly poison.
0.101	So if you are sometimes forced to use it as a medicine, use it with fear and trembling. It is such a dangerous medicine that only absolute necessity can excuse you for using it at all.
0.102	So use it as rarely as possible, and only when it is truly necessary. And even then, use as little of it as you can, only as much as you need for the purpose.
0.103	At all other times, “go and tell him his fault, just between the two of you.”
0.104	II. 1. But what “if he will not listen”?
0.105	What if he pays you back evil for good?
0.106	What if he becomes angry instead of convinced?
0.107	What if he hears you but it does no good, and he keeps on in his evil ways?
0.108	We must expect this to happen often. The mildest and kindest correction will have no effect. But the blessing we wished for the other person will come back to us.
0.109	What should we do then?
0.110	Our Lord has given us clear and complete directions.
0.111	Then “take one or two others with you.” This is the second step.
0.112	Take one or two people whom you know to have a loving spirit, people who love God and their neighbor.
0.113	Also make sure they have a humble spirit and are “clothed with humility.”
0.114	They should also be gentle and kind, patient and long-suffering. They should not be quick to “pay back evil for evil, or insult for insult, but instead give a blessing.”
0.115	They should be people of understanding, who have wisdom from above. They should be fair, not taking sides, and free from any kind of prejudice.
0.116	You should also make sure that he knows these people well, and knows their character. Choose people he accepts rather than anyone else.
0.117	2. Love will show them how to go about it, depending on the kind of case.
0.118	No single method can be laid down for every case.
0.119	But in general, perhaps this advice would help. Before they begin with the matter itself, let them tell him gently and warmly that they are not angry with him or prejudiced against him. They come only out of goodwill, and that is the only reason they are involved in his affairs at all.
0.120	To make this even clearer, they could then calmly listen as you repeat your earlier conversation with him, and what he said to defend himself. They should do this before they try to decide anything.
0.121	After this they will be better able to judge how to go on, “so that every word may be confirmed by two or three witnesses.” Then whatever you said will have its full force, with the added weight of their authority.
0.122	3. To do this, could they not (1)
0.123	Briefly repeat what you said, and what he answered?
0.124	(2) Explain more fully, make clear, and support the reasons you had given?
0.125	(3) Give weight to your correction, by showing how fair, how kind, and how timely it was?
0.126	And finally, press home the advice and appeals you had added to it?
0.127	And later, if necessary, these people can also be witnesses of what was said.
0.128	4. As with the first rule, we can see that our Lord gives us no choice here. He leaves us no alternative. He clearly commands us to do this, and nothing else instead.
0.129	He also tells us when to do this, not sooner and not later: after we have taken the first step, and before we take the third.
0.130	Only then are we allowed to tell the evil someone has done to those we want to join us in this great act of brotherly love.
0.131	But let us be careful not to tell it to anyone else until both these steps have been taken.
0.132	If we fail to take these steps, or take other ones, is it any wonder that we still feel burdened?
0.133	For we are sinning against God and against our neighbor. And however nicely we may dress it up, if we have any conscience, our sin will find us out and bring a burden on our soul.
0.134	III. 1. Our Lord wants us to be fully taught in this important matter. So He has given us one more direction: “If he will not listen to them,” then, and not before, “tell it to the church.”
0.135	This is the third step.
0.136	The only question is how we should understand the word “church” here.
0.137	But the very nature of the matter makes this clear beyond any reasonable doubt.
0.138	You cannot tell it to the national church, the whole body of people called “the Church of England.”
0.139	And even if you could, it would not serve any Christian purpose. So this is not what the word means.
0.140	Nor can you tell it to the whole body of people in England with whom you are more closely connected.
0.141	And this would not do any good either. So the word should not be understood in this way.
0.142	It would not serve any useful purpose to tell the faults of each member to the church (if you want to call it that), meaning the congregation or society joined together in London.
0.143	What remains is to tell it to the elder or elders of the church. These are the overseers of the flock of Christ to which you both belong. They watch over your soul and his, “as those who must give an account.”
0.144	If possible, this should be done with the person present. And it should be done plainly, but with all the tenderness and love that the matter allows.
0.145	It is properly their job to make decisions about the behavior of those in their care. It is also their job to rebuke “with all authority,” as the offense deserves.
0.146	So when you have done this, you have done everything God’s Word, or the law of love, requires of you. You no longer share in his sin. If he is lost, his blood is on his own head.
0.147	2. Here too, notice that this, and nothing else, is the third step we must take. We must take it in its proper order, after the other two. It must not come before the second step, much less before the first, except in some very special situation.
0.148	In fact, in one case the second step may be the same as this one. In a way, they may be one and the same.
0.149	The elder or elders of the church may be so closely connected with the brother who has sinned that the one or two witnesses are not needed. The elders can take their place. So it may be enough to tell it to them, after you have told it to your brother “just between the two of you.”
0.150	3. When you have done this, you have delivered your own soul.
0.151	“If he will not listen to the church,” if he keeps on in his sin, “let him be to you like a pagan and a tax collector.”
0.152	You no longer need to think about him, except when you commit him to God in prayer.
0.153	You do not need to talk about him any more. Leave him to his own Master.
0.154	Of course, you still owe him sincere and tender goodwill, as you owe it to all other pagans.
0.155	You owe him politeness. And when the chance comes, you owe him every kindness that one human being owes another.
0.156	But have no friendship or close relationship with him. Have no more to do with him than with an open pagan.
0.157	4. But if this is the rule Christians live by, where is the land where Christians live?
0.158	You may perhaps find a few people here and there who make it a matter of conscience to keep it.
0.159	But how very few!
0.160	How thinly scattered across the earth!
0.161	And where is there any group of people who all live by it?
0.162	Can we find them in Europe?
0.163	Or, without going further, in Great Britain or Ireland?
0.164	I am afraid not. I fear we could search every part of these kingdoms and still search in vain.
0.165	How sad for the Christian world!
0.166	How sad for Protestants, for Reformed Christians!
0.167	Oh, “who will stand up with me against the wicked?”
0.168	“Who will take God’s side” against those who speak evil?
0.169	Are you the one?
0.170	By the grace of God, will you be one who is not swept away by the flood?
0.171	Are you fully determined, with God as your helper, from this very hour to set a guard, a constant “guard in front of your mouth,” and to “keep the door of your lips”?
0.172	From now on, will you live by this rule: “Speak evil of no one”?
0.173	If you see your brother do wrong, will you “tell him his fault, just between the two of you”?
0.174	And after that, “take one or two witnesses,” and only then “tell it to the church”?
0.175	If this is truly what your heart intends, then learn one lesson well: “Hear evil of no one.”
0.176	If there were no one to listen to evil, there would be no one to speak it.
0.177	And as the common saying goes, isn’t the one who receives stolen goods as bad as the thief?
0.178	So if anyone starts to speak evil where you can hear, stop him right away.
0.179	Refuse to listen to the charmer’s voice, no matter how sweetly he charms. He may use a very soft manner and a very mild tone. He may say again and again that he wishes well to the person he is stabbing in the dark, the one he strikes under the fifth rib!
0.180	Firmly refuse to listen, even if the whisperer complains that he is “burdened until he speaks.”
0.181	Burdened! You fool! Are you in labor with your cursed secret, like a woman giving birth to a child?
0.182	Then go, and be set free from your burden in the way the Lord has appointed!
0.183	First, “go and tell your brother his fault, just between the two of you.” Next, “take one or two” friends you both know, and tell him while they are there. If neither of these steps works, then “tell it to the church.”
0.184	But, at the risk of your soul, tell it to no one else, before or after! The only exception is that one case, when it is absolutely necessary to protect the innocent.
0.185	Why should you burden someone else as well as yourself, by making him share in your sin?
0.186	5. Oh, that all of you who bear the shame of Christ, who are mockingly called Methodists, would set an example to the so-called Christian world, at least in this one thing!
0.187	Put away evil-speaking, telling tales, and whispering. Do not let any of them come out of your mouth!
0.188	Make sure you “speak evil of no one.” Say nothing but good about those who are absent.
0.189	If you must be marked out as different, whether you want it or not, let this be the mark of a Methodist: “He criticizes no one behind his back. By this fruit you may know him.”
0.190	What a blessed effect of this self-denial we would soon feel in our hearts!
0.191	How our “peace would flow like a river,” when we “pursued peace with everyone” in this way!
0.192	How God’s love would overflow in our own souls, as we proved our love for our brothers in this way!
0.193	And what an effect it would have on all who were united in the name of the Lord Jesus!
0.194	How brotherly love would keep growing, once this great obstacle to it was removed!
0.195	Then all the members of Christ’s mystical body would naturally care for one another.
0.196	“If one member suffered, all would suffer with it. If one was honored, all would rejoice with it.” And everyone would love his brother “with a pure heart, fervently.”
0.197	And that is not all. What an effect this might have even on the wild, thoughtless world!
0.198	How soon they would see in us what they could not find among all the thousands of their brothers! And they would cry out (as Julian the Apostate did to his pagan courtiers), “See how these Christians love one another!”
0.199	This above all is how God would convince the world and also prepare them for His kingdom. We can easily learn this from the remarkable words of our Lord’s last, solemn prayer: “I pray for those who will believe in Me, that they may all be one, just as You, Father, are in Me and I am in You, so that the world may believe that You sent Me!”
0.200	[John 17:21] May the Lord bring that time soon!
0.201	May the Lord help us to love one another like this, not only “with words and talk, but with actions and in truth,” just as Christ has loved us.
