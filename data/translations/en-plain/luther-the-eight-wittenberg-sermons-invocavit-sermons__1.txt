3.48	First: Maybe you cannot give up meat without hurting yourself, or maybe you are sick. Then you may eat whatever you like. And if anyone is offended, let him be offended.
3.49	Even if the whole world were offended, you would still not be sinning. God can excuse you. He has kindly given you this freedom, and your health needs it. Going without meat would put your health in danger.
3.50	Second: Someone may push you to eat fish instead of meat on Friday. Or they may push you to eat fish and stay away from eggs and butter during Lent, and so on. That is what the pope has done with his foolish laws. When that happens, you must never let yourself be pulled away from the freedom God has given you. Instead, do exactly the opposite, just to spite him. Say to him: "You forbid me to eat meat. You dare to turn my freedom into a law. So I will eat meat, whatever you say."
3.51	You must do the same in all the other things where we are free.
3.52	Here is an example. Suppose the pope, or anyone else, tried to force me to wear a monk's hood exactly the way he orders. Then I would take the hood off, just to spite him.
3.53	But since it is left up to me, I wear it or take it off, just as I like.
3.54	Third: Some people are still weak in faith. They need to be taught, and they would gladly believe as we do.
3.55	But they do not know any better, and that holds them back. If this were preached to them faithfully, as it was to us, they would be one with us.
3.56	We must treat these well-meaning people in a completely different way from the stubborn ones.
3.57	We must be patient with them and not use our freedom. Holding back does not put body or soul in any danger or do them any harm. In fact, it is good for us. And on top of that, we do our brothers and sisters a great service.
3.58	But suppose we use our freedom when there is no need, and we offend our neighbor on purpose. Then we drive away the very person who would have come to our faith in time.
3.59	That is why St. Paul circumcised Timothy. Some simple Jewish people had been offended [Acts 16:3]. Paul thought, "What harm can it do? They are only offended because they do not know better."
3.60	But in Antioch some people insisted that he had to circumcise Titus. They said he must do it. Then Paul stood up against all of them. And to spite them, he refused to have Titus circumcised [Gal. 2:3].
3.61	He did not give in.
3.62	Paul did the same when St. Peter used his freedom in a way that gave people who had not been taught a wrong idea [Gal. 2:11ff.].
3.63	This is what happened. When Peter was with the Gentiles, he ate pork and sausage with them. But when the Jews came in, he would not touch that food, and he stopped eating with the Gentiles.
3.64	Then the Gentiles who had become Christians thought, "Oh no! We must be like the Jews too. We must not eat pork, and we must live by the law of Moses."
3.65	But Paul saw that this would damage the freedom of the Gospel. So he corrected Peter in front of everyone and gave him an apostle's lecture. He said, "You are a Jew, yet you live like the Gentiles. So why do you force the Gentiles to live like Jews?"
3.66	[Gal. 2:14] We should live the same way. We should use our freedom at the right time. Then Christian freedom will not be damaged. And we will not offend our weak brothers and sisters who do not yet know about this freedom.
T:4	Sermon Five: A Sermon on the Sacrament (Thursday after Invocavit)
4.1	We have heard about the necessary things, such as the mass, which people treat as a sacrifice. We have also heard about the things that are not necessary, such as monks leaving their monasteries, priests getting married, and the images.
4.2	We have seen how we must handle these matters. We must not force them or make laws about them. No one should be dragged away from them by the hair. We must let the Word of God alone do the work.
4.3	Now let us think about how we should treat the blessed sacrament.
4.4	You have heard how I preached against the pope's foolish law and opposed his rule. His rule says that no woman may wash the altar cloth that the body of Christ has lain on—not even a pure nun—unless a pure priest has washed it first.
4.5	In the same way, when anyone touches the body of Christ with his hand, the priests come running and scrape his fingers. And there is much more of the same kind.
4.6	But when a priest lives an unchaste life, the pope looks the other way.
4.7	If the woman has a baby, he lets that go too.
4.8	But the altar cloth and the sacrament must not be touched!
4.9	We have preached against foolish laws like these. We have taught that there is no sin in breaking the pope's foolish rules. A layperson does not sin if he touches the cup or the body of Christ with his hands.
4.10	You should thank God that you understand this so clearly. Many great men have not understood it.
4.11	But now you have become just as foolish as the pope. You think you must hold the sacrament in your hands. You want to prove you are good Christians by touching the sacrament with your hands.
4.12	The sacrament is our greatest treasure. Yet you have treated it in such a way that it is a wonder you were not struck down by thunder and lightning.
4.13	God would have put up with the other things you did. But forcing people in this matter—
4.14	that He will never put up with.
4.15	If you do not turn back from this, no emperor or anyone else will need to drive me away from you. I will leave without being asked. In fact, I dare to say this: my enemies have caused me a lot of sorrow, but none of them has hurt me the way you have hurt me in this matter.
4.16	Suppose you could prove you are good Christians by handling the sacrament and bragging about it to everybody. Then Herod and Pilate would be the greatest and best Christians of all!
4.17	After all, I think they handled the body of Christ when they had Him nailed to the cross and killed.
4.18	No, my dear friends. The kingdom of God is not made of outward things that can be touched or seen. It is made of faith [Luke 17:20].
4.19	But you may say, "We live by the Scriptures, and we should. And God set up the sacrament so that we take it with our hands. For He said, 'Take and eat, this is my body.'"
4.20	[Matt. 26:26] Here is my answer. I am completely sure that the Lord's disciples took it with their hands. And I admit that you may do the same without sinning. But I still cannot make it a rule. And I cannot prove that it is the only way.
4.21	Here is why. The devil comes after us and argues, "Where have you read in the Scriptures that 'take' means 'grab with the hands'?" How could I prove it or defend it?
4.22	In fact, how could I answer him when he quotes the Scriptures to show the very opposite? He can prove that "take" does not only mean to receive with the hands. It also means to receive something in other ways.
4.23	He says, "Look, my good friend. Three Gospel writers use the word 'take' when they describe how the Lord took the gall and vinegar [Matt. 27:34; Mark 15:23; Luke 23:26].
4.24	You have to admit that the Lord did not touch it or hold it with His hands. His hands were nailed to the cross."
4.25	That verse is a strong argument against me.
4.26	Then he quotes another passage: Et accepit omnes timor—"And fear took hold of all" [Luke 7:16]. Here again we have to admit that fear has no hands.
4.27	So I am backed into a corner. Even though I do not want to, I have to agree that "take" does not only mean to receive with the hands. It also means to receive something in any other way it can be done.
4.28	So you see, dear friends, we must stand on solid ground if we want to stand up to the devil's attack.
4.29	I have to admit that you did not sin when you touched the sacrament with your hands. But I must still tell you that it was not a good thing to do, because it offended people everywhere.
4.30	For everywhere, the custom is to receive the blessed sacrament straight from the priest's hands.
4.31	Why won't you serve those who are weak in the faith in this too, and hold back from using your freedom?
4.32	Doing it does not help you, and not doing it does not hurt you.
4.33	So no new practices should be started until the Gospel has first been fully preached and understood, the way it has been among you.
4.34	For this reason, dear friends, let us be serious and wise about the things of God. God will not be mocked.
4.35	You may make fun of the saints, but it is a very different matter with God.
4.36	So I beg you, stop doing this.
4.37	Now let us talk about the two kinds.
4.38	I believe the sacrament should be received in both kinds, the way the Lord set it up. But it still must not be forced on anyone or made into a law for everyone.
4.39	We must busy ourselves with the Word. We must practice it and preach it.
4.40	As for the results, we should leave them completely to the Word. And we should let everyone have his freedom in this matter.
4.41	If we do not do that, the sacrament turns into an outward ritual and a show of fake religion. That is exactly what the devil wants.
4.42	But when the Word is allowed to run freely and is not tied to any ritual, it takes hold of one person today and sinks into his heart. Tomorrow it touches someone else, and so on.
4.43	In this way it will do its work quietly and calmly. And no one will know how it all happened.
4.44	I was glad when someone wrote to tell me that some people in this city had started to receive the sacrament in both kinds.
4.45	You should have left it that way and not turned it into a law.
4.46	But now you rush at it without thinking, and you force everyone to do it.
4.47	Dear friends, you will not succeed that way.
4.48	Suppose you want people to see you as better Christians than others because you take the sacrament in your hands and receive both kinds. Then you are poor Christians indeed!
4.49	If that were enough, even a pig could be a Christian. After all, she has a snout big enough to take in the sacrament on the outside.
4.50	We must be serious about such high things.
4.51	Dear friends, this must not be treated as a joke. If you will listen to me, stop doing it.
4.52	If you will not listen to me, no one needs to drive me away from you. I will leave you without being asked. And I will be sorry that I ever preached even one sermon here.
4.53	We could let the other things go, but not this one. You have gone so far that people are saying, "There are very good Christians in Wittenberg. They take the sacrament in their hands and hold the cup. Then they go off to their brandy and drink until they are drunk."
4.54	This drives away the weak and simple people. They would come to us if they had been taught as much as we have been taught.
4.55	But suppose someone is so foolish that he just has to touch the sacrament with his hands. Then let him have it brought to his house. There he can handle it as much as he likes.
4.56	But in public he should hold back. That will not hurt him. And it will keep us from offending our brothers, sisters and neighbors. Right now they are so angry with us that they are ready to kill us.
4.57	I can honestly say that none of the enemies who have fought against me so far have caused me as much grief as you have.
4.58	That is enough for today. We will continue tomorrow.
T:5	Sermon Six (Friday after Invocavit)
5.1	In talking about the main things, we have come to receiving the sacrament. We have not finished that yet.
5.2	Today we will see how we should behave here. We will also see who is worthy to receive the sacrament and who belongs there.
5.3	Here it is very important that your hearts and consciences are well taught. Then you can clearly tell the difference between receiving the sacrament outwardly and receiving it inwardly and spiritually.
5.4	Receiving it with the body, on the outside, means this: a person takes the body and blood of Christ with his mouth.
5.5	Anyone can receive the sacrament this way. It can be done without any faith or love.
5.6	But receiving it that way does not make a person a Christian. If it did, even a mouse would be a Christian! A mouse can also eat the bread and drink from the cup.
5.7	It is such an easy thing to do.
5.8	But the true, inward, spiritual receiving is something very different. It means using the sacrament rightly and receiving its fruits.
5.9	First of all I would say this: the true inward receiving is receiving in faith.
5.10	We Christians have no other outward sign that sets us apart from others except this sacrament and baptism. But just receiving it on the outside, without faith, is worth nothing.
5.11	There must be faith. Faith makes a person well prepared to receive it and makes him acceptable to God. Without faith it is all fake, just an outward show. And that is not Christianity at all.
5.12	Christianity is a matter of faith. And faith is never tied to any outward action.
5.13	We all need faith if we want to come to the sacrament in a worthy way. And faith is a firm trust. It trusts that Christ, the Son of God, stands in our place. It trusts that He has carried all our sins on His shoulders. It trusts that He is the payment for our sin forever and brings us back to peace with God the Father.
5.14	Whoever has this faith belongs at this sacrament. Then no devil, no hell and no sin can hurt him.
5.15	Do you ask why?
5.16	Because God protects and defends him.
5.17	When I have this faith, I am sure that God is fighting for me. Then I can stand up against the devil, death, hell and sin, and against all the harm they threaten me with.
5.18	This is the great treasure that God has given us in Christ. It is worth more than anyone can count, and human words cannot describe it.
5.19	Only faith can take hold of the heart. And not everyone has that kind of faith.
5.20	So this sacrament must not be made into a law. But that is what the most holy father, the pope, has done with his foolish command. It says all Christians must go to the sacrament at holy Easter time. And anyone who does not go will not be buried in holy ground.
5.21	Isn't that a foolish law the pope has made?
5.22	Do you ask why? Because we are not all the same. We do not all have the same faith. One person's faith is stronger than another's.
5.23	So the sacrament can never be made into a law. In fact, the worst sins are committed at Easter, only because of this unchristian command that drives everyone to the sacrament.
5.24	Imagine all the stealing, all the greedy moneylending, all the sexual sin and all the other sins piled up in one big heap. This sin would be bigger than the whole pile. And it happens at the time and place where it seems the silliest of all.
5.25	And why? Because the pope cannot look into anyone's heart to see whether he has faith or not.
5.26	But suppose you believe that God is with you. Suppose you believe He puts all His treasures and His blood on the line for you. It is as if He were saying: "Come and follow right behind Me. Do not be afraid, and do not wait. Then let anything come that tries to hurt you. Let the devil, death, sin, hell and all creation try it. I will go ahead of you. I will be your captain and your shield. Trust Me and depend on Me completely." Whoever believes like this cannot be hurt by the devil, hell, sin or death. If God is fighting for him, what can you do to him?
5.27	Whoever has this kind of faith is ready for the altar. He receives the sacrament as a promise, a seal or a sign. It makes him sure of God's promises and grace.
5.28	But we do not all have this kind of faith. I wish to God that even one out of ten Christians had it!
5.29	Look: God in His grace pours out these rich treasures on us, more than can be measured. But not everyone can have them. They belong only to those who suffer hardship in body or in spirit. Hardship in the body comes when people persecute them. Hardship in the spirit comes when their conscience is in despair. It comes from outside or from inside—when the devil makes your heart weak, fearful and discouraged, so that you do not know where you stand with God, and when he throws your sins in your face.
5.30	God wants to live only in hearts like these, hearts that are terrified and trembling. That is what the prophet Isaiah says [Isa. 66:2].
5.31	Some people have never felt this battle inside themselves. Their sins do not trouble them, and they do not fight against them every day. They do not want anyone to protect them, defend them and stand in front of them like a shield. People like that are not ready for this food yet.
5.32	This food is for someone who is hungry and longing for it. It loves to come into a hungry soul—a soul that is always fighting its sins and wants to be free of them.
5.33	Whoever is not prepared like this should stay away from this sacrament for a while. This food is not for a heart that is already stuffed and full. If it comes to a heart like that, it does harm.
5.34	So if we think about these things and feel this trouble of conscience and the fear of a timid heart, we will come with all humility and respect. We will not rush at it carelessly, with pride and without fear and humility.
5.35	We are not always ready for it. Today I have the grace and I am ready. But tomorrow I may not be. In fact, I may go six months without wanting it or being ready for it.
5.36	So the most worthy people are those who are always troubled by death and the devil. It is the best time for them to receive it. It reminds them that nothing can hurt them, and it makes them strong in that faith. For now He is with them, and no one can take them away from Him. Let death or the devil or sin come—they cannot hurt them.
5.37	That is what Christ did when He got ready to set up the blessed sacrament.
5.38	He filled His disciples with pain and made their hearts tremble when He told them He was going away [Matt. 26:2]. And they were tormented again when He said, "One of you will betray me" [Matt. 26:21].
5.39	Don't you think that cut them to the heart?
5.40	Yes, they heard those words with great fear. They sat there as if every one of them were a traitor against God.
5.41	He made them all shake with fear and sorrow. Only then did He set up the blessed sacrament to comfort them, and He cheered them up again.
5.42	For this bread is comfort for those who are sad. It is healing for the sick and life for the dying. It is food for everyone who is hungry, and a rich treasure for all who are poor and in need.
5.43	That is enough for now about the right way to use this sacrament.
5.44	I put you in God's care.
T:6	Sermon Seven (Saturday before Reminiscere)
6.1	Yesterday we heard how to use the holy and blessed sacrament. We saw who is worthy to receive it. It is those who are afraid of death, whose consciences are fearful and in despair, and who live in fear of hell.
6.2	All these people come ready to share in this food. It makes their weak faith stronger and comforts their conscience.
6.3	That is the true and right way to use this sacrament. If someone does not find himself in this condition, he should stay away. He should wait until God takes hold of him too and draws him through His Word.
6.4	Now we will talk about the fruit of this sacrament. That fruit is love. It means we should treat our neighbor the same way God has treated us.
6.5	God has given us nothing but love and kindness. Christ has promised us His righteousness and given it to us, along with everything He has. He has poured out on us all His treasures. No one can measure them, and no angel can understand them or get to the bottom of them. For God is a blazing furnace of love that reaches all the way from the earth to the heavens.
6.6	Love, I say, is a fruit of this sacrament.
6.7	But I do not see it yet among you here in Wittenberg. You hear a lot of preaching about love, and you should practice it more than anything else.
6.8	Love is the main thing. It is the only thing that truly suits a Christian.
6.9	But no one is eager to love. Instead you want to do all kinds of things that are not needed and do not matter.
6.10	If you do not want to show you are Christians by your love, then do not bother with the other things either. For St. Paul says in 1 Corinthians, "If I speak in the languages of people and of angels, but I do not have love, I am only a noisy piece of brass or a jingling cymbal."
6.11	[1 Cor. 13:1] These are frightening words from Paul.
6.12	And he goes on: "If I have the gift of prophecy and understand all God's secrets and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so that I could move mountains, but I do not have love, I am nothing.
6.13	And if I give away everything I own to feed the poor, and if I give up my body to be burned, but I do not have love, it does me no good at all."
6.14	[1 Cor. 13:2–3] You have not come that far yet. And yet God has given you great and rich gifts, especially an understanding of the Scriptures.
6.15	It is true that you have the pure Gospel and the true Word of God. But none of you has given his things to the poor yet. None of you has been burned yet. And even those things would be worth nothing without love.
6.16	You want to take all of God's good gifts in the sacrament. But you do not want to pour them out again in love.
6.17	No one will lend anyone else a helping hand. No one thinks of others first. Everyone looks out for himself and what he can get. He only cares about his own things and lets everything else go however it goes. If someone happens to get help, fine.
6.18	No one takes care of the poor or tries to find ways to help them.
6.19	It is sad. You have heard many sermons about this. All my books are full of it. They have only one goal: to urge you to faith and love.
6.20	If you will not love one another, God will send a great disaster on you. Let this be a warning to you. God will not show His Word and have it preached for nothing.
6.21	My friends, you are testing God too far.
6.22	If someone had preached the Word to our ancestors long ago, maybe they would have acted differently.
6.23	Or if the Word were preached today to many of the poor children in the monasteries, they would receive it with much more joy than you do.
6.24	You pay no attention to it at all. Instead you give yourselves to other things that are not needed and are foolish.
6.25	I put you in God's care.
T:7	Sermon Eight: A Short Summary of Dr. M. Luther's Sermon on Private Confession, Preached on Reminiscere Sunday
7.1	We have now heard about all the things we needed to think about here, except confession.
7.2	Now we will talk about that.
7.3	First, there is a kind of confession that is based on the Scriptures. It is for when someone sins in public, or in a way other people know about, and he is accused in front of the church.
7.4	If he turns away from his sin, the church prays to God for him.
7.5	But if he will not listen to the church, he is put out of the church and sent away. Then no one will have anything to do with him.
7.6	God commands this kind of confession in Matthew 18: "If your brother sins against you (so that you and others are offended), go and point out what he did wrong, just between the two of you."
7.7	[Matt. 18:15] But you cannot find even a trace of this kind of confession anymore. In this matter the Gospel has been pushed aside here.
7.8	Anyone who could bring it back would be doing a good work.
7.9	This is where you should have put in the effort. You should have brought back this kind of confession and let the other things go.
7.10	No one would have been offended by that, and it could have been done without any trouble.
7.11	This is how it should be done. When you see someone who lends money at unfair interest, or who cheats on his marriage, or who steals, or who gets drunk, go to him privately. Warn him to give up his sin.
7.12	If he will not listen, take two other people with you. Warn him once more, like a brother, to give up his sin.
7.13	But if he laughs that off, tell the pastor in front of the whole church. Bring your witnesses with you. Accuse him before the pastor with all the people there. Say: "Dear pastor, this man has done this and that. We warned him like brothers to give up his sin, but he would not listen.
7.14	So I am accusing him, together with my witnesses who were there."
7.15	Then, if he will not give up his sin and willingly admit his guilt, the pastor should put him out. In front of the whole gathering, he should shut him out of the church's fellowship. This is done for the good of the church. It lasts until the man comes to his senses and is welcomed back again.
7.16	That would be Christian.
7.17	But I cannot take this on and carry it out all by myself.
7.18	Second, we need to confess when we go off into a corner by ourselves. There we confess to God Himself and pour out all our faults to Him.
7.19	This kind of confession is also commanded.
7.20	That is where the well-known saying from Scripture comes from: "Facite judicium et justitiam"—"Do judgment and righteousness."
7.21	[Gen. 18:19] To do judgment means to accuse and condemn ourselves. To do righteousness means to trust in God's mercy.
7.22	As it is written, "Happy are those who keep judgment and do righteousness at all times."
7.23	[Ps. 106:3] Judgment is simply this: a person sees himself as he is, judges himself and condemns himself. That is true humility, bringing yourself low.
7.24	Righteousness is simply this: a person knows himself and prays to God for mercy and help. And through that mercy and help, God lifts him up again.
7.25	That is what David means when he says, "I have sinned. I will confess my wrongs to the Lord" [Ps. 32:5f.]. He also says, "You forgave the guilt of my sin. That is why all Your holy people will pray to You."
7.26	Third, there is also a kind of confession where one person takes another aside. He tells him what is troubling him, so that he can hear a word of comfort from him. This is the kind of confession the pope has commanded.
7.27	What I condemned when I wrote about confession was this pushing and forcing. I refuse to go to confession just because the pope wants it and has ordered it.
7.28	I want him to keep his hands off confession. He should not turn it into something forced or commanded. He does not have the power to do that.
7.29	Still, I will not let anyone take private confession away from me. I would not give it up for all the treasures in the world. I know how much comfort and strength it has given me.
7.30	No one knows what it can do for him unless he has fought hard against the devil.
7.31	Yes, the devil would have killed me long ago if confession had not held me up.
7.32	A person has many doubts that he cannot settle by himself. So he takes a brother aside and tells him his trouble.
7.33	What harm is there in that? He humbles himself a little in front of his neighbor and lets himself be ashamed. He looks to him for a word of comfort. Then he takes that word for himself and believes it, as if he heard it from God Himself. We read about this in Matthew 18: "If two of you agree about anything they ask for, it will be done for them." [Matt. 18:19]
7.34	We need many absolutions. They make our fearful consciences and hopeless hearts strong against the devil and against God.
7.35	So no one should forbid confession. No one should keep anyone away from it or drive anyone away from it.
7.36	Maybe someone is struggling with his sins. He wants to be free of them and is looking for some promise from the Scriptures to make him sure. Let him go and confess to another person in private. And whatever is said to him there, let him receive it as if it came straight from God's own mouth.
7.37	If someone has a strong, firm faith that his sins are forgiven, he can skip this kind of confession and confess to God alone.
7.38	But how many people have such strong faith?
7.39	So, as I said, I will not let anyone take this private confession away from me.
7.40	But I would not force anyone to do it. I leave it up to each person's free choice.
7.41	Our God is not stingy. He has not left us with just one comfort or one source of strength for our conscience, or just one absolution. No, we have many absolutions in the Gospel, and He pours them out on us richly.
7.42	For example, we have this in the Gospel: "If you forgive other people when they wrong you, your Father in heaven will forgive you too."
7.43	[Matt. 6:14] We have another comfort in the Lord's Prayer: "Forgive us our trespasses" [Matt. 6:12], and so on.
7.44	A third comfort is our baptism. I reason like this: "Look, my Lord, I am baptized in Your name, so I can be sure of Your grace and mercy."
7.45	Next we have private confession. I go and receive a sure absolution, as if God Himself were speaking it. Then I can be sure that my sins are forgiven.
7.46	Finally I receive the blessed sacrament for myself. I eat His body and drink His blood as a sign that I am free of my sins and that God has set me free from all my weaknesses. To make me sure of this, He gives me His body to eat and His blood to drink. So I will not despair, and I cannot despair. I cannot doubt that God is kind to me.
7.47	So we see that confession must not be looked down on. It is a true comfort.
7.48	We need many absolutions and comforts, because we have to fight against the devil, death, hell and sin. So we must not let any of our weapons be taken away. We must keep the whole set of armor and equipment that God has given us to use against our enemies.
7.49	You do not know yet how hard it is to fight the devil and beat him.
7.50	But I know it well. I have eaten salt with him once or twice.
7.51	I know him well, and he knows me well too.
7.52	If you only knew him, you would not throw out confession like this.
7.53	I put you in God's care. Amen.
