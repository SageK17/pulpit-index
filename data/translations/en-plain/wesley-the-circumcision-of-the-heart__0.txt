0.1	“Circumcision is a matter of the heart. It is in the spirit, not in the letter.” Romans 2:29.
0.2	1. An excellent man once made a sad remark. He said that anyone who now preaches the most essential duties of Christianity runs a risk. A large part of his hearers may think of him as “a preacher of new teachings.”
0.3	Most people still claim to follow the Christian religion, but the way they live has drained away its real substance. Some truths show how the Spirit of Christ differs from the spirit of the world. As soon as anyone puts forward one of these truths, they cry out, “You are telling us strange things. We want to know what these things mean.” Yet he is only preaching to them “Jesus and the resurrection,” and what necessarily follows from it: If Christ has risen, then you must die to the world and live completely for God.
0.4	2. This is a hard saying for the natural man. He is alive to the world and dead to God. He will not easily be persuaded to accept it as God’s truth. He will accept it only if it is explained in such a way that it has no use or meaning left.
0.5	He “does not accept the” words “of the Spirit of God” when they are taken in their plain and obvious meaning. “They are foolishness to him. And” indeed “he cannot understand them, because they are understood only in a spiritual way.” They can be perceived only by a spiritual sense, and in him that sense has never yet been awakened. Because he lacks it, he rejects as idle human fancies the very things that are both the wisdom and the power of God.
0.6	3. “Circumcision is a matter of the heart, in the spirit, not in the letter.” In other words, the mark of a true follower of Christ, of someone God has accepted, is not outward circumcision, or baptism, or any other outward form. It is a right state of soul, a mind and spirit made new in the image of the One who created it. This is one of those important truths that can only be understood in a spiritual way.
0.7	The Apostle himself hints at this in his next words: “That person’s praise does not come from people, but from God.”
0.8	It is as if he said: “Whoever you are, you follow your great Master in this way. But do not expect the world—the people who do not follow him—to say, ‘Well done, good and faithful servant!’
0.9	Understand that the world thinks the circumcision of the heart, the seal of your calling, is foolishness.
0.10	Be content to wait for your praise until the day when your Lord appears.
0.11	On that day you will receive praise from God, in the great gathering of people and angels.”
0.12	First, I plan to look closely at what this circumcision of the heart is. Second, I will mention some thoughts that naturally come from this study.
0.13	I. 1. First, then, I will look at what the circumcision of the heart is that will receive God’s praise.
0.14	In general, we can say that it is a settled condition of the soul, which the sacred writings call holiness. It directly means being cleansed from sin, “from everything that makes flesh and spirit unclean.” As a result, it also means having the same virtues that were in Christ Jesus. It means being so “made new in the spirit of our mind” that we are “perfect as our Father in heaven is perfect.”
0.15	2. To be more specific: circumcision of the heart includes humility, faith, hope, and love.
0.16	Humility is a right view of ourselves. It cleans our minds of proud ideas about our own perfection, and of thinking too highly of our own abilities and achievements. These ideas are the natural fruit of a corrupted nature.
0.17	Humility completely cuts off the empty thought, “I am rich and wise, and I need nothing.” It convinces us that by nature we are wretched, poor, miserable, blind, and naked.
0.18	It convinces us that even at our best we are, in ourselves, nothing but sin and emptiness. Confusion, ignorance, and error rule our understanding. Unreasonable, earthly, sensual, and devilish passions have taken over control of our will. In a word, no part of our soul is healthy, and all the foundations of our nature are out of place.
0.19	3. At the same time we are convinced that we cannot help ourselves by our own power. Without the Spirit of God, we can do nothing but add sin to sin. He alone works in us by his almighty power, so that we either want to do what is good or actually do it. Without the supernatural help of his Spirit, we cannot even think a good thought. That is as impossible for us as creating ourselves, or making our whole souls new in righteousness and true holiness.
0.20	4. Once we have formed this right view of how sinful and helpless our nature is, one result is sure. We stop caring about the “honor that comes from people.” That honor is usually given for some good quality people suppose we have.
0.21	Someone who knows himself does not want or value praise that he knows he does not deserve.
0.22	So it is “a very small thing to him to be judged by people.”
0.23	He compares what the world has said for or against him with what he feels in his own heart. So he has every reason to think that the world, like the god of this world, has been “a liar from the beginning.”
0.24	And what about those who are not of the world? If it were God’s will, he would like them to see him as someone who wants to be found a faithful manager of his Lord’s goods. Perhaps that would help him be more useful to his fellow servants. Since that is his only reason for wanting their approval, he does not depend on it at all. For he is sure that whatever God wants done, God will never lack people to do it. God can raise up servants even from these stones to do what pleases him.
0.25	5. This is the humble mind that people learn from Christ when they follow his example and walk in his steps.
0.26	They come to know their disease, and more and more they are cleansed from one part of it: pride and vanity. This knowledge makes them willing and ready to accept the second thing that circumcision of the heart includes. That is faith, which alone can make them whole. It is the one medicine given under heaven to heal their sickness.
0.27	6. Faith is the best guide for the blind, the surest light for those in darkness, and the most perfect teacher for the foolish.
0.28	But it must be a faith that is “mighty through God for pulling down strongholds.” It must overturn all the prejudices of corrupt reason, all the false sayings that people honor, all evil customs and habits, and all the “wisdom of the world that is foolishness to God.” It must be a faith that “throws down imaginations,” reasonings, “and every proud thing that sets itself up against the knowledge of God, and takes every thought captive to obey Christ.”
0.29	7. “Everything is possible for the one who” believes in this way.
0.30	“The eyes of his understanding have been opened,” and he sees what his calling is. It is to glorify God in his body and in his spirit, because God bought him at such a high price. Both now belong to God, because God redeemed them as well as created them.
0.31	He feels “the exceeding greatness of this power.” God raised Christ from the dead, and in the same way he is able to “make us alive when we were dead in sin, by his Spirit who lives in us.”
0.32	“This is the victory that overcomes the world: our faith.” This faith is not only a firm agreement with everything God has revealed in Scripture. That includes especially these important truths: “Jesus Christ came into the world to save sinners.” “He carried our sins in his own body on the tree.” “He is the sacrifice that pays for our sins, and not only for ours, but also for the sins of the whole world.” But this faith is also the revealing of Christ in our hearts. It is a God-given evidence or conviction of his love, his free, undeserved love for me, a sinner. It is a sure confidence in his forgiving mercy, produced in us by the Holy Spirit. Through this confidence every true believer is able to testify: “I know that my Redeemer lives.” I have an “Advocate with the Father.” “Jesus Christ the righteous” is my Lord and “the sacrifice for my sins.” I know he has “loved me and given himself for me.” He has made peace between God and me, even me. And I “have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins.”
0.33	8. A faith like this will always clearly show the power of the One who gives it. He sets his children free from the yoke of sin and “cleans their consciences from dead works.” He makes them so strong that they no longer have to obey sin and its desires. Instead of offering the parts of their body to sin “as tools for doing wrong,” they now “offer themselves” completely “to God, as people who have come back from death to life.”
0.34	9. Those who are born of God through faith in this way also have strong comfort through hope.
0.35	This is the next thing that circumcision of the heart includes: the witness of their own spirit together with the Spirit, who testifies in their hearts that they are God’s children.
0.36	In fact, it is the same Spirit who gives them a clear and cheerful confidence that their heart is upright before God. He gives them a firm assurance that, through his grace, they are now doing what pleases him. They are now on the path that leads to life, and by God’s mercy they will stay on it to the end.
0.37	It is he who gives them a living expectation of receiving every good thing from God’s hand. He gives them a joyful hope of the crown of glory that is kept in heaven for them.
0.38	This hope is an anchor. It keeps a Christian steady among the waves of this troubled world. It keeps him from crashing on either of two deadly rocks: presumption or despair.
0.39	He is not discouraged by a false idea that his Lord is harsh. Nor does he look down on the riches of his goodness.
0.40	He does not think the difficulties of the race ahead of him are too great for his strength to overcome. But he also does not expect them to be so small that they will give way before he has used all his strength.
0.41	He already has experience in the Christian battle. It assures him that his “work is not wasted” if he does “whatever his hand finds to do with all his might.” That same experience keeps him from the empty idea that he can gain anything in any other way. No virtue can be shown and no praise won by weak hearts and feeble hands. It can only be done by those who follow the same path as the great Apostle to the Gentiles. “I run,” he says, “but not without a clear aim. I fight, but not like someone beating the air. I keep my body down and bring it under control. Otherwise, after I have preached to others, I myself might be rejected.”
0.42	10. With this same discipline every good soldier of Christ must train himself to endure hardship.
0.43	Made firm and strong by it, he will be able to give up not only the works of darkness, but also every appetite and every desire that is not subject to God’s law.
0.44	For as St. John says, “Everyone who has this hope makes himself pure, just as he is pure.”
0.45	Every day he makes it his care to clean the deepest corners of his soul. He does this by God’s grace in Christ and through the blood of the covenant. He cleans out the lusts that once possessed and polluted it: impurity, envy, malice, and anger. He clears away every passion and attitude that is of the flesh, whether it springs from his inborn corruption or feeds it. He knows well that his very body is God’s temple, and so he must let nothing common or unclean into it. He knows that holiness always suits the house where the Spirit of holiness kindly chooses to live.
0.46	11. But whoever you are, you still lack one thing. You have joined a lively hope to deep humility and steady faith, and in this way you have largely cleansed your heart from the pollution you were born with.
0.47	If you want to be perfect, add love to all these. Add love, and you have the circumcision of the heart. “Love fulfills the law; it is the goal of the commandment.”
0.48	Very excellent things are said about love. It is the essence, the spirit, and the life of every virtue.
0.49	It is not only the first and great commandment. It is all the commandments in one.
0.50	“Whatever is right, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely,” or honorable, “if there is any virtue, if there is anything worthy of praise”—all of it is included in this one word: love.
0.51	In love there is perfection, glory, and happiness.
0.52	This is the royal law of heaven and earth: “You are to love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.”
0.53	12. This does not forbid us to love anything besides God. It means that we also love our brother.
0.54	Nor does it forbid us (as some have strangely imagined) to enjoy anything except God.
0.55	To think that would mean that God, the Fountain of holiness, is directly the author of sin. For he has joined pleasure, in a way that cannot be separated, to the use of the created things we need to sustain the life he has given us.
0.56	So this can never be what his command means.
0.57	Our blessed Lord and his apostles tell us what it really means. They say it so often and so plainly that it cannot be misunderstood.
0.58	They all agree with one voice about what these statements truly mean: “The Lord your God is one Lord.” “You shall have no other gods but me.” “You shall love the Lord your God with all your strength.” “You shall hold on to him.” “Your soul’s desire shall be for his name.” They mean nothing other than this: the one perfect Good shall be your one final goal.
0.59	You must desire one thing for its own sake: to enjoy the One who is All in All.
0.60	You must set one happiness before your souls: union with the One who made them. That means having “fellowship with the Father and the Son” and being joined to the Lord in one Spirit.
0.61	You are to follow one purpose to the end of time: enjoying God, now and in eternity.
0.62	Desire other things only as far as they lead to this.
0.63	Love created things only as they lead you to the Creator.
0.64	But in every step you take, let this be the glorious point where your view ends.
0.65	Let every feeling, thought, word, and action be placed under this goal.
0.66	Whatever you desire or fear, whatever you seek or avoid, whatever you think, say, or do—let it all aim at your happiness in God. He is the only Goal as well as the Source of your being.
0.67	13. Have no goal, no final goal, except God.
0.68	This is what our Lord says: “Only one thing is needed.” And if your eye is fixed only on this one thing, “your whole body will be full of light.”
0.69	This is what St. Paul says: “I do this one thing: I press on toward the goal for the prize of the high calling in Christ Jesus.”
0.70	This is what St. James says: “Wash your hands, you sinners, and make your hearts pure, you who are double-minded.”
0.71	This is what St. John says: “Do not love the world or the things in the world.
0.72	For everything in the world—the desires of the flesh, the desires of the eyes, and the pride of life—does not come from the Father, but from the world.”
0.73	Think of seeking happiness in any of these things. It may be the desire of the flesh, which pleasantly stirs the outward senses. It may be the desire of the eye, which delights the imagination with what is new, great, or beautiful. Or it may be the pride of life: show, grandeur, and power, or what usually comes with them, applause and admiration. Seeking happiness in these things “does not come from the Father.” It neither comes from the Father of spirits nor is approved by him. It comes “from the world.” It is the clear mark of those who refuse to let him rule over them.
0.74	II. 1. So I have looked closely at what the circumcision of the heart is that will receive God’s praise.
0.75	Second, I will mention some thoughts that naturally come from this study. They give a plain rule by which every person can judge whether he belongs to the world or to God.
0.76	First, it is clear from what has been said that no one has a right to God’s praise unless his heart is circumcised by humility. He must be little, lowly, and vile in his own eyes. He must be deeply convinced of the inborn “corruption of his nature,” “by which he has gone very far from original righteousness.” He is inclined to all evil and turned away from all good; he is corrupt and detestable. He has a “fleshly mind that is an enemy of God. It is not subject to God’s law, and in fact it cannot be.” He must feel constantly, deep in his soul, that without the Spirit of God resting on him, he cannot think, desire, say, or do anything good or pleasing in God’s sight.
0.77	I say again: no one has a right to God’s praise until he feels his need of God. And not until he seeks the “honor that comes from God alone.” He must neither desire nor pursue the honor that comes from people, except only as far as it leads to this.
0.78	2. Another truth naturally follows from what has been said. No one will receive the honor that comes from God unless his heart is circumcised by faith, a “faith that is God’s own work.” He must refuse to be led any longer by his senses, appetites, or passions. He must not even be led by natural reason, that blind leader of the blind which the world idolizes. Instead he lives and walks by faith. He takes every step as one “seeking him who is invisible.” He “does not look at the things that are seen, which are temporary, but at the things that are not seen, which are eternal.” He governs all his desires, plans, and thoughts, all his actions and conduct, as one who has entered behind the veil, where Jesus sits at the right hand of God.
0.79	3. Some people spend much of their time and effort laying another foundation. It would be good if they knew this faith better. They base religion on the eternal fitness of things, on the inner excellence of virtue, and on the beauty of the actions that come from it. They also base it on what they call the reasons of good and evil, and on how beings relate to each other.
0.80	These explanations of the basis of Christian duty either agree with Scripture or they do not.
0.81	If they agree, why confuse well-meaning people and draw them away from the more important matters of the law with a cloud of terms? These terms explain the simplest truths until they become obscure.
0.82	If they do not agree, then these people should consider who the author of this new teaching is. Is he likely to be an angel from heaven who preaches a gospel different from that of Christ Jesus? Even if he were, God, not we, has already pronounced his sentence: “Let him be cursed.”
0.83	4. Our gospel knows no other foundation for good works than faith, and no other foundation for faith than Christ. So it clearly tells us something. We are not his disciples if we deny that he is the Author of both our faith and our works. Nor are we his disciples if we deny that his Spirit inspires them and makes them perfect.
0.84	“Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him.”
0.85	Only he can give life to those who are dead to God. Only he can breathe into them the breath of Christian life. He goes before them, walks with them, and follows them with his grace, so that their good desires lead to good results.
0.86	And “all who are led by the Spirit of God in this way are sons of God.”
0.87	This is God’s short and plain description of true religion and virtue. And “no one can lay any other foundation.”
0.88	5. Third, we can learn from what has been said that no one is truly “led by the Spirit” unless “the Spirit bears witness with his spirit that he is a child of God.” He must see the prize and the crown before him, and “rejoice in hope of the glory of God.”
0.89	So those who have taught that we should not think of our own happiness when we serve God are badly mistaken!
0.90	No! God often and clearly teaches us to “look to the reward.” He teaches us to weigh our hard work against the “joy set before us,” and these “light troubles” against that “far greater weight of glory.”
0.91	Yes, we are “strangers to the covenant of promise” and “without God in the world” until God, “in his great mercy, has given us new birth into a living hope of an inheritance that cannot decay, is not defiled, and does not fade away.”
0.92	6. But if this is true, it is high time for certain people to be honest with their own souls. They are far from finding in themselves this joyful assurance that they meet the terms of that covenant and will receive its promises. In fact, they argue with the covenant itself and speak evil of its terms. They complain that the terms are too strict, and that no one ever did or ever will live up to them.
0.93	What is this but accusing God of being a hard Master, who demands more from his servants than he enables them to do? It is as if he mocked the helpless works of his hands by binding them to do the impossible. It is as if he commanded them to overcome when neither their own strength nor grace was enough for them.
0.94	7. These blasphemers might almost convince people at the opposite extreme that they are not guilty. Those people hope to keep God’s commands without making any effort at all.
0.95	What a vain hope! A child of Adam expects to see the kingdom of Christ and of God without striving and struggling first “to enter through the narrow gate.” He was “conceived and born in sin,” and his “inner parts are very wicked.” Yet he thinks he can be “made pure as his Lord is pure” without walking in his steps and “taking up his cross daily.” He thinks so without “cutting off his right hand” and “plucking out his right eye and throwing it away.” He dreams of getting rid of his old opinions, passions, and attitudes, and of being “made holy through and through in spirit, soul, and body”—without a constant, continuing life of self-denial in everything!
0.96	8. What less than this can we conclude from the words of St. Paul quoted above? For Christ’s sake he lived “in weaknesses, in insults, in needs, in persecutions, in troubles.” He was full of “signs, and wonders, and mighty works.” He had been “caught up to the third heaven.” Yet, as a recent writer strongly puts it, he believed that without this constant self-denial all his virtues would be unsafe, and even his salvation would be in danger.
0.97	“I run,” he says, “but not without a clear aim. I fight, but not like someone beating the air.” With these words he plainly teaches us that whoever does not run like this, and does not deny himself daily like this, is running without a clear aim. He fights as uselessly as someone who “beats the air.”
0.98	9. Lastly, we can conclude from what has been said that a person whose heart is not circumcised by love talks just as uselessly about “fighting the fight of faith.” He hopes just as vainly to win the crown that never decays.
0.99	Love cuts off the desires of the flesh, the desires of the eye, and the pride of life. It engages the whole person, body, soul, and spirit, in the eager pursuit of that one goal. Love is so essential to a child of God that without it, anyone who lives is counted as dead in God’s sight.
0.100	“If I speak with the tongues of people and of angels, but do not have love, I am like sounding brass or a tinkling cymbal.
0.101	If I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so that I can move mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing.”
0.102	Even more, “if I give all I own to feed the poor, and give my body to be burned, but do not have love, it does me no good.”
0.103	10. So here is the sum of the perfect law. This is the true circumcision of the heart.
0.104	Let the spirit return to God who gave it, with all its affections following after it.
0.105	“Let all the rivers flow back again to the place they came from.
0.106	He did not want other sacrifices from us. But he has chosen the living sacrifice of the heart.
0.107	Let it be offered up to God continually through Christ, in flames of holy love.
0.108	And let no created thing be allowed to share it with him. For he is a jealous God.
0.109	He will not share his throne with anyone else. He will reign without a rival.
0.110	Let no plan and no desire be allowed there unless its final goal is God.
0.111	This is the way those children of God once walked. Though they are dead, they still speak to us.” “Do not desire to live except to praise his name. Let all your thoughts, words, and actions lead to his glory.
0.112	Set your heart firmly on him, and on other things only as they are in him and come from him.
0.113	Let your soul be so completely filled with love for him that you love nothing except for his sake.”
0.114	“Have a pure intention of heart. Keep his glory steadily in view in everything you do.”
0.115	“Fix your eyes on the blessed hope of your calling, and make everything in the world serve it.”
0.116	For then, and only then, do we have that “mind in us which was also in Christ Jesus.” That is when, in every movement of our heart, in every word of our tongue, and in every work of our hands, we “pursue nothing except in relation to him, and under what pleases him.” It is when we, too, neither think, nor speak, nor act to do our “own will, but the will of him who sent us.” It is when, “whether we eat or drink, or whatever we do, we do it all for the glory of God.”
