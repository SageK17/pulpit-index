T:0	The Prologue
0.1	Holy brothers and sisters, you remember that we usually preach on the Gospel of John, as it is read to us in order, lesson by lesson. But now the holy festival days have come in between. On these days certain readings must be read in the Church. They come around every year, and they cannot be anything other than what they are. So for a little while we have to interrupt the order we started. We are not dropping it completely.
0.2	So I was thinking about what part of the Scriptures I could preach on with you this week, as the Lord gives me grace. I wanted something that fits the joy of these days, and something we could finish in these seven or eight days. Then the letter of blessed John came to my mind. We have stopped reading his Gospel for a while, but by preaching on his letter we will not leave his side. There is another good reason too. This letter is very sweet to everyone whose heart has a healthy taste for the Bread of God. It is very much worth remembering in God’s holy Church. And above all, it praises love.
0.3	He says many things, and nearly all of them are about love.
0.4	If someone already has inside him what he is about to hear, he cannot help being glad when he hears it.
0.5	For that person, this reading will be like oil poured on a flame. If there is something there to feed, it gets fed, and it grows, and it lasts.
0.6	For others, it should be like a flame put to wood. If someone was not burning before, the words he hears may set him on fire.
0.7	In some people, what is already there gets fed. In others, it gets lit, if it was not there before. And so we can all be glad together in one love.
0.8	But where there is love, there is peace. And where there is humility, there is love.
0.9	Now let us listen to John himself. And whatever the Lord puts in our minds as we hear his words, let us tell you that too, so that you can understand it well.
T:1	Homily 1 (1 John 1:1–2:11)
1.1	“What was there from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our own eyes, what our hands have touched—this is about the Word of life. The life appeared. We have seen it, and we are witnesses to it. We announce to you that eternal life, which was with the Father and appeared to us. We tell you what we have seen and heard, so that you also can share in fellowship with us. And we want our fellowship to be with the Father and with His Son, Jesus Christ.
1.2	We write these things to you so that your joy may be complete.
1.3	This is the message we have heard from Him, and we pass it on to you: God is light, and in Him there is no darkness at all.
1.4	If we say we have fellowship with Him but keep walking in darkness, we are lying, and we are not doing what is true. But if we walk in the light, as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another. And the blood of His Son Jesus Christ will wash us clean from all sin.
1.5	If we say we have no sin, we are lying to ourselves, and the truth is not in us.
1.6	If we confess our sins, He can be trusted, and He is just. He will forgive our sins and wash away all our wrongdoing.
1.7	If we say we have not sinned, we make Him out to be a liar, and His word is not in us.
1.8	My little children, I write these things to you to keep you from sinning.
1.9	But if anyone sins, we have a defender who speaks for us to the Father: Jesus Christ, the righteous one. He is the sacrifice that wins forgiveness for our sins. And not just for ours, but also for the sins of the whole world.
1.10	This is how we know Him: if we keep His commandments.
1.11	Whoever says he knows Him but does not keep His commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in him.
1.12	But whoever keeps His word truly has the love of God made complete in him.
1.13	This is how we know that we are in Him: if we are perfect in Him.
1.14	Whoever says he stays in Him ought to walk just the way He walked.
1.15	Beloved, the commandment I am writing to you is not new. It is an old commandment, one you have had since the beginning.
1.16	The old commandment is the message you have heard.
1.17	Then again, I am writing you a new commandment. It is true in Him and in you. For the darkness is gone, and now the true light shines.
1.18	Whoever says he is in the light but hates his brother is still in darkness, even now.
1.19	Whoever loves his brother stays in the light, and there is nothing in him that makes anyone stumble.
1.20	But whoever hates his brother is in darkness and walks around in darkness. He does not know where he is going, because the darkness has made his eyes blind.”
1.21	1. “What was there from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what our hands have touched—this is about the word of life.”
1.22	Who could touch the Word with his hands? It was only possible because “the Word became flesh and lived in us.”
1.23	This Word became flesh so that He could be touched. He began to be flesh from the Virgin Mary. But the Word Himself did not begin then. For the apostle says, “What was there from the beginning.”
1.24	See how his letter backs up his Gospel. You heard it read recently: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God.”
1.25	Someone might think that “about the word of life” is just a way of speaking about Christ. He might think it does not mean the actual body of Christ that hands touched.
1.26	But look at what comes next: “And the Life appeared.”
1.27	So Christ is “the word of life.”
1.28	And how did it appear? It was there “from the beginning,” but it had not yet been shown to human beings. It had been shown to the angels, though. They saw it and fed on it as their bread.
1.29	But what does the Scripture say?
1.30	“Human beings ate the bread of angels.”
1.31	So “the Life appeared” in the flesh. It was put out in the open, so that something that can only be seen with the heart could also be seen with the eyes. And the purpose was to heal our hearts.
1.32	The Word can only be seen with the heart. But flesh can be seen with our physical eyes too.
1.33	We had eyes that could see flesh, but we had nothing that could see the Word. So “the Word became flesh,” something we could see. That way, the part of us that could see the Word would be healed.
1.34	2. “And we have seen and are witnesses.”
1.35	Some of our brothers and sisters do not know Greek. So maybe they do not know what the Greek word for “witnesses” is. Yet it is a word everyone uses, and people say it with deep respect. What we call “witnesses” in our language is “martyrs” in Greek.
1.36	Is there anyone who has not heard of martyrs? Is there any Christian who does not say the word “martyrs” every day? If only it lived in our hearts as well as on our lips! Then we would copy the sufferings of the martyrs instead of persecuting them with our drinking cups!
1.37	So “We have seen and are witnesses” means the same as “We have seen and are martyrs.”
1.38	The martyrs bore witness to what they had seen. They also bore witness to what they had heard from those who had seen. Their testimony made the people they spoke against angry. That is why the martyrs suffered everything they suffered.
1.39	The martyrs are God’s witnesses.
1.40	God was pleased to have human beings as His witnesses, so that human beings could also have God as their witness.
1.41	“We have seen,” he says, “and are witnesses.”
1.42	Where did they see?
1.43	Out in plain view. What does “in plain view” mean?
1.44	In the sun—that is, in this daylight.
1.45	But how could the One who made the sun be seen in the sun? Only in the way the Scripture says: “He set His tent in the sun. And He Himself came out like a bridegroom from his room, and rejoiced like a giant to run His race.”
1.46	He existed before the sun, and He made the sun. He existed before the morning star, before all the stars, before all the angels. He is the true Creator (“for all things were made by Him, and without Him nothing was made”). He wanted to be seen by human eyes, the eyes that see the sun. So He set His tent in the sun. That is, He showed His flesh out in plain view, in this daylight. And the Bridegroom’s room was the Virgin’s womb. For in that virgin womb two were joined together, the Bridegroom and the bride. The Bridegroom was the Word, and the bride was the flesh. As it is written, “And the two shall become one flesh.” And the Lord says in the Gospel, “So they are no longer two, but one flesh.
1.47	And Isaiah knows very well that they are two. Speaking as Christ, he says, “He put a headdress on me like a bridegroom, and decorated me with jewelry like a bride.”
1.48	One person seems to be speaking, and yet He makes Himself both Bridegroom and Bride. That is because they are “not two, but one flesh.” It is because “the Word became flesh and lived in us.”
1.49	The Church is joined to that flesh. And so the whole Christ is formed, Head and body together.
1.50	3. “And we are witnesses, and we announce to you that eternal life which was with the Father and was shown to us.” That means it was shown among us. Or, to say it more plainly, it was shown to us.
1.51	So, “what we have seen and heard, we announce to you.”
1.52	They saw the Lord Himself there in the flesh. They heard words from the Lord’s own mouth. And they told them to us.
1.53	So we too have heard, but we have not seen.
1.54	Does that make us less happy than those who saw and heard?
1.55	Then how can he add, “So that you also can share in fellowship with us”?
1.56	They saw, and we have not seen. Yet we are their partners, because we share the same faith.
1.57	There was one disciple who did not believe even when he saw. He insisted on touching, so that he could believe. He said, “I will not believe unless I push my fingers into the place of the nails and touch His scars.”
1.58	The Lord always lets the angels see Him. But for a short time He let human hands touch Him. That disciple did touch Him, and he cried out, “My Lord and my God!”
1.59	He touched the Man, and so he confessed the God.
1.60	And the Lord wanted to comfort us. Now that He sits in heaven, we cannot touch Him with our hands. We can only reach Him by faith. So He said to that disciple, “You believe because you have seen. Blessed are those who have not seen and still believe.”
1.61	That describes us. We are the ones He meant.
1.62	So let that blessing come true in us, just as the Lord said it would. Let us hold firmly to what we do not see, because those who did see have told us.
1.63	“So that you also,” he says, “can share in fellowship with us.”
1.64	But is it such a great thing to have fellowship with human beings?
1.65	Do not look down on it. See what he adds: “and so that our fellowship may be with God the Father and with Jesus Christ His Son.
1.66	We write these things to you,” he says, “so that your joy may be complete.”
1.67	He means complete joy in that fellowship, in that love, in that unity.
1.68	4. “And this is the message we have heard from Him, and we announce it to you.”
1.69	What is this message? These same people saw the Word of life and touched Him with their hands. He “was there from the beginning.” For a time He was made visible and touchable—the only Son of God.
1.70	Why did He come? What new thing did He tell us?
1.71	What did He want to teach?
1.72	Why did He do what He did? Why did the Word become flesh? Why did “God over all things” put up with insults from people? Why did He let Himself be slapped in the face by hands He Himself had made?
1.73	What did He want to teach?
1.74	What did He want to show?
1.75	What did He want to announce?
1.76	Let us listen. If we do not get the fruit of His teaching, then hearing the story of how Christ was born and how Christ suffered is just entertainment for the mind. It does not make the mind stronger.
1.77	What great thing are you hearing?
1.78	Make sure you hear it in a way that bears fruit.
1.79	What did He want to teach?
1.80	What did He want to announce? Listen. “God is light,” he says, “and there is no darkness in Him at all.”
1.81	So far he has named the light, but his words are still dark. It would be good for us if the very light he named would shine into our hearts, so that we could see what he means.
1.82	This is what we announce: “God is light, and there is no darkness in Him at all.”
1.83	Who would dare to say there is darkness in God?
1.84	But what is this light?
1.85	And what is this darkness? We must not think he is talking about the kind of things these eyes of ours can see.
1.86	“God is light.” Someone will say, “The sun is light too. The moon is light too. A candle is light.”
1.87	But this light must be something far greater than these, far better, far higher.
1.88	God is far above what He created. The Maker is far above what He made. Wisdom is far above the things Wisdom made. In the same way, this light must be far above everything.
1.89	And maybe we will come near to it if we learn what this light is and draw close to it, so that it can shine on us. In ourselves we are darkness. Only when it shines on us can we become light. Then it will not put us to shame, the way we are put to shame by ourselves.
1.90	Who is put to shame by himself?
1.91	The person who knows he is a sinner.
1.92	Who is not put to shame by the light?
1.93	The person the light shines on.
1.94	What does it mean to have the light shine on you?
1.95	It means this. A person now sees that sin has made him dark. He wants the light to shine on him, so he comes close to it. That is why the Psalm says, “Come close to Him, and let His light shine on you, and your faces will not be ashamed.”
1.96	The light will show you to yourself, and you will see how dirty you are. If your own dirtiness then displeases you, the light will not put you to shame. And you will be able to see how beautiful it is.
1.97	This is what He wanted to teach.
1.98	5. But are we saying this too quickly?
1.99	Let the apostle himself make it clear in what comes next.
1.100	Remember what I said at the start: this letter praises love. “God is light,” he says, “and there is no darkness in Him at all.”
1.101	And what did he say before that?
1.102	“So that you can have fellowship with us, and our fellowship may be with God the Father and with His Son Jesus Christ.”
1.103	So think about it. “God is light, and there is no darkness in Him at all, and we must have fellowship with Him.” Then the darkness must be driven out of us too, so that light can be created in us. For darkness cannot have fellowship with light.
1.104	That is why he goes on: “If we say we have fellowship with Him but walk in darkness, we are lying.”
1.105	The apostle Paul says it too: “What fellowship can light have with darkness?”
1.106	You say you have fellowship with God, but you walk in darkness. “And God is light, and there is no darkness in Him at all.” So how can light and darkness have fellowship?
1.107	At this point a person might say to himself: What am I going to do? How can I become light?
1.108	I live in sin and wrongdoing.
1.109	A kind of hopelessness and sadness creeps over him.
1.110	There is no salvation except in fellowship with God.
1.111	“God is light, and there is no darkness in Him at all.”
1.112	But sins are darkness. The apostle says this about the devil and his angels: they are “rulers of this darkness.”
1.113	He would not call them rulers of darkness unless he meant rulers of sins, who have power over wicked people.
1.114	So what are we to do, my brothers and sisters?
1.115	We must have fellowship with God. There is no other hope of eternal life. But “God is light, and there is no darkness in Him at all.” And our sins are darkness. Our sins weigh us down, so that we cannot have fellowship with God. So what hope do we have?
1.116	Didn’t I promise that during these days I would say something that would make you glad?
1.117	If I don’t keep that promise, this is sad.
1.118	“God is light, and there is no darkness in Him at all.” Sins are darkness. What will happen to us?
1.119	Let us listen and see if He will comfort us, lift us up, and give us hope, so that we do not give up on the way.
1.120	For we are running, and we are running home to our own country. If we give up hope of getting there, that very hopelessness will make us fail.
1.121	But He wants us to get there, so that He can keep us safe in our own land. So He feeds us along the way.
1.122	So let us listen: “If we say we have fellowship with Him but walk in darkness, we are lying, and we are not doing what is true.”
1.123	Let us not claim to have fellowship with Him if we are walking in darkness.
1.124	“If we walk in the light, as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another.”
1.125	Let us walk in the light, as He is in the light, so that we can have fellowship with Him.
1.126	But what are we to do about our sins?
1.127	Listen to what comes next: “And the blood of His Son Jesus Christ will wash us clean from all sin.”
1.128	What great confidence God has given us!
1.129	We have good reason to celebrate the Passover, when the Lord’s blood was shed! By that blood we are made clean “from all sin”!
1.130	We can be sure of this. The devil held “the written record that was against us,” the contract that made us slaves. But the blood of Christ has wiped it out.
1.131	“The blood of His Son,” he says, “will wash us clean from all sin.”
1.132	What does “from all sin” mean?
1.133	Look! Just now, in the name of Christ, all the sins of these people here have been washed away. They have just confessed Christ, and they are called “infants.”
1.134	They came in old, and they went out new.
1.135	What do I mean, they came in old and went out new?
1.136	They came in as old men, and they went out as babies.
1.137	The old life is old age, with all its foolishness. But the new life is the babyhood of being born again.
1.138	But what are we to do?
1.139	Our past sins have been forgiven—not only theirs, but ours too. But after all our sins were forgiven and wiped away, we have gone on living in this world, surrounded by temptations. So perhaps we have picked up some new sins.
1.140	So let each person do what he can. Let him admit what he is, so that he can be healed by the One who always is what He is. For He always was and always is. We once did not exist, and now we do.
1.141	6. For look at what He says: “If we say we have no sin, we are lying to ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
1.142	So if you have admitted that you are a sinner, the truth is in you. For the Truth itself is light.
1.143	Your life does not yet shine with perfect brightness, because there are sins in you. But you have already started to be lit up, because you confess your sins.
1.144	For look at what comes next: “If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just. He will forgive us our sins and wash us clean from all wrongdoing.”
1.145	This means not only our past sins, but also any we may have picked up in this life. For as long as a person lives in this body, he cannot avoid having at least some light sins.
1.146	But these sins that we call light—do not take them lightly.
1.147	If you think little of them when you weigh them, be afraid when you count them.
1.148	Many light sins add up to one huge sin. Many drops fill a river. Many grains make a heap.
1.149	So what hope is there?
1.150	First of all, confession. No one should think he is righteous. God sees what truly is. A human being once did not exist, and now he does. So he must not proudly lift up his neck before God’s eyes.
1.151	So first, confession. Then, love. For what is said about love?
1.152	“Love covers a great many sins.”
1.153	Now let us see whether he praises love because of the sins that catch up with us later. For only love puts out sins.
1.154	Pride puts out love. So humility makes love strong. And love puts out sins.
1.155	Humility goes together with confession. It is the humility that makes us admit we are sinners. This is humility: not saying it with our tongue as if only to avoid looking proud, so that people won’t be annoyed with us if we say we are righteous.
1.156	That is what ungodly people do, people who have lost their senses. They say, “I know I am righteous, but what should I say in front of people?
1.157	If I call myself righteous, who will put up with it? Who will stand it? Let God know that I am righteous. But I will say I am a sinner, just so people won’t hate me for being proud.”
1.158	Tell people what you are. Tell God what you are. For if you do not tell God what you are, God will condemn what He finds in you.
1.159	Do you want Him not to condemn you?
1.160	Then condemn yourself. Do you want Him to forgive you? Then admit your sin, so that you can say to God, “Turn Your face away from my sins.”
1.161	Say to Him also these words from the same Psalm: “For I admit my wrongdoing.”
1.162	“If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just. He will forgive us our sins and wash us clean from all wrongdoing.
1.163	If we say we have not sinned, we make Him out to be a liar, and His word is not in us.”
1.164	If you say, “I have not sinned,” you make Him a liar, while you are trying to make yourself truthful.
1.165	How could God be a liar and a human being be truthful? The Scripture says the opposite: “Every human being is a liar; God alone is true.”
1.166	So God is true by Himself. You are true through God. By yourself you are a liar.
1.167	7. Someone might think he had given people permission to sin without punishment, because he said, “He is faithful and just to wash us clean from all wrongdoing.” Then people might say to themselves from now on, “Let’s sin! Let’s do whatever we want without worrying! Christ washes us clean. He is faithful and just. He washes us clean from all wrongdoing.” So He takes away your bad feeling of safety and puts in a fear that is good for you.
1.168	You would like to feel safe, but it would hurt you. You need to be careful and concerned.
1.169	For “He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” only if you always stay unhappy with yourself and keep changing until you are made perfect.
1.170	So what comes next? “My little children, I write these things to you to keep you from sinning.”
1.171	But maybe sin catches up with us because of this mortal life. What should we do then?
1.172	What? Should we give up hope now?
1.173	Listen: “And if anyone sins, we have a defender with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous one. He is the one who wins forgiveness for our sins.”
1.174	So He is the defender. You do your best not to sin. But if sin catches you because this life is weak, deal with it right away. Right away, be unhappy with it. Right away, condemn it. And when you have condemned it, you can come to the Judge with confidence.
1.175	There you have your defender. Don’t be afraid that confessing will make you lose your case.
1.176	In this life, people often hand their case over to a skilled speaker, and they don’t lose. You are handing yourself over to the Word. Will you lose?
1.177	Cry out, “We have a defender with the Father!”
1.178	8. Look how humble John himself is.
1.179	He was certainly a righteous and great man. From the Lord’s chest he drank in the secrets of His mysteries. By drinking from the Lord’s chest he wrote about His divine nature: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God.” Yet even a man like this does not say, “You have a defender with the Father.” Instead he says, “If anyone sins, we have a defender.”
1.180	He does not say “you have.” He does not say “you have me.” He does not even say “you have Christ Himself.” He puts Christ forward, not himself. And he says “we have,” not “you have.”
1.181	He preferred to count himself among the sinners, so that he could have Christ as his defender. He did not want to put himself in Christ’s place as the defender, and be found among the proud people who will be condemned.
1.182	Brothers and sisters, it is Jesus Christ the righteous one whom we have as our defender with the Father. “He,” He Himself, “is the sacrifice that wins forgiveness for our sins.”
1.183	Whoever holds on to this has not started a heresy. Whoever holds on to this has not caused a split in the Church.
1.184	Where did splits come from?
1.185	They came when people said, “We are righteous.” When people said, “We make the unclean holy. We make the ungodly righteous. We ask, and we receive.”
1.186	But what does John say?
1.187	“And if anyone sins, we have a defender with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous one.”
1.188	But someone will say, “Then don’t the saints pray for us?
1.189	Don’t bishops and leaders pray for the people?”
1.190	Yes, they do. But look at the Scriptures. You will see that leaders also ask the people to pray for them.
1.191	That is why the apostle says to the church, “Pray for us too.”
1.192	The apostle prays for the people, and the people pray for the apostle.
1.193	We pray for you, brothers and sisters. But you must pray for us too.
1.194	Let all the members pray for each other. And let the Head plead for everyone.
1.195	So it is no surprise that he goes on here to shut the mouths of those who divide the Church of God.
1.196	He has just said, “We have Jesus Christ the righteous one, and He is the sacrifice that wins forgiveness for our sins.” He was thinking of people who would split themselves off and say, “Look, here is Christ! Look, there He is!” They would try to show Him in just one part, though He bought the whole and owns the whole. So he goes right on to say, “Not only for our sins, but also for the sins of the whole world.”
1.197	What does this mean, brothers and sisters?
1.198	Certainly “we have found it in the fields of the forest.” We have found the Church in all nations.
1.199	Look! Christ “is the sacrifice that wins forgiveness for our sins, and not only ours, but also the sins of the whole world.”
1.200	Look, you have the Church all over the world. Do not follow false people who claim they make others righteous. The truth is, they cut people off.
1.201	Stay on that mountain that has filled the whole earth. For “Christ is the sacrifice that wins forgiveness for our sins, and not only ours, but also the sins of the whole world.” He bought that world with His blood.
1.202	9. “And this is how we know Him,” he says: “if we keep His commandments.”
1.203	Which commandments? “Whoever says, ‘I know Him,’ but does not keep His commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in him.”
1.204	But you are still asking, “Which commandments?”
1.205	“But whoever keeps His word,” he says, “truly has the love of God made complete in him.”
1.206	Let us see whether this very commandment is called love.
1.207	We were asking, “Which commandments?” And he says, “But whoever keeps His word truly has the love of God made complete in him.”
1.208	Look at the Gospel and see whether this is the commandment. “I give you a new commandment,” says the Lord: “love one another.” “This is how we know that we are in Him: if we are made perfect in Him.”
1.209	He calls them people made perfect in love. What is perfect love?
1.210	It is loving even your enemies, and loving them with this goal: that they may become your brothers.
1.211	For our love should not be an earthly, fleshly love.
1.212	It is good to wish someone well in this passing life. But even if that fails, let the soul be safe.
1.213	Do you wish life to someone who is your friend?
1.214	That is good. Are you glad when your enemy dies?
1.215	That is wrong. But maybe the life you want for your friend is not good for him. And maybe the death you are glad about was actually good for your enemy.
1.216	No one can be sure whether this present life is good for a person or not. But the life that is with God is good, without any doubt.
1.217	So love your enemies in a way that wishes for them to become your brothers. Love your enemies in a way that invites them into your fellowship.
1.218	That is how He loved. As He hung on the cross, He said, “Father, forgive them, because they do not know what they are doing.”
1.219	He did not say, “Father, let them live a long time. They are killing Me, but let them live.”
1.220	He was driving out of them the death that lasts forever and ever. He did it by His most merciful prayer and by His power, which is greater than all.
1.221	Many of them came to believe, and they were forgiven for shedding the blood of Christ.
1.222	At first they shed it in their rage. Later they drank it as believers.
1.223	“This is how we know that we are in Him: if we are made perfect in Him.”
1.224	The Lord spoke about this very perfection of loving our enemies. He urged us, “So be perfect, as your Father in heaven is perfect.”
1.225	“So whoever says he stays in Him ought to walk just the way He walked.”
1.226	How should we do this, brothers and sisters? What is he telling us to do?
1.227	“Whoever says he stays in Him”—that is, in Christ—“ought to walk just the way He walked.”
1.228	Does this mean we should walk on the sea?
1.229	Of course not!
1.230	No, it means we should walk in the way of righteousness.
1.231	What way is that? I have already told you.
1.232	He was nailed to the cross, and yet He was walking in this very way. This way is the way of love: “Father, forgive them, because they do not know what they are doing.”
1.233	So if you have learned to pray for your enemy, you are walking in the Lord’s way.
1.234	10. “My dear ones, I am not writing you a new commandment, but the old commandment you had from the beginning.”
1.235	Which commandment does he call “old”?
1.236	The one “you had,” he says, “from the beginning.”
1.237	So it is “old” in this sense: you have already heard it. Otherwise he would be contradicting the Lord, who says, “I give you a new commandment: love one another.”
1.238	But why is it an “old” commandment?
1.239	Not because it belongs to the old self.
1.240	Then why? “Which you had from the beginning.
1.241	The old commandment is the message you have heard.”
1.242	So it is old in this sense: you have already heard it.
1.243	And he shows that this same commandment is new. He says, “Then again, I am writing you a new commandment.”
1.244	It is not a different one. The same commandment he called old is also new.
1.245	Why? “It is true in Him and in you.”
1.246	Why is it old? You have already heard: because you already knew it.
1.247	But why is it new? “Because the darkness is gone, and now the true light shines.”
1.248	See, this is why it is new. The darkness belongs to the old self, but the light belongs to the new self.
1.249	What does the apostle Paul say?
1.250	“Take off the old self, and put on the new.”
1.251	And what else does he say?
1.252	“Once you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord.”
1.253	11. “Whoever says he is in the light”—now he is making clear everything he has been saying—“whoever says he is in the light but hates his brother is still in darkness, even now.”
1.254	What is this, my brothers and sisters? How long will we have to tell you, “Love your enemies”?
1.255	Look and see whether you are doing something even worse: hating your brothers.
1.256	If you loved only your brothers, you would not yet be perfect. But if you hate your brothers, what are you? Where are you?
1.257	Let each of you look into his own heart. Do not hold on to hatred against your brother because of some harsh word. Do not fight over earthly things and so become earth yourself.
1.258	Whoever hates his brother must not say that he walks in the light.
1.259	“Whoever says he is in the light but hates his brother is still in darkness, even now.”
1.260	For example, take a man who was a pagan and has become a Christian. Pay attention. Look, he was in darkness while he was a pagan. Now, from here on, he has become a Christian. Everyone happily says, “Thanks be to God!” The apostle’s words are read, where he joyfully says, “For once you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord.”
1.261	He used to worship idols; now he worships God. He used to worship things he had made; now he worships the One who made him.
1.262	He has changed. “Thanks be to God!” say all the Christians, as they joyfully welcome him.
1.263	Why? Because from now on he worships the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. And he hates demons and idols.
1.264	But John is still worried about our new convert. Many people welcome him with joy, but John still looks at him with concern.
1.265	Brothers and sisters, let us be glad for a mother’s worried care.
1.266	The mother has good reason to worry about us even while others are celebrating. By “the mother” I mean love. She lived in John’s heart when he spoke these words.
1.267	Why would he worry, unless there is something in us that he is afraid of, even while people now welcome us with joy?
1.268	What is he afraid of?
1.269	“Whoever says he is in the light”—what does this mean?
1.270	It means whoever says he is now a Christian—“but hates his brother is still in darkness, even now.”
1.271	This needs no explaining. We only need to be glad if it is not true of us, or to weep over it if it is.
