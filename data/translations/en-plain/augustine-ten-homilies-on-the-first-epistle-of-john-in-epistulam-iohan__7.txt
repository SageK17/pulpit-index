8.153	9. Look at all the great things pride does.
8.154	Keep this in your hearts: the things pride does look very much like the things love does. They seem almost equal.
8.155	Love feeds the hungry, and so does pride. Love does it so that God will be praised. Pride does it so that pride itself will be praised.
8.156	Love gives clothes to people who have none, and so does pride. Love fasts, and so does pride. Love buries the dead, and so does pride.
8.157	Love wants to do every kind of good work, and it does them. Pride goes after the very same things. You could say it keeps its horses running hard to keep up.
8.158	But love stands in the way and gives no room to pride, which is being driven badly. I don’t mean that pride drives badly. I mean that pride is being driven badly.
8.159	How terrible for the person whose chariot driver is pride! He is sure to crash headfirst!
8.160	But when good is being done, how can we tell that pride is not what is pushing us on? Who knows? Who can see it? Where is it? We only see the actions. Mercy feeds people, and pride feeds people too. Mercy welcomes strangers, and pride welcomes strangers too. Mercy speaks up for the poor, and pride speaks up for them too.
8.161	How can this be? When we look at the actions, we see no difference.
8.162	I dare to say something. But it is not me saying it; Paul said it. Love dies. That is, a person who has love declares the name of Christ and suffers as a martyr. Pride also declares His name, and it also suffers as a martyr.
8.163	One has love. The other does not have love.
8.164	But the person who does not have love should listen to the apostle: “If I give away everything I own to the poor, and if I give my body to be burned, but I do not have love, it does me no good at all.”
8.165	So the Bible calls us away from the face we show on the outside and points us to what is inside. It calls us away from this outer surface that people show off in front of others, and it points us to what is inside.
8.166	Go back to your own conscience and question it.
8.167	Don’t look at the flowers blooming on the outside. Look at what root is in the ground.
8.168	Is selfish desire rooted there?
8.169	Then there may be a show of good deeds, but there cannot be truly good works.
8.170	Is love rooted there?
8.171	Then don’t be afraid. Nothing evil can grow from that.
8.172	A proud person pats you gently. Love is strict.
8.173	One gives you clothes. The other hits you.
8.174	The proud person gives clothes to please people. Love hits to correct and discipline.
8.175	A blow from love is more welcome than a gift from pride.
8.176	So come inside, brothers and sisters. In everything you do, look to God, who is your witness.
8.177	Ask yourselves whether He sees what attitude you have when you do it.
8.178	If your heart does not accuse you of doing it to show off, that’s good. Don’t be afraid.
8.179	But when you do good, don’t be afraid that someone else will see you.
8.180	Be afraid of doing it just so that you will be praised. Let the other person see it, so that God will be praised.
8.181	If you hide it from people’s eyes, you also keep people from copying you. Then you take away from God the praise that belongs to Him.
8.182	When you give to the poor, you are giving to two people. Both are hungry. One is hungry for bread, and the other is hungry for righteousness.
8.183	You, the one doing the good deed, stand between these two starving people. As the Bible says, “Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled.” If love is doing the work, then while it helps one of them, it has pity on both. It wants to help both.
8.184	One person wants something to eat. The other wants someone to copy.
8.185	You feed the first one. Give yourself as an example to the second. Then you have given to both. The first one thanks you because you ended his hunger. The second one copies you because you set him an example.
8.186	10. So show mercy, as people with merciful hearts. When you love your enemies, you are also loving your brothers and sisters.
8.187	John keeps talking about loving our brothers and sisters. But don’t think that this means he gave no command about loving our enemies.
8.188	You love your brothers and sisters. “How,” you ask, “do we love our brothers and sisters?”
8.189	Let me ask you why you love an enemy.
8.190	Why do you love him?
8.191	So that he will be healthy in this life? What if that is not good for him?
8.192	So that he will be rich? What if his riches make him blind?
8.193	So that he will get married? What if his married life turns out bitter?
8.194	So that he will have children? What if they turn out bad?
8.195	So the things you seem to wish for your enemy when you love him are uncertain. They are all uncertain.
8.196	Wish for him to have eternal life with you. Wish for him to become your brother. Then, when you love him, you are loving a brother.
8.197	You don’t love him for what he is now. You love him for what you want him to become.
8.198	Dear friends, I told you once before, if I remember right, about a log of wood lying out in the open. A skilled worker saw the log. It was still rough, just as it had been cut in the forest. He liked it, and he wanted to make something out of it.
8.199	He did not love it so that it would stay that way forever.
8.200	His skill showed him what it would become. His liking was not about what it was. He liked it for the thing he would make out of it, not for the thing it was then.
8.201	That is how God loved us sinners.
8.202	We say that God loved sinners, because He says, “People who are well do not need the Doctor, but sick people do.”
8.203	Did He love us sinners so that we would stay sinners?
8.204	Our Carpenter saw us like rough wood from the forest. In His mind He saw the building He would make out of it, not the rough wood it was then.
8.205	It is the same when you see your enemy fighting against you. He is raging. He bites with his words. He provokes you with insults. He harasses you with hatred. But you pay attention to this one thing in him: he is a human being.
8.206	You see all these things aimed against you, and you see that a man did them. But you also see in him that God made him.
8.207	God made him a human being. But his hatred for you is his own doing. His ill will toward you is his own doing.
8.208	So what do you say in your mind?
8.209	“Lord, be merciful to him. Forgive his sins. Put fear into him. Change him.”
8.210	You don’t love him for what he is now. You love him for what you want him to become.
8.211	So when you love an enemy, you are loving a brother.
8.212	That is why perfect love means loving an enemy. And this perfect love is found in love for our brothers and sisters.
8.213	No one should say that the apostle John asked a little less of us, and the Lord Christ asked a little more.
8.214	John told us to love our brothers and sisters. Christ told us to love even our enemies.
8.215	Notice why Christ told you to love your enemies.
8.216	So that they would stay enemies forever?
8.217	If that were why He told you, so that they would stay enemies, then you would be hating them, not loving them.
8.218	Notice how He Himself loved. He did not want them to keep on persecuting Him as they were. So He said, “Father, forgive them, because they don’t know what they are doing.”
8.219	Those He wanted forgiven, He wanted changed. And those He wanted changed, He was willing to turn from enemies into brothers. And He really did make them brothers.
8.220	He was killed. He was buried. He rose again. He went up into heaven. He sent the Holy Spirit to His disciples. They began to preach His name boldly. They did miracles in the name of the One who was crucified and killed. The very people who had killed the Lord saw them. And those who had shed His blood in anger came to believe, and so they drank that blood.
8.221	11. I have said these things, brothers and sisters, and I have said them at some length. But love needed to be urged on you more strongly, dear friends, and this was the way to do it.
8.222	If there is no love in you, then we have said nothing.
8.223	But if love is in you, it is as if we have poured oil on a fire.
8.224	And in people who did not have it, perhaps our words have lit it.
8.225	In one person, the love that was there has grown. In another, love that was not there has begun.
8.226	So we have said these things so that you will not be slow to love your enemies.
8.227	Is someone raging at you? He rages; you pray. He hates; you feel pity.
8.228	It is the fever in his soul that hates you. He will get well, and then he will thank you.
8.229	How do doctors love sick people?
8.230	Do they love them for being sick?
8.231	If they loved them for being sick, they would want them to stay sick forever.
8.232	This is why they love sick people: not so that they will stay sick, but so that they will go from being sick to being well.
8.233	And doctors often have to put up with so much from patients who are out of their minds!
8.234	What insults they hear! Very often the patients even hit them.
8.235	The doctor fights the fever and forgives the person.
8.236	What shall I say, brothers and sisters? Does he love his enemy?
8.237	No, he hates his enemy, which is the disease. That is what he hates. But he loves the person who hits him. He hates the fever.
8.238	Who or what is hitting him? The disease, the sickness, the fever.
8.239	He takes away the thing that is fighting him, so that what remains is the person who will thank him.
8.240	You should do the same. If your enemy hates you, and hates you unfairly, know that desire for the things of the world is ruling him. That is why he hates you.
8.241	If you hate him too, then you are paying back evil with evil.
8.242	What good does it do to pay back evil with evil?
8.243	I was weeping over one sick man who hated you. Now I will be weeping over you too, if you hate as well.
8.244	But he attacks your property. He takes some of the things you own here on earth. So you hate him, because he squeezes you in here on earth.
8.245	Don’t let yourself be squeezed. Move yourself up to heaven. There your heart will be in a place with plenty of room, so that you won’t feel squeezed in your hope of eternal life.
8.246	Think about what he is taking from you. He could not take even these things unless he was allowed to by the One who “whips every son He welcomes.”
8.247	This enemy of yours is, in a way, a tool in God’s hands for healing you.
8.248	If God knows it is good for you to be robbed by him, God allows it. If He knows it is good for you to be hit, He allows him to hit you. God is taking care of you through him. So wish for him to be made well.
8.249	12. “No one has ever seen God.”
8.250	Look, dear friends: “If we love each other, God will live in us, and His love will be made perfect in us.”
8.251	Start to love, and you will be made perfect.
8.252	Have you started to love?
8.253	Then God has started to live in you. Love Him who has started to live in you, so that by living in you more fully He may make you perfect.
8.254	“This is how we know that we live in Him and He lives in us: He has given us some of His Spirit.”
8.255	That’s good! Thanks be to God!
8.256	We come to know that He lives in us.
8.257	But how do we come to know this very fact, that we really do know He lives in us?
8.258	Because John himself has said it: “Because He has given us some of His Spirit.”
8.259	But how do we know that He has given us some of His Spirit?
8.260	This very thing, that He has given you some of His Spirit, how do you come to know it?
8.261	Ask your own heart, deep inside. If it is full of love, you have the Spirit of God.
8.262	How do we know that this is how you know the Spirit of God lives in you?
8.263	“Because God’s love has been poured into our hearts by the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us.”
8.264	13. “And we have seen, and we are witnesses, that God has sent His Son to be the Savior of the world.”
8.265	Calm your minds, you who are sick. Such a great Doctor has come. Are you still going to give up hope?
8.266	The diseases were terrible. The wounds could not be cured. The sickness was hopeless.
8.267	Do you notice how bad your sickness is, but not notice that the Doctor is all-powerful?
8.268	You feel hopeless, but He is all-powerful. His witnesses are the people who were healed first and who tell others about the Doctor. Yet even they are healed in hope more than in reality.
8.269	That is what the apostle says: “For we are saved by hope.”
8.270	So we have started to be made well by faith. But our healing will be complete “when what decays has put on what cannot decay, and what dies has put on what cannot die.”
8.271	This is hope, not the reality.
8.272	But whoever is glad in hope will also hold on to the reality. And whoever does not have the hope will not be able to reach the reality.
8.273	14. “Whoever declares that Jesus is the Son of God, God lives in him and he lives in God.”
8.274	We can say it in a few words now. “Whoever declares”: not in words but in actions, not with the tongue but with the life.
8.275	Many people declare it with their words but deny it with their actions. “And we have known and believed the love that God has in us.”
8.276	And again, how have you come to know this?
8.277	“Love is God.” He already said it earlier. Look, he says it again.
8.278	Love could not be praised to you any more highly than by being called God.
8.279	Maybe you were ready to look down on a gift from God.
8.280	But will you look down on God?
8.281	“Love is God. Whoever lives in love lives in God, and God lives in him.”
8.282	Each one lives in the other: the One who holds, and the one who is held.
8.283	You live in God, but so that you may be held. God lives in you, but so that He may hold you and keep you from falling.
8.284	Don’t imagine that you become God’s house in the same way that your house holds up your body. If the house you live in falls out from under you, you fall. But if you pull away, God does not fall.
8.285	When you leave Him, He is not any less. When you come back to Him, He is not any greater.
8.286	You are healed, but you give Him nothing. You are made clean, you are made new, you are set straight. He is medicine for the sick, a straight ruler for what is crooked, light for those in darkness, and a home for those who are abandoned.
8.287	So everything is given to you. Be careful not to imagine that God gains anything when you come to Him. No, He does not even gain a slave.
8.288	Will God really be left without servants if you don’t want to serve, or if nobody wants to?
8.289	God does not need servants. The servants need God. That is why the psalm says, “I said to the Lord, You are my God.”
8.290	He is the true Lord.
8.291	And what does the psalm say?
8.292	“For You don’t need my good things.”
8.293	But you need the good you get from your servant.
8.294	Your servant needs the good he gets from you, so that you will feed him. And you need the good you get from your servant, so that he will help you.
8.295	You can’t draw water for yourself. You can’t cook for yourself. You can’t run ahead of your horse. You can’t take care of your animal.
8.296	You see that you need the good you get from your servant. You need his help.
8.297	So you are not a true master, because you need someone below you.
8.298	The true Lord is the One who wants nothing from us. And how terrible for us if we do not look for Him!
8.299	He wants nothing from us. Yet He looked for us when we were not looking for Him.
8.300	One sheep had wandered off. He found it and carried it back on His shoulders, full of joy.
8.301	Did the Shepherd need the sheep? Or did the sheep need the Shepherd? The more I love to talk about love, the less I want this letter to end.
8.302	No one is more passionate in praising love.
8.303	Nothing sweeter is preached to you. Nothing healthier is drunk by you. But this is true only if you make God’s gift strong in you by living a godly life.
8.304	Don’t be ungrateful for His great grace. He had one only Son, but He did not want Him to be the only Son. He wanted Him to have brothers. So He adopted as His own those who would share eternal life with Him.

9.1	“This is how love is made perfect in us, so that we can be confident on the day of judgment: because as He is, so are we in this world.

9.72	I have already explained to you that “as” does not always mean “equal to.” Sometimes it means “somewhat like.”
9.73	Think about how you say, “My picture has ears just as I do.”
9.74	Is it exactly the same? And yet you still say “just as.”
9.75	So if we were made in God’s image, why are we not like God?
9.76	Not equal to Him, but like Him in our own measure.

9.100	But once love starts to live there, the fear that got the place ready is driven out.
9.101	As love grows, fear shrinks. The more love comes inside, the more fear is driven out.
9.102	More love, less fear. Less love, more fear.

9.104	Think of sewing. The thread is pulled in by the bristle. The bristle goes in first, but unless it comes out again, the thread cannot take its place. In the same way, fear first fills the mind. But fear does not stay there, because it only comes in to bring love in.

9.110	Something in it itches and stings.

9.112	“You turned my mourning into joy for me. You took off my sackcloth and dressed me with gladness, so that my glory may sing to You and I will not be stung.”
9.113	What does “so that I will not be stung” mean?
9.114	It means that there will be nothing left to prick my conscience.
9.115	Fear pricks. But don’t be afraid. Love comes in, and she heals the wound that fear makes.
9.116	The fear of God wounds the way a doctor’s knife does. It cuts away the rotten part, and it seems to make the wound bigger.
9.117	Look: when the rot was in the body, the wound was smaller, but it was dangerous. Then comes the knife. Before, the wound hurt less than it hurts now, while the doctor is cutting it.
9.118	It hurts more while he is operating on it than if he left it alone. It hurts more during the healing, but only so that it will never hurt again once it is healed.
9.119	So let fear fill your heart, so that it can bring love in. Let the scar come after the doctor’s knife.
9.120	He is such a great Healer that the scars can’t even be seen. Just put yourself in His hands.
9.121	If you have no fear, you cannot be made right with God.
9.122	The Scriptures say it plainly: “Whoever is without fear cannot be made right with God.”
9.123	So fear must come in first, so that love can come through it.
9.124	Fear is the operation. Love is the good health.
9.125	“But whoever is afraid has not been made perfect in love.”
9.126	Why? “Because fear brings torment,” just as the surgeon’s knife brings torment when it cuts.
9.127	5. But there is another saying that seems to contradict this one, unless someone understands it properly.
9.128	In one place in the Psalms it says, “The fear of the Lord is pure, lasting forever.”
9.129	He shows us a fear that lasts forever, but a pure fear.
9.130	But if the psalm shows us a fear that lasts forever, does this letter perhaps contradict it when it says, “There is no fear in love, but perfect love drives out fear”?
9.131	Let’s ask both of these sayings of God what they mean.
9.132	There is one Spirit, even though there are two books, two mouths, and two tongues.
9.133	This was said through John’s mouth, and that through David’s mouth. But don’t think there is more than one Spirit.
9.134	If one breath can fill the two pipes of a double flute, can’t one Spirit fill two hearts and move two tongues?
9.135	And if two pipes filled by one breath play in tune together, can two tongues filled by the Spirit, the Breath of God, be out of tune?
9.136	So they are in tune there. There is harmony. But it takes someone who knows how to listen.
9.137	Look: this Spirit of God breathed into two hearts and filled them. He moved two tongues. From one tongue we have heard, “There is no fear in love. But perfect love drives out fear.” From the other we have heard, “The fear of the Lord is pure, lasting forever.”
9.138	How can that be? The notes seem to clash.
9.139	No, they don’t. Wake up your ears. Listen to the tune.
9.140	There is a reason why the word “pure” is added in one place and not in the other. It is because there is one fear that is called pure, and there is another fear that is not called pure.
9.141	Let’s look at the difference between these two kinds of fear. Then we will understand how the flutes play in harmony.
9.142	How should we understand them? How can we tell them apart?
9.143	Listen, dear friends. Some people fear God because they don’t want to be thrown into hell. They are afraid they might burn with the devil in everlasting fire.
9.144	This is the fear that brings love in. But it comes in so that it can leave again.
9.145	If you still fear God because of punishment, you do not yet love the God you fear in this way.
9.146	You don’t long for the good things. You are just afraid of the bad things.
9.147	But because you are afraid of the bad things, you correct yourself and start to long for the good things.
9.148	Once you have started to long for the good, the pure fear will be in you.
9.149	What is pure fear?
9.150	It is the fear of losing those good things themselves.
9.151	Pay attention! Fearing God because He might throw you into hell with the devil is one thing. Fearing God because He might leave you is something else.
9.152	The fear that makes you afraid of being thrown into hell with the devil is not yet pure. It does not come from loving God, but from being afraid of punishment. But when you fear God because you don’t want His presence to leave you, you hold on to Him. You long to enjoy God Himself.
9.153	6. The best way to explain the difference between these two fears is to picture two married women. One fear is the kind love drives out. The other is pure and lasts forever. Imagine that one of the wives is willing to be unfaithful to her husband. She enjoys doing wrong. She is only afraid that her husband will condemn her.
9.154	She is afraid of her husband. But the reason she is afraid of him is that she still loves doing wrong.
9.155	To this woman, having her husband around is not welcome. It is a burden. And if she happens to be living wickedly, she is afraid of her husband, because he might come home.
9.156	People who are afraid of the coming day of judgment are like her.
9.157	Now imagine that the other wife loves her husband. She feels that she owes him pure embraces. She does not dirty herself with the filth of unfaithfulness. She wants her husband to be with her.
9.158	So how can we tell these two fears apart?
9.159	One woman is afraid, and the other is afraid too.
9.160	Ask them, and they seem to give the same answer. Ask the first one, “Are you afraid of your husband?” She answers, “Yes, I am.”
9.161	Ask the other one if she is afraid of her husband. She answers, “Yes, I am afraid of him.”
9.162	The words are the same, but the hearts are different.
9.163	Now ask them, “Why?”
9.164	One says, “I’m afraid of my husband, because he might come home.” The other says, “I’m afraid of my husband, because he might leave me.”
9.165	One says, “I’m afraid of being condemned.” The other says, “I’m afraid of being abandoned.”
9.166	Let the same thing happen in the minds of Christians. Then you will find a fear that love drives out, and another fear that is pure and lasts forever.
