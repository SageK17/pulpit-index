0.1	Amos 3:3 — “Can two people walk together unless they agree?”
0.2	In the Bible, we often find a “no” statement written in the form of a question.
0.3	Our text is an example of this. So we should understand the prophet to be saying that two people cannot walk together unless they agree.
0.4	For two people to agree means more than agreeing in their ideas or their understanding. We often see people who agree in their beliefs but are very different in how they feel and how they live.
0.5	Their minds may accept the same truths, while those truths affect their hearts and actions in very different ways.
0.6	Saints and sinners often hold the same religious beliefs on paper. Yet it is clear that they are very different in how they feel and live.
0.7	We have reason to believe that holy angels and demons understand and accept the same truths in their minds. And yet how differently those truths affect them!
0.8	The same truths have different effects on different minds. This happens because each person’s heart, or feelings, are in a different state.
0.9	To put it another way, the difference lies in how each person receives these truths—how they feel and act because of them.
0.10	We should also notice that the same things and truths will affect the same person very differently at different times.
0.11	This, too, comes from the different state of that person’s feelings at those times.
0.12	Or, to say it better, this difference lies in the different way the mind is acting at those times.
0.13	All pleasure and pain, all happiness and misery, all sin and holiness, live in the heart and its feelings. That is where they belong.
0.14	Whenever a truth or a thing is put before our minds, we feel either satisfied or dissatisfied, pleased or pained. That reaction depends completely on the state of our feelings at that moment. In fact, the reaction is made up of those feelings.
0.15	If the thing matches our feelings, and stirs them up and feeds them in a pleasant way, of course we are pleased. That is because our pleasure and happiness are made of those pleasant feelings.
0.16	So the higher something lifts these feelings when it is put before our minds, the greater our pleasure is.
0.17	But if the thing or truth does not match our feelings, it cannot please us. Suppose it is off to the side of what we are feeling right now, and we refuse to change the direction of our feelings. Then one of two things will happen. Either we will not care about it, because our feelings are busy with something else. Or, if it keeps pushing on us, we will turn away from it and resist it.
0.18	Now suppose it is not only off to the side of what we care about right now, but actually against it. Then, as long as our feelings stay the same, we will—and must—resist it and fight it.
0.19	Sometimes a subject is brought up and pushed on us that is different from the one our feelings are caught up in. Then we feel bored, or even annoyed and disgusted. But that is not all. Even on the same subject, something may be presented that is far above or far below the level of our feelings. If our feelings stay the same, and we refuse to be drawn in and lifted to that level, we will feel bored—and perhaps hurt and offended.
0.20	Suppose the subject is shown in a way that is below the level of our present feelings. Then we cannot be interested until it comes up to our level. And suppose the subject keeps being held before us in this cooling way, which seems cheap to us, while we are struggling to keep our high feelings. Then we become unhappy, because our feelings are not being fed. They are being fought.
0.21	Now suppose the subject is presented in a way that is far above the level of our feelings, and our feelings stay low and refuse to rise. Then it does not match or feed our feelings, so we cannot be interested. It seems like wild fanaticism to us. We are annoyed by the warmth we do not choose to share. And the higher it is above our own temperature, the more disgusted we are.
0.22	Everyone’s experience will prove these things true. They hold true on every subject and in every situation, because they are based on principles built into human nature itself.
0.23	Take a passionate politician. Talk to him about his favorite subject, in the way he likes best. Once his feelings are caught up in it, add the fire of powerful speaking. Make it burn and blaze in his mind, and you will delight him greatly.
0.24	But change your style and tone. Turn down your fire and feeling. Turn the subject over. Present it in a drier way. At once he loses almost all his interest and becomes uneasy as things go downhill.
0.25	Now change the subject. Bring up death and the solemn judgment. He is shocked and stunned. Keep pressing him with them, and he is disgusted and offended.
0.26	Why does he lose interest in his favorite subject? It is the natural result of taking away the burning picture of it that was pouring fire through his feelings. Why is he disgusted when you change the subject? It is the natural result of putting in front of him something that went directly against the way he felt at that moment.
0.27	Unless he chooses to turn his mind along with you as you change the subject, he cannot help being displeased.
0.28	Picture a trained musician listening, almost carried away with joy, as a beautiful piece of music is played with great skill.
0.29	Now throw some clashing, off-key notes at him. In a moment he is in pain.
0.30	Make the harsh sounds louder and longer, and he walks out of the room in disgust.
0.31	Or imagine this. You love music, but right now you are sad. You are going through great trouble, and you feel like crying. The gentle, mournful sound of a wind harp drifts softly to your ears and seems to melt around your heart. Your tears flow fast. But then the racket of trumpets, drums, and cymbals bursts in, along with a shrill fife playing a cheerful, quick march. It drowns out the soft whispers of the harp. You feel upset. You turn away and cover your ears.
0.32	The mournful harp touched you in a tender spot. It matched your feelings, so you enjoyed it.
0.33	The military music went against the way you felt. You were too sad for it to lift and cheer your feelings. So it could not help but upset you.
0.34	Now suppose your heart is glowing with religious feeling. At that moment you do not want anyone to bring up a different subject. You are also not interested in anything on the same subject that is far below the level of your feelings.
0.35	Suppose you hear a cold man preach or pray. As long as he stays cold and you are warm with feeling, you are not interested. Your feelings are not fed and warmed unless he comes up to your level. If that does not happen, you are upset, and perhaps disgusted by his coldness.
0.36	This is only natural.
0.37	Suppose that, like Paul, “you have great heaviness and constant sorrow in your heart” for sinners who are dying. Suppose “the Spirit helps you in your weakness, pleading for you just as God wills, with groanings that cannot be spoken.” While you feel this way, you hear someone pray who never mentions sinners. You hear a minister preach who says very little to them, and says it in a heartless, empty way. You are not interested. Feeling as you do, you cannot be. Instead, you are hurt and upset.
0.38	Now suppose you are lukewarm, and your feelings are worldly and earthly. You hear someone encourage, pray, or preach who is very spiritual, passionate, and loving. Maybe you cling to your sins and your feelings will not rise. Maybe prejudice, or pride, or the earthly, fleshly state of your feelings makes you refuse to catch fire and take hold of the subject. Then even though you agree with every word he says, you are not pleased.
0.39	He is warmer than you are. You are annoyed by the man’s manner, his fire, and his spirit.
0.40	If your feelings stay low, then the higher he rises, the farther apart you are, and the more annoyed you become.
0.41	While your heart is wrong, the more right he is, the more he burns you. If your heart will not catch fire, the more disgusted you become.
0.42	In both of these cases, the people whose feelings are at or near the same level as the one speaking or praying will not feel bothered. They will be pleased.
0.43	Lukewarm people will listen to the dull man and say, “That was pretty good.”
0.44	Their pleasure will be small, because their feelings are low. But overall, they are pleased.
0.45	People who have no feelings at that moment will, of course, feel nothing at all.
0.46	Everyone who feels deeply will listen with sadness and pain.
0.47	These same people would listen to the passionate man with great interest.
0.48	Let him glow and blaze, and they are thrilled.
0.49	But as long as worldly, cold-hearted people refuse to rise, they cannot help being bothered and offended by his fire.
0.50	From what we have said, we can learn several things.
0.51	First, we learn why people who disagree about points of doctrine, and who belong to different church groups, will often walk together for a while in great harmony and love.
0.52	It is because they feel deeply, and they feel the same way.
0.53	While their feelings match, their differences are mostly lost or forgotten. They will walk together as long as their hearts agree.
0.54	Next, we see why new believers love to spend time with each other, and with older Christians who have the most religious feeling. These walk together because they feel the same way.
0.55	Next, we see why lukewarm people who say they are Christians, and sinners who have not repented, have the same problems with the methods used in revivals.
0.56	We often hear them complain about the way people preach and pray.
0.57	Their complaints are the same. They find fault with the same things, and they use the same arguments to back up their complaints.
0.58	The reason is that, at that time, their feelings are almost the same. It is the fire and the spirit that bother their frozen hearts.
0.59	For now they walk together, because in their feelings they agree.
0.60	Next, we see why ministers and Christians who visit revivals often raise objections at first to the methods being used. They nitpick, and sometimes they even take the side of the wicked.
0.61	The truth is, they often come from places where no revivals are happening at the time. So they often feel scolded and annoyed by the warmth and spirit they see.
0.62	The praying, preaching, and talking are warmer than they are right now.
0.63	Sometimes something holds their feelings down so that they do not enter into the spirit of the work. It may be prejudice because the revival is happening in a church group different from theirs. It may be prejudice against the preacher or the people. Or perhaps it is pride, or envy, or love of the world, or something like that.
0.64	While their hearts stay wrong, of course they will nitpick. And while their feelings stay low, the more right something is—the more spiritual and holy—the more it is bound to displease them.
0.65	Next, we see why ministers and ordinary Christians disagree about which methods are wise.
0.66	Think of a man who sees and feels the endlessly serious things of eternity. He will judge what is wise or unwise in the use of methods very differently from someone whose spiritual eyes are almost shut.
0.67	A man whose heart is breaking for sinners who are perishing will, of course, think it wise, right, and necessary to “speak very plainly.” He will think he must deal with them in a very earnest and loving way.
0.68	He would think the opposite approach was very unwise, dangerous, and even criminal.
0.69	But a man who feels very little for them, and sees little of their danger, will be happy to use very different methods, or to use them in a very different way. And of course he will have very different ideas about what is wise.
0.70	This is why we see the same person holding very different ideas about wisdom at different times. As a result, he acts very differently at those times.
0.71	In fact, a man’s ideas about which methods are wise in revivals will depend on the state of his own feelings, and on the feelings of the people around him. And to a large degree, they ought to.
0.72	After all, what would be wise in some situations would be very unwise in others.
0.73	What would be wise for one man might be very unwise for another.
0.74	What would be wise for a man when his feelings are in a certain state, and in certain circumstances, would be extremely unwise for the same man when his feelings are different and his circumstances have changed.
0.75	Suppose God’s word does not condemn a method. In most cases, it is then extremely hard to form the opinion that the method is unwise. And it is often very wrong to say so in public. That is, unless you are in a position to enter into the feelings and circumstances of the person and the people at the time the method was used.
0.76	If Christians and ministers would remember these things, a great many unkind and judgmental speeches would be avoided. And much harm to the cause of truth and goodness would be prevented.
0.77	Next, we see why lukewarm Christians and sinners are not bothered by dull preaching or praying.
0.78	It does not touch their feelings at all. So it does not upset or offend them.
0.79	So we see what happens during a revival, when cold and wicked hearts are being stirred up by plain, sharp preaching. If a dull minister is invited to preach to the people, the wicked and cold-hearted will praise his preaching.
0.80	This explains why, during times of revival, we often hear sinners and lukewarm Christians wishing their minister would preach the way he used to. They wish he would be himself again.
0.81	The reason is plain. He never used to move them. But now his fire, his spirit, and his sharpness annoy them and disturb their worldly sleep.
0.82	Next, we can learn here how much weight to give to the opinions of ministers and Christians—and to our own opinions—when our feelings are in a bad state.
0.83	How much does this man approve of what was said or done?
0.84	What does he think about the methods being used? People often ask and answer questions like these. Then they trust the answer as a great authority, without paying any attention to the state of that man’s feelings at the time.
0.85	In most cases, we are completely wrong to put much trust in our own opinions, or in other people’s opinions, about which methods are wise—unless we have evidence that our feelings, or theirs, are in the right state. If our feelings changed, we would almost certainly see things differently and change our opinion.
0.86	Christians would do well to remember and follow the resolution of President Edwards: “that he would always do what seemed most fitting to him at the times when he saw the things of religion most clearly.”
0.87	Next, we learn why churches are sometimes shaken up by revivals.
0.88	Most churches probably have at least a few hypocrites in them. Sometimes a revival has much of its mere human emotion stripped away and becomes very spiritual. Then its fire and spirit bother these people. They oppose it inside, and sometimes openly.
0.89	But sometimes only part of the true Christians in a church wake up from their sleep and become very spiritual and heavenly-minded, while the rest stay worldly and earthly in their feelings. Then the church is in danger of being torn in two.
0.90	As those who are awake become more committed, more spiritual, and more active, the others will become jealous and offended, if they will not wake up. The commitment of the others makes them feel scolded. So they will nitpick. And the higher the more spiritual people rise above them, the more annoyed they become.
0.91	The closer the committed ones come to the right state of feeling, the farther apart the two groups are. As the committed ones climb higher in holy feeling, if the others will not climb with them, those others will almost certainly sink lower. In the end they really share no feelings at all. They can no longer walk together, because they do not agree.
0.92	When a church is in this state, all its members need to search their hearts deeply. This situation is greatly to be feared and deeply to be mourned when it happens. But it is easy to explain by these simple principles of our nature. And sometimes it will happen, no matter how wisely men or angels try to prevent it.
0.93	Next, we see why ministers sometimes lose their churches because of revivals.
0.94	Sometimes, even when the minister has not acted unwisely, many people in his church and congregation will not enter into the spirit of a revival.
0.95	If his own feelings catch fire, and he cares deeply for his flock and for the honor of his Master, he will certainly press the truth on them. He will annoy them with his spirit, his sharpness, and his fire, until he offends them.
0.96	If their feelings are wrong, then the more powerfully and forcefully he pushes the truth on them, the more he will offend them—unless their feelings change. In the end, he may find it best to leave them.
0.97	All this may happen, and it may be just as right and necessary for a minister as it was for Paul. Paul left places and people when some became hardened, argued against him, spoke against God, and said evil things about the Way in front of the crowds.
0.98	Another situation may happen too. The church and people may wake up while the shepherd sleeps and refuses to wake.
0.99	This will surely turn their hearts away from him and destroy their trust in him.
0.100	In either case, they may find they cannot walk together, because they do not agree.
0.101	In the first case, the minister should obey Christ’s command and “shake the dust off his feet as a witness against them.”
0.102	In the second case, the church should shake off their sleepy minister. They are better off without him than with him.
0.103	“How terrible it will be for the shepherds who feed only themselves!
0.104	Aren’t shepherds supposed to feed the sheep?
0.105	But you do not feed the flock.
0.106	So listen, you shepherds, to the word of the Lord.
0.107	This is what the Lord God says: Look, I am against the shepherds. I will demand my flock back from them. I will stop them from feeding the flock, and the shepherds will not feed themselves anymore. I will save my flock from their mouths, so that the sheep will not be their food anymore.”
0.108	Ezekiel 34:2, 3, 9, 10. President Edwards says:
0.109	“Ministers may preach very good teaching, and work very hard and carefully. But if they show their people that they do not like this work, and are doubtful and suspicious of it, they will very likely do their people much more harm than good.
0.110	Think about what could happen if their people were allowed to believe that this great and unusual work is God’s work. The very nature of the work itself, together with the example of other towns and whatever preaching they might hear now and then, would likely do much more to wake up their people and stir them up in their faith than all the minister’s other efforts with them.
0.111	What is more, the minister’s opinion will make them suspicious of the work they hear about in other places. Because of this, the mighty hand of God that shows in it will lose its effect on their minds. But that is not all. It will also make them suspicious of anything like it that appears among themselves. They will think it is part of the same sickness that has spread so widely through the land.
0.112	And what does this really do? It makes people suspicious of all living faith. It gets people talking against it and discouraging it wherever it shows up, and knocking it on the head as fast as it rises.
0.113	We ministers, by frowning at this work year after year, will keep the sheep away from their pasture instead of doing a shepherd’s job and feeding them. In a time like this, our people would be much better off with no regular minister at all.
0.114	“Those of us who hold this sacred office need to be careful about what we do and how we behave at this time. A smaller thing in a minister will hinder God’s work than in other people.
0.115	We might be very silent, or say little about the work in our public prayers and preaching, or seem to carefully avoid speaking about it in conversation. If so, our people will take it to mean that we are suspicious of it—we who are their guides, the ones they look to for spiritual teaching. This will tend to make them suspicious too. And then the results I described earlier will follow.
0.116	It is our job more than anyone else’s to promote the work of God. So if we actually hinder it and stand in its way, how can we expect to share in its wonderful blessings?
0.117	And by keeping others from those blessings, we will keep them out of heaven. So we should think about those terrible words Christ spoke to the Jewish teachers in Matthew 23:13:
0.118	‘How terrible for you! You lock up the kingdom of heaven. You do not go in yourselves, and you do not let those who are trying to enter go in.’
0.119	If we keep the sheep away from their pasture, how will we answer to the great Shepherd? He bought the flock with his precious blood, and he has put them in our care.
0.120	I humbly ask every minister who has for so long been against this work, and has looked down on it, to think about this: has he been like Michal, who had no child? Or at least, has he been mostly barren and unsuccessful in his work? I pray to God it may not be a barrenness that lasts forever, as hers did.”
0.121	Next, we can see that worldly church members and sinners have no problem with mere human emotion.
0.122	During revivals, it is common to hear a lot of complaints against what people call “mere emotion.”
0.123	No one denies that a lot of this kind of emotion is sometimes stirred up in revivals. That is not strange. In fact, it is impossible for real religious feelings to be stirred up very much without also stirring up our natural human emotions and sensitivities. To be surprised at this, or to object to a revival because of it, is clearly foolish.
0.124	But in most cases, it is not the human emotion that offends people. As far as those emotions go, saints and sinners, and worldly and spiritual Christians, all feel exactly the same.
0.125	Sinners have as much natural emotion as saints. Cold church members have as much of it as warm, spiritual Christians.
0.126	So as far as natural emotion goes, they can all share it. And in fact, we often see that they do.
0.127	Try a style of encouraging or preaching that is designed to stir up only sympathy and natural emotion. You will soon see that cold Christians, warm Christians, and sinners all feel exactly alike. They will all cry and seem to melt, and no one will be offended. And, I might add, no one will be convinced of their sin or converted.
0.128	But change your style. Make your message more spiritual and holy. Throw yourself into it with passion and power, and appeal directly to the conscience and the heart. Their tears will soon dry up. The worldly and cold-hearted will become uneasy, and soon they will find themselves offended.
0.129	As far as natural emotion goes, they walk together, because in this they agree. But as soon as the feeling becomes spiritual and holy, they cannot go any farther together. Here they do not agree. And as long as sinners refuse to repent and cold hearts stay cold, they cannot agree.
0.130	Next, we can see why sinners who have not repented cannot like pure revivals.
0.131	It is because God is in them.
0.132	Sinners hate God. That is why God commands them to make themselves a new heart.
0.133	This is the reason—the only reason—why sinners need a new heart.
0.134	While they are ruled by “a worldly mind, which is at war with God,” it is obvious that they do hate, and must hate, everything that is like God. They hate it exactly as much as they see that it looks like him.
0.135	So we see that the more a revival has mere emotion and everything wrong stripped away, the more it is bound to offend wrong hearts.
0.136	The more of God and the less human weakness people can see in revivals, the more they will—and must—stir up the hatred of worldly hearts.
0.137	Next, we learn how to judge what look like revivals when the wicked do not oppose them.
0.138	If people ruled by a worldly mind do not oppose a revival, it must be for one of three reasons.
0.139	First, they may be so convinced of their sin that they do not dare to oppose it openly (and even then, they oppose it in their hearts). Second, there may be nothing of the Holy Spirit in these revivals. Or third—and this often happens—the methods may have been used unwisely to stir up the crowd’s emotions. Then the work of the Holy Spirit is kept out of the sinner’s sight, buried under a pile of mere emotion.
0.140	Anything that keeps the Holy Spirit’s work out of the sinner’s sight tends to prevent opposition.
0.141	And anything that lets the sinner see the hand of God will surely stir up the opposition of his unchanged heart.
0.142	So if a stirring does not bring out the opposition of the wicked and those whose hearts are wrong, then either it is not a revival at all, or it is being run in such a way that sinners do not see God’s finger in it.
0.143	So we see this: the purer and holier the methods used to promote a revival, the more they are stripped of human weakness and mere sympathy, and the more like God they are, the more they are bound to stir up the opposition of every wrong heart.
0.144	When a man’s heart is wrong about any subject, it is obvious that he cannot truly approve of what is right about that subject. That would be a contradiction.
0.145	It would be like saying he could feel both right and wrong about the same thing at the same time.
0.146	So it seems that, if all other things are equal, some methods and ways of preaching are nearest to right. They are the ones that bring out the most of the heart’s natural hatred, and that stand most directly against wrong hearts—both in what is said and in how it is said.
0.147	This shows how foolish it is to work hard at pleasing people whose feelings about religion are in a wrong state.
0.148	They cannot be pleased with anything right and holy while their hearts are in this wrong state. As we have just seen, that would be a contradiction.
0.149	This shows why so many wrong feelings are often stirred up during revivals.
0.150	Pure revivals naturally stir up wrong feelings in wrong hearts.
0.151	Revivals on earth stir up wrong feelings in hell. Whenever they come in contact with rebellious hearts, whether in the church or outside it, they will disturb that same spirit and stir up those same feelings.
0.152	Wherever the Holy Spirit comes, or is seen at work, the opposite spirit is naturally disturbed.
0.153	When saints have a great deal of right and holy feeling, it will naturally stir up a great deal of unholy and wicked feeling in all the hearts that are set on being wrong.
0.154	The more right and holy feeling there is, the more wrong and unholy feeling there will be—unless sinners and worldly church members bow down and give in.
0.155	They cannot walk together, because they do not agree. And the more holy and heavenly the saints become in their feelings and actions, the farther apart they will be. In the end, the light of eternity will set them as far apart in their feelings as heaven is from hell.
0.156	This shows what the difference between heaven and hell really is, when it comes to moral character, happiness, and misery. It is the different state of the hearts and feelings of the people who live there.
0.157	This proves beyond any argument that sinners cannot be saved unless they are born again.
0.158	In other words, by the very nature of things, it is clearly impossible for sinners to walk with saints and holy angels and be happy with them—unless their feelings are completely changed.
0.159	Sinners cannot walk with the saints here on earth.
0.160	As soon as the saints stop living “the way this world lives,” sinners think it strange that they no longer rush with them into the same wild living. So they start “speaking evil of them.”
0.161	As soon as Christians wake up and become spiritual, active, holy, and heavenly-minded, and break away from their empty and wicked friendships with the world, sinners are always upset and offended.
0.162	They try to imagine that it is something wrong in the saints, and in revivals, that offends them.
0.163	But the truth is, it is the little bit that is right in the saints—and the part of revivals that has the most of God in it—that offends them most.
0.164	If the saints were as holy as the angels, or as holy as they will be in heaven, sinners would be that much farther from sharing any feelings with them. And as saints rise in holiness and sinners sink in sin, they will move farther and farther apart forever and ever.
0.165	Lastly, I note that this shows why the lives and preaching of the prophets, of Christ and his apostles, and the revivals of the early church met with so much more violent opposition than preachers and revivals meet today. That opposition came from worldly people who claimed to be religious and from ungodly sinners.
0.166	No one can deny that the saints in those days “were cruelly mocked and whipped, and even chained and put in prison. They were stoned, sawn apart, tempted, and cut down with the sword. They wandered around in sheepskins and goatskins. They were poor, suffering, and tortured (the world was not worthy of them). They wandered in deserts and on mountains, and in caves and hideouts in the earth.”
0.167	No one does or can deny this: many people who claimed to be saints, and huge numbers of ungodly sinners, bitterly opposed the preaching of the prophets, of Christ and his apostles, and of the first ministers. They opposed it more than they oppose any preacher today.
0.168	We should not hide the fact that religious people were often the leaders of this opposition. They stirred up the Romans to crucify Jesus, and later to persecute and destroy his followers and crucify his apostles.
0.169	Even the religious teachers and scholars of the law tried to turn the crowds against the Savior and keep them from listening to his teaching. “He has a devil in him, and he is mad,” they said. “Why do you listen to him?”
0.170	They led the way in opposing the apostles in the revivals the apostles were part of.
0.171	We must also admit that those revivals made a great deal of noise in the world. The apostles were even accused of “turning the world upside down.” Sinners were often badly hardened by the preaching of Christ and his apostles. They “were filled with great anger” and opposed them so bitterly that Christ told his apostles to “leave them alone.”
0.172	In some places where the apostles preached, “some” became so “hardened” that they “argued, spoke against God, and said evil things about the Way.” So the apostles were forced to leave and go to other places. Sometimes they had to leave in very humiliating ways, barely escaping with their lives.
0.173	We do not need to be embarrassed to face these facts. They are easy to explain by the principle in our text, which this sermon has illustrated.
0.174	None of these things proves that the prophets, and Christ and his apostles, were unwise and unholy men. It does not prove that their preaching was too harsh and severe, or that revivals were being handled wrongly in those days.
0.175	The truth is this. The prophets lived much holier lives, and they were much bolder and more faithful in giving their messages. Christ was much more searching, plain, sharp, and personal in his preaching, and in his life he was completely “separate from sinners.” The apostles were very sharp and plain in dealing with sinners and with people who claimed to be saints, and they lived very holy lives of self-denial. That is why worldly church people and ungodly sinners could not walk with them.
0.176	The methods used back then to promote revivals were holier and purer than they are now.
0.177	There was less mere sympathy. There was less of that fake, smooth manner, and less of that fancy language designed to win the applause of the ungodly.
0.178	They “refused to use hidden, dishonest methods. They did not act with trickery or twist the word of God.” They preached “not with the smooth, tempting words of human wisdom,” but “very plainly.” As a result, the ungodly, both inside and outside the church, were filled with anger.
0.179	Stephen spoke in such a holy and searching way that the leaders of Israel “ground their teeth at him.”
0.180	But this does not prove that he was unwise.
0.181	Today’s revivals move much more quietly and slowly than they did on the day of Pentecost and at many other times and places. They also cause much less noise and opposition among cold church members and ungodly sinners. But that does not prove that people understand revivals better now than they did then. Nor does it prove that the ministers and Christians involved in today’s revivals are wiser than the apostles and the first Christians. To think so would show great spiritual pride in us.
0.182	And we must not say that the human heart has changed, or that God’s character has become less offensive “to the worldly mind.”
0.183	No! The truth is that the prophets, Christ, his apostles, and the early saints were holier, bolder, and more active. They were plainer and sharper in their preaching. They were less like this crazy world. In a word, they were wiser and more like heaven than we are. That is why they were hated more than we are, and why their preaching and praying offended people so much more than ours does.
0.184	In their days, revivals were freer from worldly scheming, and from the kind of management that tends to hide the bare hand of God from the sinner’s sight. That is why those revivals made so much more noise than the ones we see today. That is why they stirred up so much of earth and hell to oppose them, and why they shook the world and turned it upside down.
0.185	Back then, people knew that “people could not serve God and riches.”
0.186	They could see that it was true: “if anyone is determined to live for God in Christ Jesus, he will suffer persecution.”
0.187	People understood then that if “ministers pleased people, they were not the servants of Christ.”
0.188	The church and the world could not walk together, because back then they did not agree.
0.189	We must not become proud and imagine that we are wise and clever. We must not think we have learned how to handle worldly church members and sinners, whose “worldly mind is at war with God,” in a way that does not stir up their opposition to truth and holiness. Christ and his apostles did stir it up.
0.190	Instead, we should understand this: if they have less trouble with us—with our lives and our preaching—than they had with Christ and the apostles, it is because we are less holy, less heavenly, and less like God than they were.
0.191	If we walk with the lukewarm and the ungodly, or they walk with us, it is because we agree.
0.192	For two cannot walk together unless they agree.
