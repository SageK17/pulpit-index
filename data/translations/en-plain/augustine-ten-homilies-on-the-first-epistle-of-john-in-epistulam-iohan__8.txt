9.167	7. So first, let’s talk to people who fear God the way a woman who enjoys doing wrong fears her husband. She is afraid he will condemn her. Let’s speak to people like that first.
9.168	Listen, soul. You fear God because you are afraid He will condemn you. That is just like the woman who enjoys doing wrong. She fears her husband because he might condemn her. You don’t approve of that woman. So don’t approve of yourself either.
9.169	If you have a wife, would you want her to fear you like that, just so you won’t condemn her? Would you want her to enjoy doing wrong and be held back only by the weight of her fear of you, instead of hating the wrong itself?
9.170	No. You would want her to be pure and faithful because she loves you, not because she is afraid of you.
9.171	So be toward God the way you would want your wife to be toward you.
9.172	And if you don’t have a wife yet but want one, that is the kind of wife you would want.
9.173	But wait, what are we saying, brothers and sisters?
9.174	Think about that woman whose fear of her husband is really a fear of being condemned by him. Maybe she doesn’t cheat on him because she is afraid he will somehow find out and take her life. But a husband can be fooled and kept in the dark. After all, he is only human, just like she is, the one who can fool him.
9.175	She fears a man she can hide from. So don’t you fear the face of your true Husband, which is always watching you?
9.176	“The Lord sets His face against people who do evil.”
9.177	She sees that her husband is away, and maybe she is tempted by the pleasure of cheating. But she still tells herself, “I won’t do it. He is away, yes. But it’s hard to stop him from finding out somehow.”
9.178	She holds herself back so that a mortal man won’t find out. Yet he might never find out. He might even be fooled, so that he thinks a bad woman is good and thinks an unfaithful wife is faithful. So don’t you fear the eyes of the One no one can fool? Don’t you fear the presence of the One you can never get away from?
9.179	Pray that God will look at you and turn His face away from your sins. Pray, “Turn Your face away from my sins.”
9.180	But how can you expect Him to turn His face away from your sins if you won’t turn your own face away from them?
9.181	The same voice says in the Psalm, “For I admit my wrongdoing, and my sin is always in front of me.”
9.182	Admit it, and He forgives.
9.183	8. We have spoken to the soul that still has the kind of fear that does not last forever, the fear that love shuts out and throws out. Now let’s speak to the soul that already has the pure fear that lasts forever.
9.184	Do you think we will find that soul so we can speak to it? Do you think it is here in this congregation? Is it up here where the clergy sit? Is it anywhere on earth?
9.185	It must be here somewhere. It is just hidden.
9.186	Right now it is winter. But inside the root, the green life is still there.
9.187	Maybe we can get that soul to listen.
9.188	But wherever that soul is, I wish I could find it! Then, instead of it listening to me, I could listen to it!
9.189	It should be teaching me, not learning from me!
9.190	It is a holy soul, a soul on fire, longing for God’s kingdom. I am not the one who speaks to that soul. God Himself speaks to it. He comforts it while it patiently puts up with living here on earth. He says: “You want Me to come right now. I know you wish I would come right now. I know what you are like. You can wait for My coming without fear. I know waiting is hard for you. But wait a little longer. Hold on. I am coming, and I am coming soon.”
9.191	But to the soul that loves, time moves slowly.
9.192	Listen to her sing. She is like a lily among the thorns. Listen to her sigh and say, “I will sing, and I will understand the blameless way. When will You come to me?”
9.193	But in the blameless way she has no reason to be afraid, because “perfect love throws fear out.”
9.194	And when He has come and she is in His arms, she still has fear. But it is the fear of someone who feels safe.
9.195	What is she afraid of?
9.196	She will watch herself and guard against her own wrongdoing so that she doesn’t sin again. She is not afraid of being thrown into the fire. She is afraid of being left by Him.
9.197	And what will there be in her? The “pure fear that lasts forever.”
9.198	We have heard two flutes playing the same tune.
9.199	One speaks of fear, and the other speaks of fear too. But the first speaks of the fear a soul has of being condemned. The second speaks of the fear a soul has of being left alone.
9.200	The first is the fear that love throws out. The second is the fear that lasts forever.
9.201	9. “Let us love, because He loved us first.”
9.202	How could we love at all, unless He had loved us first?
9.203	By loving, we became His friends. But He loved us when we were His enemies, so that we could become His friends.
9.204	He loved us first, and He gave us the gift of loving Him.
9.205	We did not love Him yet. Loving is what makes us beautiful.
9.206	Suppose an ugly, unattractive man loves a beautiful woman. What can he do?
9.207	Or suppose a woman is ugly, unattractive and dark-skinned, and she loves a handsome man. What can she do?
9.208	Can she become beautiful by loving?
9.209	Can he become handsome by loving?
9.210	He loves a beautiful woman. But when he looks at himself in a mirror, he is too ashamed to lift his face toward the lovely woman he is in love with.
9.211	What can he do to become handsome?
9.212	Should he wait for good looks to come?
9.213	No. While he waits, he only gets older, and that makes him even less attractive.
9.214	So there is nothing he can do. There is no advice to give him except this: he should hold himself back and not dare to love someone so far above him. Or if he does love her and wants to marry her, let him love her purity, not just her pretty face.
9.215	But our soul, my brothers and sisters, is ugly because of sin. By loving God it becomes beautiful.
9.216	What a love that must be, that makes the one who loves beautiful!
9.217	But God is always beautiful. He is never ugly, and He never changes.
9.218	The One who is always beautiful loved us first. And what were we when He loved us? Nothing but dirty and ugly.
9.219	But He did not love us so He could leave us dirty. No, He loved us to change us, to make us beautiful instead of ugly.
9.220	How do we become beautiful?
9.221	By loving the One who is always beautiful.
9.222	As love grows in you, your beauty grows too. Love itself is the beauty of the soul.
9.223	“Let us love, because He loved us first.”
9.224	Listen to the apostle Paul: “But God showed His love in us: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” The good died for the bad. The beautiful died for the ugly.
9.225	How do we know Jesus is beautiful?
9.226	“You are more beautiful than any of the sons of men. Grace pours from Your lips.”
9.227	Why is that? Look again at why He is beautiful: “More beautiful than any of the sons of men.” It is because “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”
9.228	But when He took on a human body, He took on your ugliness, so to speak. That means He took on your mortality, the fact that you die. He did this to fit Himself to you and become like you, so He could stir you up to love the beauty inside.
9.229	So where in Scripture do we find that Jesus was plain and ugly? We have already found Him beautiful, “more beautiful than any of the sons of men.” So where do we find Him ugly too?
9.230	Ask Isaiah: “We saw Him, and He had no beauty or attractiveness.”
9.231	Now here are two flutes that seem to play notes that clash. But one Spirit breathes into both of them.
9.232	One says, “More beautiful than any of the sons of men.” The other, in Isaiah, says, “We saw Him, and He had no beauty or attractiveness.”
9.233	One Spirit fills both flutes. They do not clash at all.
9.234	Don’t close your ears. Use your understanding.
9.235	Let’s ask the apostle Paul to explain to us how these two flutes play in harmony.
9.236	Let him play us the first note: “More beautiful than any of the sons of men.—Who, being in the form of God, did not think it was robbery to be equal with God.”
9.237	Let him also play us the second note: “We saw Him, and He had no beauty or attractiveness.—He gave up His high standing. He took the form of a servant. He was made like human beings, and people found Him looking just like a man.
9.238	He had no beauty or attractiveness,” so that He could give you beauty and attractiveness.
9.239	What kind of beauty? What kind of attractiveness?
9.240	The kind of loving that comes from real, selfless love. So that as you love, you run, and as you run, you love.
9.241	Now you are beautiful. But don’t keep staring at yourself, or you might lose what you were given. Let your eyes rest on the One who made you beautiful.
9.242	Be beautiful only for this reason: so that He will love you.
9.243	But aim everything at Him. Run to Him. Look for His embrace. Be afraid of ever leaving Him. Then you will have the pure fear that lasts forever.
9.244	“Let us love, because He loved us first.”
9.245	10. “If anyone says, ‘I love God.’”
9.246	Which God? And why do we love?
9.247	“Because He loved us first,” and He gave us the ability to love.
9.248	He loved us when we were ungodly, to make us godly. He loved us when we were wicked, to make us good. He loved us when we were sick, to make us well.
9.249	Ask anyone you like. Let each person tell you whether he loves God.
9.250	He will shout it out and declare, “I love Him! God knows I do.”
9.251	But there is another question to ask.
9.252	“If anyone says, ‘I love God,’ and yet hates his brother, he is a liar.”
9.253	How can you prove that he is a liar?
9.254	Listen: “If someone doesn’t love his brother whom he can see, how can he love God whom he can’t see?”
9.255	What does that mean, then? Does someone who loves his brother also love God?
9.256	He has to love God. He cannot help loving the One who is Love itself.
9.257	Can anyone love his brother and not love Love?
9.258	He has to love Love.
9.259	What then? If he loves Love, does that mean he loves God?
9.260	Yes, it certainly does.
9.261	By loving Love, he loves God.
9.262	Or have you forgotten what you said a little while ago, “Love is God”?
9.263	If “Love is God,” then whoever loves Love loves God.
9.264	So love your brother, and you can be sure.
9.265	You cannot say, “I love my brother, but I don’t love God.”
9.266	If you say “I love God” but don’t love your brother, you are lying. In the same way, if you say “I love my brother” but think you don’t love God, you are fooling yourself.
9.267	If you love your brother, you have to love Love itself. But “Love is God.” So whoever loves his brother has to love God.
9.268	But if you don’t love the brother you can see, how can you love God, whom you can’t see?
9.269	Why can’t he see God?
9.270	Because he doesn’t have Love itself.
9.271	He can’t see God because he doesn’t have love. And he doesn’t have love because he doesn’t love his brother.
9.272	So the reason he can’t see God is that he doesn’t have Love.
9.273	If he has Love, he sees God, because “Love is God.” And love keeps cleaning that inner eye more and more, so that it can see God, the One who never changes. He will always rejoice in God’s presence. He will enjoy Him forever when he is together with the angels.
9.274	Only let him keep running now, so that at last he can be glad in his true home.
9.275	He must not fall in love with the journey or with the road. Let everything taste bitter except the One who calls us, until we hold on to Him tightly. Then we can say what the Psalm says: “You have destroyed all who leave You to chase after other lovers.” Who are these people who chase other lovers? The people who walk away from God and love the world. But what will you do? The Psalm goes on to say: “But for me, it is good to cling to God.”
9.276	All my good is to cling to God, and to do it freely, for nothing in return.
9.277	Suppose you ask him, “Why do you cling to God?” And he answers, “So that He will give me—” Give you what?
9.278	He is the One who made heaven and earth. What more is He going to give you?
9.279	You are already clinging to Him! Find something better than Him, and He will give it to you.
9.280	11. “If someone doesn’t love his brother whom he can see, how can he love God whom he can’t see?
9.281	And this is the command we have from Him: the person who loves God should love his brother as well.”
9.282	What a wonderful thing you said, “I love God”—while you hate your brother!
9.283	You murderer, how can you love God?
9.284	Didn’t you hear earlier in this same letter, “Whoever hates his brother is a murderer”?
9.285	“Yes, but I really do love God, even though I hate my brother.”
9.286	No, you really do not love God if you hate your brother.
9.287	And now I will prove it another way.
9.288	This same apostle said, “He gave us a command to love one another.”
9.289	How can anyone say you love Him when you hate His command?
9.290	Who would ever say, “I love the emperor, but I hate his laws”?
9.291	The emperor can tell whether you love him by this: whether his laws are kept all through the provinces.
9.292	So what is our Emperor’s law?
9.293	“I give you a new command: love one another.”
9.294	So you say you love Christ? Then keep His command, and love your brother.
9.295	But if you don’t love your brother, how can anyone say you love the One whose command you look down on?
9.296	Brothers and sisters, I never get tired of talking about love in the name of the Lord.
9.297	The more you have an endless hunger for this love, the more we hope love itself is growing in you. We hope it is driving out fear, so that only that pure fear remains, the fear that lasts forever.
9.298	Let’s put up with the world. Let’s put up with troubles. Let’s put up with the traps of temptation that make us stumble.
9.299	Let’s not leave the road. Let’s hold on to the unity of the Church. Let’s hold on to Christ. Let’s hold on to love.
9.300	Let’s not be torn away from the members of His Bride, and let’s not be torn away from the faith. Then we can be proud and glad when He comes. And we will live safely in Him, now by faith and later by sight. He has given us such a great down payment on this: the gift of the Holy Spirit.
T:10	Homily 10 (1 John 5:1–3)
10.1	“Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God. And everyone who loves the Father also loves the One who is born of Him.
10.2	This is how we know that we love God’s children: because we love God and do what He commands.
10.3	For this is what loving God is: keeping His commands.”
10.4	1. Those of you who were here yesterday probably remember how far we got in going through this letter. We had reached this: “If someone doesn’t love his brother whom he can see, how can he love God whom he can’t see?
10.5	And this is the command we have from Him: the person who loves God should love his brother as well.”
10.6	That is as far as we got.
10.7	So let’s see what comes next.
10.8	“Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God.”
10.9	Who is it that doesn’t believe that Jesus is the Christ?
10.10	The person who doesn’t live the way Christ commanded.
10.11	Lots of people say, “I believe.” But faith without actions does not save.
10.12	And what faith does is love. As the apostle Paul says, “Faith that works through love.”
10.13	Before you believed, your works were either nothing at all, or, if they looked good, they were worth nothing.
10.14	If you had no good works, you were like a man with no feet, or with sore feet that can’t walk. If your works looked good before you believed, you were running, yes. But you were running off the road. So you got lost instead of reaching the finish line.
10.15	So we need to do two things: run, and run on the right road.
10.16	Someone who runs off the road is running for nothing. Or really, he is just wearing himself out.
10.17	The more he runs off the road, the more lost he gets.
10.18	What is the road we run on?
10.19	Christ told us, “I am the Way.”
10.20	What is the home we are running to?
10.21	“I am the Truth.”
10.22	You run by Him, you run to Him, and you rest in Him.
10.23	But so that we could run by Him, He came all the way to us. We were far away, like foreigners in a distant country.
10.24	And being far away wasn’t the only problem. We were also so weak that we couldn’t even move.
10.25	He came like a Doctor to the sick. He stretched Himself out like a Road to reach those who were in a distant country.
10.26	Let’s be saved by Him. Let’s walk in Him.
10.27	This is what it means to “believe that Jesus is the Christ.” It means believing the way real Christians believe, Christians not just in name, but in their actions and their lives. It does not mean believing the way the devils believe.
10.28	For as the Scripture tells us, “The devils also believe, and they tremble.”
10.29	What more could the devils believe? They even said, “We know who You are: the Son of God.”
10.30	Peter said the same thing the devils said.
10.31	The Lord asked His disciples who He was and who people said He was. They answered, “Some people say You are John the Baptist. Some say You are Elijah. Others say Jeremiah, or one of the prophets.
10.32	He said to them, ‘But who do you say I am?’
10.33	And Peter answered, ‘You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.’”
10.34	And then the Lord said to him, “Simon son of Jonah, how blessed you are! It wasn’t flesh and blood that showed you this. It was My Father in heaven.”
10.35	Look at the praise that comes after this faith.
10.36	“You are Peter, and on this rock I will build My Church.”
10.37	What does “On this rock I will build My Church” mean?
10.38	It means on this faith. On what was just said: “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.
10.39	On this rock,” He says, “I will build My Church.”
10.40	What great praise! So Peter says, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” But the devils also say, “We know who You are: the Son of God, the Holy One of God.”
10.41	Peter said this, and the devils said it too. The words were the same, but the heart behind them was not.
10.42	So how can we tell that Peter said it with love?
10.43	Because a Christian’s faith comes with love, but a devil’s faith has no love.
10.44	How is it without love? Peter said it because he wanted to hold on to Christ. The devils said it because they wanted Christ to go away from them.
10.45	Before they said, “We know who You are: the Son of God,” they said, “What do You want with us?
10.46	Why have You come to destroy us before the time?”
10.47	So it is one thing to confess Christ so you can hold on to Christ. It is a very different thing to confess Christ so you can push Christ away.
10.48	So you see, when John says here, “Everyone who believes,” he means a faith that is truly your own. He doesn’t mean a faith that simply agrees with what many others say.
10.49	So, brothers and sisters, don’t let any heretics tell you, “We believe too.”
10.50	That is why I gave you the example of the devils. I don’t want you to be happy just with the words “I believe.” I want you to look closely at how people actually live.
10.51	2. So let’s see what it means to believe in Christ, what it means to believe that Jesus is the Christ.
10.52	John goes on: “Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God.”
10.53	But what does it mean to believe that?
10.54	“And everyone who loves the Father also loves the One who is born of Him.”
10.55	Right away John joined love to faith, because faith without love is worth nothing.
10.56	Faith with love is a Christian’s faith. Faith without love is a devil’s faith. But people who don’t believe at all are worse than the devils. They are even more foolish than the devils.
10.57	Some people refuse to believe in Christ. In that way, they are not even as good as the devils.
10.58	Another person does believe in Christ, but hates Christ. He confesses his faith because he is afraid of being punished, not because he loves the crown of reward. The devils were afraid of being punished too.
10.59	Add love to this faith, so that it becomes the kind of faith the apostle Paul talks about, “faith that works through love.” Then you have found a Christian. You have found a citizen of the heavenly Jerusalem, a fellow citizen of the angels, a traveler sighing for home along the way. Join up with him. He is your travel companion. Run with him, if you really are one too.
10.60	“Everyone who loves the Father also loves the One who is born of Him.”
10.61	Who is the One who “fathered”? The Father.
10.62	Who is the One who “is born”? The Son.
10.63	So what is John saying?
10.64	“Everyone who loves the Father loves the Son.”
10.65	3. “This is how we know that we love the sons of God.”
10.66	What does this mean, brothers and sisters?
10.67	Just a moment ago John was talking about the Son of God, not the sons of God. Here one Christ was set in front of us to think about. We were told, “Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God. And everyone who loves the One who fathered,” meaning the Father, “also loves the One who is born of Him,” meaning the Son, our Lord Jesus Christ.
10.68	Then John goes on: “This is how we know that we love the sons of God.” It sounds as if he was about to say, “This is how we know that we love the Son of God.”
10.69	Just before, he was talking about the Son of God, but now he says “the sons of God.” Why? Because the sons of God are the Body of God’s only Son. He is the Head and we are the members, so together it is one Son of God.
10.70	So whoever loves the sons of God loves the Son of God. And whoever loves the Son of God loves the Father. No one can love the Father without loving the Son. And whoever loves the sons also loves the Son of God.
10.71	Which sons of God?
10.72	The members of the Son of God.
10.73	And by loving, a person becomes a member himself. Through love he becomes part of the structure of Christ’s body. So there will be one Christ, loving Himself.
10.74	When the members of a body love one another, the body is loving itself.
10.75	“When one member is in pain, every member hurts along with it. When one member receives honor, every member shares the joy.”
10.76	And then Paul goes on to say, “Now you are Christ’s body, and you are its members.”
10.77	A little earlier John was talking about loving your brothers and sisters. He said, “If someone doesn’t love his brother whom he can see, how can he love God whom he can’t see?”
10.78	But if you love your brother, could it be that you love your brother and don’t love Christ?
10.79	How could that be, when you are loving members of Christ?
10.80	So when you love members of Christ, you love Christ. When you love Christ, you love the Son of God. When you love the Son of God, you love the Father too.
10.81	So love can’t be cut up into separate pieces.
10.82	Pick one to love, and the rest come along with you.
10.83	Suppose you say, “I love only God, God the Father.”
10.84	You are lying. If you love, you don’t love Him alone. If you love the Father, you love the Son too.
10.85	“All right,” you say, “I love the Father, and I love the Son. But that’s all. I love God the Father and God the Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, who went up into heaven and sits at the Father’s right hand. He is the Word through whom everything was made, and ‘the Word became flesh and lived in us.’ That’s all I love.”
10.86	You are lying. If you love the Head, you also love the members. And if you don’t love the members, you don’t love the Head either.
10.87	Doesn’t it make you tremble to hear what the Head said from heaven for the sake of His members: “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting ME?”
10.88	He called the person who persecuted His members His own persecutor. And He calls the person who loves His members His own lover.
10.89	And you know who His members are, brothers and sisters. They are none other than the Church of God.
10.90	“This is how we know that we love the sons of God: when we love God.”
10.91	How can that be? Aren’t the sons of God one thing and God Himself something else?
10.92	But whoever loves God loves His commands.
10.93	And what are God’s commands?
10.94	“I give you a new command: love one another.”
10.95	Let no one make excuses by saying he has a different love, that he loves one thing instead of another. This love works one way only. This love is bound together into one. So it makes everything that holds on to it into one. It is like fire that melts things down into one.
10.96	It is like gold. The lump is melted and becomes a single piece.
10.97	But unless the heat of love is applied, many things can never be melted down into one.
10.98	“When we love God,” that is how “we know that we love the sons of God.”
10.99	4. And how do we know that we love the sons of God?
10.100	By this: “when we love God and do what He commands.”
10.101	Here we sigh, because it seems so hard to do what God commands.
10.102	Listen to what comes next. Tell me, what makes your loving such hard work?
10.103	Loving greed does. What you love costs you hard work. But there is no hard work in loving God.
10.104	Greed will order you to work hard, face dangers, go through terrible hardships and troubles. And you will do what it tells you.
10.105	What for? So that you can have enough to fill your money chest, and lose your peace of mind.
10.106	Maybe you felt safer before you had it than you have ever felt since you started having it.
10.107	Look at what greed has ordered you to do.
10.108	You have filled your house, and now you are terrified of robbers. You have gotten gold, and you have lost your sleep.
10.109	Look at what greed has ordered you to do.
10.110	It said, “Do it,” and you did.
10.111	What does God order you to do?
10.112	“Love Me.” You love gold, so you will look for gold, and maybe you won’t find it. But whoever looks for Me, I am already with him.
10.113	You will love honor, and maybe you won’t get it. But who ever loved Me and did not reach Me?
10.114	God says to you: You want to get yourself a patron or a powerful friend. So you try to win his favor by going through someone lower down.
10.115	But God says to you, “Love Me. You don’t win My favor by going through someone else. Your love itself brings Me close to you.”
10.116	Brothers and sisters, what could be sweeter than this love?
10.117	There was a good reason you heard this in the Psalm just now: “Wicked people told me about pleasures, but nothing like Your law, O Lord.”
10.118	What is God’s Law?
10.119	God’s command.
10.120	What is God’s command?
10.121	That “new command.” It is called new because it makes people new: “I give you a new command: love one another.”
10.122	Listen, and you will see that this is God’s law.
10.123	The apostle says, “Help carry one another’s heavy loads. That is how you will fulfill the law of Christ.”
10.124	This, yes, this is the goal and completion of everything we do: love.
10.125	Love is the finish line. This is what we run for. This is what we run toward. When we reach it, we will rest.
10.126	5. You heard in the Psalm, “I have seen the end of all perfection.”
10.127	He said, “I have seen the end of all perfection.” What had he seen?
10.128	Do we think he climbed to the top of some very high, pointed mountain and looked out and saw the whole earth and the circle of the round world? Is that why he said, “I have seen the end of all perfection”?
10.129	If that is something to praise, then let’s ask the Lord for really sharp physical eyes. Then all we would need is some extremely high mountain on earth, so that from its top we could see the end of all perfection.
10.130	You don’t need to go far. Look, I’m telling you, it is right here. Climb the mountain and see the end.
10.131	Christ is the Mountain. Come to Christ, and from there you will see the end of all perfection.
10.132	What is this end?
10.133	Ask Paul. He says, “The end of the command is love, coming from a pure heart, a good conscience, and faith that is not fake.” And in another place he says, “Love is the fullness,” or the fulfilling, “of the law.”
10.134	What is more finished and complete than “fullness”?
10.135	You see, brothers and sisters, the apostle uses the word “end” here as a word of praise.
10.136	Don’t think of an end that means using something up. Think of an end that means completing something.
10.137	When someone says, “I have finished my bread,” he means one thing. When he says, “I have finished my coat,” he means something else.
10.138	I finish the bread by eating it. I finish the coat by making it.
10.139	In both cases the word is “end” or “finish.” But the bread is finished by being used up, and the coat is finished by being made. The bread is finished so that it is gone. The coat is finished so that it is complete.
10.140	So take the word “end” in this sense too when the Psalm is read and you hear the title, “On the end, a Psalm of David.”
10.141	You hear this all the time in the Psalms, so you should understand what you are hearing.
10.142	What does “On the end” mean? “For Christ is the end of the law for everyone who believes.”
10.143	And what does “Christ is the end” mean?
10.144	It means this: Christ is God, and “the end of the command is love,” and “Love is God.” For the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are One.
10.145	There He is your End. In another place He is your Way.
10.146	Don’t get stuck on the road and never reach the end.
10.147	Whatever else you come to along the way, go past it until you reach the end.
10.148	What is the end?
10.149	“It is good for me to hold on tight to God.”
10.150	Have you taken firm hold of God? Then you have finished the road. You will stay in your true home.
10.151	Pay close attention! Someone goes after money. Don’t let money be your end. Pass it by, like a traveler in a foreign land.
10.152	But if you love it, greed will tangle you up. Greed will be like chains on your feet. You won’t be able to move forward anymore.
10.153	So pass this by too. Look for the end.
10.154	You want good health. Still, don’t stop there.
10.155	What is good health, anyway? Death ends it. Sickness weakens it. It is a weak, mortal thing that doesn’t last.
10.156	Yes, go after good health, so that sickness doesn’t get in the way of your good works. But that is exactly why health is not the end. You want it for the sake of something else.
10.157	Anything you want for the sake of something else is not the end. Anything you love for its own sake, freely, is the end.
10.158	You go after honors. Maybe you want them so you can do something, so you can get something done and please God that way. But don’t love the honor itself, or you will stop there.
10.159	Do you want praise? If you want God’s praise, that’s good. If you want praise for yourself, that’s bad. You are stopping short on the road.
10.160	But look, people love you and praise you. Don’t be happy when they praise you for yourself. Let yourself be praised in the Lord, so that you can sing, “My soul will be praised in the Lord.”
10.161	You give a good speech, and people praise it.
10.162	Don’t let it be praised as if it were yours. The end is not there.
10.163	If you make that your end, it will be the end of you. But not an end where you are made complete. It will be an end where you are used up.
10.164	So don’t let your speech be praised as if it came from you, as if it were yours.
10.165	Then how should it be praised? The way the Psalm says: “In God I will praise the speech. In God I will praise the word.”
10.166	If you do this, what comes next in that Psalm will happen to you: “I have put my hope in God. I will not be afraid of what people can do to me.”
10.167	When everything that is yours is praised in God, you don’t need to fear losing your praise, because God never fails.
10.168	So pass this by too.
10.169	6. See, brothers and sisters, how many things we pass by that are not the end.
10.170	We use these things along the way. It is like stopping to rest and eat at the rest stops on a journey, and then moving on.
10.171	So where is the end?
10.172	“Beloved ones, we are sons of God, but what we will be has not yet been made clear.” That is said right here in this letter.
10.173	So we are still on the road. Wherever we get to, we still have to keep going, until we reach some end.
10.174	“We know that when He shows Himself, we will be like Him, because we will see Him just as He is.
10.175	That is the end. There we will praise God forever. There we will sing Alleluia always, without ever stopping.
10.176	So this is the end he talked about in the Psalm: “I have seen the end of all perfection.” And it is as if someone asked him, “What is this end you have seen?”
10.177	He answers, “Your command, which is very wide.”
10.178	This is the end: how wide the command is.
10.179	The wideness of the command is love, because where there is love, nothing feels cramped.
10.180	The apostle was in this wide, roomy place when he said, “We have opened our mouths to you, Corinthians. Our hearts are wide open. We haven’t closed you out of them.”
10.181	This is what it means when it says, “Your command is very wide.”
10.182	What is this wide command?
10.183	“I give you a new command: love one another.”
10.184	So love is never cramped.
10.185	Do you want to avoid feeling cramped here on earth?
10.186	Then live in that wide, roomy place.
10.187	Whatever people do to you, they can’t squeeze you in. That is because you love what people can’t hurt. You love God. You love your brothers and sisters. You love God’s law. You love God’s Church. And it will last forever.
10.188	You work hard here on earth, but you will come to the joy that was promised.
10.189	Who can take away what you love?
10.190	If no one can take away what you love, you can sleep safely. Or better, you can stay awake safely, so that you don’t fall asleep and lose what you love.
10.191	It is said for a good reason, “Light up my eyes, so that I never fall asleep in death.”
10.192	People who close their eyes to love fall asleep in the cravings of bodily pleasures.
10.193	So stay awake! Here are those pleasures: eating, drinking, living it up in luxury, playing games, going hunting. All kinds of evil follow these empty shows.
10.194	Don’t we know these things are pleasures? Who can deny that they are enjoyable?
10.195	But God’s law is loved even more.
10.196	Shout back at the people who try to tempt you: “Wicked people have told me about pleasures, but nothing like Your law, O Lord.”
10.197	This joy lasts. It doesn’t only last as the goal you can reach. It also calls you back when you run away.
