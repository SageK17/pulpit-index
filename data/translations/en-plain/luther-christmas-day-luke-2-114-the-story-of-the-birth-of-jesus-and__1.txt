0.239	47. From this we see that the law and the prophets cannot be rightly preached and understood unless we see Christ wrapped up in them.
0.240	It is true that Christ does not seem to be in them. And the Jews do not find him there.
0.241	They look like small, unimportant cloths, just simple words. They seem to speak about unimportant outward matters, and their real meaning is not recognized. But the New Testament, the Gospel, must open it up, shine its light on it, and reveal it, as we have said.
0.242	48. So first of all the Gospel must be heard. The angel's appearing and his voice must be believed.
0.243	If the shepherds had not heard from the angel that Christ was lying there, they could have seen him ten thousand times. They would never have known that the child was Christ.
0.244	That is why St. Paul says in 2 Corinthians 3:16 that the law stays dark and covered for the Jews until they turn to Christ.
0.245	Christ must first be heard in the Gospel. Then people will see how beautiful and lovely the whole Old Testament is, and how well it agrees with him. Then a person cannot help but give himself over to faith. He will be able to see the truth of what Christ says in John 5:46: "If you believed Moses, you would believe me, because he wrote about me."
0.246	49. So let us beware of all teaching that does not present Christ.
0.247	What more do you want to know?
0.248	What more do you need, if you really know Christ as described above? What more do you need, if you live by faith in God and by love for your neighbor, doing for others what Christ has done for you?
0.249	This is truly the whole Bible in its shortest form. No more words or books are needed, only life and action.
0.250	50. He lies in the manger.
0.251	Notice here that nothing but Christ should be preached in the whole world.
0.252	What is the manger? It is the Christians who gather in the churches to hear the preaching.
0.253	We are the animals in front of this manger. And Christ is laid before us, so that we can feed our souls on him.
0.254	Whoever goes to hear preaching goes to this manger. But it must be preaching about Christ.
0.255	Not all mangers have Christ in them, and not all sermons teach the true faith.
0.256	There was only one manger in Bethlehem where this treasure lay. And it was an empty, despised manger, with no feed in it.
0.257	So the preaching of the Gospel is set apart from everything else. It has nothing and teaches nothing except Christ. If anything else is taught, it is no longer Christ's manger. It is the manger of war horses, full of earthly things and food for the body.
0.258	51. Christ in his baby cloths stands for the faith in the Old Testament. To show this, we will give several examples here.
0.259	We read in Matthew
0.260	8:4 that when Christ made the leper clean, he said to him, "Go and show yourself to the priest. Offer the gift that Moses commanded, as a testimony to them."
0.261	Here you see that the law of Moses was given to the Jews as a testimony, or sign, as the angel also says here. That is, this law stands for something other than itself.
0.262	What does it stand for? Christ is the priest. All people are spiritual lepers because of unbelief. But when we come to faith in him, he touches us with his hand. He gives us his merit and lays it on us. Then we become clean and whole, without any merit of our own at all.
0.263	So we should show our thanks to him. We should admit that we have not become godly by our own works, but through his grace. Then we will be on the right path before God.
0.264	We should also offer our gifts. That means giving what is ours to help others, doing good to them as Christ has done to us.
0.265	In this way Christ is served, and an offering is brought to the true priest. For it is done for his sake, to love and praise him.
0.266	Do you see how, in picture language, Christ and faith are wrapped up in the simple words of Scripture?
0.267	Here it becomes clear that Moses in the law only bore witness to Christ and explained him.
0.268	The whole Old Testament should be understood this way. It should be seen as the baby cloths, a sign that points to Christ and makes him known.
0.269	52. Again, it was commanded that the Sabbath be kept strictly and that no work be done. This shows that not our works, but Christ's works should live in us. For it is written that we are not saved by our works, but by the works of Christ.
0.270	Now these works of Christ are of two kinds, as shown before.
0.271	First, there are the works Christ did himself, without us. These are the most important, and we believe in them.
0.272	Second, there are the works he does in us, in our love for our neighbor.
0.273	The first can be called the evening works, and the second the morning works. So evening and morning make one day, as it is written in Genesis 1:5. For the Scriptures begin the day in the evening and end it in the morning. That is, the evening with the night is the first half, and the morning with the daytime is the second half of the whole natural day.
0.274	The first half is dark and the second half is light. In the same way, Christ's first works are hidden in our faith. But the others, the works of love, are meant to be seen and openly shown to others.
0.275	So here you see how the whole Sabbath is kept and made holy.
0.276	55. Do you see how beautifully Christ lies in these cloths?
0.277	How beautifully the Old Testament shows the faith and love of Christ and of his Christians?
0.278	Now, baby cloths usually come in two kinds. The outer one is made of rough wool, and the inner one of linen.
0.279	The outer, rough wool cloth stands for the testimony of the law. But the linen stands for the words of the prophets.
0.280	For example, Isaiah says in 7:14, "Look, a virgin will conceive and bear a son, and will call his name Immanuel." There are other passages like it. They would not be understood as being about Christ if the Gospel had not revealed it and shown that Christ is in them.
0.281	54. So here we have these two things, the faith and the Gospel. These, and nothing else, should be preached in all of Christendom.
0.282	Now let us see who should be the preachers and who should be the learners.
0.283	The preachers should be angels, that is, God's messengers. They should live a heavenly life and always be busy with God's Word, so that they never preach human teaching.
0.284	It makes no sense at all to be God's messenger and not spread God's message.
0.285	"Angelus" means a messenger, and Luke calls him God's messenger ("Angelus Domini").
0.286	The message is also more important than the messenger's life.
0.287	If he lives a wicked life, he only harms himself. But if he brings a false message instead of God's message, he leads astray and harms everyone who hears him. He causes idolatry among the people, because they accept lies as truth, honor people instead of God, and pray to the devil instead of to God.
0.288	55. There is no more terrible plague, disaster, or cause of grief on earth than a preacher who does not preach God's Word. Sadly, the world today is full of them. And yet they think they are godly and doing good. In fact their whole work is nothing but murdering souls, blaspheming God, and setting up idolatry. It would be much better for them if they were robbers, murderers, and the worst criminals, because then they would know they were doing wrong.
0.289	But now they go around with spiritual titles and outward show, as priest, bishop, pope. At the same time they are vicious wolves in sheep's clothing. It would be good if no one ever heard them preach.
0.290	56. The learners are shepherds, poor people out in the fields.
0.291	Here Jesus does what he says in Matthew
0.292	11:5: "And the poor have the good news preached to them." And in Matthew
0.293	5:3: "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs."
0.294	Here there are no learned people, no rich people, no powerful people. Such people usually do not accept the Gospel.
0.295	The Gospel is a heavenly treasure. It will not put up with any other treasure, and it will not get along with any earthly guest in the heart.
0.296	So whoever loves the one must let go of the other, as Christ says in Matthew
0.297	6:24: "You cannot serve God and mammon."
0.298	The shepherds show this by being out in the field, under the open sky, and not in houses. This shows that they do not hold on tightly to earthly things. Also, they are in the fields at night. The world does not know them and looks down on them. The world sleeps at night, and by day it loves to walk around so that it will be noticed. But the poor shepherds do their work at night.
0.299	They stand for all the humble people on earth. They are often despised and unnoticed, but they live only under heaven's protection. They long for the Gospel.
0.300	57. The fact that there were shepherds means that no one should hear the Gospel for himself alone. Everyone should tell it to others who do not know it.
0.301	For whoever believes has enough for himself. He should try to bring others to the same faith and knowledge. In this way one can be a shepherd to another, caring for him and leading him into the pasture of the Gospel in this world, during the night of this earthly life.
0.302	At first the shepherds were very frightened by the angel. For human nature is shocked when it first hears in the Gospel that all our works are nothing and are condemned before God. It does not easily give up its prejudices and its proud ideas.
0.303	58. Now let everyone examine himself in the light of the Gospel. Let him see how far he is from Christ, and what his faith and love are really like.
0.304	Many people are stirred with dreamy devotion when they hear about Christ's poverty. They are almost angry with the people of Bethlehem. They condemn their blindness and ingratitude. They think that if they had been there, they would have served the Lord and his mother better. They would not have let them be treated so miserably.
0.305	But they do not look right beside them to see how many people need their help. They leave them in their misery without help.
0.306	Who on earth does not have poor, miserable, sick, straying, or sinful people around him?
0.307	Why doesn't he show his love to them?
0.308	Why doesn't he do for them what Christ has done for him?
0.309	59. It is completely wrong to think that you have done much for Christ if you do nothing for those needy people.
0.310	If you had been in Bethlehem, you would have paid as little attention to Christ as they did. But now that it is known who Christ is, you claim to serve him.
0.311	If he came now and laid himself in a manger, and sent you word that it was he, the one you now know so much about, you might do something for him. But you would not have done it before.
0.312	Suppose the rich man in the Gospel had been told for certain how highly Lazarus would be raised up, and suppose he had been convinced of it. Then he would not have let him lie there and die as he did.
0.313	60. So if your neighbor were now what he will be in the future, and lay before you, you would surely pay attention to him.
0.314	But now, since that is not how it is, you are beating the air. You do not recognize the Lord in your neighbor. You do not do for him what he has done for you.
0.315	That is why God lets you be blinded and deceived by the pope and false preachers. So you waste on wood, stone, paper, and wax what you could have used to help others.
H:0.80	III. What the Angels' Song of Praise Means
0.316	61. Finally we must also talk about the angels' song, which we use every day in our worship: "Gloria in excelsis Deo."
0.317	There are three things to look at in this song: glory to God, peace to the earth, and good will to people.
0.318	The good will could be understood as God's own good will toward people through Christ.
0.319	But we will take it to mean the good will that is given to people through this birth. That is how it is put in the words "en anthropis eudokia, hominibus beneplacitum."
0.320	62. The first is glory to God.
0.321	This is also where we should begin. In all things the praise and glory should be given to God, as the one who does, gives, and owns all things. No one should take credit for anything or claim any merit for himself.
0.322	For the glory belongs to no one but God alone. It cannot be made common by being shared with any person.
0.323	63. Through the evil spirit Adam stole the glory and took it for himself. So all people have fallen into disgrace with him. This evil is so deeply rooted in all people that no vice in them is as great as vanity.
0.324	Everyone is very pleased with himself. No one wants to be nothing, and they want nothing. This spirit of vanity is the cause of all the trouble, fighting, and war on earth.
0.325	64. Christ has brought the glory back to God. He did this by teaching us that all we have or can do is nothing but wrath and displeasure before God. So we should not boast or be pleased with ourselves. Instead we should be filled with fear and shame. In this way our glory and self-satisfaction are crushed, and we are glad to be rid of them. Then we can be found and kept safe in Christ.
0.326	65. The second is peace on earth.
0.327	Where God's glory is not found, there must be fighting, as Solomon says in Proverbs 13:10: "By pride comes nothing but quarreling." In the same way, where God's glory is, there must be peace.
0.328	Why would they quarrel when they know that nothing is their own? All they are, all they have, and all they can want comes from God. They leave everything in his hands and are content to have such a gracious God.
0.329	He knows that all he has is nothing before God. He does not look for his own honor. Instead he thinks of the one who is something before God, that is, Christ.
0.330	66. So it follows that where there are true Christians, there is no fighting, quarreling, or discord. As Isaiah says in 2:4, "They will beat their swords into plowshares and their spears into pruning hooks. Nation will not lift up sword against nation, and they will not learn war anymore!"
0.331	67. That is why our Lord Christ is called a king of peace. He is pictured by King Solomon, whose name means "rich in peace." Inwardly, he wants to give us peace in our conscience toward God through faith. Outwardly, he wants us to show love to our fellow people. So through him there may be peace everywhere on earth.
0.332	68. The third is good will toward people.
0.333	Good will here does not mean the will that does good works. It means the good will and peace of heart that accepts equally whatever may happen, whether good or bad.
0.334	The angels knew very well that the peace they sang about does not reach beyond the Christians who truly believe. Such people certainly have peace among themselves.
0.335	But the world and the devil will not be corrected. They do not let them have peace, but persecute them to death. As Christ says in John 16:33, "In me you may have peace.
0.336	In the world you have trouble."
0.337	69. So it was not enough for the angels to sing about peace on earth. They added the good will toward people. That means that they take pleasure in all that God does, see all his dealings with them as wise and good, and praise and thank him for it.
0.338	They do not complain, but they gladly submit to God's will.
0.339	They know that God can do all things, and they have received him through faith in Christ as a gracious Father. So they celebrate and rejoice even when they are persecuted, as St. Paul says in Romans 5:3: "We also rejoice in our troubles."
0.340	They see all that happens to them as being for the best, because they are so richly satisfied in Christ.
0.341	70. See, this is the good will, the pleasure, the good opinion in all things, good or bad, that the angels want to express in their song. For where there is no good will, peace will not last long.
0.342	Unbelievers put the worst meaning on everything. They always make the bad bigger and double every misfortune.
0.343	So they do not like the way God deals with them. They want it to be different. And what is written in Psalm 18:25-26 comes true: "With the merciful you show yourself merciful, with the perfect man you show yourself perfect; with the pure you show yourself pure." That means: whoever takes pleasure in all that you do, you and all who are yours will take pleasure in him too. "And with the perverse you show yourself contrary." That means: since you and all you do do not please him, he does not please you or any who are yours.
0.344	71. About this good will St. Paul says in 1 Corinthians 10:33, "Just as I also please everyone in everything."
0.345	How does he do that?
0.346	If you are content and satisfied with everything, you will in turn please everyone.
0.347	It is a short rule: If you want to please no one, be pleased with no one. If you want to please everyone, be pleased with everyone. But only as far as you do not go against God's Word. For there all pleasing and displeasing stops.
0.348	But whatever can be left undone without doing violence to God's Word may be left undone. In this way you can please everyone and at the same time be faithful to God. Then you have this good will that the angels sing about.
0.349	72. From this song we can learn what kind of beings the angels are.
0.350	Do not pay attention to what the great masters dream up about them. Here they are all painted in such a way that you can see their hearts and their own thoughts.
0.351	First, they sing joyfully and give the glory to God. This shows how full of his light and fire they are. They do not praise themselves. They know that all things belong to God alone. So with great earnestness they give the glory to him, to whom it belongs.
0.352	So if you want to picture a humble, pure, obedient, and joyful heart that praises God, think of the angels.
0.353	This is their first step, the way they serve God.
0.354	73. The second is their love for us, as has been shown.
0.355	Here you see what great and kind friends we have in them. They care for us no less than for themselves. They rejoice in our well-being just as much as in their own. In fact, in this song they give us a very comforting reason to see them as the best of friends.
0.356	This is the right way to understand the angels. Not by what they are in their being, which the masters boldly try to describe, but by their inner heart, spirit, and mind. Though I do not know what they are, I know what they most desire and what they are always doing. In this way you look into their heart.
0.357	That is enough about this Gospel.
0.358	What Mary, Joseph, and Nazareth mean will be explained in Luke 1.
H:0.94	The Armor of This Gospel
0.359	74. This Gospel is the foundation of the article of our faith where we say, "I believe in Jesus Christ, born of the virgin Mary."
0.360	This article is also founded on other passages of Scripture, but on none as clearly as on this one.
0.361	St. Mark says no more than that Christ has a mother. It is the same with St. John. Neither of them says anything about his birth.
0.362	St. Matthew says he was born of Mary in Bethlehem, but he leaves it at that. He does not gloriously proclaim that Mary was a virgin, as we will hear in due time.
0.363	But Luke describes it clearly and carefully.
0.364	75. Long ago it was also announced by the patriarchs and prophets. For example, God says to Abraham in Genesis 22:17, "And in your offspring all the nations of the earth will be blessed."
0.365	And he says to David in Psalm 89:4 and 132:11, "Jehovah has sworn to David in truth, and he will not go back on it: from the fruit of your body I will set someone on your throne."
0.366	But those words are unclear compared with the Gospel.
0.367	76. It is also pictured in many figures. One is the rod of Aaron. Although it was a dry piece of wood, it budded in a supernatural way (Numbers 7:5).
0.368	In the same way Mary, without any natural conception, gave birth in a supernatural way to a real and true natural son. Just so the rod bore natural almonds and still remained a natural rod.
0.369	Another figure is Gideon's fleece (Judges 6:37). It was wet with the dew of heaven while the ground around it stayed dry. There are many similar figures that we do not need to list.
0.370	These figures do not conflict with faith. Instead they adorn it. For it must first be firmly believed before I can believe that the figure helps to illustrate it.
0.371	77. There is a great deal in this article that we would not want to lose in times of temptation. For the evil spirit attacks nothing as fiercely as our faith.
0.372	So it is very important for us to know where in God's Word this faith is taught. Then in times of temptation we can point to that, for the evil spirit cannot stand against God's Word.
0.373	78. There are also many moral teachings in this Gospel, such as meekness, patience, poverty, and the like. But these have been touched on enough, and they are not points of dispute. For they are fruits of faith and good works.
