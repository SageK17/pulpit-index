2.233	12. The word “world” doesn’t only mean the things God made—heaven and earth, the sea, things we can see and things we can’t. The people who live in the world are also called “the world.” It’s like the word “house.” We use it for the walls, and also for the people who live inside.
2.234	Sometimes we praise a house but find fault with the people who live in it.
2.235	We say, “That’s a good house,” because it has marble and beautiful ceilings. But we also say, “That’s a good house,” in another way. We mean that no one there is treated unfairly. No one there robs or bullies anyone.
2.236	Then we are praising the people who live in the building, not the building itself. Yet we call both of them a “house.”
2.237	All who love the world live in the world through their love. In the same way, some people live in heaven: their hearts are up there, even while their bodies walk on earth. So, as I said, all who love the world are called “the world.”
2.238	These people have only three things: “the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and empty pride in this life.”
2.239	They crave eating, drinking and sex. They want to enjoy these pleasures.
2.240	Surely it doesn’t mean there is no proper amount of these things. When it says, “Don’t love these things,” does that mean you must not eat, or drink, or have children?
2.241	That is not what it is saying.
2.242	Just keep a limit, for the Creator’s sake, so that these things don’t tie you down because you love them. Don’t love for pleasure the things you should only use.
2.243	But you are only really tested when two things are put in front of you and you must choose one: Do you want to do what is right, or do you want to make money?
2.244	“But I don’t have enough to live on. I don’t have enough to eat or drink.”
2.245	But what if the only way you can get these things is by doing wrong?
2.246	Isn’t it better to love what you can never lose than to lose yourself by doing wrong?
2.247	You see the gold you gain. You don’t see the faith you lose.
2.248	So this is what he tells us “the desires of the body” means: craving the things that belong to the body, like food and sex, and all other things like that.
2.249	13. “And the desires of the eyes.” By “the desires of the eyes” he means every kind of curiosity.
2.250	And curiosity reaches so far!
2.251	Curiosity is what is at work in public shows, in theaters, in the devil’s rituals, in magic, and in dealings with dark powers. It is nothing but curiosity.
2.252	Sometimes it even tempts God’s servants. They want to work a miracle, so to speak. They want to test God to see if He will answer their prayers by doing miracles. That is curiosity. That is “the desires of the eyes.” It “does not come from the Father.”
2.253	If God has given you the power, then do the miracle, because He has made it possible for you. But don’t think that people who have never done miracles won’t be part of God’s kingdom.
2.254	The apostles were once very happy that the demons obeyed them. What did the Lord say to them then?
2.255	“Don’t be happy about this. Be happy because your names are written in heaven.”
2.256	He wanted the apostles to be happy about the very same thing that makes you happy.
2.257	How terrible for you if your name is not written in heaven!
2.258	But is it terrible for you if you don’t raise the dead? Is it terrible for you if you don’t walk on the sea? Is it terrible for you if you don’t drive out demons?
2.259	If you have been given the power to do these things, use it humbly, not proudly.
2.260	The Lord even said that certain false prophets “will do signs and wonders.”
2.261	So don’t have “worldly ambition.” Ambitio saeculi means pride.
2.262	Such a man wants to make himself important through the honors he receives. He thinks he is great because he is rich or because he has some power.
2.263	14. There are these three things. You will not find anything that can tempt human desire except these three: the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and pride in this life.
2.264	The devil tempted the Lord with these same three things.
2.265	He was tempted with the desires of the body when He was hungry after His fast. The devil said to Him, “If You are the Son of God, tell these stones to become bread.”
2.266	But how did He push the tempter away? And how did He teach His soldier to fight?
2.267	Listen to what He said to him: “A person does not live on bread alone, but on every word of God.”
2.268	He was also tempted with the desires of the eyes, through a miracle. The devil said to Him, “Throw Yourself down. For it is written: He will give His angels orders to watch over You. They will hold You up in their hands, so that You never strike Your foot against a stone.”
2.269	He resisted the tempter. If He had done the miracle, it would only have looked as though He had given in, or had done it out of curiosity. For He did miracles whenever He wanted, because He is God—but He did them to heal the weak.
2.270	If He had done it at that moment, people might have thought He just wanted to perform a miracle.
2.271	So that no one would think that, listen to how He answered. And when you face a temptation like this, you say the same thing: “Get behind Me, Satan! For it is written: You must not test the Lord your God.” In other words: if I do this, I will be testing God.
2.272	He said what He wants you to say.
2.273	The enemy may whisper to you, “What kind of person are you? What kind of Christian are you?
2.274	Have you done even one miracle yet? Have your prayers raised anyone from the dead? Have you healed anyone with a fever? If you really mattered, you would do some miracle.” Then answer him: “It is written: You must not test the Lord your God.” So I will not test God. I will not act as if I belong to God only if I do a miracle, and don’t belong to Him if I don’t. Otherwise, what happens to His words, “Be happy because your names are written in heaven”?
2.275	How was the Lord tempted with “pride in this life”?
2.276	The devil took Him up to a high place and said to Him, “I will give You all of this if You will bow down and worship me.”
2.277	He wanted to tempt the King of all worlds with the greatness of an earthly kingdom. But the Lord who made heaven and earth crushed the devil under His feet.
2.278	Is it any great thing for the Lord to defeat the devil?
2.279	So what was He doing when He answered the devil? He was teaching you the answer He wants you to give.
2.280	“It is written: Worship the Lord your God, and serve Him alone.”
2.281	If you hold on to these things, you will not have the world’s cravings. And if you don’t have the world’s cravings, then the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and pride in this life will not rule over you. And you will make room for Love when she comes, so that you can love God.
2.282	For if love for the world is there, love for God will not be there.
2.283	Instead, hold on to the love of God. Then, just as God lasts forever and ever, you too can last forever and ever. For each person is like what he loves.
2.284	Do you love the earth? You will be earth.
2.285	Do you love God? What should I say—that you will be a god?
2.286	I don’t dare say that on my own. Let’s listen to the Scriptures: “I have said, You are gods, and all of you are sons of the Most High.”
2.287	So if you want to be gods and sons of the Most High, then “don’t love the world or the things in the world.
2.288	If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.
2.289	For everything in the world—the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and pride in this life—does not come from the Father. It comes from the world.” That means it comes from people who love the world.
2.290	“And the world is passing away, and its desires with it. But whoever does the will of God lasts forever, just as God also lasts forever.”
T:3	Homily 3 (1 John 2:18–27)
3.1	“Children, it is the last hour. You have heard that the antichrist is coming, and even now many antichrists have appeared. That is how we know it is the last hour.
3.2	They went out from us, but they were not truly part of us. If they had been part of us, they would surely have stayed with us. But they went out so that it would become clear that not all of them were part of us.
3.3	But you have been anointed by the Holy One, and you know all things.
3.4	I am writing to you, not because you don’t know the truth, but because you do know it, and you know that no lie comes from the truth.
3.5	Who is a liar? Whoever denies that Jesus is the Christ.
3.6	[Whoever denies the Father and the Son is the antichrist.]
3.7	Whoever denies the Son does not have the Father or the Son. But whoever acknowledges the Son has both the Father and the Son.
3.8	So let what you heard from the beginning stay in you.
3.9	If what you heard from the beginning stays in you, you will also remain in the Son and in the Father.
3.10	And this is what He has promised us: eternal life.
3.11	I have written these things to you about those who are trying to lead you astray. I want you to know that you have an anointing, and that the anointing you received from Him should stay in you.
3.12	You don’t need anyone to teach you, because His anointing teaches you about all things.”
3.13	1. “Children, it is the last hour.”
3.14	In this reading he speaks to the children. He wants them to hurry up and grow, because “it is the last hour.”
3.15	You can’t choose how old or how tall your body is.
3.16	A person doesn’t grow physically whenever he wants, any more than he is born whenever he wants. But where being born depends on the will, growing depends on the will too.
3.17	No one is “born of water and the Spirit” unless he is willing.
3.18	So if he chooses, he grows and increases. If he chooses, he shrinks.
3.19	What does it mean to grow?
3.20	To move forward by making progress.
3.21	What does it mean to shrink?
3.22	To slide backward by falling short.
3.23	Whoever knows he has been born should understand that he is a baby. He should eagerly hold on to his mother’s breasts, and he will grow quickly.
3.24	His mother is the Church. Her breasts are the two Testaments of the holy Scriptures.
3.25	So he should drink the milk of all the things that happened in history for our eternal salvation, as signs of spiritual truths. Then, fed and made strong, he can go on to eat solid food. That solid food is this: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”
3.26	Our milk is Christ in His humility. Our solid food is the very same Christ, equal with the Father.
3.27	He feeds you with milk so that later He can feed you with bread. For to touch Christ spiritually with your heart means to know that He is equal with the Father.
3.28	2. That is why He told Mary not to touch Him. He said to her, “Don’t touch Me, because I have not yet gone up to the Father.”
3.29	What does this mean? He let the disciples handle Him. Did He then avoid Mary’s touch?
3.30	Isn’t He the same one who said to the doubting disciple, “Put your fingers here, and feel the scars”?
3.31	Had He gone up to the Father by then?
3.32	Then why does He stop Mary and say, “Don’t touch Me, because I have not yet gone up to the Father”?
3.33	Or should we say that He wasn’t afraid of being touched by men, but was afraid of being touched by women?
3.34	His touch makes all flesh clean.
3.35	He chose to show Himself first to women. Was He afraid of being handled by them?
3.36	Wasn’t His resurrection announced to the men by women? That way the serpent would be defeated by a kind of counter-plan.
3.37	The serpent first brought news of death to man through a woman. So news of life was also brought to men through a woman.
3.38	Then why did He not want to be touched? Only because He wanted us to understand a spiritual kind of touch.
3.39	Spiritual touch comes from a pure heart.
3.40	A person touches Christ with a pure heart when he understands that Christ is fully equal with the Father.
3.41	But someone who does not yet understand that Christ is God reaches only His body. He does not reach His divine nature.
3.42	And what is so great about reaching only what the persecutors who crucified Him reached?
3.43	The great thing is this: to understand that the Word is God with God, in the beginning, and that all things were made through Him. That is how He wanted to be known when He said to Philip, “I have been with you such a long time, and you still don’t know Me, Philip?
3.44	Whoever sees Me also sees the Father.”
3.45	3. But in case anyone is lazy about moving forward, let him listen: “Children, it is the last hour.”
3.46	Move forward! Run! Grow! “It is the last hour.”
3.47	This last hour is long, but it is still the last.
3.48	He used the word “hour” to mean “the last time,” because our Lord Jesus Christ will come in the last times.
3.49	But some people will say, How can these be the last times? How can this be the last hour?
3.50	Surely the antichrist will come first, and after that the day of judgment will come.
3.51	John saw that people would think like this. He didn’t want them to feel too safe and think it wasn’t the last hour yet just because the antichrist was still to come. So he said to them, “You have heard that the antichrist is coming, and now many antichrists have come.”
3.52	Could there be many antichrists if it were not “the last hour”?
3.53	4. Who are the people he calls antichrists?
3.54	He goes on and explains it.
3.55	“That is how we know it is the last hour.”
3.56	How do we know? Because “many antichrists have come.
3.57	They went out from us.” Look—there are the antichrists!
3.58	“They went out from us.” So we grieve over losing them.
3.59	Now hear the comfort: “But they were not truly part of us.”
3.60	All heretics and all who split the Church went out from us. That means they leave the Church. But they would not leave if they were truly part of us.
3.61	So even before they left, they were not part of us.
3.62	And if they were not part of us even before they left, then many people are still inside. They have not left, but they are still antichrists.
3.63	We dare to say this. Why? So that no one who is inside will be an antichrist.
3.64	For he is about to describe the antichrists and point them out, and we will see them in a moment.
3.65	And each person should question his own conscience and ask whether he is an antichrist.
3.66	In our language, “antichrist” means “against Christ.”
3.67	Some people think the name means he will come ante Christum—“before Christ.” That is,
3.68	that Christ will come after him. But it doesn’t mean that, and that is not how it is written. It is written Antichristus, which means “against Christ.”
3.69	You already see from the apostle’s own explanation who is against Christ. You understand that only antichrists can go out. Those who are not against Christ can never go out.
3.70	Anyone who is not against Christ holds on firmly in His body and is counted as one of its parts.
3.71	The parts of a body never fight against each other.
3.72	The whole body is made up of all its parts.
3.73	And what does the apostle say about how the parts agree with each other?
3.74	“If one part of the body suffers, every part suffers along with it; and if one part is given glory, every part shares its joy.”
3.75	So when one part is given glory, the other parts share its joy; and when it suffers, all the parts suffer. In a body whose parts agree like this, there is no antichrist.
3.76	But some people are inside the body of our Lord Jesus Christ in a different way. His body is still being healed, and it will not be perfectly healthy until the resurrection of the dead. These people are in Christ’s body the way bad fluids are in a sick body.
3.77	When the body vomits them up, it feels better. In the same way, when bad people leave, the Church feels relief.
3.78	And when the body vomits and throws them out, a person says, “These fluids came out of me, but they were not really part of me.”
3.79	How were they not part of me?
3.80	They were not cut out of my flesh. They just weighed on my chest while they were in me.
3.81	5. “They went out from us, but”—don’t be sad—“they were not truly part of us.”
3.82	How can you prove this?
3.83	If they had been part of us, they would surely have stayed with us.
3.84	So you can see from this that many people who are not part of us still receive the sacraments with us. They receive baptism with us. They receive with us what the faithful know they receive: the blessing, the Eucharist, and everything in the holy sacraments. They even share in the very altar with us—and yet they are not part of us.
3.85	Temptation proves that they are not part of us.
3.86	When temptation comes, they are blown away as if by the wind, because they were never grain.
3.87	And as we must often tell you, all of them will be blown away when the Lord begins to separate the grain from the chaff on His threshing floor on the day of judgment.
3.88	“They went out from us, but they were not truly part of us. If they had been part of us, they would surely have stayed with us.”
3.89	Do you want to know, dear friends, how very certain this saying is? Those who may have gone out but then come back are not antichrists. They are not against Christ.
3.90	Those who are not antichrists cannot possibly stay outside.
3.91	But each person chooses for himself whether to be an antichrist or to be in Christ.
3.92	Either we are among the parts of the body, or among the bad fluids.
3.93	Whoever changes for the better is in the body, as one of its parts. But whoever stays in his wickedness is a bad fluid. And when he has gone out, those who were weighed down by him will feel relief.
3.94	“They went out from us, but they were not truly part of us. If they had been part of us, they would surely have stayed with us. But they went out so that it would become clear that not all of them were part of us.”
3.95	He added the words “so that it would become clear” because even while they are inside, they are not part of us. But it isn’t clear then. It only becomes clear when they go out.
3.96	“And you have an anointing from the Holy One, so that you can be seen clearly by your own selves.”
3.97	The spiritual anointing is the Holy Spirit Himself. The visible anointing is the sacrament—the outward sign—of this.
3.98	He says that everyone who has this anointing of Christ can tell the bad from the good. They don’t need to be taught, because the anointing itself teaches them.
3.99	6. “I am writing to you, not because you don’t know the truth, but because you do know it, and you know that no lie comes from the truth.”
3.100	Look—here we are shown how to recognize the antichrist.
3.101	What is Christ? The Truth.
3.102	He Himself said, “I am the Truth.”
3.103	But “no lie comes from the truth.”
3.104	So everyone who lies does not yet belong to Christ.
3.105	He did not say that some lies come from the truth and some lies don’t.
3.106	Pay attention to these words. Don’t pamper yourselves. Don’t flatter yourselves. Don’t fool yourselves. Don’t cheat yourselves. “No lie comes from the truth.”
3.107	So let’s see how the antichrists lie, because there is more than one kind of lying.
3.108	“Who is a liar? Whoever denies that Jesus is the Christ.”
3.109	The word “Jesus” means one thing, and the word “Christ” means another. There is only one Jesus Christ our Savior, but “Jesus” is His personal name.
3.110	Moses was called by his personal name, and so were Elijah and Abraham. In the same way, our Lord has “Jesus” as His personal name. But “Christ” is the name of His holy office.
3.111	It is like when we say “Prophet” or “Priest.” The name “Christ” tells us that He is the Anointed One, through whom the whole people would be rescued.
3.112	The Jewish people were hoping for this Christ to come. But because He came humbly, they did not recognize Him. The stone was small, so they tripped over it and were broken.
3.113	But “the stone grew and became a great mountain.” And what does Scripture say?
3.114	“Whoever trips over this stone will be broken, and anyone this stone comes down on will be crushed to powder.”
3.115	We need to notice the difference in the words. It says whoever trips will be broken, but whoever it comes down on will be crushed to powder.
3.116	At first, people tripped over Him because He came humbly. But He will come in greatness to judge, and He will crush to powder anyone He comes down on.
3.117	But when He comes in the future, He will not crush anyone whom He did not break when He first came.
3.118	Whoever did not trip over the humble Christ will not need to fear the glorious Christ.
3.119	You have heard it in a few words, brothers and sisters: whoever did not trip over the humble Christ will not need to fear the glorious Christ.
3.120	For Christ is a stumbling stone to all bad people. Everything Christ says tastes bitter to them.
3.121	7. So listen and see.
3.122	All who go out from the Church and are cut off from the Church’s unity are certainly antichrists. No one should doubt it. The apostle himself pointed them out: “They went out from us, but they were not truly part of us. If they had been part of us, they would surely have stayed with us.”
3.123	So those who do not stay with us but go out from us are clearly antichrists.
3.124	And how can we prove that they are antichrists?
3.125	By their lying. “And who is a liar? Whoever denies that Jesus is the Christ.”
3.126	Let’s question the heretics. Where will you find a heretic who denies that Jesus is the Christ?
3.127	Look now, my dear friends—here is a great mystery.
3.128	Notice what the Lord God may have inspired in us, and what I would love to plant in your minds.
3.129	Look: they went out from us and became Donatists. We ask them whether Jesus is the Christ, and right away they declare that Jesus is the Christ.
3.130	So if an antichrist is someone who denies that Jesus is the Christ, then they can’t call us antichrists, and we can’t call them that either. That would mean they did not go out from us, and we did not go out from them.
3.131	And if we have not gone out from each other, then we are united. But if we are united, why are there two altars in this city? Why are there divided households and divided marriages? Why do husband and wife share one bed but have a divided Christ?
3.132	He is warning us. He wants us to admit the truth: either they went out from us, or we went out from them.
3.133	But no one should imagine that we went out from them.
3.134	We have the Lord’s will, which names His inheritance. We read it aloud, and there we find: “I will give You the nations as Your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as Your possession.”
3.135	We hold on to Christ’s inheritance. They do not, because they are not in fellowship with the whole world. They are not in fellowship with the worldwide body that was bought back by the Lord’s blood.
3.136	We have the Lord Himself, risen from the dead, who let His doubting disciples touch Him with their hands. While they were still doubting, He said to them, “Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead on the third day, and repentance and forgiveness of sins had to be preached in His name”—Where? In which direction? To whom?—“to all nations, starting from Jerusalem.”
3.137	Our minds are at peace about the unity of the inheritance!
3.138	Whoever is not in fellowship with this inheritance has gone out.
3.139	8. But let’s not be sad: “They went out from us, but they were not truly part of us. If they had been part of us, they would surely have stayed with us.”
3.140	So if they went out from us, they are antichrists. If they are antichrists, they are liars. If they are liars, they deny that Jesus is the Christ.
3.141	Once again we come back to the hard part of the question.
3.142	Ask them one at a time, and they all declare that Jesus is the Christ.
3.143	The difficulty that holds us back comes from taking the words of the letter in too narrow a way.
3.144	Anyway, you see the question. If it isn’t understood, this question leaves both us and them stuck.
3.145	Either we are antichrists or they are antichrists. They call us antichrists and say we went out from them. We say the same about them.
3.146	But this letter has given us a mark to recognize the antichrists by: “Whoever denies that Jesus is the Christ” is “an antichrist.”
3.147	So now let’s find out who denies Him. And let’s look not at the tongue, but at the actions.
3.148	For if you ask everyone, they will all say with one voice that Jesus is the Christ.
3.149	Let the tongue be quiet for a little while. Ask the life instead.
3.150	Suppose we find this: suppose Scripture itself tells us that you can deny Christ not only with your words but also with your actions. Then we will certainly find many antichrists. With their mouths they say they believe in Christ, but the way they live disagrees with Christ.
3.151	Where do we find this in Scripture?
3.152	Listen to Paul the apostle. He says about such people, “They say openly that they know God, but their actions deny Him.”
3.153	We find that these people are antichrists too. Anyone who denies Christ by his actions is an antichrist.
3.154	I don’t listen to what he says. I look at how he lives.
3.155	Actions speak—so do we need words?
3.156	After all, what bad person doesn’t want to speak well?
3.157	But what does the Lord say to people like that?
3.158	“You hypocrites! How can you say good things when you are evil?”
3.159	You bring your voices to My ears, but I look into your thoughts.
3.160	I see an evil will there, and you are showing off fake fruit.
3.161	I know what I should pick, and where to pick it from. I don’t “pick figs from thistles.” I don’t pick “grapes from thorns.” For “every tree is known by its fruit.”
3.162	The antichrist who lies even more is the one who says with his mouth that Jesus is the Christ, but denies Him with his actions.
3.163	He is a liar because he says one thing and does another.
3.164	9. So, brothers and sisters, if we are to judge by actions, we find not only many antichrists who have gone out, but also many who are not yet exposed and have not gone out at all.
3.165	The Church has many people inside it who break their oaths, cheat others, practice dark magic, go to fortune-tellers, commit adultery, get drunk, lend money at unfair interest, and kidnap boys—plus all the other sins we can’t even list. All these things go against the teaching of Christ. They go against the word of God.
3.166	Now the Word of God is Christ. So whatever goes against the Word of God belongs to the Antichrist.
3.167	For “Antichrist” means “against Christ.”
3.168	Do you want to know how openly these people resist Christ?
3.169	Sometimes they do something evil, and someone begins to correct them. They don’t dare to insult Christ, so they insult His ministers who are correcting them. But if you show them that you are speaking Christ’s words and not your own, they try as hard as they can to prove that you are speaking your own words and not Christ’s. But if it is clear that you are speaking Christ’s words, they go even against Christ. They start to find fault with Christ. “How,” they say, “and why did He make us the way we are?”
3.170	Don’t people say this every day when their actions are exposed?
3.171	Twisted by a wicked will, they blame their Maker.
3.172	Their Maker calls to them from heaven (for the One who made us is the same One who made us new): What did I make you?
3.173	I made a human being, not greed. I made a human being, not robbery. I made a human being, not adultery.
3.174	You have heard that My works praise Me.
3.175	You heard it from the mouths of the Three Young Men—it was that very hymn that protected them from the fire.
3.176	The Lord’s works praise the Lord. The heavens, the earth and the sea praise Him. Everything in the heavens praises Him. The angels praise Him, the stars praise Him, the lights praise Him. Whatever swims, whatever flies, whatever walks and whatever crawls praises Him. All these praise the Lord.
3.177	Did you hear in that hymn that greed praises the Lord?
3.178	Did you hear that drunkenness praises the Lord?
3.179	Or that wild living praises Him, or that silliness praises Him?
3.180	Whatever you do not hear praising the Lord in that hymn, the Lord did not make.
3.181	Fix what you have made, so that what God made in you can be saved.
3.182	But if you refuse, and you love and cling to your sins, you are against Christ.
3.183	Whether you are inside or outside, you are an antichrist. Whether you are inside or outside, you are chaff.
3.184	So why aren’t you outside?
3.185	Because no wind has come along yet to blow you away.
3.186	10. These things are now clear, my brothers and sisters.
3.187	No one should say, “I don’t worship Christ, but I worship God His Father.”
3.188	“Everyone who denies the Son has neither the Son nor the Father. And whoever confesses the Son has both the Son and the Father.”
3.189	He is speaking to you who are grain. And let those who were chaff listen and become grain.
3.190	Each person should look carefully at his own conscience. If he loves the world, he should change. He should become someone who loves Christ, so that he will not be an antichrist.
3.191	If someone tells him he is an antichrist, he gets angry. He thinks he has been treated unfairly. And if the person calling him an antichrist is someone he is quarreling with, he may even threaten to take him to court.
3.192	Christ says to him: Be patient. If people have said false things about you, be glad with Me, because the antichrists say false things about Me too. But if what they say about you is true, make peace with your own conscience. And if you are afraid of being called this, be even more afraid of being it.
3.193	11. “So let what you heard from the beginning stay in you.
3.194	If what you heard from the beginning stays in you, you will also stay in the Son and in the Father.
3.195	And this is what He has promised us.”
3.196	Maybe you will ask about the reward and say: Look, “I keep safe in me what I heard from the beginning. I obey it. To keep holding on, I put up with dangers, hard work and temptations. But what good does it do me? What is my reward? What will He give me later, since in this world I see myself struggling among temptations?
3.197	I don’t see any rest here. Just being mortal weighs my soul down, and my body, which decays, drags it down to lower things. But I put up with everything, so that ‘what I heard from the beginning’ will ‘stay’ in me, and so that I can say to my God, ‘Because of the words from Your lips, I have kept to hard roads.’”
3.198	So what is the reward?
3.199	Listen, and don’t give up.
3.200	If you were growing weak in your work, let the promised reward make you strong.
3.201	What worker in a vineyard would let the pay he is going to get slip out of his mind?
3.202	If he forgot it, his hands would grow weak.
3.203	Remembering the promised pay keeps him working steadily. And yet the one who promised it is only a human being, who might let you down.
3.204	How much stronger should you be in God’s field! The One who made the promise is the Truth. No one else can ever take His place, He can never die, and He can never deceive the person He made the promise to!
3.205	And what is the promise?
3.206	Let’s see what He has promised.
3.207	Is it gold, which people here love so much? Or silver?
3.208	Or property, which people spend their gold on, no matter how much they love gold?
3.209	Or beautiful land, big houses, many slaves, lots of animals?
3.210	These are not the wages, so to speak, that he urges us to work hard for.
3.211	What is this reward called? “Eternal life.”
3.212	You heard it, and you shouted for joy. Love what you have heard, and you will be set free from your hard work into the rest of eternal life.
3.213	Look, this is what God promises: “eternal life.”
3.214	Look, this is what God warns of: eternal fire.
3.215	What will He say to those placed on His right?
3.216	“Come, you whom My Father has blessed. Take the kingdom made ready for you since the world began.”
3.217	And what to those on the left?
3.218	“Go into the eternal fire that was prepared for the devil and his angels.”
3.219	You don’t love that yet—so at least be afraid of this.
3.220	12. So remember, my brothers and sisters, that Christ has promised us eternal life. “This,” he says, “is the promise He has promised us: eternal life.
3.221	I have written these things to you about those who are trying to lead you astray.”
3.222	Don’t let anyone lead you astray to death. Long for the promise of eternal life.
3.223	What can the world promise?
3.224	Let it promise whatever you like—it may be making that promise to someone who will die tomorrow.
3.225	And how will you dare to face Him who lasts forever when you leave this life?
3.226	“But a powerful man is threatening me, to force me to do something evil.”
3.227	What does he threaten you with?
3.228	Prison, chains, fire, torture, wild animals. Yes—but not eternal fire, is it?
3.229	Fear what the One Almighty threatens. Love what the One Almighty promises. Then the whole world will seem worthless to us, whether it makes promises or makes threats.
3.230	“I have written these things to you about those who are trying to lead you astray. I want you to know that you have an anointing, and that the anointing we have received from Him should stay in you.”
3.231	In the anointing we have the sacramental sign [of something unseen], but its power is invisible. The invisible anointing is the Holy Spirit. The invisible anointing is that love which will be like a root in anyone who has it. No matter how hot the sun gets, he cannot dry up.
3.232	Anything that has roots is fed by the sun’s heat, not dried up by it.
