0.1	“And the second is like it: You must love your neighbor as yourself.” Matthew 22:39
0.2	I will speak about this part of our Lord’s summary of God’s law. First, I will ask what KIND of love is required here.
0.3	Second, I will name some of the things this love includes.
0.4	Third, I will show that nothing less than this love is true humanity.
0.5	Last, I will show that nothing less is true morality.
0.6	I. The love we must have for our neighbor is certainly not being pleased with his character.
0.7	Being pleased with someone’s character means approving of it and delighting in it. But our Lord makes no difference between good people and bad people. So he requires us to love them all.
0.8	But he cannot be asking us to approve of bad people’s character and delight in it. So we must conclude that he does not have this kind of love in mind. It is not what he requires.
0.9	To be pleased with the character of wicked people because they are wicked is to be as bad as they are.
0.10	No sensible person can think that this is what the Savior requires. No one can think that this is what he says God’s law requires.
0.11	Also, it is not the fond affection we sometimes feel for certain people.
0.12	Some people are naturally pleasing to certain others. Some are pleasing to everyone. They are naturally likable and stir up pleasant feelings.
0.13	But this is not the love the text is talking about.
0.14	Also, this love cannot be something that happens to us apart from our will.
0.15	Earlier I spoke about the love we must have for God. I said it must be a free choice. It cannot happen apart from our will. If it did, God could not fairly demand it. I say the same about this love for our neighbor.
0.16	It cannot happen apart from our will. If it did, no fair and just being could require it.
0.17	To put it positively, this love for our neighbor is goodwill. It must be goodwill.
0.18	God’s love for people is goodwill. It is a pure and strong concern for their welfare. It is a desire for their happiness. He actively wills their happiness as the thing dearest to his heart.
0.19	The way his great love has shown itself proves this.
0.20	Our own reason tells us that this is the love God has had for our race, and still has.
0.21	So we must conclude that this is the love he requires people to show to each other.
0.22	Notice also that this love requires us to value our neighbor’s interests as our own.
0.23	This rule applies to all our neighbors. It applies to our enemies as well as to our friends.
0.24	Also, it is a steady love. It does not come and go, and it does not fade away. It does not act on sudden impulse. It flows from a spring of goodwill that lasts forever.
0.25	It is a state of mind. It is a settled state that treats our neighbor’s interests as our own.
H:0.11	II. Now I will name some things this love includes.
0.26	(1.) It does not mean we must divide our energy and resources equally among all people. It does not even mean we must divide them equally among everyone near enough for us to know.
0.27	It cannot mean this. If it did, no one could obey it.
0.28	There is no doubt that God’s law demands goodwill toward all people, always and in every situation. But some situations rule out ways of showing it that would be right at other times.
0.29	Think of a criminal who is serving a just sentence under human law. We must not do the same kind things for him that would be fitting after he has served his sentence, or if he were not under sentence at all.
0.30	Sinners come to stand under the sentence of God’s law. Their relationship to God is then such that he cannot bless them.
0.31	It is not that he has stopped loving them. He still has a deep, intense concern for their happiness. But he loves all the rest of his thinking creatures just as much. And their interests demand that he carry out his righteous law against the wicked.
0.32	So he cannot give them even as much good as a cup of cold water.
0.33	Sometimes the same kind of situation may require us, too, to hold back from active efforts to do good to the wicked.
0.34	Also, this law requires each person to love his neighbor. So it is clear that God means these kindnesses to go both ways.
0.35	If God does good to us, we should gratefully try to do good to him.
0.36	If he advances our interests, we should work hard to advance his.
0.37	The same is true of children toward their parents.
0.38	Children should not always receive and never give. They should count it a great privilege to repay their parents for all the work and care given to them.
0.39	Parents may live long enough to become old and helpless. Then their children should be glad for the chance to repay the kindness shown to them when they too were helpless.
0.40	The same is true of citizens and rulers.
0.41	In the same way, students and teachers have interests that go both ways.
0.42	Neither side should do all the receiving and none of the giving. Both sides should give and receive.
0.43	In the same way, this school, with its teachers and students, has a close relationship with the people who founded it and support it.
0.44	Others have done the work, and we have come in to enjoy the results of their work.
0.45	Others have given their money, and we are enjoying the benefit.
0.46	Every building here owes something to donors from other places.
0.47	Others have prayed, and God has blessed us because of those prayers.
0.48	So we should try to repay those kindnesses. We should do all we can to advance the very goals to which these Christian friends have given so much of their wealth and prayers.
0.49	In the same way, ministers who preach and the people who listen should give good to each other and receive good from each other.
0.50	All of us should do more than just receive good from others. We should work hard to do good to others as well, and give back to them generously.
0.51	Why shouldn’t this principle apply to all people in their relationship with God?
0.52	Some of you have never cared about God. Is it right for you to receive everything from God and give him nothing back? Is it right to give him no love and no obedience?
0.53	Do you have no zeal for his honor? Are you not devoted to his interests at all?
0.54	He has fed you and raised you like a child, and you have done nothing but rebel against him.
0.55	Is that right? Why don’t you say instead, “God has given me talents. I must give them back to him by using them whenever I have the chance”?
0.56	Surely you must see God as your neighbor in this sense: He has interests and rights. And you have the highest duty to repay Him for countless kindnesses.
0.57	The same is also true of how you relate to the church of Jesus Christ.
0.58	How much do you owe Him?
0.59	After all this, do you have any right to say, “He will never get one word of thanks from me! He will never get one thank offering from me!”?
0.60	What don’t you owe him!
0.61	Have you ever realized that you really owe him your very life? Without his mission of mercy, you would never have lived.
0.62	Without that offering and sacrifice on Calvary, none of us could have existed at all.
0.63	Without it, Adam and Eve would have been cut down at once in their sin. The law would have carried out its righteous judgment: “On the day you eat of it, you will surely die.”
0.64	So you are alive only because God has had mercy on our race.
0.65	Come now, step up right away and face what this great truth demands of you.
0.66	Don’t you owe God everything?
0.67	And will you pay back nothing at all?
0.68	Here you are in God’s house, surrounded by prayer. You could be in hell instead, screaming and wailing in the depths of despair!
0.69	Do you say, “I don’t owe Christ anything”?
0.70	But you claim to be a respectable person.
0.71	Yet who can respect you if you treat Jesus Christ like this?
0.72	Don’t you feel anything for the great sacrifices he made and the suffering he went through to save you?
0.73	Would you leave all the work and sacrifice to him, and give nothing back in love or thanks?
0.74	Will you flatly refuse to love him?
0.75	Do you say, “He is welcome to love me and die for me. But I have nothing to give him in return.
0.76	I leave it to him to do everything and suffer everything. He will not get a single word of thanks from me”?
0.77	Do you think this is right?
0.78	Is it generous? Should it be called respectable?
0.79	III. Nothing less than the love required here is true humanity.
0.80	It is not true humanity to do good only to your own children.
0.81	People think of their children as part of themselves. So doing good only to them is nothing more than selfishness stretched a little wider.
0.82	Only one kind of love is truly love for people. Only one kind is true humanity. It is the love that values human well-being for its own worth, and loves people simply because they are human.
0.83	IV. Nothing less than this is true morality.
0.84	Our reason and our conscience require nothing less.
0.85	It is not true morality to put special weight on our own interests just because they are ours.
0.86	It is still not true morality, even if we try to be honest in seeking the good of certain people.
0.87	If we love only certain people, that is favoritism.
0.88	Also, no one loves his friends in the way that pleases God unless he also truly loves his enemies.
0.89	Suppose someone does love his friends.
0.90	Listen to what Jesus Christ says about exactly this case: “If you love those who love you, what reward do you get?”
0.91	“Don’t even tax collectors [notorious sinners] do the same?”
0.92	Jesus Christ says, “Love your enemies.”
0.93	No one can ever be thought to do right unless he loves his enemies.
0.94	Someone says, “There are certain people I never wrong.”
0.95	What is your reason for doing them good and not harm?
0.96	If you truly loved your neighbor for the right reasons, you would love every neighbor. You would see every living person as your neighbor, in the sense this law means.
0.97	You would love every being you know of. That is because you would love to increase the happiness of every living, feeling being. You would try to love each one according to how much his well-being is worth.
0.98	Real goodwill would try just as hard to do good to enemies as to friends, if it could reach them. It would do them just as much good.
0.99	Understand this: to love your friend in the right way, you must love him as God does, and for the same reasons.
0.100	You cannot love him in the right way unless you also love your enemies, and for a similar reason.
0.101	Nothing a person does for his friend is acceptable to God unless he would do just as much for his enemies if he could.
0.102	God cannot give him any credit for doing good to his friends unless he does it on a principle that would lead him to do just as much for his enemies if he could.
0.103	No one does any duty for one person in a way that pleases God while he refuses or deliberately neglects to do the same for another. I say this because God’s law requires you to love all your neighbors, every single one. And if you have a spirit of obedience to God, you will.
0.104	No one does right in any true sense unless he acts from a love that is for everyone and is not self-seeking.
0.105	On any other basis it cannot be acceptable for even a moment.
0.106	Think of a mother nursing her baby. God gives her no credit for this if she does it for no higher reason than an animal does.
0.107	She has a duty to love her own children, because God has placed her in exactly that relationship.
0.108	But she must never think that God gives her credit for simply following her animal instincts.
0.109	Her soul should rise higher than a mere animal.
0.110	She has a duty to make it her aim to please God.
0.111	Nothing less than this can be the condition for being saved.
0.112	No one can be free from sin and living in grace unless he has been brought into a state of true love for his neighbor.
0.113	Imagine a man who comes to heaven’s gate and asks to be let in. What would happen to him if he met an enemy there? Suppose it was a man he had never loved, a man he had hated and never prayed for.
0.114	Could he walk past such a man into heaven?
0.115	How could you enjoy heaven without a holy heart?
0.116	Some of you would hurry out. You would be like the rude, bad-mannered boys we have sometimes seen rushing out of church before the worship service was even over.
0.117	A person who loves his neighbor will understand this: his neighbor has a right to enjoy the public worship of God without being disturbed.
0.118	Without this love, salvation is impossible by its very nature.
0.119	It is also impossible because of how God governs. It cannot happen as long as God rules and cares for the interests of his great kingdom.
0.120	The entrance to heaven is guarded on every side. Nothing that does what is detestable will ever get in. Nothing unholy will enter.
0.121	Imagine a man going there in his selfishness!
0.122	Not if God can keep him out!
