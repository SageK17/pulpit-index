0.1	Why is there so little love among Christians? Love is the very sign by which everyone should know that we are followers of the holy Jesus. So why has it almost disappeared from the Christian world? If we look into it, we will find that a big part of the reason is this: we neglect intercession, or we do it in a shallow way. Intercession is a wonderful part of prayer. It means asking God to show his grace and mercy to other people.
0.2	Some people forget to pray for others because they hardly ever remember to pray for themselves. And even those who faithfully pray to their Father in heaven are often selfish when they come before God's throne of grace. They do not widen their prayers to include the good of their fellow Christians as they should. Because of this, they fall short of true Christian love, a sincere love for their brothers and sisters. Their holy calling as Christians requires them to reach for that love. Without it, even if they gave away everything they owned to feed the poor, and even gave their bodies to be burned, it would do them no good at all.
0.3	Since this is how things are, I will use the words of my text to try to show you three things. (Those words were first meant in a narrower way.)
0.4	I. First, that every Christian has a duty to pray for others as well as for himself.
0.5	II. Second, whom we should pray for, and how we should do it. And,
0.6	III. Third, I will give some reasons that should move all Christians to do much more of this great duty of praying for others.
0.7	I. First, then, I will try to show that every Christian has a duty to pray for others, as well as for himself.
0.8	Prayer is a duty that even natural religion teaches. Even the heathen never neglected it, though many among us who are Christians in name but heathen in practice do. Prayer is so essential to being a Christian that you could just as well expect to find a living man who does not breathe as a true Christian who does not have the spirit of prayer and pleading with God.
0.9	That is why, as soon as St. Paul was converted, the Lord Almighty said, "Look, he is praying."
0.10	And it will be the same with every child of God as soon as he becomes one. Prayer has rightly been called the natural cry of a newborn soul.
0.11	In the heart of every true believer there is a pull toward heaven, a divine attraction. It draws him to talk with God just as clearly as a magnet pulls a needle.
0.12	True believers feel deeply how weak they are and how full Christ is. They are strongly convinced that their nature is corrupt and that they need God's grace to make them new. This will not let them rest. They cry out day and night to their Almighty Redeemer. They ask that God's image, which they lost in Adam, would be begun in them, carried forward, and made fully complete in both soul and body. They ask this through Christ's all-powerful work as their go-between, and through the work of his blessed Spirit, who makes them holy.
0.13	That is how earnest and persistent all sincere Christians are when they pray for themselves. But they do not feel the needs of their Christian brothers and sisters as strongly, as deeply, or as constantly. So, for the most part, they are too careless and weak in their prayers for them.
0.14	But suppose the love of God were poured into our hearts. Suppose we loved our neighbor the way the Son of God, our Savior, loved us, as he commanded and showed us. Then we could not help praying just as hard for their good, both for their souls and for their daily lives, as for our own. We would long and work just as hard for others to share in the blessings of Jesus Christ's suffering and death as for ourselves.
0.15	No one should think this is some rare kind of love, a high level of perfection that only a few can reach. We are all commanded to "love our neighbor (that is, every person) as ourselves." We are even told to "lay down our lives for our brothers and sisters." So it is everyone's duty to pray for their neighbors as much as for themselves. At all times, by every act and word of love and kindness, we should show that we are ready even to give our lives for them, if God should ever call us to do it.
0.16	Our blessed Savior "gave us an example, so that we would follow in his steps" in everything else, and he did so especially in this. Just before his suffering, he prayed a divine, perfect prayer that no one could ever equal. It is recorded in John 17. In it we find only a few requests for himself, but many for the good of his disciples. He also gave us a perfect pattern for prayer. In it he teaches us to say, not "My Father," but "Our Father." This reminds us that whenever we come before God's throne of grace, we should not pray only for ourselves, but for all our brothers and sisters in Christ.
0.17	So praying for others is clearly a duty for every Christian.
0.18	II. Next we must think about whom we should pray for, and how we should do it.
0.19	1. First, our prayers for others must include everyone.
0.20	The apostle says, "I want prayers, requests, and intercessions to be made for all people."
0.21	God's mercy is over everything he has made. Jesus Christ died to rescue a people out of every nation and language. So we should pray that "all people may come to know the truth and be saved."
0.22	The Holy Scriptures contain many precious promises that the gospel will be preached through the whole world. They say that "the earth will be covered with the knowledge of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea." So it is our duty not to limit our prayers to our own country. We should pray that all the nations who now sit in darkness and in the shadow of death would have the glorious gospel shine on them, just as it shines on us.
0.23	But you do not need anyone to teach you this. God himself, and Jesus Christ himself, have taught you to pray that his kingdom would come. Part of what that request means is that "God's ways may be known on earth, and his saving power among all nations."
0.24	2. Next to praying for all people, St. Paul tells us to pray for kings. We should pray especially for our present ruler, King George, and for everyone who holds authority under him. We pray this so that we may live quiet lives, full of godliness and honesty.
0.25	Think how heavy the load of governing is. Think how much the well-being of any people depends on the zeal and godly lives of those who rule over them. Picture the many dangers and difficulties that rulers face because of their position, and the constant temptations they face to live in luxury and please themselves. Then we will not only feel sorry for them, but pray for them. God kept Esther, David, and Josiah "unstained by the world" in the middle of the splendor of a royal court, and he gave success to their plans. We will ask him to keep our rulers holy and blameless in the same way, and to bless all the work of their hands. But
0.26	3. Third, you should pray in a very special way for those whom "the Holy Spirit has made overseers over you."
0.27	St. Paul asks this again and again of the churches he writes to. In our text he says, "Brothers and sisters, pray for us." And in his letter to the Ephesians he says, "Pray at all times, with every kind of request. And pray for me too, that I may open my mouth boldly and announce the mystery of the gospel."
0.28	In another place he shows how earnest this request is and how much their prayers mean to him. He tells the church to "strive together with him in their prayers." (The word he used in the original language means to be in agony.)
0.29	The great St. Paul was God's chosen instrument and a favorite of heaven. Yet he needed his converts to pray for him with all their might. If that is true, then surely ordinary ministers of the gospel need the prayers of their own congregations even more.
0.30	I must press this part of your duty on you in a very special way, because it matters so much. No doubt many people often miss out on much good because they neglect to pray for their ministers. They would have received that good if they had prayed for them as they should.
0.31	And that is not to mention that people often complain that there are not enough hard-working and faithful pastors.
0.32	But how can people deserve good pastors if they will not pray earnestly to God for them?
0.33	If we will not pray to the Lord of the harvest, how can we expect him to send workers into his harvest?
0.34	Besides, how ungrateful it is not to pray for your ministers!
0.35	They keep watch and work hard, preaching and teaching for you and for your salvation. Shouldn't you pray for them in return?
0.36	If someone does something kind for your body, you think it is right and proper, and your clear duty, to pray for them. So shouldn't you remember in your prayers those who feed and nourish your souls every day?
0.37	On top of all this, praying for your ministers clearly shows that you believe this: Paul may plant and Apollos may water, but only God makes things grow.
0.38	You will also find that it is the best way to do yourself good. In answer to your prayers, God may give them a double portion of his Holy Spirit. Then they will be better equipped to give you a fuller knowledge of spiritual things. They will also be able to handle the word of truth with more skill.
0.39	People should always follow this advice. When their ministers pray to God on their behalf, they should humbly ask God to grant all those requests. When their ministers speak to them on God's behalf, they should pray that the Holy Spirit would come down on everyone who hears the word. If people did this, we would see their teaching do more visible good. We would also see more love between ministers and their people.
0.40	The people's prayers would then hold up their ministers' hands. And people will never dare to insult or slander those they are always praying for.
0.41	4. After our ministers, our friends deserve a place in our prayers. But we should not be satisfied with praying for them in general terms. We should fit our prayers to their particular situations.
0.42	When God struck Miriam with leprosy, Moses cried out, "Lord, heal her."
0.43	And when the nobleman came to ask Jesus Christ for help for his child, he said, "Lord, my little daughter is close to death. I beg you, come and heal her."
0.44	In the same way, when our friends are going through hard times, we should try to pray for them with their particular troubles in mind.
0.45	For example, is a friend sick?
0.46	We should pray that, if God is willing, the sickness will not end in death. But if God wills otherwise, we should pray that he will give our friend grace to accept this trial in such a way that, when this painful life is over, he may live with God forever.
0.47	Is a friend unsure about an important matter?
0.48	We should bring his case before God, as Moses did with the case of the daughters of Zelophehad. We should pray that God's Holy Spirit will lead him into all truth and give him the guidance he needs at the right time.
0.49	Is he poor and in need?
0.50	We should pray that his faith will never fail, and that God will help him when the right time comes.
0.51	In every other situation too, we should not pray for our friends only in general. We should fit our prayers to their particular sufferings and troubles. Otherwise, we might never ask for the very things our friends need most.
0.52	It is true that praying this way will often force some people to set aside the written prayers they usually use. But if we get used to it, and feel deeply what we are asking for, even people with no education will not be short of the right words.
0.53	The Bible gives us many great examples of how well this kind of specific prayer works. But none is more remarkable than the story of Abraham's servant in the book of Genesis. He was sent to find a wife for Abraham's son Isaac, and he prayed for him in a very specific way.
0.54	The rest of the story tells us how remarkably his prayer was answered.
0.55	If Christians today prayed for their friends in the same specific way, and with the same faith, as Abraham's servant did for his master, they would no doubt often receive answers just as clear. They would have just as much reason to thank God for them as he did. But
0.56	5. Just as we should pray for our friends, we must also pray for our enemies.
0.57	Jesus Christ says, "Bless those who curse you, and pray for those who treat you cruelly and persecute you."
0.58	He backed up this command in the strongest way by his own example. In the very agony and pain of death, he prayed even for the people who were killing him: "Father, forgive them, because they do not understand what they are doing!"
0.59	It must be admitted that this is a hard duty. But it is not impossible for those who have given up the things of this life. (All hatred between people comes from loving those things too much.) They know the terrible warnings given against those who cause Christ's little ones to stumble. So, out of real pity and concern for the danger those people are in, they can pray for the ones who cause such harm.
0.60	6. Finally, to finish this point: we should pray for everyone who is suffering in any way, whether in mind, body, or circumstances. We should pray for all who want and need our prayers, and for all who do not pray for themselves.
0.61	Oh, how I wish that everyone who hears me would set aside some time every day to carry out this most necessary duty properly!
0.62	To help you do that,
0.63	I will now go on
0.64	III. To show you the benefits of praying for others every day, and to give you some reasons that should move you to do it. And
0.65	1. First, it will fill your hearts with love for one another.
0.66	Someone who prays with all his heart for all people every day at the throne of grace cannot help being filled with love and kindness toward everyone before long. As he keeps practicing love in this way, his heart will grow bigger without his even noticing. He will come to share in the overflowing love that is in Christ Jesus our Lord!
0.67	Envy, spite, revenge, and other hellish attitudes like them can never stay long in the heart of a person of grace who prays for others. Instead, he will be filled with joy, peace, gentleness, patience, and all the other gifts of the Holy Spirit.
0.68	By often bringing his neighbor's needs to God, he will come to feel those needs as if they were his own. He will rejoice with those who rejoice and cry with those who cry.
0.69	When others are blessed, he will not feel envy. Instead, he will see each blessing as an answer to his own prayers for them. And it will fill his soul with a joy too great for words, full of glory.
0.70	So pray for others often, both for everyone in general and for specific people. When you hear about your neighbor's faults, do not tell others about them or put them on display. Instead, bring them to God in private, and ask him to correct them and set them right.
0.71	When you hear about someone who is known for his sins, do not think you are right to be angry. Instead, ask Jesus Christ to convert him and make him a living reminder of his free grace. You cannot imagine what a wonderful change this habit will make in your heart! You will grow day by day in a spirit of love and gentleness toward all people!
0.72	But there is more. To encourage you to keep practicing this duty of praying for others, think about the many examples in the Bible that show how powerful and effective it is.
0.73	The Bible records great and wonderful things that happened because of this holy work.
0.74	It has stopped plagues. It has opened and shut the sky. And it has often turned God's anger away from his people.
0.75	Think how Abimelech's household was set free from the disease God sent on them when Abraham prayed for them!
0.76	When "Phinehas stood up and prayed," how quickly the plague stopped!
0.77	When Daniel humbled himself in sorrow and prayed for the Lord's own people, how quickly an angel was sent to tell him "his prayer was heard!"
0.78	And to give just one more example: Moses was praying for his people after they had worshiped idols. And God speaks as if Moses' persistent pleading had almost overpowered him. "Leave me alone," God says!
0.79	This is enough to show that praying for others is, I could almost say, all-powerful. It shows how we can wrestle with God like Jacob did, and with a holy kind of force win blessings both for ourselves and for others.
0.80	No doubt God has still spared this terribly sinful nation because of the hidden, powerful prayers of the few righteous people who are still among us. If there were not some faithful people like Moses left to stand in the gap, we would soon be destroyed, just like Sodom, and burned to ashes like Gomorrah.
0.81	But here is another reason to stir you up to pray for others. Very likely, this is something even the saints in glory often do. They have been set free from the burden of the body. They have been brought into the glorious freedom of God's children. But their happiness cannot be fully complete until the resurrection on the last day, when all their brothers and sisters will be glorified with them. So we can only think that they often plead with our heavenly Father to soon complete the number of his chosen people and to hurry the coming of his kingdom.
0.82	So shouldn't we who are on earth often join in this holy work with that glorious company, the spirits of good people who have been made perfect?
0.83	Our happiness in heaven will depend so much on the fellowship of the saints in the victorious church above. So shouldn't we often pray for the church that is still fighting its battles here below? Shouldn't we earnestly ask that we may all be one, just as the holy Jesus and his Father are one, so that we too may be made perfectly one?
0.84	To move you to this great work of love, remember this: it is what the holy and highly exalted Jesus himself does without ever stopping. He sits at the right hand of God, hears all our prayers, and always prays for us!
0.85	So someone who keeps praying for others is doing on earth what the eternal Son of God is always doing in heaven.
0.86	So when you lift up holy hands to pray for one another, imagine that you see heaven opened. Imagine you see the Son of God in all his glory, the great High Priest who brings you salvation. He stands before the throne of his heavenly Father and pleads for you. He points to his sacrifice, which is fully enough to cover everything!
0.87	So join your prayers to his. Ask him to make them rise through him like incense, and to have them received like a sweet fragrance that pleases God!
0.88	Picturing this will strengthen your faith. It will make your prayers earnest and holy. It will make you wrestle with God like Jacob, who saw God face to face and yet lived. You will wrestle like Abraham when he pleaded for Sodom. And you will wrestle like Jesus Christ himself, who, the night before his bitter suffering, was in agony and prayed even more earnestly.
0.89	And now, brothers and sisters, what more can I say? Jesus Christ himself has taught you to be rich in love, and in this good work of praying for one another.
0.90	Even if you are very lowly, even if you are as poor as Lazarus, you will then do good to all people. Thousands, even twenty times ten thousand, will be blessed because of you!
0.91	And after you have spent a few years in this holy work here, you will be taken up to that happy place where you have so often wished others could go. There, in the kingdom of his heavenly Father, you will be lifted up to sit at the right hand of the one who prays for us, who is all-powerful and always wins his case.
0.92	But I must urge this on you especially now. Most likely, none of you I have been preaching to here will ever see me again. I am now leaving you, trusting that God's most Holy Spirit is guiding me, and I do not know what will happen to me. So I need you to pray for me as hard as you can. Pray that nothing will turn me away from my duty. Pray that I will "not value even my own life, as long as I can finish my race with joy, and the work the Lord Jesus gave me, to tell the good news of God's grace!"
0.93	While I have been here, as far as I know, I have not failed to tell you the whole will of God. To some, my preaching may have been a smell of death that leads to death. But I trust that to others it has been a fragrance of life that leads to life. So I earnestly hope those people will not forget to remember me in their prayers.
0.94	As for me, you have shown me so much kindness that I did not deserve, and I cannot forget you. So I trust that my cry will rise to God even from the deep. While the winds and storms blow over me, I will pray to the Lord for you.
0.95	For in only a little while, "we must all stand before the judgment seat of Christ." There I must give a strict account of what I have preached, and you must give an account of how you have grown under it.
0.96	Oh, may I never be called as a quick witness against any of you whose salvation I have longed and worked for! I have done it sincerely, though far too weakly.
0.97	It is true that some people have accused me of acting from bad and selfish motives. "But it matters very little to me if I am judged by human judgment." I hope my motives are pure. But I beg you, brothers and sisters, by the mercies of God in Christ Jesus, pray that they will become even purer!
0.98	And pray that, as God's grace increases in me, I will grow in knowing and loving God through Jesus Christ our Lord.
0.99	And now, brothers and sisters, what more can I say?
0.100	I wish I could keep speaking much longer, because I can never fully express how much my soul longs for you!
0.101	So finally, brothers and sisters, "whatever is holy, whatever is pure, whatever is honest, whatever is well spoken of; if there is any comfort in Christ, if there is any fellowship of the Spirit," if there is any hope that we will meet each other with joy before the solemn judgment seat of Jesus Christ, "think about the things you have heard." Think about what your pastors have told you and will still tell you. Keep sitting under their ministry, and "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling." Then, whether I never see you again or God brings me back to you some day, I will always have the joy of knowing that you live in a way that "fits the gospel of Christ."
0.102	I have almost convinced myself that I would gladly suffer anything, if it could help in any way to save your precious souls, which will live forever. And I beg you, as my last request, "obey those who lead you in the Lord." Always be ready to listen to their preaching, as it is your clear duty to do.
0.103	Do not think that I want to be praised at the cost of anyone else's good name. Instead, remember this: do not admire any one person too much. But hold all your ministers in high regard and love them, as they rightly deserve because of their work.
0.104	And now, "brothers and sisters, I place you in God's hands, and in the care of the word of his grace. That word has power to build you up and to give you a share of the inheritance among all who are made holy."
0.105	May God reward you for everything you have done out of faith and all your hard work of love. May he help you do more and more of every good thing, in word and action, for all people.
0.106	May he truly turn to himself all who have been made aware of their sin, and wake up all who are dead in their wrongdoing and sins!
0.107	May he make everyone who is wavering strong and steady!
0.108	And may you all keep growing from one level of grace to the next, until you reach the full maturity of Christ. May you in this way become ready to stand before God, "in whose presence there is complete joy, and at whose right hand there are pleasures forever!" Amen! Amen!
