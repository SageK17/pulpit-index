0.302	34. So learn what condition the world is in. See how far it is, not only from Christ’s example, which no one can measure, but also from the commandment in this verse.
0.303	Where can you find anyone who understands what the little words “your neighbor” mean? And this is so even though, besides this commandment, the natural law of service is written in the hearts of all people.
0.304	There is not a single person who does not see, and who is not forced to admit, that the natural law is right and true. That law is described in the command (Matthew 7:12): “All the things you would want people to do to you, you must also do to them.”
0.305	The light of this law shines in the reason that every person is born with.
0.306	If people would only pay attention to it, what need would they have for books, teachers or laws?
0.307	Deep in their hearts they carry a living book with them. It can teach them fully what to do and what not to do, what to accept and what to turn down, and what to decide.
0.308	Now, the command to love our neighbors as ourselves means the same as that other command: “All the things you would want people to do to you,” and the rest.
0.309	Every person wants to be loved and not hated. And each one also feels and sees that he has a duty to have the same attitude toward others.
0.310	Carrying out this duty means loving someone else as himself.
0.311	But evil desire and sinful love darken the light of the natural law. They blind a person, until he no longer sees the guidebook in his heart and no longer follows the clear command of reason.
0.312	So he has to be held in check and pushed back by outward laws and written books, by the sword and by force.
0.313	He has to be reminded of the natural light he has, and his own heart has to be shown to him.
0.314	But warning him does not help. He does not see the light.
0.315	Evil desire and sinful love make him blind.
0.316	So he still has to be held back from the outside, by the sword and by the laws of the state. That keeps him from actually committing crimes.
0.317	35. The fourth thing the commandment shows us is the measure we are to use for our love. It is an excellent model.
0.318	Instructions and commandments that give examples are especially valuable.
0.319	This commandment holds up an example that is truly alive: “yourself.”
0.320	It is a better model than any example the saints have given us.
0.321	The saints are dead and what they did is in the past. But this example is always alive.
0.322	Everyone has to admit that he knows he loves himself. He knows how eagerly he cares for his life here on earth. He knows how carefully he feeds his body and gives it food, clothing and every good thing. He knows how he runs from death and keeps away from evil.
0.323	This is self-love. It is something we know is in us.
0.324	So what does the commandment teach?
0.325	To treat someone else the way you treat yourself. To value his body and his life just as much as your own body and life.
0.326	How could God have pointed you to an example that is dearer, more pleasing and more fitting than this one: the deep instinct of your own nature?
0.327	In fact, how deep your character goes is measured by this command that is written in your heart.
0.328	36. How will things go for you with God if you do not love your neighbor?
0.329	You feel this commandment written inside your heart, so your conscience will condemn you.
0.330	Your whole way of living will be an example that speaks against you. It will show that you did not do to others what the natural instinct of your own being taught you to do. And that instinct taught you more forcefully than all the examples of the saints.
0.331	But how will it go especially for the clergy? I mean the churchmen with their singing and praying, their monks’ hoods and shaved heads, and all their tricks.
0.332	I will not even mention that they have never kept the commandment.
0.333	But I do ask this: When has their monkish fanaticism ever given them the time and the chance to notice this law in their hearts even once? When has it let them become aware of the example their own human instinct gives them? When has it let them even read this command in books or hear it preached?
0.334	Poor, miserable people! Do you really dare to think that God will cancel this commandment of love? It is pressed so deeply and clearly into the heart. It is shown so beautifully and so plainly in your own nature, and also in the many words that have been written and spoken. Do you think God will cancel it because of your monks’ hoods and shaved heads? Do you think he will pay attention to what you have been making up and doing?
0.335	37. How sad it is! How shamelessly the world has ignored this beautiful and powerful commandment! It so skillfully shows the person, the task, the model and the field where the work is done.
0.336	And on the other hand, how shameful it is that the world keeps busy with the exact opposite of what this commandment teaches!
0.337	Its whole way of doing things and its whole aim seem to be to put our responsibility on other people. Monks and priests must be righteous for us and pray in our place, so that we ourselves can be let off.
0.338	In place of the noblest virtue, love, we put works we have made up ourselves. In place of our neighbors we put wood and stone, clothing and food, and even dead souls: the saints in heaven.
0.339	These are what we serve. These are what keep us busy. They are the field where we put in all our effort.
0.340	Instead of the noblest example, “as yourself,” we look to the legends and works of the saints.
0.341	We dare to copy such outward examples. Meanwhile we leave out the duty that our own nature and life set before us, and that God’s command describes. Yet that duty gives us more to do than we could ever carry out.
0.342	Even if we could do everything it sets before us, we would still not be equal to Christ.
H:0.49	Love Does Only Good to Its Neighbor.
0.343	“Love does no harm to its neighbor. So love is the fulfilling of the law.”
0.344	38. The Ten Commandments forbid us to do evil to our neighbor: “Do not kill. Do not commit adultery,” and so on.
0.345	The apostle uses similar words. He says that love keeps all these commands and harms no one.
0.346	And not only that: it does good to everyone.
0.347	If we can help our neighbor out of danger and we leave him in it, that is almost the same as doing evil to him. This is true even if we had no part in putting him there.
0.348	If he is hungry and we do not feed him when we are able to, we are in effect letting him starve to death.
0.349	We should see it the same way whenever our neighbors are in any danger or any kind of trouble.
0.350	Now we have heard how love fulfills the Law.
