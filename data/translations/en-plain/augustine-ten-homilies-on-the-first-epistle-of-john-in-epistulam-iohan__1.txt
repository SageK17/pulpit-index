1.272	12. “Whoever loves his brother stays (manet) in the light, and there is nothing in him that makes anyone stumble.” I beg you by Christ: God is feeding us. Soon we will go and refresh our bodies with food, in the name of Christ. In a good measure both have already been fed, and both will be fed again. So now let your mind be fed.
1.273	I am not saying this because I plan to preach for a long time. Look, the reading is almost finished. I say it so that we don’t get tired and listen less carefully than we should to something very important. “Whoever loves his brother stays in the light, and there is no scandal in him”—or, “nothing in him that makes anyone stumble.”
1.274	Who are the people who stumble, or who make others stumble?
1.275	They are people who take offense at Christ, and at the Church.
1.276	Those who take offense at Christ are like people burned by the sun. Those who take offense at the Church are like people burned by the moon.
1.277	But the Psalm says, “The sun will not burn you by day, and the moon will not burn you by night.” This means: if you hold on to love, you will not stumble over Christ or over the Church. You will not leave Christ, and you will not leave the Church.
1.278	For if someone leaves the Church, how can he be in Christ, when he is not among Christ’s members?
1.279	How can someone be in Christ if he is not in Christ’s body?
1.280	So the people who stumble, or fall, are the ones who leave Christ or the Church.
1.281	But how do we know what the Psalm means when it says, “The sun will not burn you by day, nor the moon by night”? How do we know that the “burning” means stumbling, or taking offense?
1.282	First of all, look at the picture itself.
1.283	Think of someone who is being burned by something. He says, “I can’t stand it! I can’t take it!” and he pulls back. It is the same with people who can’t stand certain things in the Church. They pull away, either from the name of Christ or from the Church. Those people are stumbling.
1.284	Look how some people stumbled, as if the sun had burned them. They were people who thought only in earthly ways. Christ was telling them about His flesh. He said, “Whoever does not eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood will have no life in him.”
1.285	About seventy people said, “This teaching is hard.” They turned back and left Him, and only the twelve stayed.
1.286	The sun burned all of those people. They turned back because they could not bear the power of the Word.
1.287	So the twelve stayed.
1.288	People might imagine that they are doing Christ a favor by believing in Him, when really He is the one doing them a favor. So that no one would think that, the Lord spoke to the twelve who were left. He said to them, “Are you going to leave too?”
1.289	In other words: so that you may know that you need Me, not that I need you.
1.290	But the ones the sun had not burned answered through Peter. He said, “Lord, You have the word of eternal life. Where would we go?”
1.291	But who are the people that the Church burns at night, like the moon?
1.292	The ones who have caused splits in the Church.
1.293	Listen to the very word the apostle uses: “Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn?”
1.294	So what does it mean that there is nothing to stumble over in the person who loves his brother?
1.295	It means this: the one who loves his brother puts up with everything for the sake of unity. For love between brothers lives in the unity of love.
1.296	Maybe someone offends you. I don’t know who. Maybe he really is a bad man. Maybe you only think he is bad. Or maybe you just pretend he is bad. And because of him, will you leave so many good people?
1.297	What kind of brotherly love have these people shown?
1.298	They accuse the Africans, and meanwhile they have left the whole world!
1.299	What? Were there no holy people anywhere in the whole world?
1.300	Or could you really condemn them without even hearing them?
1.301	Oh, if only you loved your brothers! Then there would be nothing in you that makes you stumble.
1.302	Listen to what the Psalm says: “Those who love Your law have great peace, and nothing makes them stumble.”
1.303	The Psalm says there is great peace for those who love God’s law. That is why nothing makes them stumble.
1.304	So people who stumble, or take offense, destroy peace.
1.305	And who does he say neither stumble nor make others stumble?
1.306	Those who love God’s law.
1.307	So they are living in love.
1.308	But someone will say, “He said that about people who love God’s law, not about loving our brothers.”
1.309	Then listen to what the Lord says: “I give you a new commandment: love one another.”
1.310	What is the Law, if not a commandment?
1.311	Besides, why is it that they do not stumble? Because they put up with one another.
1.312	As Paul says, “Put up with one another in love. Work hard to keep the unity of the Spirit, tied together by peace.”
1.313	And to show that this is Christ’s law, listen to the same apostle praising this very law.
1.314	He says, “Carry one another’s burdens. That is how you will keep the law of Christ.”
1.315	13. “For whoever hates his brother is in the dark. He walks in the dark, and he does not know where he is going.”
1.316	This is important, my brothers and sisters. Please pay attention to it.
1.317	“Whoever hates his brother walks in the dark and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has made his eyes blind.”
1.318	Who could be more blind than people who hate their brothers?
1.319	Here is how you can tell they are blind: they have tripped over a Mountain.
1.320	I say the same things often so that you won’t forget them.
1.321	The Stone that was “cut out of the Mountain without hands”—isn’t that Christ? He came from the kingdom of the Jews without any human being doing the work.
1.322	Hasn’t that Stone smashed all the kingdoms of the earth to pieces? That means all the power of idols and demons.
1.323	Hasn’t that Stone grown into a great mountain that fills the whole earth?
1.324	Do we have to point at this Mountain with our finger, the way people point at the moon when it is three days old?
1.325	For example, when people want others to see the new moon, they say, “Look, the moon! Look, there it is!” If some people there can’t see well and ask, “Where?” then someone points a finger so they can see it.
1.326	Sometimes people are embarrassed to seem blind, so they say they saw it when they really didn’t.
1.327	My brothers and sisters, do we have to point out the Church like that?
1.328	Isn’t it out in the open?
1.329	Isn’t it plain to see?
1.330	Hasn’t it spread through all nations?
1.331	Hasn’t the promise made to Abraham so many years before come true—that all nations would be blessed through his offspring?
1.332	The promise was made to one believer, and now the world is full of thousands of believers.
1.333	Look! Here is the mountain that covers the whole face of the earth!
1.334	Look! Here is the city described in these words: “A city built on a mountain cannot be hidden!”
1.335	But those people trip over the mountain. When someone tells them, “Go up,” they say, “There is no mountain.” They would rather bang their heads against it than look for a home there.
1.336	Isaiah was read yesterday. Some of you were awake—not only with your eyes but with your ears, and not just the ears of your body but the ears of your heart. You noticed this: “In the last days the mountain of the Lord’s house will be plain to see, set up on the top of the mountains.”
1.337	What is easier to see than a mountain?
1.338	But there are also mountains that people don’t know about, because they are in just one part of the world.
1.339	Which of you knows Mount Olympus?
1.340	In the same way, the people who live there don’t know our Giddaba.
1.341	These mountains are in different parts of the world.
1.342	But that Mountain is not like that. It has filled the whole face of the earth. It is the one described as “set up on the top of the mountains.”
1.343	It is a Mountain higher than the tops of all the other mountains.
1.344	He says, “And all nations will gather to it.”
1.345	Who could miss this Mountain?
1.346	Who would break his head by tripping over it?
1.347	Who doesn’t know about the city built on a mountain?
1.348	But don’t be surprised that people who hate their brothers don’t know it. They walk in the dark and don’t know where they are going, because the darkness has made their eyes blind.
1.349	They don’t see the Mountain. I don’t want you to be surprised: they have no eyes.
1.350	Why do they have no eyes?
1.351	Because the darkness has blinded them.
1.352	How can we prove this?
1.353	Because they hate their brothers. They are offended at some Africans, and so they cut themselves off from the whole world. For the sake of Christ’s peace, they will not put up with the people they slander. Yet for the sake of Donatus, they do put up with the people they condemn.
T:2	Homily 2 (1 John 2:12–17)
2.1	“I am writing to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven through His name.
2.2	I am writing to you, fathers, because you have known the One who is from the beginning.
2.3	I am writing to you, young men, because you have beaten the evil one.
2.4	I am writing to you, children, because you have known the Father.
2.5	I am writing to you, fathers, because you have known the One who is from the beginning.
2.6	I am writing to you, young men, because you are strong. The word of God lives in you, and you have beaten the evil one.
2.7	Do not love the world or the things in the world.
2.8	If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love is not in him.
2.9	For everything in the world is the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and pride in this life. These things do not come from the Father. They come from the world.
2.10	The world is passing away, and so are its desires. But whoever does what God wants will last forever (just as God also lasts forever).”
2.11	1. Everything read from the Holy Scriptures is there to teach us and to save us. So we should listen to all of it very carefully.
2.12	But we must remember most of all the parts that are strongest against heretics. Heretics never stop setting sly traps for anyone who is weaker or careless.
2.13	Remember that our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ died for us and rose again. He died for our sins, and He rose again to make us right with God.
2.14	You just heard about the two disciples He met on the road. “Their eyes were kept from recognizing Him.” He found them with no hope left that Christ would set them free. They thought He had suffered and died like any man. They did not think that, as the Son of God, He lives forever. They also thought He was so dead in His body that He would never come back to life, just like one of the prophets. Those of you who were paying attention just heard their own words.
2.15	Then “He explained the Scriptures to them, starting with Moses.” He went through all the prophets. He showed them that everything He had suffered had been predicted. He did this so that they would not be even more shaken when the Lord rose again. If those things had not been told ahead of time, they might have found it even harder to believe Him.
2.16	For our faith is made firm by this: everything that happened to Christ was predicted.
2.17	So the disciples did not recognize Him until “the breaking of the bread.”
2.18	And truly, whoever does not eat and drink judgment on himself in the breaking of the bread does know Christ.
2.19	Later those eleven also “thought they were seeing a spirit.”
2.20	So He let them touch Him. He is the same One who let Himself be crucified. He let enemies crucify Him, and He let friends touch Him. But He is the Doctor who heals everyone: He healed the enemies of their wickedness and the friends of their unbelief.
2.21	You heard in the reading from the Acts of the Apostles how many thousands of the people who killed Christ came to believe.
2.22	If people who had killed Him believed later on, shouldn’t those believe who had doubted only for a little while?
2.23	And yet, even for them—and you should especially notice this and remember it—touching Him was not enough. (God chose to put into the Scriptures the things that make us strong against sly errors. And no one who wants to look like a Christian at all dares to speak against the Scriptures.) So after He let them touch Him, He did not stop there. He also wanted to make the hearts of believers strong by using the Scriptures. He was looking ahead to us, who would come later. For we have nothing of Him that we can touch, but we do have something we can read.
2.24	For if those disciples believed only because they held Him and touched Him, what are we supposed to do?
2.25	Christ has now gone up into heaven. He will not come back until the end, when He comes to judge the living and the dead.
2.26	So how will we believe? By the same thing He wanted to use to make even those who touched Him strong.
2.27	For He explained the Scriptures to them. He showed them that Christ had to suffer. He showed them that everything written about Him in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms had to come true.
2.28	In what He said, He covered the whole of the ancient Scriptures.
2.29	Everything in those older Scriptures speaks about Christ—but only if there are ears to hear it.
2.30	He also “opened their minds so they could understand the Scriptures.”
2.31	So we too must pray that He will open our minds.
2.32	2. But what did the Lord show them was written about Him in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms?
2.33	What did He show them?
2.34	Let Him tell us Himself. The Gospel writer put it briefly, so that we would know what to believe and understand out of all the huge amount of Scripture.
2.35	There are certainly many pages and many books. But all of them are summed up in what the Lord told His disciples in a few words.
2.36	What is it? That “Christ had to suffer and rise again on the third day.”
2.37	So now you have what was said about the Bridegroom: “Christ had to suffer and rise again.” The Bridegroom has been shown to us.
2.38	Now let’s see what He says about the Bride. Then, when you know both the Bridegroom and the Bride, you will have good reason to come to the wedding.
2.39	For every celebration is a wedding celebration: we are celebrating the wedding of the Church.
2.40	The King’s Son is about to get married, and that King’s Son is a King Himself. And the guests who crowd into the wedding are themselves the Bride.
2.41	In an ordinary human wedding, some people are guests and someone else is the bride. It is not like that in the Church. In the Church, those who come as guests—if they come with the right heart—become the Bride.
2.42	For the whole Church is Christ’s Bride. Its beginning and first fruit is the flesh of Christ. That is where the Bride was joined to the Bridegroom in the flesh.
2.43	That is why, when He wanted to point to that same flesh, He broke bread. And that is why, “in the breaking of the bread,” the eyes “of the disciples were opened, and they recognized Him.”
2.44	So then, what did the Lord say was written about Him in the Law, the Prophets and the Psalms?
2.45	That “Christ had to suffer.”
2.46	If He had not added, “and rise again,” those whose eyes were kept from seeing might well have been sad. But “rising again” was also predicted.
2.47	Why was that? Why did Christ have to suffer and rise again?
2.48	Because of the Psalm we especially asked you to pay attention to last week, on the fourth day, at the first station.
2.49	Why did Christ have to suffer and rise again?
2.50	For this reason: “All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will worship before Him.”
2.51	So that you would know that Christ had to suffer and rise again, what did He add here too? After showing us the Bridegroom, He also wanted to show us the Bride.
2.52	He says, “And in His name, turning from sin and forgiveness of sins must be preached to all nations, starting at Jerusalem.”
2.53	You have heard it, brothers and sisters. Hold on to it.
2.54	No one should doubt that the Church is “in all nations.” No one should doubt that it started at Jerusalem and has filled all nations.
2.55	We know the field where the Vine was planted. But now that the Vine has grown, we can’t see the field anymore, because the Vine has covered all of it.
2.56	Where did it start?
2.57	“At Jerusalem.” How far has it reached?
2.58	To “all nations.” A few are left, but it will take over all of them.
2.59	Meanwhile, as it spreads to all of them, the Gardener has decided to cut off some useless branches. Those branches have started heresies and splits.
2.60	Don’t let the branches that were cut off talk you into being cut off too. Instead, urge those who were cut off to be grafted back in.
2.61	It is clear that Christ suffered, rose again and went up into heaven. The Church is clear to see too, because “turning from sin and forgiveness of sins are preached in His name to all nations.”
2.62	Where did it start?
2.63	“Starting at Jerusalem.” A man hears this, and he is foolish and empty-headed and—how can I put it?—worse than blind! Such a huge mountain, and he doesn’t see it! A lamp set on a lampstand, and he shuts his eyes to it!
2.64	3. We say to them, “If you are Catholic Christians, join in fellowship with the Church from which the Gospel has spread over the whole earth. Join in fellowship with Jerusalem.” When we say this, they answer, “We will not share fellowship with the city where our King was killed, where our Lord was killed.” They act as if they hate the city where our Lord was killed.
2.65	The Jews killed Him when they found Him on earth. These people mock Him while He sits in heaven!
2.66	Who is worse? Those who looked down on Him because they thought He was just a man? Or those who mock the sacraments of the One they now say is God?
2.67	But they say they hate the city where their Lord was killed!
2.68	What holy and kind people! They are very sad that Christ was killed—and yet they kill Christ in people!
2.69	But He loved that city and felt sorry for it. He commanded that the preaching about Him should start there: “starting at Jerusalem.”
2.70	He had the preaching of His name begin there. And you shudder at the idea of sharing fellowship with that city!
2.71	No wonder you hate the root, now that you have been cut off.
2.72	What did He say to His disciples?
2.73	“Stay in the city, because I am sending My promise on you.”
2.74	Look what kind of city it is that they hate!
2.75	Maybe they would love it if Christ’s murderers still lived there.
2.76	For it is well known that all of Christ’s murderers—that is, the Jews—have been driven out of that city.
2.77	The city that once held people who raged against Christ now holds people who worship Christ.
2.78	So that is why these men hate it: because Christians live there.
2.79	That is where He wanted His disciples to wait. That is where He wanted to send them the Holy Spirit.
2.80	Where did the Church begin? It began where the Holy Spirit came from heaven and filled the hundred and twenty people who were sitting together in one place.
2.81	The number twelve had been multiplied by ten.
2.82	A hundred and twenty people were sitting there. Then the Holy Spirit came, “and filled the whole place. There came a sound like a powerful wind rushing in, and there were tongues that looked like fire, split apart.”
2.83	You have heard the Acts of the Apostles. It was today’s reading: “They began to speak in languages, as the Spirit gave them the words.”
2.84	Everyone who was there—Jews who had come from many different nations—each recognized his own language. They were amazed. Those men had no schooling and no learning, and yet suddenly they had learned not just one or two languages, but the languages of every nation.
2.85	So in that place, where all the languages were heard, it was a sign that people of every language would believe.
2.86	But these men love Christ so much that they refuse to share fellowship with the city that killed Christ. And they honor Christ by claiming that He has only two languages left, Latin and Punic, that is,
2.87	African. Imagine Christ owning only two languages!
2.88	For those are the only two languages on Donatus’s side. They have no others.
2.89	Let’s wake up, my brothers and sisters! Let’s look instead at the gift of God’s Spirit. Let’s believe what was said ahead of time about Him, and let’s see the words of the Psalm come true: “There are no speeches or words where their voices are not heard among them.”
2.90	Someone might think that all the languages came together to one place, instead of Christ’s gift going out to all languages. So listen to what comes next: “Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world.”
2.91	Why is that? Because “He has set His tent in the sun”—that is, out in the open light.
2.92	His tent is His body. His tent is His Church. It is set “in the sun”—not in the night, but in the day.
2.93	So why don’t those people recognize it?
2.94	Go back to the reading where it stopped yesterday, and you will see why they don’t recognize it: “Whoever hates his brother walks in the dark and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has made his eyes blind.”
2.95	As for us, let’s look at what comes next, so that we are not in the dark.
2.96	How can we stay out of the dark?
2.97	By loving our brothers and sisters.
2.98	How can it be shown that we love the family of believers?
2.99	By not tearing apart our unity, and by holding on to love.
2.100	4. “I am writing to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven through His name.”
2.101	He calls you “little children” because you are born when your sins are forgiven.
2.102	But through whose name are sins forgiven?
2.103	Through Augustine’s name? No! So not through the name of Donatus either.
2.104	It is up to you to see who Augustine is, or who Donatus is. But sins are not forgiven through the name of Paul, or through the name of Peter.
2.105	Some people divided up the Church among themselves and tried to break unity into different groups. To them, Mother Love speaks through the apostle. She is like a mother in labor with her little ones. She shows them her very heart. With her words she almost tears open her chest. She weeps for her children, whom she sees being carried out dead. They insisted on having many names, and she calls them back to the one Name. She pushes them away from loving her, so that they will love Christ. And she says, “Was Paul crucified for you?
2.106	Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?”
2.107	What is he saying? “I don’t want you to belong to me—that way you can be with me. Be with me! We all belong to Him who died for us, who was crucified for us.” That is why it says here too, “Your sins are forgiven through His name”—not through the name of any human being.
2.108	5. “I am writing to you, fathers.”
2.109	Why does he call them sons first? “Because your sins are forgiven through His name.” You are born again into a new life, so you are sons.
2.110	Why fathers? “Because you have known the One who is from the beginning.” Being a father has to do with a beginning.
2.111	Christ is new in His human body, but ancient as God.
2.112	How ancient do we think He is? How many years old?
2.113	Do we think He is older than His mother?
2.114	He is certainly older than His mother, for “all things were made through Him.”
2.115	If He made all things, then the Ancient One made the very mother from whom the New One would be born.
2.116	Do we think He existed only before His mother?
2.117	In fact, He is even older than His mother’s ancestors.
2.118	His mother’s ancestor was Abraham. And the Lord says, “Before Abraham, I am.”
2.119	Do we say, “Before Abraham”?
2.120	Heaven and earth were made before there were any people.
2.121	The Lord existed before these. Better still, He simply is.
2.122	That is why He puts it so perfectly. He does not say, “Before Abraham I was,” but, “Before Abraham I Am.”
2.123	For what we say “was” does not exist anymore, and what we say “will be” does not exist yet. But He knows only how to be.
2.124	As God, He knows “to be.” He does not know “was” and “will be.”
2.125	There, it is one day—but a day that lasts forever and ever.
2.126	That day is not stuck between a yesterday and a tomorrow. Here, when “yesterday” is over, “today” begins, and then “today” is ended by the coming “tomorrow.”
2.127	That one day there is a day with no darkness, no night, no gaps, no measuring, no hours.
2.128	Call it whatever you like. If you like, it is a day. If you like, it is a year. If you like, it is years.
2.129	For Scripture says about it, “And Your years will never run out.”
2.130	But when is it called a day?
2.131	When these words are said to the Lord: “Today I have fathered You.”
2.132	He is born of the eternal Father. He is born from eternity, born in eternity. He has no beginning, no limit, no stretch of space. That is because He is the One who is. He Himself is “He Who Is.”
2.133	He told Moses this name of His: “Tell them, ‘He Who Is has sent me to you.’”
2.134	So why just say “before Abraham”? Why not before Noah? Why not before Adam?
2.135	Listen to the Scripture: “Before the morning star I fathered You.”
2.136	In short, before heaven and earth.
2.137	Why? Because “all things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made.”
2.138	This is how you can tell who the “fathers” are: they become fathers by recognizing “the One who is from the beginning.”
2.139	6. “I am writing to you, young men.”
2.140	There are sons, there are fathers, and there are young men. They are sons because they have been born. They are fathers because they recognize the Beginning. Why are they young men?
2.141	“Because you have beaten the evil one.”
2.142	In the sons, we see birth. In the fathers, we see great age. In the young men, we see strength.
2.143	If the evil one is “beaten” by the young men, that means he fights against us.
2.144	He fights, but he doesn’t win.
2.145	Why not? Is it because we are strong? Or is it because He is strong in us—the One who was found weak in the hands of those who persecuted Him?
2.146	He made us strong—He who did not fight back against the people who persecuted Him.
2.147	“For He was crucified because of weakness, but He is alive by the power of God.”
2.148	7. “I am writing to you, children.”
2.149	Why children? “Because you have known the Father.
2.150	I am writing to you, fathers.” He stresses this and says it again: “Because you have known the One who is from the beginning.”
2.151	Remember that you are fathers. If you forget “the One who is from the beginning,” you have lost your fatherhood.
2.152	“I am writing to you, young men.”
2.153	Think about it again and again: you are young men. Fight, so that you can win. Win, so that you can be crowned. Stay humble, so that you don’t fall in the fight.
2.154	“I am writing to you, young men, because you are strong. The word of God lives in you, and you have beaten the evil one.”
2.155	8. My brothers and sisters, think about all these things: “because we have known the One who is from the beginning, because we are strong, because we have known the Father.” In a way, they all praise knowledge. But don’t they also praise love?
2.156	If we know, then let’s love. For knowledge without love does not save anyone.
2.157	“Knowledge makes people proud, but love builds them up.”
2.158	If you want to confess Him but not love Him, you are starting to be like the demons.
2.159	The demons confessed that He was the Son of God. They said, “What do we have to do with You?” And they were driven away.
2.160	Confess Him and hold Him close. The demons were afraid because of their sins. But you, love Him who forgives your sins.
2.161	But how can we love God if we love the world?
2.162	So He gets us ready for love to live in us.
2.163	There are two loves: love of the world and love of God. If love of the world lives in you, love of God has no way to get in. Let love of the world move out, and let love of God move in. Let the better love have the room.
2.164	You used to love the world. Stop loving the world. When you have emptied your heart of earthly love, you will drink in God’s love. From then on, love begins to live in you, and nothing evil can come from it.
2.165	So listen to his words. See how he works like someone clearing land.
2.166	He comes to people’s hearts like a field he wants to take over. But what shape does he find it in?
2.167	If he finds a forest, he pulls it up by the roots. If he finds the field already cleared, he plants it.
2.168	He wants to plant a tree there: love.
2.169	And what is the forest he wants to pull up?
2.170	Love of the world.
2.171	Listen to him, the one who pulls up the forest!
2.172	“Do not love the world,” (this is what comes next) “or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love is not in him.”
2.173	9. You have heard it: “If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love is not in him.”
2.174	Brothers and sisters, no one should say in his heart that this is not true. God says it. The Holy Spirit has spoken through the apostle. Nothing is truer: “If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love is not in him.”
2.175	Do you want the Father’s love, so that you can share the inheritance with the Son?
2.176	Then don’t love the world.
2.177	Shut out the bad love of the world, so that you can be filled with God’s love.
2.178	You are a container, but right now you are full.
2.179	Pour out what you have, so that you can receive what you don’t have.
2.180	Our brothers and sisters have just now been born again of water and the Spirit. Some years ago we too were born again of water and the Spirit.
2.181	It is good for us not to love the world. Otherwise the sacraments will stay in us to condemn us, instead of making us strong to be saved.
2.182	What makes us strong to be saved is having the root of love. It is having “the power of godliness,” not just “the outward form.”
2.183	The form is good. The form is holy. But what use is the form if it isn’t attached to the root?
2.184	When a branch is cut off, isn’t it thrown into the fire?
2.185	Have the form, but keep it in the root.
2.186	But how can you be rooted so that you won’t be pulled up?
2.187	By holding on to love. As the apostle Paul says, “rooted and firmly set in love.”
2.188	How can love take root there, in a heart that is an overgrown wilderness of love for the world?
2.189	Clear away the forest completely.
2.190	You are about to plant a mighty seed. Don’t let anything in the field choke it.
2.191	These are the words he spoke to pull things up by the roots: “Do not love the world or the things in the world.
2.192	If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love is not in him.”
2.193	10. “For everything in the world is the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and pride in this life.” He has named three things. They do not come from the Father, but from the world.
2.194	“The world is passing away, and so are its desires. But whoever does what God wants will last forever, just as He lasts forever.”
2.195	Why shouldn’t I love what God made?
2.196	What do you want? Do you want to love the things of time and pass away with time? Or do you want to stop loving the world and live forever with God?
2.197	The river of passing things sweeps people along. But our Lord Jesus Christ is like a tree that has grown up beside the river.
2.198	He took on a human body, died, rose again and went up into heaven.
2.199	In a way, He chose to plant Himself beside the river of passing things.
2.200	Are you being swept down the stream, rushing toward the deep?
2.201	Grab hold of the tree.
2.202	Is love of the world spinning you around?
2.203	Grab hold of Christ. For your sake He became part of time, so that you could become eternal. For He became part of time in such a way that He still stayed eternal.
2.204	Something from time was added to Him, but nothing was taken away from His eternity.
2.205	But you were born into time, and sin made you a creature of time. You were made part of time by sin. He was made part of time by mercy, to forgive sins.
2.206	Think how different two people in a prison can be—the criminal and the one who visits him!
2.207	Sometimes a person goes to see his friend and goes inside to visit him. Both of them seem to be in prison, but there is a huge difference between them.
2.208	One is held down by his crime. The other is brought there by kindness.
2.209	It is the same in this mortal life of ours. We were held tight by our guilt. He came down out of mercy. He came in to the prisoner as a Rescuer, not as an oppressor.
2.210	The Lord shed His blood for us. He bought us back. He changed our hope.
2.211	For now we still carry bodies that will die, and we trust that we will live forever in the future. We are tossed by the waves on the sea, but the anchor of our hope is already fixed on land.
2.212	11. But let’s “not love the world or the things in the world.
2.213	For the things in the world are the desires of the body, the desires of the eyes, and pride in this life.”
2.214	These are the three. He says this so that no one will say, “God made the things in the world: heaven and earth, the sea, the sun, the moon, the stars, and everything that decorates the sky.
2.215	What decorates the sea? All the creeping creatures.
2.216	What decorates the earth? Animals, trees and birds.
2.217	These are ‘in the world,’ and God made them.
2.218	So why shouldn’t I love what God has made?”
2.219	May God’s Spirit be in you, so that you can see that all these things are good. But how terrible for you if you love the things that were made and abandon the One who made them!
2.220	They are beautiful to you, but how much more beautiful is the One who formed them!
2.221	Pay close attention, dear friends. Examples can teach you. Otherwise Satan might sneak up on you and say what he usually says: “Enjoy the things God has created. Why else did He make them, if not for you to enjoy?”
2.222	And people get themselves drunk, and they are ruined, and they forget their own Creator. When they use created things with greed instead of self-control, the Creator is treated with contempt.
2.223	The apostle speaks about people like that: “They worshiped and served what He created, rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever.”
2.224	God does not forbid you to love these things. But you must not set your heart on them as if they could make you truly happy. Instead, value them and praise them, so that you will love your Creator.
2.225	My brothers and sisters, it is like this. Suppose a bridegroom made a ring for his bride. And suppose that after she received it, she loved the ring more than she loved the bridegroom who made it for her. Wouldn’t her heart be guilty of adultery in the very gift the bridegroom gave her? And that is true even though all she loved was his gift.
2.226	Of course she should love what the bridegroom gave her. But what if she said, “This ring is enough for me. I don’t want to see his face anymore”? What kind of woman would she be?
2.227	Who wouldn’t hate such foolishness? Who wouldn’t say she has an unfaithful heart?
2.228	You love the gold instead of the man. You love a ring instead of the bridegroom. Suppose that is true of you—you love a ring instead of your bridegroom and have no wish to see him. Then the gift he gave you as a promise is not tying you to him. It is turning your heart away from him!
2.229	The reason a bridegroom gives a promise-gift is so that he himself will be loved through it.
2.230	So then: God gave you all these things. Love the One who made them.
2.231	He wants to give you even more. He wants to give you Himself, the One who made these things.
2.232	But if you love these things—even though God made them—and ignore the Creator and love the world, won’t your love be counted as unfaithful, like adultery?
