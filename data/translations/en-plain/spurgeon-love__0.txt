0.1	“We love him, because he loved us first.” — 1 John 4:19
0.2	For the last two Sundays I have been preaching the good news to people who have not yet turned to God.
0.3	I have strongly urged even the very worst sinners to look to Jesus Christ. I told them that they do not need good works or a good attitude to get ready to come to Christ. They can come just as they are, to the foot of the cross. There they can receive the blood that forgives and everything the Lord Jesus Christ has earned for them, which is all they need.
0.4	Since then, a thought has come to me. Some people who do not understand the gospel might ask, “Will this help people live good lives?”
0.5	If the gospel announces forgiveness even to the very worst sinners, won’t that give people permission to sin?
0.6	In what way can the gospel be called a message that leads to holiness?
0.7	What effect will preaching like this have?
0.8	Will it make people better?
0.9	Will they pay more attention to the rules about how people treat one another?
0.10	Will they obey the commands about how people relate to God more closely?
0.11	So I thought we would go one step further this morning. I want to show you something about the gospel of God. At the start, it speaks to people who have nothing good in them at all. Yet it is meant to lead those very people to the highest kind of goodness. Yes, in the end it leads them to perfect holiness.
0.12	Our text tells us what happens when the gospel is welcomed into someone’s heart. It pushes and drives that heart to love God.
0.13	“We love him, because he loved us first.”
0.14	When the gospel comes to us, it does not find us loving God. It does not expect anything from us. Instead, the Holy Spirit brings it home to us, and it simply tells us that God loves us, no matter how deep we are in sin. Then this message of love has a result: “we love him because he loved us first.”
0.15	Can you imagine a being who is placed halfway between this world and heaven?
0.16	Can you picture him with such great powers that he could easily see what was happening in heaven and what was happening on earth?
0.17	I can imagine that, before humanity fell into sin, such a being would have been amazed by something. He would have noticed the wonderful harmony between God’s great world, called heaven, and the little world, the earth.
0.18	Whenever the bells of heaven rang, the great note of those huge bells was love. And when the little bells of earth were rung, and this small world sounded its music, the note was exactly the same: love.
0.19	The shining spirits gathered around God’s great throne in heaven to praise the Lord. At that same moment you could see the world dressed like a priest, offering its gift of the purest praise.
0.20	The cherubim and seraphim kept crying out, “Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of armies.” And a note was heard coming up from paradise. It was perhaps weaker, but it was just as sweet: “Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of armies.”
0.21	There was no clash and no sour note. The thunder of heaven’s music matched the whispers of earth’s music exactly.
0.22	There was “glory to God in the highest,” and there was glory on earth too. The heart of man was like the heart of God. God loved man, and man loved God.
0.23	But now imagine that same great Spirit still standing between heaven and earth. How sad he must be when he hears the clashing noise and feels it scrape against his ear!
0.24	The Lord says, “I have made peace with you. I have taken away your sin.” But what does this earth answer?
0.25	The world answers, “Man is God’s enemy. God may be willing to make peace, but man is not.
0.26	Most people are still God’s enemies because of the evil things they do.”
0.27	When the angels praise God, they may listen to the sounds coming from earth. If they do, they hear the trumpet of cruel war. They hear the shouts of drunken parties and the songs of people who live for lust. What a clash that is in the great harmony of the universe!
0.28	Here is the truth. The world was once one great string on the harp of the universe. When the Almighty swept his kind fingers across that harp, nothing could be heard but praise. Now that string has snapped. Where grace has put it back, it is still not fully in tune. The note that comes from it has very little sweetness and a great deal of clashing.
0.29	But, shining Spirit, stay where you are, and live on.
0.30	The day is racing toward us on glowing wheels, and its axle is hot from the speed.
0.31	The day is coming when this world will be a paradise again.
0.32	Jesus Christ came the first time to bleed and suffer, so that he could wash the world clean of its sin. He is coming a second time to reign and win, so that he can dress the earth in glory. And the day will come when you, Spirit, will hear that everlasting harmony again.
0.33	Once again the bells of earth will be tuned to the music of heaven. Once again the eternal choir will find that no singer is missing and the music is complete.
0.34	But how will this happen?
0.35	How will the world be brought back?
0.36	How will it be restored?
0.37	Here is our answer. At the beginning there was harmony between earth and heaven because there was love between the two. And here is our great reason for hoping that perfect harmony between heaven and earth will one day be set up again. God has already shown his love to us. And in return, hearts that his grace has touched already love him now. When there are many more of those hearts, and love is set up again, the harmony will be complete.
0.38	Now that I have introduced my text, I must dive right into it.
0.39	We will look at where love comes from, what feeds it, and how it walks. And I will urge all the believers here to love God, because he loved them first.
0.40	I. First, WHERE TRUE LOVE FOR GOD COMES FROM.
0.41	A planet has no light except the light that comes from the sun. The moon has no light except borrowed light. In the same way, there is no true love in the heart except the love that comes from God.
0.42	Love is the light, the life, and the way of the whole universe.
0.43	And God is life, and light, and the way. Best of all, God is love.
0.44	All our love for God must flow out of this overflowing fountain, God’s endless love.
0.45	This will always be a great and certain truth: we love him for one reason only, because he loved us first.
0.46	Some people think that you could come to love God just by thinking about the things he has made.
0.47	We do not believe that.
0.48	We have heard a lot about philosophers who admire nature. And we agree that you can easily admire God when you study the things he has made.
0.49	We have heard a lot about explorers who are filled with wonder. We agree that a mind must be very low indeed if it does not wonder when it looks at God’s works. Sometimes we have also heard of a love for God that grew out of beautiful scenery. But we have never believed that such a love exists.
0.50	We do believe this: when love has already been born in someone’s heart, all the wonders of God’s care and creation can stir that love up again, because it is already there. But we do not believe, and we cannot believe, that just looking at God’s works could ever lift anyone up to real love. We have never seen it happen even once.
0.51	In fact, this big question has already been tested, and the answer was no.
0.52	Listen to what the poet says:
0.53	“The spicy breezes may blow softly over the island of Java; / every view there is lovely, and only man is wicked.”
0.54	In the places where God shows off his works most brightly and gives his gifts most freely, people have been the most wicked and God has been the most forgotten.
0.55	Other people have taught that human nature can reach love for God on its own. Maybe they did not say it in so many words, but their teaching leads there.
0.56	Our simple answer is that we have never seen it happen.
0.57	We have carefully asked God’s people about this, and we believe others have asked them in every age. But we have only ever gotten one answer to this question: “Why have you loved God?”
0.58	The only answer has been, “Because he loved me first.”
0.59	I have heard people preach about free will. But I have never yet heard of a Christian who gave the credit to free will when he talked about his own life.
0.60	I have heard people say that humans can turn to God by their own free choice, and believe, and repent, and love. But I have heard those very same people describe their own experience differently. They say they did not turn to God that way. Instead, Jesus came looking for them when they were strangers, wandering away from God’s sheep pen.
0.61	The whole idea may sound convincing when it is preached. But when you actually feel it in your own life, it turns out to be a ghost.
0.62	A man might think it is fine to tell someone else that his own free will can save him. But when he has an honest talk with his own conscience, he has to say something else, no matter how wild his teaching is. He has to say, “Oh, yes, I do love Jesus, because he loved me first.”
0.63	I have been amazed at a Wesleyan brother. Sometimes he has attacked this teaching from the pulpit. Then he has announced this very hymn, and all the church members have sung it with all their hearts. Yet while they sang, they were ringing the funeral bell for their own special beliefs. For if that hymn is true, then Arminianism must be false.
0.64	Suppose it is a sure fact that we love God only because his love has been poured into our hearts. Then it simply cannot be true that anyone ever did or ever will love God before God has first shown his love to that person.
0.65	But let’s stop arguing. Don’t we all agree that our love for God is the sweet child of God’s love for us?
0.66	Oh, dear friends, anyone can have cold admiration. But the warmth of love can only be lit by the fire of God’s Spirit.
0.67	Let each Christian speak for himself. We will all hold on to this great and central truth: we love God because of the sweet influence of his grace.
0.68	Sometimes I am amazed that people like us were ever brought to love God at all.
0.69	Is our love so precious that God would try to win it, dressed in the red robes of a dying Redeemer?
0.70	If we had loved God, that would have been no more than he deserved.
0.71	But we rebelled, and he still went after our love. That was truly surprising.
0.72	It was a wonder when he took off all his glory, came down, and wrapped himself in a body of clay. But I think there is an even greater wonder. After he had died for us, we still did not love him. We rebelled against him. We rejected the message of the gospel. We resisted his Spirit. But he said, “I will have their hearts.” And he followed us day after day, hour after hour.
0.73	Sometimes he brought us down low, and he said, “Surely they will love me if I lift them up again!”
0.74	At other times he filled us with grain and wine, and he said, “Surely they will love me now.” But we still turned away. We still rebelled.
0.75	At last he said, “I will not keep struggling. I am the Almighty. I will not let a human heart be stronger than I am.
0.76	I turn a person’s will the way streams of water are turned.” And look! He used his power, and in a moment the current changed. We loved him, because now we could see God’s love in sending his Son to be our Redeemer.
0.77	But, dear friends, let’s go back to the truth we started with. We must admit that we would never have had any love for God if God had not planted it in us. His love for us was the sweet seed it grew from.
0.78	If anyone here loves Christ and disagrees with this teaching, let him disagree for now. But let him know that he will not disagree later. For in heaven they all sing praise to God’s free grace.
0.79	They all sing, “Salvation belongs to our God and to the Lamb.”
0.80	II. So love’s parent is the love of God poured into our hearts.
0.81	But once God has brought love to life in our hearts, God must also FEED it.
0.82	Love is like a plant from a faraway country. It does not grow naturally in human soil.
0.83	Love for God is a rich and rare thing. If it were left out in the icy winds of our selfishness, it would freeze and die. If the only food it got came from the rock of our own hard hearts, it would starve.
0.84	Love comes from heaven, so it must eat bread from heaven.
0.85	It cannot survive in this desert unless it is cared for from above and fed with manna from heaven.
0.86	So what does love feed on?
0.87	Well, it feeds on love.
0.88	The very thing that gave it life becomes its food.
0.89	“We love him because he loved us first.”
0.90	What keeps our love for God moving, and what keeps it going, is his love for us.
0.91	And I should point out that this great storehouse of love has different kinds of food.
0.92	When we are first made new, the only food we can live on is milk. That is because we are just babies and are not yet strong enough to eat deeper truths.
0.93	So the first thing our love feeds on while it is still a baby is knowing how much kindness it has received.
0.94	Ask a young Christian why he loves Christ. He will tell you, “I love Christ because he bought me with his blood!”
0.95	“Why do you love God the Father?”
0.96	“I love God the Father because he gave his Son for me.”
0.97	“And why do you love God the Spirit?”
0.98	“I love him because he has made my heart new.”
0.99	In other words, we love God for what he has given us.
0.100	Our first love lives on simple food: remembering with thanks the kindness we have received.
0.101	And notice this: no matter how much we grow in grace, this will always be a big part of what feeds our love.
0.102	But as the Christian grows older and has more grace, he loves Christ for another reason.
0.103	He loves Christ because he feels that Christ deserves to be loved.
0.104	I hope I can say that I now have a love for God in my heart that has nothing to do with my own salvation.
0.105	I feel that even now I have to love him, because his character is more lovely than words can say.
0.106	His love for other people seems like such a great thought to me. So, apart from what I get out of it, I hope I can say that I love Christ. I have seen something of Christ in the roles he fills, and something of the thrilling beauty of who he is as both God and man.
0.107	I feel as if I could come to his feet and say, “Sweet Lord, at first I loved you because of your gifts to me. But now it is the look in your eyes. You have thrilled my spirit with the glory of who you are. Now I love you, not just because I have eaten your bread and you have met my needs. I love you for who you are.” But notice: at the same time, we must always mix this with the old reason.
0.108	We must still feel that we start from that first stepping-stone, loving Christ because of his mercies. We have climbed higher. We have come to love him with a love that has a better reason. Even so, we still bring the old reason along with us.
0.109	We love him because he has been kind to us.
0.110	In fact, I really think a person can be so full of the love of Christ, and so strengthened by God’s grace, that he rises to an amazing level of love for Christ. If you could hear him speak, you would sit there in wonder, as if an angel were talking to you.
0.111	Have you ever read the heavenly letter of Rutherford?
0.112	I really think that if any bit of the old inspiration that guided Solomon’s pen is still left among people, it rested on Rutherford.
0.113	If you read the poems of sweet George Herbert, oh, how sweetly he sings about his Master!
0.114	If any of heaven’s harps was accidentally left behind on earth, George Herbert found one. He played its living strings with such heavenly skill that he made every string point to his Master.
0.115	These men did not love Christ only because of what he had done for them. You will find in their poems and letters that they loved him because he had spent close time with them. He had shown them his hands and his side. They had walked with him in the villages. They had rested with him on the beds of spices. They had entered the secret circle of friendship with him. And they felt that they loved Christ because he was glorious all over. He was so heavenly beautiful that if all nations could see him, surely they would be moved to love him too.
0.116	This, then, is what love feeds on. But when love grows rich (and sometimes it does), even the most loving heart grows cold toward Christ.
0.117	Did you know that the only food that suits sick love is the food it ate at the beginning?
0.118	I have heard doctors say that when a person is sick, the best place for him is the place where he was born. In the same way, if love gets sick and cold, the best place for it to go is the place where it was born: the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.
0.119	Where was love born?
0.120	Was she born among romantic scenery? Was she raised on beautiful thoughts, sitting on the lap of beauty?
0.121	Oh, no. Was she born on the steep slopes of Mount Sinai, when God came from Sinai and the Holy One came from Mount Paran? When he melted the mountains with the touch of his foot and made the rocks run down like wax before his terrifying presence?
0.122	Oh, no. Was love born on Mount Tabor, when the Savior was transfigured and his clothes became whiter than wool, whiter than anyone could ever wash them?
0.123	Oh, no. Darkness rushed over the eyes of those who looked at him then. They fell asleep, because the glory was too much for them.
0.124	Let me tell you where love was born.
0.125	Love was born in the garden of Gethsemane, where Jesus sweated great drops of blood. It was raised in Pilate’s hall, where Jesus bared his back to the whip that cut into it like a plow. There he let his body be spit on and beaten.
0.126	Love was raised at the cross, among the groans of a dying God, under the drops of his blood. That is where love was raised.
0.127	Children of God, you can back me up.
0.128	Where did your love come from, if not from the foot of the cross?
0.129	Have you ever seen that sweet flower growing anywhere except at the foot of Calvary?
0.130	No. It was when you saw “love divine, all loves excelling,” going beyond even itself. You saw love tied up by its own choice, dying by its own blow. You saw it lay down its life, even though it had the power to keep it and to take it up again. That is where your love was born. So if your love is sick and you want it to get better, take it to those sweet places. Make it sit in the shade of the olive trees. Make it stand on the stone pavement and stare while the blood is still pouring down.
0.131	Take it to the cross, and tell it to look and see the bleeding Lamb all over again. Surely this will make your love grow from a dwarf into a giant. This will fan it from a spark into a flame.
0.132	And then, once your love has been built back up like this, let me urge you to give your love plenty of exercise, because that will make it grow.
0.133	You ask, “Where should I exercise my love by thinking, so that it will grow?”
0.134	Oh, sacred Dove of love, spread your wings and be an eagle now!
0.135	Come! Open your eyes wide and look straight into the face of the Sun. Fly upward, upward, upward, far above the highest heights of this created world. Keep going up until you are lost in eternity.
0.136	Remember that God loved you before the world was even made.
0.137	Doesn’t this make your love stronger?
0.138	Oh, how fresh and bracing the air of eternity is!
0.139	When I fly up into it for a moment and think about the great teaching of election—about
0.140	“That vast love that no one can measure, / which from long ago / held all the chosen children close, / like sheep inside the fold.”
0.141	It brings tears to our eyes to think that we have a share in that decision made in the council of the Almighty Three. At that time, the name of every person who would be bought with blood was written in God’s eternal book.
0.142	Come, soul, I tell you now to stretch your wings a little. See if this does not make you love God.
0.143	He thought about you before you even existed.
0.144	He did this before there was a sun or a moon. The sun, the moon, and the stars were still asleep in God’s mind, like unborn forests inside an acorn cup. The old sea had not yet been born. This baby world was not yet wrapped in its blanket of mist. Long before all that, God had written your name on the heart and on the hands of Christ, where it can never be erased and will stay forever.
0.145	And doesn’t this make you love God?
0.146	Isn’t this sweet exercise for your love?
0.147	This is where my text comes in. It is like the final charge in this sweet battle of love, a charge that sweeps everything out of its way.
0.148	“We love God, because he loved us first.” He loved us before time began, when he lived alone in eternity.
0.149	And when you have flown back into the eternity before time, I have another flight for you.
0.150	Fly back over your whole life. Think of how the Lord your God has led you through the desert. Think of how he has fed you and clothed you every day. He has put up with your bad manners. He has put up with all your complaining and all your longing for the meat pots of Egypt. He has opened the rock to provide for you and fed you with manna that came down from heaven.
0.151	Think of how his grace has been enough for you in all your troubles. Think of how his blood has forgiven you in all your sins. Think of how his rod and his staff have comforted you.
0.152	And when you have flown over this sweet field of love, you can fly on even further. Remember that the oath, the covenant, and the blood hold more than just the past. It is true that “he loved us first.” But that does not mean he will ever stop loving. He is the Alpha, and he will be the Omega. He is the first, and he will be the last. So think about this: when you walk through the valley of the shadow of death, you do not need to fear any evil, because he is with you.
0.153	When you stand in the cold waters of the Jordan, you do not need to be afraid, because death cannot separate you from his love. And when you enter the mysteries of eternity, you do not need to tremble. For “I am sure that no rulers, no powers, nothing now, nothing to come, nothing high, nothing deep, and nothing else that was ever made will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”
0.154	And now, soul, isn’t your love refreshed?
0.155	Doesn’t this make you love him?
0.156	When you fly over those endless open skies of love, doesn’t it set your heart on fire? Doesn’t it make you delight in the Lord your God?
0.157	This is the food of love.
0.158	“We love him, because he loved us first.” And we love him because that first love holds a pledge and a promise that he will love us all the way to the end.
0.159	III. Now we come to the third point: the WALK OF LOVE.
0.160	“We love him.” Children of God, if Christ were here on earth, what would you do for him?
0.161	Suppose there was news tomorrow that the Son of Man had come down from heaven, as he did the first time. What would you do for him?
0.162	Suppose there was a witness you could not doubt, who said that the feet that walked the holy fields of Palestine were now walking the roads of Great Britain. What would you do for him?
0.163	Oh, I can imagine the excitement of happy hearts. So many generous hands would be held out. There would be a sea of tearful eyes looking at him.
0.164	“Do for him!” someone says. “Do for him!
0.165	If he were hungry, I would give him food, even if it were my last crust of bread.
0.166	If he were thirsty, I would give him a drink, even if my own lips were burning as if on fire.
0.167	If he had no clothes, I would take off my own and shiver in the cold to dress him.
0.168	Do for him! I would hardly know what to do.
0.169	I would rush to him and throw myself at his dear feet. I would beg him to step on me and crush me into the dust, if that would honor him, if it would lift him even one inch higher.
0.170	If he wanted a soldier, I would join his army. If he needed someone to die, I would let my body be burned, as long as he stood by to watch the sacrifice and cheer me on in the flames.”
0.171	You daughters of Jerusalem, wouldn’t you go out to meet him? Wouldn’t you celebrate with tambourines and dancing?
0.172	Then you might dance like Miriam beside the waters of Egypt, red with blood.
0.173	And we men would dance like David before the ark, jumping for joy, if Christ had come.
0.174	Ah, we think we love him so much that we would do all that. But after all, there is a serious question about whether this is really true.
0.175	Don’t you know that Christ’s wife and family are here?
0.176	And if you love him, wouldn’t it naturally follow that you would love his bride and his children?
0.177	“Ah!” someone says, “Christ has no bride on earth.”
0.178	Doesn’t he? Hasn’t he promised himself in marriage to his church?
0.179	Isn’t his church, the mother of all believers, his own chosen wife?
0.180	And didn’t he give his blood as his wedding gift for her?
0.181	And hasn’t he said that he will never divorce her, because he hates divorce? Hasn’t he said that he will complete the marriage on the last great day, when he comes to reign with his people on the earth?
0.182	And doesn’t he have children here?
0.183	“The daughters of Jerusalem and the sons of Zion—who has brought these children into being for me?”
0.184	Aren’t they the children of the everlasting Father, the Prince of Peace, the child who was born, the son who was given?
0.185	Of course they are. And if we love Christ, as we think we do and as we claim we do, we will love his church and his people.
0.186	And do you love his church?
0.187	Maybe you love the part of it you belong to.
0.188	You love the hand.
0.189	Maybe it is a hand decorated with many sparkling rings of grand ceremonies, and you love that.
0.190	Or maybe you belong to some poor, struggling denomination. Maybe it is the foot, and you love the foot. But you speak with scorn about the hand, because it has more honors.
0.191	And meanwhile, maybe you who belong to the hand are looking down on those who belong to the foot.
0.192	Brothers and sisters, all of us tend to love only one part of Christ’s body and not the whole body. But if we love him, we should love all his people.
0.193	When we kneel to pray, I am afraid that when we pray for the church, we don’t mean everything we say.
0.194	We are really praying for our own church, our own part of it.
0.195	Now think of someone who loves Christ. Say he is a Baptist. He loves the teaching about baptism, because he knows it comes from Scripture. But at the same time, whenever he sees God’s grace in anyone’s heart, he loves that person, because he is part of the living church. He does not hold back his heart, his hand, or his home from him just because they happen to disagree on one point.
0.196	I pray that the church today will have a more loving spirit toward herself.
0.197	We should be glad whenever any denomination moves forward.
0.198	Is the Church of England waking up from its sleep?
0.199	Is she rising like a phoenix from her ashes?
0.200	May God be with her, and may God bless her!
0.201	Is another denomination taking the lead, and trying through its ministers to bring wanderers into God’s house?
0.202	May God be with it!
0.203	Is the Primitive Methodist working hard in the hedges and ditches, laboring for his Master?
0.204	God help him! Is the Calvinist trying to hold up Christ crucified in all his glory?
0.205	May God be with him!
0.206	And is there another man with much less knowledge who preaches a lot of error, but still holds on to the truth that “you are saved by grace through faith”? Then God bless him, and may he have success forever.
0.207	If you loved Christ more, you would love all of Christ’s church and all of Christ’s people.
0.208	Don’t you know that Christ still has a mouth on earth, and has left a hand and a foot on earth too? If you want to prove your love for him, don’t think you cannot feed him. Don’t imagine that you cannot fill his hand or wash his feet.
0.209	You can do all of this today.
0.210	He has left behind his poor and suffering people. Their mouths are hungry because they need bread. Their tongues are dry because they need water.
0.211	You meet them. They come to you. They have nothing, and they are suffering.
0.212	Do you turn them away?
0.213	Do you know who it was you turned away at your door?
0.214	“Every time you failed to do it for one of the least important of these my brothers, you failed to do it for me.”
0.215	When you turned down a poor person’s request when you could have helped, you turned down Christ.
0.216	In reality, Christ was the man you stingily refused to give the money he needed. So your Savior was turned away at the door of someone he himself had died for.
0.217	Do you want to feed Christ?
0.218	Then open your eyes, and you will see him everywhere. In our back streets, in our lanes, in our alleys, in all our churches, in every branch of Christ’s people, you will find the poor and the suffering.
0.219	If you want to feed Christ, feed them.
0.220	But you say you are willing to wash Christ’s feet. Well, you can do that.
0.221	Doesn’t he have children who have fallen?
0.222	Aren’t there brothers who have sinned and have gotten dirty by it?
0.223	You say that if Christ’s feet were dirty, you would wash them. Then if a Christian has wandered off the path, try to bring him back, and lead him once again in the right way.
0.224	And do you want to fill Christ’s hands with your generous giving?
0.225	His church is the storehouse for his gifts to the needy. The hand of his church is stretched out for help, because she always needs it.
0.226	She has a work to do that must be finished.
0.227	She is short of what she needs because you are holding back your help. Pour your gifts into her treasury, because everything you give to her is given to the Lord Jesus Christ.
0.228	Finally, to stir up your love, let me remind you that Christ Jesus faced two tests of his love. He stood firm through them, but they are often too much for us.
0.229	When Christ was high and glorious, I am amazed that he loved us.
0.230	I have known many men who loved a friend while they were both equally poor. But then the man rose up in the world, and he was too proud to know the friend whose table he had eaten at.
0.231	Rising to a high position tests the love we have for those who are below us in rank.
0.232	Now, Christ Jesus is the Lord of heaven and the King of angels. Yet he stooped to notice us even before he came to earth, and he always called us brothers. And since he has gone up to heaven, put his crown back on, and sat down again at God’s right hand, he has never forgotten us.
0.233	His high position has never made him look down on a single disciple.
0.234	When he rode into Jerusalem in triumph, we do not read that he was too proud to admit that the humble fishermen were his followers.
0.235	And “now, though he reigns exalted high, his love is still as great.” He still calls us brothers and friends. He still admits that we share the same blood.
0.236	And yet, strangely, we have known many Christians who lost much of their love for Christ when they rose in the world.
0.237	There was a woman who used to do a lot for Christ when she was poor. Then someone left her a large sum of money. “Ah!” she said, “I cannot do as much as I used to.”
0.238	“But why is that?” someone asked.
0.239	She answered, “When I had a purse with only a shilling in it, I had a heart worth a guinea. Now that I have a purse worth a guinea, I have a heart worth only a shilling.”
0.240	For some people, getting rich is a sad temptation.
0.241	When they had little, they were happy to go to the chapel and mix with the plain, ordinary congregation. Now they have become rich. There is an expensive Turkish carpet in the living room. Their home is now too fancy for them to invite the poor members of the church, as they once did. And Christ Jesus is not fashionable enough for them to bring up anything about faith when they meet their new friends.
0.242	On top of this, they say they now have to make this visit and that visit. They must spend so much time on their clothes and on keeping up their position and reputation that they cannot find time to pray as they used to.
0.243	They skip God’s house to go to parties, and Christ has less of their heart than he ever had.
0.244	“Is this how you treat your friend?”
0.245	Have you risen so high that you are ashamed of Christ? Have you become so rich that you look down on Christ in his poverty?
0.246	How sad, poor wealth! How sad, worthless wealth! Wicked wealth!
0.247	It would be good for you if all of it were swept away, if falling into poverty would bring back the burning warmth of your love.
