0.248	Here is one more thing. What a test of love it was when Christ began to suffer for us!
0.249	I am sure there are many men who truly believe and love their Savior, but who would shake with fear if they were put to the test of suffering.
0.250	My brother, imagine that today you are taken into a dark prison cell of the Inquisition. Picture all the horrors of the Dark Ages coming back. You are led down a long, dark staircase and rushed along, and you do not know where you are going. At last you come to a place deep under the ground. All around you, hanging on the walls, you see pincers and torture tools of every kind and shape.
0.251	Two inquisitors are there, and they ask you, “Are you ready to give up your false beliefs and come back into the arms of the church?”
0.252	My brothers and sisters, I think you would have enough strength of mind and enough grace to say, “I am not ready to deny my Savior.”
0.253	But then the pincers would begin to tear your flesh. The hot coals would begin to burn you. The rack would begin to pull your bones out of their joints. Every torture tool would be doing its evil work on you, as cruel as hell. Unless God’s supernatural hand were powerfully on you, I am sure that in your weakness you would deny your Master. In your hour of danger you would abandon the Lord who bought you.
0.254	It is true that the love of Christ in our hearts, when his grace holds it up, is strong enough to carry us through. But I am afraid that many of us here, if we had no more love than we have now, would come out of the Inquisition as miserable people who had given up the faith.
0.255	But now, think of Christ.
0.256	He faced tortures that were really far, far more terrible.
0.257	No torture machine of the cruel Roman Catholic Inquisition can equal the dreadful agony that made him sweat blood from every pore.
0.258	Christ was whipped, and he was crucified. But he also suffered other pains that we could not see, and those were the very heart of his agony.
0.259	Now, suppose that in that hour of terrible testing Christ had said, “I disown my followers. I will not die.” He could have come down from the cross. And who could have blamed him for doing wrong?
0.260	He owed us nothing. We could do nothing for him.
0.261	All he would have been rejecting was a few poor worms.
0.262	But even when his bloody sweat covered him like a red robe, our Master never once thought of rejecting us. NEVER.
0.263	Once he said, “My Father, if it is possible, let this cup be taken away from me.”
0.264	But he always kept the words “If it is possible.”
0.265	If it is possible to save without drinking it, then let the cup pass. But if not, may your will be done.
0.266	In Pilate’s hall you never hear him say a single word that would make you think he was sorry he had taken on such a costly sacrifice for us. His hands are pierced. He burns with fever. His tongue is as dry as a piece of broken pottery. His whole body is being brought down into the dust of death. Yet you never hear a groan or a scream that sounds like he wants to turn back.
0.267	It is the cry of someone determined to keep going, even though he knows he must die as he marches on.
0.268	It was a love that even death could not stop, and it conquered all the horrors of the grave.
0.269	So what do we say to this?
0.270	We live in much gentler times. Are we going to give up our Master when we are tested and tempted because of him?
0.271	Young man in the workshop! You have to put up with being mocked because you follow the Savior. Will you turn away from Christ just because someone makes fun of you?
0.272	Young woman! People laugh at you because you say you follow Christ. Should a laugh break the bond of love that ties your heart to him? All the roaring of hell could not turn his love away from you.
0.273	And some of you are suffering because you stand firm on a matter of faith. Are people shutting you out? Will you not put up with losing everything in your house, and even with eating the bread of poverty, rather than bring shame on such a Lord?
0.274	With the help of God’s Spirit, will you not leave this place making a firm promise? Promise that in life, whether poverty comes or wealth comes, and in death, whether pain comes or anything else at all, you belong to the Lord and always will. For this is written on your heart: “We love him, because he first loved us.”
