0.1	“Forgive one another, just as God has forgiven you for Christ’s sake.”—Ephesians 4:32.
0.2	The pagan teachers of right and wrong could not point to their gods as an example when they wanted to teach people to be good. Their own storytellers described the gods as a mix of every evil you can imagine. I almost said: every evil you cannot even imagine.
0.3	Many of the old gods of Greece and Rome committed worse crimes than the worst human beings. They were supposed to be far more powerful than people, and they were just as much more wicked.
0.4	It is a sad day for a nation when its gods are worse than its people.
0.5	Our holy faith is wonderfully pure. You can see this in what it commands. But you can also see it in the character of the God it shows us.
0.6	Think of any good quality you could name. You will see it shining brightly in the Lord our God. Think of any way a Christian should live well. We can point to Christ Jesus, our Lord and Master, as the perfect example of it.
0.7	At the very top of the Christian faith, you find the very highest goodness. So the highest praise belongs to God our Father and to the Lord Jesus.
0.8	We want you to have the kindest, most forgiving spirit. And we can urge you toward it by pointing to God. For Christ’s sake, he has forgiven you.
0.9	What better reason could you need to forgive one another?
0.10	With such great examples in front of us, brothers and sisters, what kind of people should we be?
0.11	Sometimes we hear of people who were better than their religion. But that can never happen to us. We can never rise as high as our wonderful, God-given faith—not in our attitude and not in our actions.
0.12	We should keep rising above ourselves. We should even rise above the kindest Christians we know. And still, high above us, we will see our God and Savior.
0.13	We may grow stronger and stronger in good thoughts and in serving God. But Jesus is always higher still. As we climb the holy hill of grace, we must keep looking up to him.
0.14	Today we want to talk a little about our duty to love and forgive. Right away we notice that the apostle gives us God himself as our example.
0.15	We will spend most of our time on that bright example. But I hope we will not spend so much time that we forget the practical part. That part is badly needed these days by some unforgiving people who still call themselves Christians.
0.16	God’s forgiving love is such a wonderful subject that we may stay on it for a while—a long while, in fact. God has given us a bright example of forgiveness. As we look at it, I hope we will receive grace to forgive others, even “seventy times seven” times.
0.17	We will go through the verse word by word. That will give us the clearest outline.
0.18	I. The first words to think about are “FOR CHRIST’S SAKE.”
0.19	We say these words very often. But we have probably never thought about how powerful they are. Even today, we cannot explain everything they mean.
0.20	Let us think about them carefully. And let us pray that the good Holy Spirit will teach us.
0.21	“For Christ’s sake.” Every good thing God has given us has come to us “for Christ’s sake.” But this is especially true of the forgiveness of our sins. It has come “for Christ’s sake.”
0.22	That is exactly what the verse says.
0.23	What does it mean?
0.24	First, it surely means this: God forgives us because of the great sacrifice Christ offered to pay for sin.
0.25	The great God is a fair Lawgiver and King. Because Christ has made the payment for sin, God can freely let go of our wrongs.
0.26	If sin were only a personal insult to God, there is plenty of proof that he would gladly let it go without punishing it. But sin is much more than that.
0.27	People who think sin is only a personal insult to God are not thinking very deeply.
0.28	Sin is an attack on God’s moral rule over the world. It eats away at the foundations of society. If it were allowed to have its way, it would turn everything into chaos. It would even destroy the power that governs, and the Ruler himself.
0.29	God has a huge kingdom to rule. It is not only the people who live on the earth. Under his rule there are angels, rulers and powers, and who knows how many worlds of thinking beings.
0.30	Think of the countless worlds we see sparkling in the night sky. It would be a strange idea that God made them all without putting any living creatures there. It makes much more sense to think that our earth is just a tiny speck in God’s kingdom. It is only one small province in the endless empire of the King of kings.
0.31	Now, this world has rebelled against God boldly and openly. If God did not demand payment for that rebellion, he would be putting up with an attack on the rule of the great Judge of all. It would weaken his royal authority over his whole kingdom.
0.32	If human sin went unpunished, the news would soon spread through countless worlds. Millions upon millions of kinds of creatures would learn that they could sin and get away with it. If one race had done it, why not all the others?
0.33	It would be like announcing to everyone that they were free to rebel.
0.34	It would probably be the worst disaster that could ever happen—for the highest Judge to let any sin go unpunished.
0.35	Think of a country. If the lawmaker does not carry out the law against a murderer, people’s lives will be in danger. Nothing will be safe. So signing the death sentence becomes an act of mercy. It is the same with God and this world of sinners.
0.36	God’s holiness and justice make him judge sin strictly. But so does his love—if I may put it that way. This is why sin cannot and must not be wiped away until a payment for it has been made.
0.37	First there must be a sacrifice for sin. But notice this: the great Father supplies that sacrifice himself, to show his love. He gives his own Son to die. So the Father himself pays the ransom through his Son. And that Son is one with the Father. Their unity is a mystery, but it is extremely deep.
0.38	God’s justice demands the penalty, and God’s love pays it himself. The way of salvation through a sacrifice that pays for sin is an amazing mystery. But this much is clear: God has now forgiven us for Christ’s sake. God’s rule was dishonored, but full payment has been made for it, and justice is satisfied.
0.39	Think for a moment about how easily God can now wipe out sin, because Christ has died.
0.40	Wiping out sin seems hard until we look at the cross. Then it seems easy enough.
0.41	I have stared at sin until its horror almost blinded me. I said to myself, “This cursed stain can never be washed out. No soap in the world can change its color. An Ethiopian could sooner change the color of his skin, or a leopard its spots.
0.42	Oh sin, you deep and never-ending evil, what can take you away?”
0.43	But then I have seen the Son of God dying on the cross. I have read about the pain in his soul. I have heard the cries that showed how his spirit suffered when God his Father left him. And then it has seemed to me that wiping out sin is the easiest thing in the world.
0.44	When I have seen Jesus die, I could not understand how any sin could be hard to remove.
0.45	Let a person stand at Calvary and look at the one he has pierced. Let him believe and accept the payment Jesus made. Then it becomes the simplest thing in the world. His debt is cancelled, because it has been paid. He is set free, because the ransom has been found. He is no longer condemned, because the guilt that condemned him has been carried away by Jesus, his great Substitute and Lord.
0.46	So God has forgiven us for Christ’s sake because of what Jesus Christ suffered in our place.
0.47	Here is a second way to understand the verse. God has forgiven us because Christ acts as our representative—he stands in for us.
0.48	We should never forget that in the beginning we fell through a representative.
0.49	Adam stood in our place. He was our covenant head, acting on behalf of all of us.
0.50	At the very beginning we did not fall by our own actions. We fell through the one who represented us.
0.51	If Adam had kept the terms of God’s covenant, we would have stood firm through him. But because he fell, we fell with him.
0.52	Please do not complain about this arrangement. That is exactly where the hope of the human race was found.
0.53	The angels probably fell one by one, each for himself. That is why they fell with no way back—there was no restoring them. But we all fell in one man, Adam. So it was still possible for us to rise again in another Adam. That is why, at exactly the right time, God sent his Son Jesus Christ. He was born of a woman and placed under the law, to become the second Adam.
0.54	He took on the job of removing our burdens and meeting every condition for bringing us back.
0.55	According to God’s covenant, he had to come in our human nature. And at the right time he did become human.
0.56	He had to bear the penalty. He did that through his own suffering and death.
0.57	He had to obey the law. He did that completely.
0.58	So now Christ Jesus has borne the penalty and kept the law. He himself is declared right before God. And he stands before God as the representative of everyone who is in him.
0.59	For Christ’s sake, God has accepted us in him. He has forgiven us in him. And in him he looks at us with a love that has no limit and never changes.
0.60	This is how all our blessings come to us: in and through Christ Jesus. If we really are in him, the Lord does more than forgive our sin. He also gives us the endless riches of his grace in him. In fact, he treats us the way he would treat his Son. He deals with us the way he would deal with Jesus.
0.61	Oh, what a happy thought! When God, who is just, looks at us, he looks through the one who brings us back together with him. He sees us through the Mediator.
0.62	Sometimes we sing a hymn that says—
0.63	“Look at him, and then see the sinner; / Look at me through Jesus’ wounds,”
0.64	and that is exactly what the Lord does.
0.65	He counts us as righteous because of our Savior’s sacrifice, and because Jesus represents us.
0.66	Now let us go a little further.
0.67	When we read “for Christ’s sake,” it surely also means this: because of the deep love the Father has for him.
0.68	My brothers and sisters, can you begin to imagine how much the Father loves his one and only Son?
0.69	We cannot poke into the amazing mystery of how the Son of God has been the Son forever. If we tried, the light would be so bright that it would blind us. But this we know: they are one God—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. And the bond between them is deeper than we can imagine.
0.70	“The Father loves the Son.” That was always true, and it is true now. But no mind can grasp how deeply and how strongly he loves the Son.
0.71	Now, brothers and sisters, the Lord will do great things for the sake of a Son he loves the way he loves Jesus. He has always loved him, because they share one nature and being. But now there is another reason for his love. It comes from everything the Lord Jesus has done as the Father’s servant.
0.72	Remember that our Lord Jesus obeyed his Father’s will. He obeyed even to death—even to dying on the cross. That is why God has lifted him to the highest place and given him a name greater than every other name.

0.74	Here is another piece of honeycomb. When I can pray in Christ’s name, I am sure God will grant my request.
0.75	“For Christ’s sake” is a plea that always touches the heart of the great God.
0.76	Show that when you receive a certain blessing, it will bring glory to Christ. Then the Father cannot hold it back, because he loves to honor Jesus.
0.77	Of course, we are speaking in human terms. We must be careful with a subject like this. But we can only speak as humans, because that is all we are.
0.78	The Father loves to show his love to his Son.
0.79	Through all the ages, the Father and the Son have been close companions. They have always agreed in all their plans. They have never disagreed about anything, and they cannot disagree. 0.80	Their love for each other is greater than we can imagine. And so, brothers and sisters, God will do anything for Jesus.
0.81	God will forgive us for Christ’s sake. In fact, he has already done that for thousands of people around me.
0.82	And you, great sinner, stained black with sin: go to God right now. Say to him, “Lord, I cannot ask you to forgive me for my own sake. But please do it out of love for your dear Son.” If you do, he will forgive you. He will do anything for the sake of Jesus.
0.83	Maybe right now you feel your sin so much that you have given up on yourself. That is good. Giving up on yourself is just common sense, because there is nothing in you that you can depend on.
0.84	But grab hold of this hope. It is not a flimsy straw. It is a strong, solid life buoy. If you can ask for forgiveness for Jesus’ sake, remember this: God will do anything for Jesus. And for the sake of the Son he loves, he will do anything for you.
0.85	So let us read our verse once more. This time we read it in the light of a truth that comes from God’s love: God forgives sin in order to bring glory to Christ.
0.86	Christ took the shame so that he could bring honor to his Father. And now his Father loves to honor him by wiping out sin.
0.87	If you can show that a gift to you would bring glory to Christ, you can count on it: you will get it.
0.88	If there were anything in the world that would make Christ more glorious, the Father would not hold it back for a moment.
0.89	Maybe you can see that having your sin forgiven would make the Savior more famous. Then go and use that argument with God, and you will surely win.
0.90	Won’t it make Christ glad to save a sinner like you?
0.91	Then go to God with this argument on your lips: “Father, bring glory to your Son. Show that he is a glorious Savior by saving me.”

That is why I beg you to be kind to me.”
0.93	You can be sure you will win if you can argue that it will bring glory to Christ. And surely you would not want anything that would not bring him glory.
0.94	Your prayer will always win if your heart is in the right place. That means you are willing to have something or not to have it, depending on whether it will honor your Lord. 0.95	“For Christ’s sake.” These are precious words. Think about them often. Store this sentence away in your memory: the Father will do anything for the sake of Jesus Christ his Son.
