0.2	Earlier in this chapter, the apostle Paul tells about the great troubles he faced while serving as a minister.
0.3	In our text and the two verses before it, he tells what comforted him and held him up during those troubles.
0.4	He names four things in particular.
0.5	1. First, his own conscience approved of him. He says in verse 12: “This is what makes us glad: our conscience tells us that we have lived in the world with simple honesty and a sincerity that comes from God. 0.6	2. The second comfort he mentions is this. His own conscience approved of him, and so did the consciences of his listeners, the Corinthians he was now writing to. And they would approve of him on the day of judgment.
0.7	3. Third, he hoped to see the good fruit of all his work and suffering as a minister. He would see it in their happiness and glory on that great day when everyone must give an account.
0.8	4. Fourth, in his work among the Corinthians, he had been approved by his Judge. And that Judge would approve and reward his faithfulness on that day.
0.9	The last three of these are shown in my text and the verse before it. In fact, all four are hinted at in the text. The text suggests that the Corinthians had accepted Paul as their spiritual father. They saw him as someone who had been faithful among them. He was the one God used to bring them their future joy and glory on the day of judgment. They would see him then and have a joyful meeting with him. The text also suggests that Paul expected to have a joyful meeting with them at that time, standing before the Judge. He expected to see their glory with joy, because it was the fruit of his work. In that way they would be his joy. The text also suggests that he expected the great Judge to approve of him when he and they met together before him. The Judge would then recognize that Paul had been faithful, and that this faithfulness had helped bring them to glory. And so the Judge would, in a way, give them to Paul as his crown of joy.
0.10	But Paul could not hope for any of this unless his own conscience was on his side.
0.11	So these words show very strongly that his own conscience approved of him.
0.12	There is one thing hidden in each of these points, and in every part of the text. That is the point I will make the subject of my sermon today. Here it is:
H:0.8	THE MAIN TEACHING
0.13	Ministers and the people in their care will have to meet each other before Christ’s judgment seat on the day of judgment.
0.14	Ministers and the people who have been in their care must be parted in this world, no matter how close they have been. If nothing separates them sooner, death will part them. And they may be separated even while they are still alive.
0.15	We live in a world of change. Nothing here is certain or steady. In a short time, in just a few years, strange things happen. Surprising changes come to people, to families, to towns and churches, and to countries and nations.
0.16	It often happens that people who seem the closest are soon the most divided and the farthest apart.
0.17	So it is with ministers and their people. They may have had the greatest respect for each other and the closest bond. Yet they may come to disagree in their opinions and grow cold in their feelings. One side may even break away from the other, and the whole relationship may be ended. The minister may be moved to a faraway place. They may never have anything to do with each other again in this world.
0.18	But even if that happens, there is one more meeting they must have. That meeting will be on the last great day, when everyone must give an account.
0.19	Here I want to show three things:
0.20	I. How ministers and the people who have been in their care will meet each other on the day of judgment.
0.21	II. Why they will meet.
0.22	III. Why God has arranged for ministers and their people to meet together then, in this way and for these purposes.
0.23	I. First, I want to show in a few points how ministers and the people who have been in their care will meet each other on the day of judgment.
0.24	About this I want to point out two general things.
0.25	1. They will not just meet the way all people will meet then. There will be something special about the way they meet.
0.26	2. Their meeting at that time will be very different from the way they used to meet in God’s house in this world.
0.27	1. On that day they will not meet the way the whole world will meet together.
0.28	I want to point out two differences.
0.29	(1) The first difference is that they will clearly see each other. They will know and notice each other distinctly.
0.30	The whole world will be there on that day. All people from every generation will be gathered in one huge crowd, along with all the angels, both the chosen angels and the fallen ones. But we don’t need to think that everyone will know every single person in that whole crowd. There will surely be many millions upon millions of them.
0.31	People will probably be able to understand far more then than they can now. But their minds will not be unlimited. Their understanding will grow enormously, but people will not become gods.
0.32	Each person will probably be able to see much of that huge crowd and much of what happens on that great day. But minds that have limits can only take in so much. So on that day, each person will notice some people and some things more than others. And we can guess that this will depend on who they were most connected with in what happens that day.
0.33	There is a special reason why people who had special dealings with each other in this world will be placed where they can see each other. This life was their time of testing. On that day, their dealings with each other will be examined and judged.
0.34	So we can expect that rulers and the people they ruled, earthly judges and the people they judged, and neighbors who talked, did business, and argued with each other will meet then. So will the heads of families and their children and servants. They will be placed together in a special way.
0.35	This will be true most of all for ministers and their people. Our text makes it clear that they will see each other. They will know each other clearly and pay special attention to each other at that time.
0.36	(2) The second difference is that they will meet as people who have special business with each other in the great events of that day.
0.37	They will meet the whole world at that time. But they will not have direct, personal business with everyone.
0.38	In fact, most of the people gathered there will be people they never had anything to do with during their time of testing on earth. So they will have nothing between them to be judged.
0.39	But ministers and the people who have been in their care are different. They have had a great deal to do with each other directly. And it was about the most important matters that people can ever share with one another.
0.40	So they especially must meet and be brought together before the Judge. They have special business with each other in the purpose and work of that great day when everyone gives an account.
0.41	So the way they meet will be different from the way people in general meet.
0.42	2. Their meeting on the day of judgment will be very different from the ways they meet with each other in this world.
0.43	While the relationship lasts, ministers and their people often meet together in this world.
0.44	They usually meet every Sunday, and at other times too. They come together for public worship, to share in the sacraments, and for the serious services of God’s house.
0.45	Besides these meetings, they also meet at times to decide and run the business of the church. They meet to carry out church discipline. And they meet to settle matters that keep the church’s public worship pure and in good order.
0.46	But their meeting on the day of judgment will be extremely different from any meeting they have with each other now, both in how it happens and in what surrounds it.
0.47	Let me show how, in a few points.
0.48	(1) Now they meet at a time of preparation, when things can still change. But then they will meet when nothing can change anymore.
0.49	Now sinners in the church meet their minister at a time when they can still be changed and saved. Through God’s blessing on the pastor’s preaching and work, they can still be turned from Satan’s power to God. They can be brought out of guilt, condemnation, and God’s anger. They can be brought into peace and friendship with God. They can enjoy the privileges of his children and have a right to their eternal inheritance.
0.50	And true believers now meet their minister while much sin still remains in them. Sometimes they are struggling with great spiritual problems and pain. So they can still be helped to reach a happier condition, with more freedom from these things. They have reason to hope for this help as they faithfully take part in worship and the sacraments. And God usually chooses to use his ministers to bring this help.
0.51	Ministers and their people meet now to bring about these happy changes. These are the great blessings they look for when they meet for worship in this world.
0.52	But when they meet together on the day of judgment, it will be very different.
0.53	They will not meet then to use the means God gives to bring about any such changes. That is because they will all be in a state that can never change.
0.54	Sinners will not be able to change. Those who are still guilty and ruled by sin then, with God’s anger resting on them, will be past all help. There will be no chance of change for them. They will meet their ministers with no hope of relief or rescue, and no hope of getting any good from them.
0.55	And the true believers will already be completely free from all the sin, temptation, and troubles of every kind that still clung to them before. They will be placed forever out of reach of those things. There will be no more rescue or happy change left to happen through the means of grace or the work of ministers.
0.56	Then it will be announced: “Let the unjust stay unjust, and the filthy stay filthy. Let the righteous stay righteous, and the holy stay holy.”
0.57	(2) Then they will meet together in a light that is clear, certain, and free from any mistake.
0.58	Ministers are placed in the church as guides and teachers. The Bible pictures them as lights set up in the churches. In this life they meet their people again and again to teach them and help them understand. They correct their mistakes. When the people turn aside to the right or to the left, the minister is like a voice behind them saying, “This is the way. Walk in it.” Ministers prove and support the truth by showing the evidence for it. They answer errors and false ideas. They try to convince those who are wrong and to make those who doubt more sure.
0.59	But when Christ comes to judge, every error and false idea will be exposed. All lies and illusions will disappear in the light of that day, the way the darkness of night disappears when the sun rises. Every teaching of God’s word will then be plainly shown to be true, and no one will remain unconvinced. Everyone will know the truth with complete certainty, and there will be no mistakes left to correct.
0.60	Now ministers and their people may disagree about some matters of faith. Sometimes they meet to talk about the things they disagree on and to hear the reasons given by each side. But all of it may fail to convince anyone of the truth. They may meet and part again, agreeing no more than before. The side that was wrong may stay wrong. Sometimes, when ministers and people disagree like this, their meetings turn into unhappy arguments and fights. People argue with a lot of prejudice and without being fair or open. Instead of bringing light and helping people see the truth, these meetings make the darkness deeper and stronger. They harden people against the truth and push them further apart from each other.
0.61	But later, when they meet together on the day of judgment before the great Judge’s throne, the mind and will of Christ will be made known. There will be no more arguments or differences of opinion. The truth will be so clear that no one can dispute it. Every disagreement will be settled once and for all, forever.
0.62	Now ministers meet their people to open the eyes of sinners’ consciences and wake them up. They show sinners how evil and dangerous sin is. They show them how strict God’s law is and how wicked their own hearts and actions are. They show them how guilty they are and that God’s anger rests on them. They show them that they are powerless, blind, poor, helpless, and lost. But often all this does no good. No matter what their ministers say, they stay numb and asleep, and their consciences are not convinced.

The eyes of their conscience will then be fully opened and will never be blinded again. The mouth of their conscience will be opened and will never be shut again.
0.64	Now ministers meet with their people, in public and in private, to help them understand the condition of their souls. They explain the rules of God’s word and apply them to the people, so that they will search their own hearts and see what condition they are in.
0.65	But ministers today cannot see perfectly into the souls of their own people. Even the wisest of them can make mistakes, and they often are wrong about these things.
0.66	The people also cannot know for sure what condition their minister is in, or what condition each other is in. 0.67	And nothing is more common than for people to be wrong about their own condition. Many people whom God finds detestable, and who are under his anger, think very highly of themselves. They believe they are his precious saints and dear children.

0.132	There are two things I want to point out now:
0.133	1. What goes on between ministers and their people is extremely important.

0.135	And what goes on between a Christian minister and his church is enormously important. In many ways it matters much more than the earthly concerns of the greatest kings and their kingdoms or empires.
0.136	It matters enormously how ministers do their job, and how they behave toward their people in their work and in everything connected with it. It also matters enormously how a church receives and treats a faithful minister of Christ, and what use they make of his ministry.

0.138	So it is especially right that these matters should be brought into judgment and openly decided and settled with truth and fairness. And for this reason, ministers and their people should meet together before the Judge who knows everything and never makes mistakes.
0.139	2. What goes on between ministers and their people is closely connected to the main things that will happen on the day of judgment.
0.140	It is closely connected to the great and divine person who will appear as Judge on that day.
0.141	Ministers are his messengers, sent out by him. In their work among their people, they represent him and stand in his place. They are sent to tell what he thinks, to do his work, and to speak and act in his name.
0.142	So it is especially right that they should go back to him and report on their work and how it turned out.
0.143	A king is the judge of all the people in his kingdom. They must all answer to him.
0.144	But it is even more important for the king’s officials to come back and report to him. They are the ones especially trusted to run his kingdom, and they are sent out on special missions. 0.145	Ministers are not only messengers of the one who will appear as Judge on the last day. The mission they are sent on, and the business he has trusted them with as his servants, directly affects his honor and the good of his kingdom.
0.146	Their work is to move forward the plans of his rule and government. So their work with their people is closely connected to the day of judgment. Everything that works against his kingdom will be removed. Everything that helps make it complete and glorious will be made perfect and firm. And so this great King will receive the honor and glory he deserves.
0.147	Again, what goes on between ministers and their people is directly connected to what happens on the day of judgment. Ministers work with their people to help people’s souls be saved forever and escape eternal damnation. And the day of judgment is the day set for that very purpose. On that day, where people will spend eternity will be openly decided and settled. Some will be fixed forever in salvation, and their salvation will be brought to its fullest completion. Others will be fixed forever in damnation and the deepest misery.
0.148	What goes on between ministers and people is most directly connected to the day of judgment, because the whole purpose of a minister’s work is to get people ready for that day.
0.149	Ministers are sent to warn people that the day is coming. They warn them ahead of time about the terrible sentence that will be announced against the wicked. They tell them about the blessed sentence that will be announced for the righteous. And they use every means with them, so that they can escape the anger that will come on the ungodly then, and receive the reward that will be given to the saints.
0.150	What goes on between ministers and their people is so closely and directly connected to that day. So it is especially right that these matters should be brought into that day and settled and finished there. And for this to happen, ministers and their people must meet and stand together before the great Judge on that day.

0.151	Here is how I want to use what we have seen. I want to lead you, the people here who have been in my care as your pastor, to think about some things. I also want to give you some advice that fits our situation right now. This has to do with what was recently done to separate us, ending the relationship we have had with each other. But we still expect to meet each other before the great judgment seat on the day of judgment.
0.152	At a time like this, it is surely fitting to think deeply and seriously about that future meeting of ours, which will be so solemn. Something has just been done that will most likely separate us forever, as far as our relationship as pastor and people is concerned.

0.154	How often I have spoken to you! I have taught you, advised you, warned you, guided you, and fed you. I have led the sacraments among you. You were the people placed in my care, and I was responsible for your precious souls.

0.160	My strength has always been weak. My body has always been sickly, and in other ways too I was not really up to such a big responsibility. Still, I have not held back my weak strength. I have used it all for the good of your souls.
0.161	I can say to you what the apostle Paul said to his listeners in Galatians 4,
0.162	verse 13: “You know that I preached the gospel to you while my body was weak.”
0.163	I have spent the best years of my life and my strength working for your eternal good.
0.164	You are my witnesses. Whatever strength I have had, I have not wasted in laziness. I have not used it to chase worldly plans or run business affairs, to get richer or to make myself and my family more important. Instead, I have given myself completely to the work of the ministry. I have worked at it night and day. I have gotten up early and put myself into this great work that Christ gave me.
0.165	I have found the work of ministry among you to be a great work indeed. It took a huge amount of care, labor, and effort. I have carried many heavy burdens in it, far too heavy for my strength.
0.166	God called me to carry these burdens. I praise his name that he has held me up so that I did not sink under them. His power has been shown in my weakness. So even though I have often been troubled on every side, I have not been overcome. I have been puzzled, but not without hope. I have been thrown down, but not destroyed.
0.167	But now I have reason to think that my work as your minister is finished. You have publicly rejected me, and my chances to serve you have come to an end.
