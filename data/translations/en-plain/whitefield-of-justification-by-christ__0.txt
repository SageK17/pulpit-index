0.1	Some people have raised a complaint against the ministers of the Church of England. Some of them have left the established church. But I must add that others who make this complaint are actually friends of that church. They say these ministers preach themselves and not Christ Jesus the Lord. They say the ministers give their people talks about mere morality and do not tell them the good news of salvation through Jesus Christ.
0.2	It is not my job to ask how well founded this complaint is. For now I will only say this. When such a serious complaint is made against the whole body of the clergy, every honest minister of Jesus Christ should do his very best. He should give no excuse at all to those who are looking for an excuse to take offense at us. They should hear us constantly proclaim the word of truth. They should hear us declare with full boldness and confidence of faith “that there is no other name given under heaven by which they can be saved except the name of Jesus Christ.” Then they may be ashamed of their confident boasting against us.
0.3	I had this complaint in mind. I was also drawn by how pleasant and delightful the subject is. (After all, who would not delight to talk about the very thing the blessed angels long to look into?) So I want to speak a little about a great and basic teaching of our faith. It is this: we are freely justified by the precious blood of Jesus Christ.
0.4	“But you are washed, but you are made holy, but you are justified in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God.”
0.5	These words begin with the word “but.” So they clearly point back to something that came before. Before I go into the details, then, it makes sense to look at how these words fit into the passage around them.
0.6	In the verses just before this, the apostle had listed many shocking sins, such as drunkenness, adultery, sexual immorality, and others like them. He tells the Corinthians that anyone who commits these sins without truly repenting from the heart will be completely shut out of God’s kingdom.
0.7	But the apostle saw two dangers. On one hand, they might become spiritually proud when they saw how different they now were from their unconverted brothers. Then they might look down on them and say, like the self-satisfied hypocrite in the prophet, “Keep away from me, for I am holier than you.” On the other hand, they might look back on all their past offenses and think their sins were too many and too serious to be forgiven. So first, to keep them humble, he reminds them of their sad condition before they were converted. He tells them plainly, “Some of you were like that” (or, as it could also be read, “these things were some of you”). In other words, some of you were once guilty, not of just one sin, but of that whole sad list of sins I have just described. But at the same time, to keep them from despair, he brings them good news of great joy: “But you are washed, but you are made holy, but you are justified in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God.”
0.8	I have already spoken to some extent about the first part of this text, about our being made holy. Now I want to speak more fully about our being freely justified by the precious obedience and death of Jesus Christ: “But you are justified in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.”
0.9	From these words I will look at three things.
0.10	First, what the word “justified” means.
0.11	Second, I will try to prove that the whole human race, and every single person, needs to be justified.
0.12	Third, that there is no way to receive this justification we need so badly, except through the perfect obedience and precious death of Jesus Christ.
0.13	First, then, I will look at what the word “justified” means.
0.14	“But you are justified,” says the apostle. It is as if he had said, “Your sins are forgiven, and God now looks at you as if you had never offended him at all.” That is what the word “justified” means in almost every passage of holy Scripture where it appears.
0.15	For example, when this same apostle writes to the Romans, he tells them that “those whom God called, he also justified.” What comes next makes it clear that this word “justified” means all our wrongs are wiped out: “those he also glorified.” That could not happen if God did not look at a justified person as if he had never offended him at all.
0.16	Again, when he speaks about Abraham’s faith, he tells them that “Abraham believed in the one who justifies the ungodly.” That means God declares the ungodly person not guilty and clears him. It is a legal term. It pictures a judge who declares an accused criminal not guilty of the charge brought against him.
0.17	The apostle himself explains this expression with a quote from the Psalms: “Blessed is the man whose sin the Lord does not count against him.”
0.18	From all these proofs, and many others we could add, it is clear what being justified means. It means being cleared in God’s sight so fully that he looks at us as if we had never offended him at all.
0.19	That is also how we should understand the part of our creed that we say we believe, when each of us declares for himself, “I believe the forgiveness of sins.”
0.20	This brings me to the
0.21	Second point I promised: to prove that the whole human race, and every single person, needs to be justified.
0.22	In fact, the apostle already assumes this in the words of our text: “But you are justified.” By saying this, he implies that the Corinthians needed to be justified. And so does the whole human race, since there is no difference, as I will show later.
0.23	But I do not want to rely only on what is assumed. So as I explain this point further, I will try to prove that we all need to be justified for two reasons: because of the sin of our nature and because of the sin of our lives.
0.24	1. First, I say that we all need to be justified because of the sin of our nature. We are all guilty of original sin, the sin of our first parents.
0.25	A self-justifying unbeliever, who “will not come to Christ to have life,” may deny this. But no one who believes that St. Paul’s letters were written by God’s inspiration can ever deny it. For there we are told that “in Adam all died.” That means Adam’s sin was counted against everyone. And so that we do not forget to apply this to ourselves, another passage adds, “There is no one who does good (that is, by nature), not even one. We have all gone off the path (of original righteousness) and are by nature children of wrath.”
0.26	Even David was a man after God’s own heart. If anyone could claim to be free from this corruption that affects everyone, surely he could. Yet he confesses that “he was shaped in wickedness, and in sin his mother conceived him.”
0.27	Let me mention just one more passage, because it fits our purpose so well. In his letter to the Romans, St. Paul says that “death came upon all people because of the disobedience of one man, Adam. It came even upon those (that is, little children) who had not sinned the way Adam did.” These children had not committed any actual sin. So they could not have been punished with physical death unless the disobedience of our first parents had been counted against them. (This same apostle tells us elsewhere that death came into the world only through sin.)
0.28	So what I have said on this point is summed up very well in the article of our church that declares: “Original sin is not a matter of following Adam’s example. It is the fault and corruption of every person who is born naturally from the descendants of Adam. Because of it, people have gone very far from original righteousness and are inclined to evil by their own nature, so that the flesh always desires what is against the spirit. And so, in every person born into this world, it deserves God’s anger and condemnation.”
0.29	I have gone into this point in more detail because it is the very foundation of the Christian religion. I am truly convinced of this: many unbelievers oppose the faith, and many who call themselves Christians are very lukewarm in their love for Jesus Christ. The only reason is that they are not well grounded in the teaching of original sin. They do not see the helpless condition, indeed, I may say, the damnable condition, in which each of us comes into the world.
0.30	This is the reason, and I could almost say the only reason, why unbelief is so widespread among us.
0.31	Sadly, we are wrong if we think that people today become or remain unbelievers, and set up their corrupted reason against God’s revelation, simply because there is not enough evidence. (In fact, I believe it could easily be shown that a modern unbeliever is the most gullible creature alive.) No, the only reason is that they lack a humble mind. They have no sense of how corrupt they are by nature, and they are not willing to admit that they are so corrupt. That is why they stubbornly shut their eyes to the light of the glorious gospel of Christ.
0.32	But suppose they were once cut to the heart with a proper and living sense of their natural corruption and of the condemnation they face. Then they would no longer mock God’s revelation and treat it as a silly story. Instead, they would cry out like the trembling jailer, “What shall I do to be saved?”
0.33	A mistake on this basic point made so many people resist the evidence that the Son of God himself gave that God had sent him, when he lived among us.
0.34	Every word he spoke, everything he did, and every miracle he performed proved that he came from God.
0.35	So why did so many harden their hearts and refuse to believe his message?
0.36	He himself tells us why: “They will not come to me to have life.” They stubbornly resist the very means God had appointed for their salvation. And St. Paul tells us, “If the gospel is hidden, it is hidden to those who are lost. The god of this world has blinded the eyes of those who do not believe, so that the light of the glorious gospel of Christ, who is the image of God, would not shine on them.”
0.37	2 Corinthians 4:3–4.
0.38	Someone may ask, “How does it fit with God’s goodness to count the guilt of one man’s sin against his innocent descendants?”
0.39	I think it would be enough to answer with the apostle’s words: “No, O man, who are you to talk back to God?
0.40	Should the thing that was made say to the one who made it, ‘Why have you made me like this?’”
0.41	But let me give a more direct answer. People would do well to remember that in the first covenant God made with humans, Adam acted as a public person. He was the representative of the whole human race. So we must stand or fall with him.
0.42	If he had kept on obeying and had not eaten the forbidden fruit, the benefits of his obedience would surely have been credited to us. But he did not keep on obeying. He broke the covenant that God made with him, and with us in him. So who would dare accuse the righteous Judge of all the earth of being unfair for counting that against us too? I will go on,
0.43	Second, to prove that we need to be justified because of the sin of our lives.
0.44	God made us, so he has the right to demand our obedience. I suppose no one will deny that. He has also given us two laws by which we will be judged: a natural law and a written law. No one who believes that St. Paul’s letter to the Romans comes with God’s authority can question this. For in that letter we are told about a law written in the heart and a law given through Moses. And our sad and frequent experience makes it all too clear that each of us has broken these laws.
0.45	So the holy Scriptures tell us that “there is no one who lives and does not sin,” that “we all do wrong in many things,” that “if we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves,” and other things like this.
0.46	If we have offended God in this way, then we need forgiveness for offending him. The only other option would be to imagine that God makes laws but does not care whether they are obeyed. That is as foolish as imagining that a ruler would set up laws to govern his country well, and then let everyone who breaks them go unpunished.
0.47	But God has not acted so foolishly with his creatures. No, since he gave us a law, he demands that we obey it. He requires us to obey it in everything and to keep on obeying it. The penalty for every single breaking of it is nothing less than his curse and eternal death. That is what Scripture says: “Cursed is anyone who does not keep on doing everything written in the law.” And Scripture says in another place, “The soul who sins will die.”
0.48	Now I have already proved that all of us have sinned. So unless some way can be found to satisfy God’s justice, we must perish forever.
0.49	So let us stop for a moment. Let us see what a terrible condition each of us is born into, and stays in, until we are brought into a state of grace.
0.50	Surely nothing could be more terrible than to be born under God’s curse and to be charged with original guilt. And that is not all. We are also convicted of actually breaking God’s law, and even the smallest breaking of it rightly deserves eternal condemnation.
0.51	Surely this is a very sad way to see ourselves. It must make us try to find some way to satisfy and calm our offended Judge.
0.52	But what could that way be?
0.53	Shall we repent? Sadly, the first covenant does not say one word about repentance: “The day you eat from it, you will surely die.”
0.54	So if God is true, we must perish unless some way is found to satisfy his justice. And we have no reason to expect God to change his mind, even if we seek it with tears.
0.55	Well then, if repentance will not work, shall we appeal to the law of works?
0.56	Sadly, “no one living will be justified by the law, for the law brings the knowledge of sin.”
0.57	The law convicts and condemns us, so it can never justify us. And the prophet says, “All our righteous deeds are nothing but filthy rags.”
0.58	What then shall we bring when we come before the Lord and bow down before the Most High God?
0.59	Shall we come before him with year-old calves, with thousands of rams, or with ten thousand rivers of oil?
0.60	Sadly, God has shown you, O man, that this will do no good. For he has declared, “I will not take a bull from your house or a goat from your pen. For all the animals of the forest are mine, and so are the cattle on a thousand hills.”
0.61	Will the Lord then be pleased to accept our firstborn children for our wrongdoing, the fruit of our bodies for the sin of our souls?
0.62	Even this will not buy our pardon. For he has declared that “children will not bear the guilt of their parents.”
0.63	Besides, our children are sinners too. They are under the same condemnation, so they need forgiveness just as much as we do.
0.64	They are impure. And will the Lord accept the blind and the lame as a sacrifice?
0.65	Then shall some angel, or archangel, take on the task of keeping the covenant we have broken, and make atonement for us?
0.66	Sadly, they are only creatures, even if they are creatures of the highest rank. So they must obey God just as we must. And after they have done everything, they must say they have done no more than their duty.
0.67	And even if it were possible for them to die, how could the death of a limited creature satisfy a justice that has been offended infinitely?
0.68	What miserable people we are!
0.69	Who will rescue us?
0.70	I thank God, our Lord Jesus Christ.
0.71	This naturally brings me to the
0.72	Third point I promised. I will try to prove that there is no way to receive this justification we need so badly, except through the perfect obedience and precious death of Jesus Christ. “But you are justified in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.”
0.73	But I have already proved this to some extent in what I said under the last point. I showed that our repentance, our righteousness, and our sacrifices could never win justification for us. Not even the obedience and death of the angels themselves could do it. So all that is left for me to do under this point is to show that Jesus Christ has won it for us.
0.74	Here too I will turn “to the law and to the testimony.”
0.75	After all the clever arguments on both sides, only the living words of God can satisfy us on this very important point. It is such an unimaginable mystery that the eternal, one and only Son of God should die for sinful people. We would never have dared even to think of it if God had not revealed it in his holy word.
0.76	It is true that reason can show us the wound. But only revelation can lead us to the way we are healed.
0.77	God has chosen a certain way to make us happy. To the unbeliever it may be a stumbling block. To the self-important wise man and debater of this world it may be foolishness. But wisdom, that is, God’s plan of our redemption, will be proved right. All her truly wise and holy children, every sincere and honest Christian, will approve of it and submit to it.
0.78	But let me come more directly to our point.
0.79	As I said before, we needed two things in order to be at peace with God.
0.80	1. To be set free from the guilt of the sin of our nature.
0.81	2. To be set free from the sin of our lives.
0.82	And both of these are made certain for believers by the obedience and death of Jesus Christ. (Thank God for this gift that is too wonderful for words!)
0.83	For what does Scripture say?
0.84	1. About the first, it tells us that “just as through the disobedience of one man (that is, through one wrongdoing, Adam’s) many were made sinners, so through the obedience of one, Jesus Christ, many were made righteous.” (This includes both his passive and his active obedience.)
0.85	And again, “Just as through the disobedience of one man judgment came on all people and brought condemnation.” That is, all people were condemned because Adam’s sin was counted against them. “So through the obedience of one, Jesus Christ, the free gift of pardon and peace came on all people (all kinds of people) and brought justification that gives life.”
0.86	I say all kinds of people. In this chapter the apostle is only comparing the first Adam and the second Adam on one point: both of them acted as representatives. Adam’s descendants had his sin counted against them. In the same way, those for whom Christ died, and whom he represents, will have his merits counted as theirs.
0.87	Some people push this comparison further to prove that Christ redeemed everyone. Whatever arguments they may take from other passages of Scripture to prove this, if they try to use this passage for that purpose, I think they stretch their interpretation beyond what Scripture allows.
0.88	2. The other thing we needed to have made certain for us, before we could be at peace with God, was pardon for the sin of our lives.
0.89	And the holy Scriptures tell us that the death of Jesus Christ has fully done this.
0.90	The gospel prophet foretold that the promised Redeemer would be “wounded for our wrongdoings and bruised for our sins. The punishment that brings us peace would be on him, and by his wounds we would be healed,” Isaiah 53:6.
0.91	At his birth the angels said that he would “save his people from their sins.”
0.92	And St. Paul declares, “This is a trustworthy saying, and it deserves to be fully accepted by everyone: Jesus Christ came into the world to save sinners.”
0.93	And here in the words of our text: “Some of you were like that (or, as I said before, these things were some of you); but you are washed,” and so on. And again: “Jesus Christ is the end of the law, bringing righteousness to everyone who believes.”
0.94	And the apostle St. Peter shows us that no one but Jesus Christ can do all this. He says, “There is salvation in no one else. For there is no other name under heaven given among people by which we must be saved, except the name of Jesus Christ.”
0.95	Then there are the Gentiles who still sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, on whom the sun of righteousness has never yet risen. How God will choose to deal with them is not for us to ask.
0.96	“What business is it of ours to judge those who are outside?”
0.97	Let us leave them to God’s mercy. And let us wait for the answer to this and every other hard question until the great day of judgment. On that day all of God’s ways, both in his providence and in his grace, will be made fully clear, in ways still unknown to us because they have not yet been revealed.
0.98	But this we do know: the Judge of all the earth will certainly do what is right.
0.99	But it is time for me to finish.
0.100	My brothers, by God’s blessing I have now spoken on the words of the text in the way I planned.
0.101	Many useful lessons could be drawn from what I have said. But I fear I have kept you too long already. So let me just make one or two reflections on what has been said, and then I will finish.
0.102	If we are freely justified by the death and obedience of Jesus Christ, then let us stop here for a moment. Before, we thought about the misery of a fallen soul. Now let us turn and look at the happiness of the believing soul.
0.103	But oh, I am lost in wonder when I think of it! When we were God’s enemies and rebels against him, God the Father still longed deeply for us, his fallen creatures who had turned away from him. Nothing but an infinite ransom could satisfy a justice that had been infinitely offended. So he sent his only and dear Son Jesus Christ, who is God, blessed forever, and who had been close to his heart from all eternity. He sent him to fulfill the covenant of works and to die a cursed, painful, and shameful death for us and for our salvation. Who can help crying out when thinking about this mystery of godliness?
0.104	“Oh, how deep are the riches of God’s love” for us, his wretched, miserable, and ruined creatures!
0.105	“How impossible it is to understand his mercy, and to trace out his ways!”
0.106	O God, now we truly know that you have loved us, “because you did not hold back your Son, your only Son Jesus Christ,” from doing all this and dying for us.
0.107	We admire the Father for sending his Son. Let us also humbly and thankfully worship the Son for coming when he was sent to die for mankind.
0.108	But oh! What mind can imagine, what words can express, how infinitely great that matchless love is? It moved the Son of God to come down from the dwelling places of his Father’s glory to obey and die for sinful people!
0.109	When he simply shed a tear at poor Lazarus’s funeral, the Jews said, “See how he loved him!”
0.110	How much more rightly, then, can we cry out, “See how he loved us!”
0.111	For he not only kept the whole moral law, but he did not hold back from shedding his own most precious blood for us.
0.112	After this, can any poor sinner who is truly convicted of sin give up hope of mercy?
0.113	Can such people see their Savior hanging on the cross, with his arms stretched out, ready to embrace them, and still doubt that he will accept them when they truly believe in him?
0.114	No! Get rid of all such thoughts. They dishonor him and lead to despair.
0.115	Look at his hands, pierced with iron nails. Look at his side, pierced with a cruel spear. It opened the floodgates of his blood and opened a fountain for sin and for all uncleanness. And then give up hope of mercy if you can!
0.116	No, just believe in him. Then, even though you have crucified him again, he will pardon you fully. “Though your sins are like scarlet, they will be like wool. Though they are deeper than crimson, they will be whiter than snow.”
0.117	May God in his infinite mercy grant this, etc.
