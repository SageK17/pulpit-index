3.239	You said, “His anointing teaches you about everything.”
3.240	Then why did you write a letter like this? What teaching did “you” give them? What instruction? What building up?
3.241	Look at this, brothers and sisters. Look at a great mystery.
3.242	The sound of our words hits your ears, but the real Teacher is inside.
3.243	Do not think that anyone learns anything from another human being.
3.244	We can warn and remind you with the sound of our voice. But if there is not Someone inside who teaches, all our noise is useless.
3.245	Yes, brothers and sisters, do you want to know it?
3.246	Haven’t you all heard this sermon? And yet how many people will leave here without having learned anything!
3.247	I have spoken to everyone. But some people are not spoken to by that Anointing inside them, and the Holy Spirit does not teach them inside. Those people go home without having learned anything.
3.248	The lessons of a teacher on the outside are only a kind of help and reminder.
3.249	The One who teaches hearts has His teaching chair in heaven.
3.250	That is why He Himself says in the Gospel: “Do not call anyone on earth your teacher. You have one Teacher, and that is Christ.”
3.251	So let Him speak to you inside, when no human being is there. Someone may be standing beside you, but no one is inside your heart.
3.252	Yet do not let your heart be empty. Let Christ be in your heart. Let His anointing be in your heart. Otherwise it will be a heart dying of thirst in the desert, with no springs of water to drink from.
3.253	So, I say, there is a Teacher inside who teaches. Christ teaches. His inspiration teaches.
3.254	Where His inspiration and His anointing are missing, words just make noise on the outside, and it is useless.
3.255	Brothers and sisters, the words we speak from the outside are like a gardener caring for a tree. He works from the outside. He waters it and takes good care of it. But whatever he does from the outside, does he shape the apples? Does he cover the bare branches with shady leaves? Does he do anything like that from the inside?
3.256	Then who does it?
3.257	Listen to the gardener, the apostle. See what we are, and listen to the Teacher inside: “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. Neither the one who plants nor the one who waters is anything. Only the One who gives the growth is: God.”
3.258	So this is what we tell you: whether we plant or water, our speaking does not make us anything. Only God, who gives the growth, counts. That is, “His anointing, which teaches you about everything.”

4.28	He attacks from the outside by throwing all kinds of temptations at us. But the person to whom God speaks inside does not give in to them—the person who has the anointing you have heard about.

4.33	And where were they sent?
4.34	You heard it when the Gospel was read: “Go and preach the Gospel to the whole creation under heaven.”
4.35	So the disciples were sent “everywhere.” Signs and wonders proved that they had seen what they talked about.
4.36	And we believe in Him, whom we have not seen, and we are waiting for Him to come.
4.37	Those who wait for Him by faith will be glad when He comes. But those who have no faith will be ashamed when the thing they do not see now finally comes.
4.38	And that shame will not last just one day and then go away. It will not be like the embarrassment people usually feel when they are caught doing something wrong and others laugh at them.
4.39	That shame will carry the people who feel it off to the left side, so that they will be told, “Go into the fire that lasts forever, which was prepared for the devil and his angels.”

4.43	“And,” He says, “you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.”

4.47	Do not be a liar who says one thing and does another. Keep faith, and He will keep His promise.

4.50	The righteousness we have right now comes from faith.
4.51	Perfect righteousness does not exist except in the angels. And even the angels hardly have it, if you compare them with God. Still, if any souls or spirits that God created are perfectly righteous, it is the angels. They are holy, just and good. No slip has turned them aside. No pride has made them fall. They always keep looking at the Word of God. Nothing else is sweet to them except the One who created them. In them righteousness is perfect. But in us it has only begun, through faith, by the Spirit.
4.52	You heard it when the Psalm was read: “Begin with the Lord by confessing.”
4.53	“Begin,” it says. Our righteousness begins when we confess our sins.
4.54	You have started to stop defending your sin. Now you have made a beginning of righteousness. But it will be made perfect in you when nothing else delights you but this. That will be when “death is swallowed up in victory.” It will be when there is no more itch of desire, and no more struggle with flesh and blood. It will be when the prize of victory is given and the enemy is defeated. Then righteousness will be perfect.

4.56	And the one who wins is the person who, even when he lands a blow, does not trust in his own strength. He relies on God, who cheers him on.
4.57	When the devil fights against us, he is alone.
4.58	If we are with God, we defeat the devil. But if you fight the devil alone, you will be beaten.
4.59	He is a skillful enemy. Think how many victories he has won!
4.60	Look how far down he has thrown us!
4.61	We are born to die because of this: at the very start, he threw our first ancestor out of Paradise.
4.62	So what should we do, since he is so experienced?
4.63	Call on the Almighty to help you against the devil’s tricks.
4.64	Let the One who cannot be beaten live in you. Then you will safely beat the one who usually wins.

4.67	I say this so you will understand, brothers and sisters. When Adam was in Paradise, he ignored God’s command. He stuck his neck up proudly, as if he wanted to be his own master and did not want to obey God’s will. And so he fell from that life without death, from that happiness.
4.68	But there was a certain man who had now become very skilled, even though he was born to die. As he sat on the dung heap, rotting with worms, he defeated the devil. Yes, Adam himself won then—in Job! Because Job came from Adam’s family line.
4.69	So Adam, who was beaten in Paradise, won on the dung heap.
4.70	In Paradise he listened to the woman, who passed on what the devil had put into her mind. But on the dung heap he said to Eve, “You are talking like one of the foolish women.”
4.71	There he listened; here he answered back. When he was happy, he listened. When he was beaten with suffering, he won.

4.73	“If you know that He is righteous,” it says, “then know that everyone who does what is right is born of Him”—born of God, of Christ.
4.74	And when he says, “is born of Him,” he is cheering us on.
4.75	So already, because we are born of Him, we are perfect.
4.76	4. Listen. “Look what great love the Father has given us: that we should be called God’s children, and really be His children.”

4.78	How many people are called doctors who do not know how to heal! How many are called watchmen who sleep all night long!
4.79	In the same way, many are called Christians, but their actions show that they are not. They are not what they are called—not in their lives, their behavior, their faith, their hope, or their love.

4.85	Pay attention and see. Perhaps there are people like that among you too.
4.86	Think of any one of you who now lives for God. He looks down on worldly things. He does not choose to go to the shows. He does not choose to get drunk as if it were a sacred custom. 4.101	We are already born of Him. But since we are His children in hope, he says, “Dear friends, we are God’s children now.”
4.102	Already, right now? Then what are we waiting for, if we are already God’s children?
4.103	“It has not yet been shown,” he says, “what we will be.”

4.109	What is called “Is”—and not only called that, but really is—never changes. It always stays the same. It cannot be changed. No part of It can decay. It does not grow, because It is already perfect. It does not shrink, because It is eternal.

4.112	And what is this?
4.113	“He was in the form of God, and He did not think it was robbery to be equal with God.”
4.114	Bad people cannot see Christ this way—Christ in the form of God, the Word of God, the only Son of the Father, equal with the Father.

4.121	To those on the right He will say, “Come, you whom My Father has blessed, receive the kingdom.” To those on the left He will say, “Go into the fire that lasts forever.”
4.122	They will see only the form of a servant. They will not see the form of God.
4.123	Why? Because they were ungodly. And the Lord Himself says, “Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.”

4.126	What has been promised to us?
4.127	“We will be like Him, because we will see Him as He is.”
4.128	My tongue has done what it could. It has spoken the words. Let your heart think about the rest.
4.129	For even John himself—what could he say compared with the One who Is? And what can we humans say, when we are so far from being as great as John?

4.132	Right now you do not yet see what you long for. But by longing, you are made big enough, so that when the thing you will see arrives, you will be filled.
4.133	Think of it like this. Suppose you want to fill a bag, and you know how big the thing is that you will be given. So you stretch open the sack or the leather bag or whatever it is. You know how much you want to put in, and you see that the bag is too narrow. By stretching it, you make it able to hold more. In the same way, God makes us wait for what we hope for, and this stretches our longing. The longing stretches our soul. And by stretching it, He makes it bigger.
4.134	So let us keep longing, my brothers and sisters, because we will be filled.
4.135	Look at Paul. It is as if he is widening his heart so that it can receive what is coming.
4.136	This is what he says: “Not that I have already received it or am already perfect. Brothers and sisters, I do not think I have taken hold of it yet.”
4.137	Then what are you doing in this life, if you have not taken hold of it yet?
4.138	“But I do this one thing: I forget what is behind me and reach out for what is ahead. Straining hard, I keep going toward the prize of the high calling.”
4.139	He says he reaches out, or stretches himself. And he says he keeps going “straining hard.”
4.140	He felt he was too small to take in the thing “that no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and that has never entered a human heart.”
4.141	This is our life: to be trained by longing.
4.142	But holy longing trains us only as much as we cut our longings away from loving the world.
4.143	We have already said, “Empty out what is going to be filled.”
4.144	You are going to be filled with good things, so pour out the bad.
4.145	Imagine that God wants to fill you with honey. If you are full of vinegar, where will you put the honey?
4.146	What was in the jar must be poured out. The jar itself must be cleaned. It must be cleaned even if it takes hard work and hard scrubbing, so that it is ready for that thing, whatever it is.
4.147	We could call it honey, or gold, or wine. Whatever we call it, it is something that cannot be put into words. Whatever we would like to call it, its name is—God.
4.148	And when we say “God,” what have we really said?
4.149	Is that one syllable all that we are waiting for?
4.150	So whatever we have been able to say falls short of Him. Let us stretch ourselves out toward Him, so that when He comes, He may fill us.
4.151	For “we will be like Him, because we will see Him as He is.”
4.152	7. “And everyone who has this hope in Him.”
4.153	You see how he has put us in our place: in “hope.”
4.154	You see how the apostle Paul agrees with his fellow apostle: “We are saved by hope.
4.155	But hope that is seen is not hope. For why would anyone hope for what he already sees?
4.156	But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it patiently.”
4.157	This patience itself trains our longing.
4.158	Keep going, because He stays. Keep on walking so that you reach the goal. The place you are heading for will not move away.
4.159	Look: “And everyone who has this hope in Him makes himself pure, just as He is pure.”
4.160	See how he has not taken away free will, since he says, “makes himself pure.”
4.161	Who makes us pure except God?
4.162	Yes, but God does not make you pure if you do not want it.
4.163	So when you join your will to God, you make yourself pure.
4.164	You make yourself pure—not by your own power, but by the One who comes to live in you.
4.165	Still, because you do something in this with your will, some of it is credited to you.
4.166	But it is credited to you only so that you will say, as in the Psalm, “Be my helper; do not leave me.”
4.167	If you say, “Be my helper,” you are doing something. For if you were doing nothing, how could He be said to “help” you?
4.168	8. “Everyone who sins also does wickedness.”
4.169	No one should say, “Sin is one thing and wickedness is another.” No one should say, “I am a sinner, but I do not do wickedness.”
4.170	For “everyone who sins also does wickedness.
4.171	Sin is wickedness.” So then, what should we do about our sins and our wickedness?
4.172	Listen to what He says: “And you know that He appeared to take away sin, and there is no sin in Him.”
4.173	He, in whom there is no sin, is the One who came to take away sin.
4.174	For if there were sin in Him, it would have to be taken away from Him. He could not take it away Himself.
4.175	“Whoever stays in Him does not sin.”
4.176	As far as he stays in Him, that far he does not sin.
4.177	“Whoever sins has not seen Him and has not known Him.”
4.178	This is a big question: “Whoever sins has not seen Him and has not known Him.”
4.179	No wonder. We have not seen Him, but we are going to see Him. We have not known Him, but we are going to know Him. We believe in Someone we have not known.
4.180	Or perhaps we have known Him by faith, but not yet by actually seeing Him?
4.181	But then, by faith, we have both seen and known Him.
4.182	For if faith does not see yet, why are we said to have been given light?
4.183	There is a light that comes by faith, and a light that comes by sight.
4.184	Right now, while we are travelers far from home, “we walk by faith, not by sight”—that is, not by actually seeing.
4.185	So our righteousness also is “by faith, not by sight.”
4.186	Our righteousness will be perfect when we actually see.
4.187	But in the meantime, let us not let go of the righteousness that comes by faith. For as the apostle says, “the righteous person lives by faith.”
4.188	“Whoever stays in Him does not sin.”
4.189	For “whoever sins has not seen Him and has not known Him.”
4.190	The person who sins does not believe. But if someone believes, then as far as his faith goes, he does not sin.
4.191	9. “Little children, do not let anyone lead you astray.
4.192	The person who does what is right is righteous, just as He is righteous.”
4.193	What? When we hear that we are “righteous just as He is righteous,” should we think we are equal with God?
4.194	You need to understand what “just as” means. He said a little earlier, “makes himself pure, just as He is pure.”
4.195	Is our purity then like God’s purity and equal to it? Is our righteousness equal to God’s righteousness?
4.196	Who could say that?
4.197	But the words “just as” are not always used to mean “equal.”
4.198	Here is an example. Suppose someone sees this large church and wants to build a smaller church with the same shape. Say this church is one unit wide and two units long. He builds his church one unit wide and two units long too. Then you can see that he has built it “just as” this one is built.
4.199	But say this church is a hundred cubits long and the other one is thirty. It is “just as” this one, and yet it is not equal.
4.200	So you see, “just as” does not always mean being the same or equal.
4.201	Here is another example. See how different a person’s face is from its reflection in a mirror. There is a face in the reflection, and there is a face on the body. The reflection only copies; the body is the real thing.
4.202	And what do we say?
4.203	We say that “just as” there are eyes here, so there are eyes there too. “Just as” there are ears here, so there are ears there too.
4.204	The things are different, but we say “just as” because they look alike.
4.205	In the same way, we also have God’s image in us. But it is not the image that the Son has, who is equal with the Father. Still, if we were not also “just as” He is, in our own measure, we could not be called like Him in any way.
4.206	So “He makes us pure, just as He is pure.” But He is pure from all eternity. We are pure by faith.
4.207	We are “righteous just as He is righteous.” But He is righteous forever, without ever changing. We are righteous by believing in Someone we do not see, so that one day we may see Him.
4.208	Even when our righteousness is made perfect, when we are equal to the angels, even then it will not be equal to Him.
4.209	So how far from Him it must be now, if even then it will not be equal!
4.210	10. “The person who sins belongs to the devil, because the devil has been sinning from the beginning.”
4.211	“Belongs to the devil.” You know what he means: by copying the devil.
4.212	For the devil did not make anyone. He did not father anyone. He did not create anyone. But whoever copies the devil becomes a child of the devil, as if the devil had fathered him. He becomes his child by copying him, not by actually being born from him.
4.213	In what way are you a child of Abraham, even though Abraham was not your father?
4.214	In the same way that the Jews, who were Abraham’s children, became children of the devil by not copying Abraham’s faith. They were born from Abraham’s body, but they did not copy Abraham’s faith.
4.215	So those who were born from him were shut out of the inheritance because they did not copy him. But you, who were not born from him, are made his child. And in this way you will be his child: by copying him.
4.216	And if you copy the devil—the way he became proud and ungodly against God—you will be a child of the devil. It will be by copying him, not because he created you or fathered you.
4.217	11. “This is why the Son of God appeared.”
4.218	Now then, brothers and sisters, pay attention!
4.219	All sinners are born of the devil, as sinners.
4.220	Adam was made by God. But when he gave in to the devil, he was born of the devil. And he fathered all people to be just like himself.
4.221	We were born with sinful desire itself. Even before we add our own sins, we are born under that condemnation.
4.222	For if we are born without any sin, why do people rush with their babies to baptism to set them free?
4.223	So pay close attention, brothers and sisters, to the two family roots, Adam and Christ. There are two men. One is a man who is only man. The other is a Man who is God.
4.224	Because of the man who is only man, we are sinners. Through the Man who is God, we are made right with God.
4.225	The first birth threw us down into death. The second birth raised us up to life. The first birth brings sin with it. The second birth sets us free from sin.
4.226	For this is why Christ came as a Man: to undo people’s sins.
4.227	“This is why the Son of God appeared: to undo the works of the devil.”
4.228	12. I leave the rest for you to think about, my dear friends, so that I do not wear you out.
4.229	For here is the question we are working hard to solve: we call ourselves sinners. For if anyone says he is without sin, he is a liar.
4.230	And in the letter of this same John we have found it written, “If we say that we have no sin, we are fooling ourselves.”

4.233	In what way are we born of God, and in what way do we confess that we are sinners?
4.234	Should we say it is because we are not born of God?
4.235	Then what are these Sacraments doing for babies?

T:5	Homily 5 (1 John 3:9–18)
5.1	“Whoever is born of God does not sin, because his seed stays in him. He cannot sin, because he is born of God.
5.2	This is how it becomes clear who are God’s children and who are the devil’s children: anyone who is not righteous is not from God, and neither is anyone who does not love his brother.
5.3	For this is the message you heard from the beginning: that we should love one another.
5.4	Do not be like Cain, who belonged to the evil one and killed his brother.
5.5	And why did he kill him?
5.6	Because his own deeds were evil, and his brother’s were righteous.
5.7	Do not be surprised, my brothers and sisters, if the world hates us.
5.8	We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brothers and sisters.
5.9	Anyone who does not love stays in death.
5.10	Anyone who hates his brother is a murderer. And you know that no murderer has eternal life living in him.
5.11	This is how we know love: He gave up His life for us. And we ought to give up our lives for our brothers and sisters.
5.12	But suppose someone has this world’s goods, and sees his brother in need, and closes his heart against him. How can God’s love live in him?
5.13	My little children, let us not love only with words and with our tongue, but with actions and in truth.”
5.14	1. Please listen carefully, I beg you, because what we have to deal with is no small matter. And since you were so eager about it yesterday, I have no doubt that you have come together today even more eager.
5.15	For it is no small question: how can he say in this letter, “Whoever is born of God does not sin,” when earlier in the same letter he said, “If we say that we have no sin, we are fooling ourselves, and the truth is not in us”?
5.16	What should a person do who is squeezed by both sayings from the same letter?
5.17	If he admits that he is a sinner, he is afraid someone will tell him, “Then you are not born of God, because it is written, ‘Whoever is born of God does not sin.’”
5.18	But if he says that he is righteous and has no sin, he gets hit from the other side by the same letter: “If we say that we have no sin, we are fooling ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.19	So he is stuck in the middle. He cannot figure out what to say, what to admit, or what to claim.
5.20	Claiming to be without sin is very dangerous. And it is not only dangerous but also wrong. “We are fooling ourselves,” he says, “and the truth is not in us, if we say that we have no sin.”
5.21	Oh, if only you had no sin and said so! Then you would be telling the truth. And by speaking the truth, you would have not even a trace of wrong to be afraid of.
5.22	But the reason you do wrong when you say it is that what you are saying is a lie.
5.23	“The truth,” he says, “is not in us, if we say that we have no sin.”
5.24	He does not say, “We have not had sin.” Otherwise it might seem he was talking about our past life.
5.25	For a person here has had sins. But since the time he was born of God, he has begun to be without sins.
5.26	If that were what he meant, there would be no question to puzzle us.
5.27	For we would say, “We used to be sinners, but now we are made right with God. We used to have sin, but now we have none.”
5.28	But he does not say this. So what does he say?
5.29	“If we say that we have no sin, we are fooling ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.30	And then a little later he says the opposite: “Whoever is born of God does not sin.”
5.31	Was John himself not born of God?
5.32	You have heard that John leaned against the Lord’s chest. If John was not born of God, does anyone dare to promise himself that he has received the new birth that was not given to that man—the man who was allowed to lean against the Lord’s chest?
5.33	The man the Lord loved more than all the others—was he the only one the Lord had not given new birth by the Spirit?
