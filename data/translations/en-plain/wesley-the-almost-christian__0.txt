0.1	“You almost persuade me to be a Christian.” Acts 26:28.
0.2	Many people go this far. Ever since the Christian religion came into the world, there have been many people in every age and nation who were almost persuaded to be Christians.
0.3	But going only this far is worth nothing before God. So it is very important for us to consider the following:
0.4	First, what it means to be almost a Christian.
0.5	Second, what it means to be altogether a Christian.
0.6	I. (I.) 1. Being almost a Christian includes, first of all, heathen honesty.
0.7	I don’t think anyone will question this. By heathen honesty I do not mean only what their philosophers recommended in their writings. I mean the honesty that ordinary heathens expected from one another, and that many of them actually practised.
0.8	Its rules taught them that they must not be unjust. They must not take their neighbour’s goods, whether by robbery or by theft. They must not oppress the poor or use extortion against anyone. They must not cheat or take advantage of anyone, rich or poor, in any business they did with them. They must not rob anyone of what was rightfully his. And, if possible, they should owe no one anything.
0.9	2. The ordinary heathens also agreed that people should have some respect for truth, as well as for justice.
0.10	So they hated anyone who broke his oath and called God to witness to a lie. They also hated anyone known for slandering his neighbour and falsely accusing others.
0.11	In fact, they thought little better of deliberate liars of any kind. They saw them as a disgrace to the human race and a plague on society.
0.12	3. There was also a kind of love and help that they expected from one another.
0.13	They expected people to give each other whatever help they could, as long as it did not harm themselves.
0.14	This included the small acts of kindness that cost no money or effort. But it also included feeding the hungry, if they had food to spare, and clothing the naked with their own spare clothes. In general, it meant giving to anyone in need the things they did not need themselves.
0.15	That, at the very least, is how far heathen honesty went. It is the first thing included in being almost a Christian.
0.16	(II.) 4. The second thing included in being almost a Christian is having a form of godliness. This is the godliness that the gospel of Christ requires. It means having the outward look of a real Christian.
0.17	So the almost Christian does nothing that the gospel forbids. He does not take God’s name in vain. He blesses and does not curse. He does not swear at all, but his words are simply “Yes, yes” and “No, no.” He does not dishonour the Lord’s Day, and he does not let anyone else dishonour it, not even the stranger staying within his gates. He avoids all actual adultery, sexual immorality, and impurity. He also avoids every word or look that leads toward them, directly or indirectly. He even avoids all careless words. He keeps away from running people down, from backbiting, from gossip, and from speaking evil. He keeps away from “all foolish talk and joking”—eutrapelia, which the heathen moral teachers counted as a kind of virtue. In short, he keeps away from all talk that is not “good for building others up.” Such talk therefore “grieves the Holy Spirit of God, who has sealed us for the day of redemption.”
0.18	5. He keeps away from “wine that leads to excess.” He keeps away from wild parties and overeating.
0.19	As far as he can, he avoids all fighting and quarrelling. He always tries to live at peace with everyone.
0.20	If someone wrongs him, he does not take revenge or pay back evil for evil. He does not insult people or pick fights. He does not mock the faults or weaknesses of his neighbour. He does not willingly wrong, hurt, or upset anyone. In everything he does and says, he follows this plain rule: “Do not do to another what you would not want him to do to you.”
0.21	6. When he does good, he does not limit himself to cheap and easy acts of kindness. He works hard and suffers for the good of many, so that by all means he may help some.
0.22	In spite of hard work or pain, “whatever his hand finds to do, he does it with all his might.” He does this for his friends and for his enemies, for the evil and for the good.
0.23	He is “not lazy” in this or in any other “work.” Whenever he “has the opportunity,” he does “good”—every kind of good—“to everyone,” to their souls as well as their bodies. He corrects the wicked, teaches the ignorant, steadies those who waver, stirs the good to new life, and comforts those who suffer. He works to wake up those who are asleep. He works to lead those whom God has already awakened to the “fountain opened for sin and for impurity,” so that they may wash there and be clean. And he works to stir up those who are saved through faith to show the beauty of the gospel of Christ in everything.
0.24	7. The person who has the form of godliness also uses the means of grace—all of them, at every opportunity. He goes to the house of God regularly. But he does not go the way some people do. They come into the presence of the Most High loaded with gold and expensive clothes, or dressed in showy vanity. By their untimely politeness to each other, or by their out-of-place light-heartedness, they give up any claim to the form of godliness as well as to its power.
0.25	I wish to God that no one even among us deserved the same blame! Some come into this house perhaps staring around, or showing every sign of being bored, careless, and indifferent. Sometimes they may seem to pray for God’s blessing on what they are about to begin. But during that awe-inspiring service they are asleep, or leaning back in the most comfortable position for sleeping. Or they talk to one another or look around, as if they thought God were asleep, and as if they had nothing at all to do.
0.26	No one should accuse these people of having the form of godliness.
0.27	No. Anyone who has even this form behaves seriously and pays attention in every part of that solemn service.
0.28	This is especially true when he comes to the Lord’s Table. He does not come in a light or careless way. His manner, gestures, and bearing say only one thing: “God, be merciful to me, a sinner!”
0.29	8. Add to this the regular practice of family prayer by those who are heads of families. Add also setting aside times to pray to God in private, and behaving seriously every day. Anyone who consistently practises this outward religion has the form of godliness.
0.30	Only one more thing is needed for him to be almost a Christian. That is sincerity.
0.31	(III.) 9. By sincerity I mean a real, inner principle of religion, from which these outward actions come.
0.32	In fact, if we do not have this, we do not even have heathen honesty. We do not have enough of it to meet the demands of a heathen Epicurean poet.
0.33	Even this poor wretch, in his sober moments, could say:
0.34	Oderunt peccare boni, virtutis amore; / Oderunt peccare mali, formidine poenae.
0.35	[Good men avoid sin because they love virtue; / Wicked men avoid sin because they fear punishment.]
0.36	So suppose a man keeps from doing evil only to avoid punishment. The pagan says to him, Non pasces in cruce corvos [You will not be hanged]. And there “you have your reward.”
0.37	But even the pagan will not allow such a harmless man to be called so much as a good heathen.
0.38	Suppose someone acts from the same motive—to avoid punishment, or to avoid losing his friends, his income, or his reputation. Suppose he not only keeps from doing evil, but also does a great deal of good, and even uses all the means of grace. Still, we could not rightly say that this person is even almost a Christian.
0.39	If he has no better principle in his heart, he is simply altogether a hypocrite.
0.40	10. So sincerity is necessarily included in being almost a Christian. Sincerity means a real purpose to serve God and a heartfelt desire to do his will.
0.41	It means that a person sincerely aims to please God in everything—in all his conduct, in all his actions, in all he does or leaves undone.
0.42	If anyone is almost a Christian, this purpose runs through his whole life.
0.43	It is what moves him when he does good, when he keeps away from evil, and when he uses the ordinances of God.
0.44	11. But someone will probably ask, “Is it possible for anyone to go this far and still be only almost a Christian?”
0.45	What more than this could being altogether a Christian include?
0.46	My first answer is this: it is possible to go this far and still be only almost a Christian. I know this not only from God’s own words, but also from the sure evidence of experience.
0.47	12. Brothers, “I am speaking to you with great boldness” about this.
0.48	“Forgive me this wrong” if I shout my own foolishness from the housetop for your sake and for the gospel’s sake. Let me speak freely about myself, as if I were speaking about someone else.
0.49	I am willing to be humbled so that you may be lifted up. I am willing to be thought even less of, for the glory of my Lord.
0.50	13. I did go this far for many years, as many people here can testify. I worked hard to avoid all evil and to keep a clear conscience. I made the most of my time. I took every opportunity to do all the good I could to everyone. I regularly and carefully used all the public and private means of grace. I tried to behave with steady seriousness at all times and in all places. And God is my witness, before whom I stand, that I did all this sincerely. I had a real purpose to serve God, and a heartfelt desire to do his will in everything. I wanted to please him who had called me to “fight the good fight” and to “take hold of eternal life.”
0.51	Yet my own conscience, in the Holy Spirit, tells me that all this time I was only almost a Christian.
0.52	II. Someone may ask, “What more than this does being altogether a Christian include?” I answer:
0.53	(I.) 1. First, the love of God.
0.54	For this is what his word says: “You must love the Lord your God with all your heart, all your soul, all your mind, and all your strength.”
0.55	This kind of love fills the whole heart. It takes up all the feelings. It fills the soul completely and uses all its powers to the full. When someone loves the Lord his God like this, his spirit continually “rejoices in God his Saviour.” His delight is in the Lord, his Lord and his All. To him “he gives thanks in everything.
0.56	All his desire is for God and for the remembrance of his name.” His heart is always crying out, “Whom do I have in heaven but You? And there is no one on earth I desire besides You.”
0.57	In fact, what could he desire besides God?
0.58	Not the world or the things of the world. For he is “crucified to the world, and the world is crucified to him.” He is crucified to “the desires of the flesh, the desires of the eyes, and the pride of life.”
0.59	Yes, he is dead to every kind of pride. For “love is not proud.” And the person who “lives in love lives in God, and God in him.” Such a person is less than nothing in his own eyes.
0.60	(II.) 2. The second thing included in being altogether a Christian is love for our neighbour.
0.61	For our Lord said in the words that follow, “You must love your neighbour as yourself.” If anyone asks, “Who is my neighbour?” we answer: every person in the world, every child of him who is the Father of the spirits of all people.
0.62	We may not leave out our enemies in any way, nor the enemies of God and of their own souls.
0.63	Every Christian loves these people too, as he loves himself—yes, “as Christ loved us.” If anyone wants to understand more fully what kind of love this is, he can look at St. Paul’s description of it.
0.64	It is “patient and kind.”
0.65	It “does not envy.” It is not rash or quick to judge.
0.66	It “is not proud.” Instead, it makes the one who loves the least of all, the servant of all.
0.67	Love “does not behave improperly,” but becomes “all things to all people.”
0.68	She “does not seek her own interests,” but only the good of others, so that they may be saved.
0.69	“Love is not provoked.”
0.70	It drives out anger. Whoever has anger is lacking in love.
0.71	“It thinks no evil.
0.72	It does not rejoice in wrongdoing, but rejoices in the truth.
0.73	It covers all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.”
0.74	(III.) 3. There is one more thing included in being altogether a Christian. It can be considered on its own, though in reality it cannot be separated from what came before. It is the foundation of everything: faith.
0.75	Throughout God’s word, very excellent things are said about faith.
0.76	“Everyone who believes,” says the beloved disciple, “is born of God.”
0.77	“To all who received him, he gave power to become sons of God—to those who believe in his name.”
0.78	And “this is the victory that overcomes the world: our faith.”
0.79	Yes, our Lord himself declares, “Whoever believes in the Son has everlasting life. He does not come into condemnation, but has passed from death to life.”
0.80	4. But here no one should deceive his own soul.
0.81	“We must carefully note this: the faith that does not produce repentance, love, and all good works is not the right, living faith. It is a dead faith, the faith of devils.
0.82	For even the devils believe that Christ was born of a virgin. They believe that he did all kinds of miracles, showing that he was truly God. They believe that for our sake he suffered a most painful death, to rescue us from everlasting death. They believe that he rose again on the third day, that he went up into heaven and sits at the right hand of the Father, and that at the end of the world he will come again to judge both the living and the dead.
0.83	The devils believe these articles of our faith. They also believe everything written in the Old and New Testaments.
0.84	And yet, for all this faith, they are still only devils.
0.85	They still remain in their damned condition, because they lack the true Christian faith.”
0.86	[Homily on the Salvation of Man.]
0.87	5. To continue in the words of our own Church: “The right and true Christian faith is not only to believe that Holy Scripture and the Articles of our Faith are true. It is also to have a sure trust and confidence that we will be saved from everlasting damnation by Christ.
0.88	It is a sure trust and confidence that a person has in God, that through the merits of Christ his sins are forgiven, and he is brought back into God’s favour. From this comes a loving heart that obeys his commandments.”
0.89	6. Now think of this faith. It “purifies the heart” (by the power of God, who lives in it) from “pride, anger, and desire, from all unrighteousness,” from “all filthiness of flesh and spirit.” It fills the heart with love stronger than death, both for God and for all people. This love does the works of God. It delights to spend and be spent for everyone. It joyfully endures not only the disgrace of Christ—being mocked, despised, and hated by everyone—but whatever the wisdom of God allows the malice of men or devils to inflict. Whoever has this faith, working in this way through love, is not only almost a Christian, but altogether a Christian.
0.90	7. But who are the living witnesses of these things?
0.91	Brothers, I beg you, as in the presence of the God before whom “hell and destruction lie uncovered—how much more the hearts of human beings!”—let each of you ask his own heart, “Am I one of them?
0.92	Do I practise justice, mercy, and truth, at least as far as the rules of heathen honesty require?
0.93	If so, do I have even the outward look of a Christian—the form of godliness?
0.94	Do I keep away from evil—from everything that the written Word of God forbids?
0.95	Whatever good my hand finds to do, do I do it with all my might?
0.96	Do I seriously use all the ordinances of God at every opportunity?
0.97	And do I do all this with a sincere purpose and desire to please God in everything?”
0.98	8. Aren’t many of you aware that you have never come this far? You have not even been almost a Christian. You have not reached the standard of heathen honesty, or at least not the form of Christian godliness. Much less has God seen sincerity in you, a real purpose to please him in everything.
0.99	You never even intended to dedicate all your words and actions, your business, studies, and pastimes, to his glory.
0.100	You never even planned or wanted everything you did to be done “in the name of the Lord Jesus,” and so to be “a spiritual sacrifice, acceptable to God through Christ.”
0.101	9. But suppose you had. Do good plans and good desires make someone a Christian?
0.102	Not at all, unless they are carried out.
0.103	“Hell is paved,” someone has said, “with good intentions.”
0.104	So the greatest question of all still remains.
0.105	Has the love of God been poured out in your heart?
0.106	Can you cry out, “My God, and my All”?
0.107	Do you want nothing but him?
0.108	Are you happy in God?
0.109	Is he your glory, your delight, your crown of joy?
0.110	And is this command written in your heart: “Whoever loves God must love his brother also”?
0.111	Then do you love your neighbour as yourself?
0.112	Do you love every person—even your enemies, even the enemies of God—as your own soul? Do you love them as Christ loved you?
0.113	Yes, do you believe that Christ loved you and gave himself for you?
0.114	Do you have faith in his blood?
0.115	Do you believe that the Lamb of God has taken away your sins and thrown them like a stone into the depths of the sea? Do you believe that he has wiped out the written record that was against you, taking it out of the way and nailing it to his cross?
0.116	Do you really have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of your sins?
0.117	And does his Spirit testify with your spirit that you are a child of God?
0.118	10. The God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ is standing among us right now. He knows that if anyone dies without this faith and this love, it would have been better for him never to have been born.
0.119	So wake up, you who are sleeping, and call on your God. Call on him while he may be found.
0.120	Do not let him rest until he makes his “goodness pass before you,” until he proclaims to you the name of the Lord: “The Lord, the Lord God, merciful and gracious, patient, and full of goodness and truth. He keeps mercy for thousands, and he forgives wrongdoing, rebellion, and sin.”
0.121	Do not let anyone use empty words to persuade you to stop short of this prize of your high calling.
0.122	But cry out day and night to him who, “while we were without strength, died for the ungodly.” Keep crying until you know whom you have believed, and can say, “My Lord and my God!”
0.123	Remember “always to pray and not to give up.” Keep on until you too can lift your hand to heaven and say to him who lives forever and ever, “Lord, You know everything. You know that I love You.”
0.124	11. May we all experience in this way what it is to be not only almost, but altogether Christians! May we be justified freely by his grace, through the redemption that is in Jesus. May we know that we have peace with God through Jesus Christ. May we rejoice in the hope of the glory of God. And may we have the love of God poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who has been given to us!
