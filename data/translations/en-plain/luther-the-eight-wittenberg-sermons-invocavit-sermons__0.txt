T:1	Sermon Two (Monday after Invocavit)
1.1	Dear friends, yesterday you heard what a Christian is like: his whole life is faith and love.
1.2	Faith is aimed at God. Love is aimed at people and at our neighbor. Love means giving our neighbor the same kind of love and service that we have received from God, even though we did nothing to earn it.
1.3	So there are two kinds of things. The first kind is the most needed. It must be done in one way and no other. The second kind is a matter of choice, not of necessity. We may keep it or not keep it, and it does not put our faith at risk or send us to hell.
1.4	In both kinds of things, love must treat our neighbor the same way God has treated us. Love must walk the straight road. It must not wander off to the left or to the right.
1.5	Some things are "musts." They are necessary, like believing in Christ. But even in these things, love never uses force or pushes people too hard.
1.6	So the mass is an evil thing, and God is not pleased with it. That is because it is performed as a sacrifice and as a work to earn merit with God.
1.7	So it must be done away with.
1.8	There is no question about this. It is as clear as asking whether you should pray to God.
1.9	We all agree on this: the private mass must be done away with, just as I have said in my writings.
1.10	I truly wish it would be done away with everywhere. I wish that only the gospel mass for all the people would be kept.
1.11	But Christian love should not be harsh here or force the issue.
1.12	We should preach and teach, by speaking and by writing, that holding mass in this way is a sin. But no one should be dragged away from it by force.
1.13	We should leave the matter to God. His Word alone should do the work, without our work.
1.14	Why? Because I do not have the power to shape people's hearts the way a potter shapes clay. I cannot do with them whatever I want.
1.15	I can reach people's ears, but no farther. I cannot reach their hearts.
1.16	And since I cannot pour faith into their hearts, I cannot force anyone to believe. And I should not try.
1.17	That is God's work alone. He is the one who makes faith come alive in the heart.
1.18	So we should let the Word run freely, and not add our own works to it.
1.19	We have the right to speak the Word (the jus verbi), but not the power to make it happen (the executio). We should preach the Word, but what comes of it must be left up to God and what pleases Him.
1.20	Now suppose I rushed in and got rid of the mass by force. Many people would be forced to go along with it without really knowing what they thought. They would say, "I don't know if it is right or wrong. I don't know where I stand. I was forced to go along with the majority."
1.21	This forcing and ordering people around leads to nothing but a joke. It is only an outward show, a foolish game, rules made up by people, fake saints and hypocrites.
1.22	If the heart is not right, I don't care at all about the outward act.
1.23	First we must win people's hearts.
1.24	That happens when I teach only God's Word and preach the gospel. I say, "Dear lords or pastors, stop holding the mass. It is not right. You are sinning when you do it. I cannot keep from telling you this."
1.25	But I would not make it a rule for them or push for a law for everyone. Whoever wanted to follow me could do so. Whoever refused would stay out.
1.26	If someone refused, the Word would still sink into his heart and do its work.
1.27	Then he would become convinced and admit he was wrong, and he would leave the mass. Tomorrow someone else would do the same. In this way God would do more with His Word than if you and I combined all power and authority into one.
1.28	If you have won the heart, you have won the whole person. Then the mass will finally fall by its own weight and come to an end.
1.29	If everyone's heart and mind agree on this goal, then do away with the mass. But if not everyone is fully for doing away with it, then please leave it in God's hands. I beg you. Otherwise things will not turn out well.
1.30	I don't mean that I would bring the mass back. I let it lie, in God's name.
1.31	Faith must not be chained up or locked in prison. It must not be tied to any work by a rule.
1.32	This is the principle you must follow.
1.33	I am sure you will not be able to carry out your plans. And if you do carry them out with laws like these for everyone, then I will take back everything I have written and preached, and I will not stand with you. So I ask you plainly: What harm can the mass do to you?
1.34	You have your faith in God, pure and strong. The mass cannot hurt you.
1.35	So love requires you to have compassion on the weak, as all the apostles did.
1.36	Once Paul came to Athens, a powerful city. In the temple he found many altars. He went from one to the next and looked at them all [Acts 17:16 ff.]. But he did not touch a single one of them, not even with his foot.
1.37	Instead he stood in the middle of the marketplace. He said they were all works of idol worship, and he begged the people to leave them behind. But he did not destroy a single one by force.
1.38	When the Word took hold of their hearts, they left their idols on their own. And so idol worship fell by itself.
1.39	Now, if I had seen them holding mass, I would have preached to them about it and warned them.
1.40	If they had listened to my warning, they would have been won. If not, I still would not have dragged them away from it by the hair or used any force. I would simply have let the Word do its work, while I prayed for them.
1.41	The Word created heaven and earth and everything. The Word must do this work, not us poor sinners.
1.42	To sum up: I will preach it, teach it and write it. But I will not force anyone, because faith must come freely, without being forced.
1.43	Look at me as an example.
1.44	I have stood against the indulgences and against all the pope's followers, but never by force.
1.45	I just taught, preached and wrote God's Word. I did nothing else.
1.46	And while I slept, or drank Wittenberg beer with my Philip and with Amsdorf, the Word weakened the papacy so much that no prince or emperor ever did it so much damage.
1.47	I did nothing. The Word did it all.
1.48	If I had wanted to stir up trouble, I could have caused a lot of bloodshed in Germany. Yes, I could have started such a little game at Worms that even the emperor would not have been safe.
1.49	But what would that have been?
1.50	A fool's game. I did nothing. I left it to the Word.
1.51	What do you think goes through Satan's mind when people try to do things by violence?
1.52	He sits back in hell and thinks, "What a great show these fools will put on for me!"
1.53	But it upsets him when we only spread the Word and let the Word alone do the work.
1.54	For the Word is all-powerful. It captures hearts. And once the hearts are captured, the evil practice will fall by itself.
1.55	Let me give an example.
1.56	Long ago there were also groups who disagreed: Jewish Christians and Gentile Christians. They disagreed about the law of Moses on circumcision.
1.57	The Jewish Christians wanted to keep it, and the Gentile Christians did not [1 Cor. 7:18 ff.].
1.58	Then Paul came and preached that it could be kept or not kept. It made no difference either way. They should not make a "must" out of it, but leave it to each person to choose. Keeping it or not did not matter.
1.59	Later Jerome came along. He wanted to make a "must" out of it, and he wanted laws and rules to forbid it.
1.60	Then St. Augustine came. He held to St. Paul's view: it could be kept or not, as each person wished. St. Jerome had missed St. Paul's meaning by a hundred miles.
1.61	The two church teachers bumped heads pretty hard over this question.
1.62	But when St. Augustine died, St. Jerome got his way.
1.63	After that came the popes. They wanted to add something of their own, and they made laws too.
1.64	So one law led to a thousand laws, until they buried us completely under laws.
1.65	And the same thing will happen here. One law will soon become two, two will grow to three, and so on.
1.66	That is enough for now about the things that are necessary. And let us be careful not to lead astray people whose consciences are weak.
T:2	Sermon Three (Tuesday after Invocavit)
2.1	We have heard about the things that are most necessary in the Christian life. We have also heard what must follow from them: getting rid of the private mass.
2.2	The works that are necessary are the ones God has either commanded or forbidden, as the Majesty on high has decided.
2.3	But no one should be dragged to them by the hair or kept from them by force. I cannot drive anyone to heaven with a club.
2.4	I said this clearly enough, and I believe you understood me.
2.5	Now we will look at the things that are not necessary. God leaves these up to our free choice, and we may keep them or not. For example: whether a person should marry or not, or whether monks and nuns should leave the monasteries.
2.6	These things are a matter of choice. No one may forbid them. If someone does forbid them, that is wrong, because it goes against what God has decided.
2.7	In things that are free, like being married or staying single, you should act like this: If you can control yourself without weighing down your conscience, then by all means do so. But there must be no law for everyone. Each person must be completely free.
2.8	Any priest, monk or nun who cannot control the desires of the body should marry. That will take the weight off his conscience.
2.9	But make sure you are well armed and prepared. Then you can stand before God and the world when you are attacked—especially when the devil attacks you in the hour of death.
2.10	It is not enough to say, "This man or that man did the same thing. I followed the crowd. I did what the provost preached, or Dr. Carlstadt, or Gabriel, or Michael."
2.11	No! Each person must stand on his own feet and be ready to fight the devil.
2.12	If you want to pass the test, you must stand on a strong and clear Bible verse.
2.13	If you can't do that, you will never hold out. The devil will pick you off like a dry, dead leaf.
2.14	So the priests who have married, and the nuns who have married, must stand firmly on a clear Bible verse to keep their consciences safe. There are many such verses, but here is one from St. Paul: "In the last times some will fall away from the faith. They will pay attention to spirits that lead people astray, and to teachings of devils (I think Paul uses plain words here!). They will forbid marriage and command people to stay away from foods, which God has made."
2.15	The devil will not be able to knock down or swallow up this verse. Instead, it will knock him down and swallow him up.
2.16	So any monk or nun who is too weak to keep the vow to stay unmarried and pure should examine himself honestly. If his heart and conscience are strong, so that he can defend himself with a clear conscience, then let him marry.
2.17	I wish to God that all monks and nuns could hear this sermon and understand this matter properly. I wish they would all leave the monasteries, so that every monastery in the world would disappear. That is my deep desire.
2.18	But right now they don't understand it, because no one preaches it to them. They hear that in other places many people are leaving the monasteries—people who are well prepared for such a step. So they want to follow their example. But they have not yet made their consciences strong, and they don't know that this is a matter of freedom.
2.19	That is bad, although it is better for the evil to be outside the monastery than inside.
2.20	So I say: what God has made free must stay free. If someone forbids it, you must not obey—just as the pope, the Antichrist, has forbidden it.
2.21	If someone can wear a monk's hood or a shaved head without harm, and out of love for his neighbor, he may do so. It will not hurt his faith. Wearing a hood will not kill him.
2.22	So, dear friends, this is clear enough. I believe you should understand it and not turn freedom into a law. Don't say, "This priest has married, so all priests must marry."
2.23	Not at all. Or, "This monk or that nun has left the monastery, so they must all come out."
2.24	Not at all. Or, "This man has smashed the images and burned them, so all images must be burned." Not at all, dear brother!
2.25	Or the other way around: "This priest has no wife, so no priest is allowed to marry."
2.26	Not at all! Those who cannot stay pure without marriage should marry. And for others who can live pure lives, it is good for them to hold back, as people who live by the spirit and not by the flesh.
2.27	They should also not be troubled about the vows they have made, like the monks' vows of obedience, chastity and poverty (though the monks are rich enough anyway).
2.28	We cannot make a vow to do anything that goes against God's commands.
2.29	God has made it a free choice to marry or not to marry. And you fool, do you try to turn this freedom into a vow against God's own order?
2.30	So you must leave freedom alone and not turn it into something forced. Your vow goes against the freedom God gives.
2.31	Suppose I made a vow to hit my father in the mouth, or to steal someone's things. Do you think God would be pleased with that vow?
2.32	I should not keep a vow to hit my father in the mouth. In the same way, I should not stay away from marriage just because I am tied by a vow of chastity. In both cases God has ordered things differently.
2.33	God has decided that I am free to eat fish or meat, and that there should be no command about them.
2.34	So all the Carthusian monks, and all other monks and nuns, turn away from the order and freedom God has given when they believe that eating meat makes them unclean.
2.35	But now we must come to the images. It is true of them too that they are not necessary. We are free to have them or not, although it would be much better if we did not have them.
2.36	I am not fond of them.
2.37	A great fight broke out over images between the Roman emperor and the pope. The emperor said he had the right to get rid of the images. But the pope insisted they must stay. Both of them were wrong.
2.38	Much blood was spilled, but the pope came out the winner and the emperor lost.
2.39	What was all this about?
2.40	They wanted to make a "must" out of something that is free. And God cannot put up with that.
2.41	Do you want to change what the Majesty on high has ordered?
2.42	No, you will not do any such thing.
2.43	You read in the Law, in Exodus 20, "Do not make any carved image for yourself, or a picture of anything in the sky above, on the earth below, or in the water under the earth."
2.44	[Ex. 20:4] That is where you take your stand. That is your firm ground.
2.45	Now let's see!
2.46	Our opponents will say, "The first commandment is aimed at this: that we should worship one God alone and not any image. That is why it says right afterward, 'Do not bow down to them or serve them.'" They will argue that worshiping images is forbidden, but not making them. And so they shake our foundation and make it unsteady.
2.47	And if you answer, "The text says, 'Do not make any images,'" they reply, "It also says, 'Do not worship them.'"
2.48	With so much uncertainty, who would be bold enough to destroy the images?
2.49	Not me. But let's go further.
2.50	They say, "Didn't Noah, Abraham and Jacob build altars?"
2.51	And who will deny that?
2.52	We have to admit it.
2.53	Again, didn't Moses set up a bronze snake [Num. 21:9], as we read in his fourth book?
2.54	How can you say Moses forbids making images when he makes one himself?
2.55	It seems to me that a snake like that is an image too.
2.56	How should we answer that?
2.57	Again, don't we read that two birds were set up on the mercy seat, the very place where God wanted to be worshiped?
2.58	[Ex. 37:7] Here we must admit that we may make images and have images. But we must not worship them. And when people do worship them, the images should be removed and destroyed, just as King Hezekiah smashed to pieces the snake Moses had set up [2 Kings 18:4].
2.59	And who will be bold enough to say, when he is asked to prove it, "They worship the images"?
2.60	They will answer, "Are you the man who dares to accuse us of worshiping images?"
2.61	Don't think they will admit it.
2.62	Of course it is true, but we cannot make them admit it.
2.63	Remember how they acted when I condemned works done without faith.
2.64	They said, "Do you think we have no faith? Do you think our works are done without faith?"
2.65	All I can do then is put my flute back in my pocket. Give them a hair's width, and they will take a hundred miles.
2.66	So it should have been preached that images are nothing, and that setting them up does not serve God. Then they would have fallen by themselves.
2.67	That is what I did. That is what Paul did in Athens, when he went into their churches and saw all their idols.
2.68	He did not strike at any of them. Instead he stood in the marketplace and said, "Men of Athens, you are all idol worshipers."
2.69	[Acts 17:22] He preached against their idols, but he did not knock down a single one by force.
2.70	And you want to rush in, cause an uproar, smash the altars and tear down the images?
2.71	Do you really think you can get rid of the images this way?
2.72	No, you will only make them stand more firmly.
2.73	Even if you tore down the images here, do you think you have torn down the ones in Nuremberg and in the rest of the world?
2.74	Not at all. St. Paul, as we read in the Book of Acts, sat in a ship that had the Twin Brothers painted or carved on its front.
2.75	He got on board and did not worry about it at all. He did not break them off either.
2.76	Why did Luke have to describe the Twins here?
2.77	No doubt he wanted to show that outward things cannot harm faith, as long as the heart does not cling to them or trust in them.
2.78	This is what we must preach and teach. And we must let the Word alone do the work, as I said before.
2.79	First the Word must capture people's hearts and give them light. We cannot do it.
2.80	That is why the apostles boasted about their service (ministerium), and not about its results (executio).
2.81	Let this be enough for today. Let us pray to God for His grace.
T:3	Sermon Four (Wednesday after Invocavit)
3.1	Dear friends, we have heard about the things that are necessary—for example, that the mass is treated as a sacrifice.
3.2	Then we looked at the things that are left to our freedom, like marriage, life in a monastery, and getting rid of images.
3.3	We have dealt with these four subjects. And we have said that in all these matters, love is the captain.
3.4	About images in particular, we saw that they should be removed if people are going to worship them, but not otherwise. Still, I wish they were removed everywhere, because they are misused. There is no point denying it.
3.5	Whoever puts an image in a church thinks he has done God a service and a good work. That is plain idol worship.
3.6	This is the biggest, most important and highest reason for getting rid of images. But you ignored it and picked up the very weakest reason.
3.7	I suppose there is hardly anyone who does not understand that the crucifix over there is not my God. My God is in heaven. The crucifix is only a sign.
3.8	But the world is full of the other misuse. Who would put an image of silver or wood in a church unless he thought he was doing God a service by it?
3.9	Do you think Duke Frederick, the bishop of Halle and the others would have put so many silver images in the churches if they thought it meant nothing to God?
3.10	No, they would not have done it.
3.11	But that is not a good enough reason to remove, destroy and burn all the images. And why not?
3.12	Because we have to admit that there are still people who do not think wrongly about images. For them, the images may be useful.
3.13	There may be only a few of them. But we cannot and should not condemn anything that still helps anyone to worship.
3.14	But you should have taught that images are nothing. God does not care about them. He is not served or pleased when we make an image for Him. It would be better to give a poor man a gold coin than to give God a golden image. For God has forbidden the golden image, but not the gift to the poor.
3.15	If people had heard this teaching—that images count for nothing—they would have stopped on their own. And the images would have fallen without any uproar or riot, as was already starting to happen.
3.16	So we must be on our guard. The devil is after us, through his apostles, with all his tricks and cunning.
3.17	It is true, and no one can deny it, that images are evil because they are misused. Still, we must not reject them for that reason. We must not condemn anything just because it is misused.
3.18	That would lead to total chaos.
3.19	God has commanded us not to lift up our eyes to the sun, and so on.
3.20	[Deut. 4:19] He said this so that we would not worship them, because they were created to serve all nations.
3.21	But there are many people who worship the sun and the stars.
3.22	So should we try to pull the sun and stars out of the sky?
3.23	No, we will not do that.
3.24	Again, wine and women bring many men to misery and make fools of them.
3.25	So should we kill all the women and pour out all the wine?
3.26	Again, gold and silver cause a lot of evil. So should we condemn them?
3.27	No. If we wanted to drive away our one worst enemy, the one who harms us most, we would have to kill ourselves. We have no greater enemy than our own heart. As Jeremiah says, "The human heart is crooked" [Jer. 17:9]. Or, as I understand it, "always twisting to one side or the other."
3.28	And what good would that do us?
3.29	Anyone who wants to paint the devil black needs good charcoal. For the devil also wears fine clothes and goes to the fair.
3.30	But I can catch him by asking, "Don't you put the images in the churches because you think it is a special way of serving God?" When he says yes, as he must, you can conclude that he has turned what was meant to serve God into idol worship by misusing the images. He eagerly went after what God has not commanded, and he ignored what God clearly did command: to help our neighbor.
3.31	But I have not caught him yet. He slips away from me by saying, "I help the poor too. Can't I give to my neighbor and also put images in churches?"
3.32	That is not true. For who would not rather give his neighbor a gold coin than give God a golden image!
3.33	No, he would not bother putting images in churches if he believed they did not serve God.
3.34	So I freely admit that images are neither here nor there. They are neither evil nor good. We may have them or not, as we like.
3.35	You caused this trouble. The devil would not have managed it with me. For I cannot deny that you can find someone for whom images are useful.
3.36	And if someone asked me about it, I would admit that none of these things bother me. And if even one person on earth used images in the right way, the devil would quickly turn that against me: "Why do you condemn something that is still useful in worship?"
3.37	I could not answer that challenge. He would have beaten me.
3.38	He would not have gotten nearly this far if I had been here.
3.39	He played a bold game and won, although it does no harm to God's Word.
3.40	You wanted to paint the devil black, but you forgot the charcoal and used chalk.
3.41	If you want to fight the devil, you must know the Bible well. And you must also use it at the right time.
3.42	Let's move on and talk about eating foods.
3.43	It is true that we are free to eat any kind of food—meat, fish, eggs or butter.
3.44	No one can deny this.
3.45	God has given us this freedom.
3.46	That is true. But we must know how to use our freedom. And we must treat the weak brother differently from the stubborn one.
3.47	So pay attention to how you must use this freedom.
