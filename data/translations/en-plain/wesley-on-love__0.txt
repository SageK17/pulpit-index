0.1	“Even if I give away everything I own to feed the poor, and even if I give my body to be burned, but I do not have love, it does me no good at all.”
0.2	1 Corinthians 13:3.
0.3	I have good reason to fear that one day this will be said about most of you who are here: this Bible passage did you no good, just like all the others you heard before.
0.4	Some of you may not be serious enough to listen to it. Some who listen will not believe it. Some who believe it will still find it a hard teaching, so they will forget it as fast as they can. And a few will accept it gladly for a while. But some of them have no roots of humility or self-denial. So when persecution comes because of the word, they will fall away rather than suffer for it.
0.5	Some people do listen to it, believe it and remember it. They even take it so deeply into their hearts that it puts down roots, survives the heat of temptation and starts to bear fruit. Yet even among these, not all will bear fruit that fully ripens.
0.6	The worries or pleasures of this world, and the desire for other things (which they may not have felt until then), will grow up along with the word and choke it.
0.7	And I, the one who speaks God’s word, am no safer from these dangers than you who hear it.
0.8	I too must grieve over “an evil heart of unbelief.”
0.9	Whenever God allows persecution to come, even if it is only the small persecution of being insulted, I might be the first to stumble.
0.10	Or suppose I am given strength to bear that. If God let the worries of the world loose on me, or stopped guarding me against pleasures that do not lead to him, and against the desire for other things [than knowing and loving him], I would surely be overwhelmed. After preaching to others, I myself would be rejected.
0.11	So why do I speak this message at all?
0.12	Why? Because God has trusted me with the task of preaching the gospel. I do not know what I will do tomorrow, but today I will preach the gospel.
0.13	And about you, this is what my orders say: “Son of man, I am sending you to them. You must say to them, ‘This is what the Lord God says’ — whether they listen or refuse to listen.”
0.14	This is what the Lord God says: “Whoever you are, if you want to enter into life, keep the commandments.”
0.15	(To do this, “believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved.”)
0.16	“Do not stay away from meeting together, as some people usually do.”
0.17	Also, in private, “pray to your Father who sees in secret,” and “pour out your heart to him.”
0.18	Let my word be “a lantern for your feet and a light for your paths.”
0.19	Keep it “in your heart and on your lips, when you sit at home, when you walk along the road, when you lie down, and when you get up.”
0.20	“Turn to me with fasting” as well as prayer. Obey your dying Redeemer: eat that bread and drink that cup, and so “show the Lord’s death until he comes.”
0.21	You will receive power from heaven through these means. With that power, do everything the Law commands, and avoid everything it forbids. For you know that “if you break one point, you are guilty of breaking all of it.”
0.22	“And do not forget to do good and to share with others.” Yes, while you have time, do all the good you can to everyone.
0.23	Then “deny yourself and take up your cross every day.” And if you are called to it, “resist even to the point of shedding blood.”
0.24	And when each of you can say, “I have done all this,” let him go on to say to himself (words that could make not just someone like Felix tremble, but even the holiest person on earth): “Even if I give away everything I own to feed the poor, and even if I give my body to be burned, but I do not have love, it does me no good at all.”
0.25	So it is extremely important for all of us to know:
0.26	I. The full meaning of these words: “Even if I give away everything I own to feed the poor, and even if I give my body to be burned.”
0.27	II. The true meaning of the word love.
0.28	III. In what sense we can say that without love, all this does us no good.
0.29	I. First, notice that the word Paul uses really means to cut into small pieces and then hand out the pieces. So it does not just mean getting rid of all our worldly goods at once, perhaps because we suddenly dislike the world or feel a sudden burst of devotion. It means a deliberate choice, carried out calmly and steadily.
0.30	It may also mean that this is done not out of vanity, but partly for the right reason: we want to carry out God’s command, and we long to gain his kingdom.
0.31	Notice also that the word give means actually handing something over, as agreed. So, like the word before it, it does not mean a hasty, thoughtless act. It means an act done with open eyes and a firm heart, following a decision made beforehand.
0.32	So the full meaning of the words is this. Anyone with ears to hear should listen: “Suppose I gave away everything in my house to feed the poor. Suppose I did this after careful choice and thought. Suppose I spent my life handing it out to them with my own hands, and did it out of obedience. Suppose that, for the same reason, I suffered not only insults and shame, and not only chains and prison — all by my own continued choice, refusing to be set free — but even death itself, yes, death in the way that is most terrible to human nature. Even after all this, if I do not have love (love for God, and love for all people, ‘poured into my heart by the Holy Spirit given to me’), it does me no good at all.”
0.33	II. Let us ask what this love is. What is the true meaning of the word?
0.34	We can look at it in terms of its qualities or its effects. And so that we cannot possibly go wrong, we will ignore human opinions completely. We will go to our Lord himself to learn what love is. And to learn its effects, we will go to his inspired apostle.
0.35	The love our Lord requires of all his followers is love for God and love for people. We love God for his own sake, and we love people for God’s sake.
0.36	Now, what does it mean to love God? It means to delight in him, to rejoice in his will, to want to please him all the time, to look for our happiness in him and find it there, and to thirst day and night to enjoy him more fully.
0.37	As for how much we should love him, our Lord has told us clearly: “You must love the Lord your God with all your heart.”
0.38	This does not mean we may love or delight in no one but him. For he has commanded us to love our neighbor — that is, all people — as ourselves. We are to want and pursue their happiness as sincerely and steadily as our own. He has also commanded us to love many of his creatures in the fullest sense: to delight in them and enjoy them. But we must do this only in a way and to a degree that, as we know and feel, does not turn us away from enjoying him but prepares us for it.
0.39	So we are called to love God with all our heart.
0.40	In the chapter we are reading, the apostle describes the effects, or qualities, of this love.
0.41	Each of these is a sure sign by which anyone can judge whether he has this love or not. So they deserve our most careful attention.
0.42	“Love suffers long” — in other words, it is patient.
0.43	If you love your neighbor for God’s sake, you will be patient with his weaknesses for a long time. If he lacks wisdom, you will pity him, not look down on him. If he is wrong, you will gently try to win him back, without any harshness or blame. If he is caught in some fault, you will work to restore him with a gentle spirit. And if that cannot be done quickly, you will be patient with him, in case God may in the end bring him to know and love the truth.
0.44	Whenever people provoke you, whether out of weakness or out of malice, you will show yourself an example of gentleness and meekness. However often it happens, you will not be conquered by evil, but you will conquer evil with good.
0.45	Do not let anyone fool you with empty words: whoever is not patient like this does not have love.
0.46	Again: “Love is kind.”
0.47	Whoever feels the love of God and of people poured into his heart feels a burning, constant thirst for the happiness of all his fellow creatures.
0.48	His soul melts with the strong desire he always has to promote their happiness. And his mouth speaks from what fills his heart.
0.49	Kindness is the rule of his tongue.
0.50	The same kindness is stamped on everything he does.
0.51	The fire inside him keeps working its way out. It spreads further and further in every act of goodwill toward everyone he deals with.
0.52	So whether he thinks or speaks, or whatever he does, it all aims at the same goal: to increase in every possible way the happiness of all his fellow creatures.
0.53	So do not fool yourselves: whoever is not kind like this does not have love.
0.54	Next: “Love does not envy.”
0.55	In fact, this is already included when it says, “Love is kind.”
0.56	For kindness and envy cannot go together. They can no more live side by side than light and darkness.
0.57	If we truly wish every kind of happiness for everyone, we cannot be upset when anyone is happy.
0.58	When our wish comes true, it will be sweet to our soul. We will be far from feeling hurt by it.
0.59	If we are always doing what good we can for our neighbor, and wishing we could do more, we cannot possibly resent any good thing he receives. In fact, it will be the greatest joy of our heart.
0.60	So however much we may flatter ourselves or each other, whoever envies does not have love.
0.61	Next it says, “Love does not boast.” Or better: love is not rash or quick to judge. For this is really the true meaning of the word.
0.62	Everyone who loves his neighbor for God’s sake will not be quick to think badly of anyone. After all, he wishes them every good thing, spiritual as well as earthly.
0.63	Such people cannot condemn someone even in their heart without evidence. They will not do it on weak evidence either. In fact, they will not do it on any evidence without first, if possible, bringing him face to face with his accuser. Or at least they will tell him about the accusation and let him speak for himself.
0.64	Each of you feels that you could not act any other way toward someone you love dearly.
0.65	So then, whoever does not act like this does not have love.
0.66	I will mention just one more quality of this love: “Love is not puffed up.”
0.67	You cannot wrong someone you love. So if you love God with all your heart, you cannot wrong him by robbing him of his glory — by taking for yourself what belongs to him alone.
0.68	You will admit that all you are and all you have belongs to him. You will admit that without him you can do nothing, that he is your light and your life, your strength and your everything, and that before him you are nothing — even less than nothing.
0.69	And if you love your neighbor as yourself, you will not be able to put yourself above him.
0.70	In fact, you will no more be able to look down on anyone than to hate him.
0.71	[In fact, you will think everyone is better than you.]
0.72	Just as wax melts in front of a fire, pride melts away in front of love.
0.73	All arrogance, whether in the heart, in speech or in behavior, disappears where love rules.
0.74	Love brings down the proud looks of the person who boasted in his own strength. It makes him like a little child: unsure of himself, willing to listen, glad to learn, easily convinced, easily persuaded.
0.75	And anyone with a different attitude should give up all empty hope. He is puffed up, and so he does not have love.
0.76	III. We still need to ask in what sense we can say: “Even if I give away everything I own to feed the poor, yes, even if I give my body to be burned, but I do not have love, it does me no good at all.”
0.77	Without doubt, the main meaning of these words is this: whatever we do and whatever we suffer, if we are not made new in the spirit of our mind by “the love of God poured into our hearts by the Holy Spirit given to us,” we cannot enter eternal life.
0.78	No one can enter there except on the basis of the covenant that God has given to people in the Son he loves.
0.79	But general truths tend to touch us less. So let us look at one or two specific areas where all we can do or suffer does us no good if we do not have love.
0.80	First, without love none of it helps to make life happy. Second, none of it helps to make death comfortable.
0.81	First, then: without love nothing can help us enough to make our lives happy.
0.82	By happiness I do not mean a small, trivial pleasure that may begin and end within the same hour. I mean a state of well-being that satisfies the soul and gives it steady, lasting contentment.
0.83	But one simple thought will show that nothing without love can help our present happiness: you cannot lack love in even one single case without pain. And the further you move away from it, the greater the pain.
0.84	Are you lacking in patience?
0.85	Then to the extent that you fall short of it, you fall short of happiness.
0.86	The more the opposite attitudes — anger, irritation, revenge — take control, the unhappier you are.
0.87	You know it; you feel it. The storm cannot be calmed, and peace can never return to your soul, unless meekness, gentleness, patience — in a word, love — takes hold of it.
0.88	Does anyone find in himself ill will, malice, envy or any other attitude that is the opposite of kindness?
0.89	Then misery is there. And the stronger that attitude, the more miserable he is.
0.90	If a lazy man can be said to eat his own flesh, this is even more true of the malicious or envious man.
0.91	His soul is a true picture of hell, full of torment as well as wickedness.
0.92	He already has the worm that never dies, and he is rushing toward the fire that can never be put out.
0.93	But for now the great gulf has not yet been fixed between him and heaven.
0.94	For now there is still a Spirit ready to help him in his weakness. The Spirit is still willing to purify his heart from evil desires, if he will stretch out his hands to heaven and grieve over his ignorance and misery. The Spirit is willing to renew his heart in the love of God, and so lead him through happiness now to eternal happiness.
0.95	Second: Without love, nothing can make death comfortable.
0.96	By comfortable I do not mean dull or unaware.
0.97	I would not say that someone died comfortably if he died of a stroke or was killed by a cannonball. Nor would I say it of someone whose conscience was seared and who died as carelessly as the animals that perish.
0.98	And I do not think you would envy anyone the comfort of dying completely insane.
0.99	By a comfortable death I mean a calm passage out of this life, full of steady, clear-minded peace and joy.
0.100	And all the doing and all the suffering in the world cannot give such a death without love.
0.101	To make this even clearer, I cannot appeal to your own experience. But I can appeal to what we have seen and to the experience of others.
0.102	I myself have seen two people leave this life in what I call a comfortable way, though not with the same amount of comfort.
0.103	One clearly had more comfort than the other, because he had more love.
0.104	I stayed with the first man during much of his final trial, and also when he gave up his soul to God.
0.105	He cried out, “God is disciplining me with severe pain. But I thank him for everything. I bless him for everything. I love him for everything!”
0.106	Not long before he was set free, someone asked him, “Do God’s comforts seem small to you?” He answered out loud, “No, no, no!”
0.107	He called everyone who was near him by name and said, “Think about heaven, talk about heaven. All the time we spend not thinking about heaven is lost.”
0.108	This was the voice of love. And as far as love was in control, everything was comfort, peace and joy.
0.109	But his love was not perfect, and so his comfort was not perfect either.
0.110	He had times of [anger or] irritation, and in those times, misery. Both of these proved beyond question that love can make both life and death sweet.
0.111	So when love is missing from the soul, or hidden in it, there is no peace or comfort there.
0.112	It was right here that I saw the other good soldier of Jesus Christ wrestling with his last enemy, death.
0.113	It truly was a sight worth seeing, for God, for angels and for people.
0.114	He used some of his last breath to sing a psalm of praise to the one who was then giving him the victory. Because he was sure of that victory, he began to celebrate it even in the heat of the battle.
0.115	When someone asked him, “Do you have the love of God in your heart?” he lifted up his eyes and his hands and answered, “Yes, yes!” with all the strength he had left.
0.116	Someone asked if he was afraid of the devil, since he had just said the devil was making his last attack on him. He replied, “No, no. My loving Savior has conquered every enemy. He is with me.
0.117	I am not afraid of anything.” Soon after, he said, “The way to our loving Savior is painful, but it is short.”
0.118	Not long after, he fell into a kind of sleep, and in it his soul gently returned to the God who gave it.
0.119	Notice that here there was no trace of any feeling or attitude opposed to love. So there was no misery. Perfect love drove out everything that could have caused torment.
0.120	And whoever you are, if you have the same amount of love, your end will be like his.
