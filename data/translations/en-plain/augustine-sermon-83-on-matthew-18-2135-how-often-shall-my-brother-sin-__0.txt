0.1	1. Yesterday the holy Gospel warned us not to ignore the sins of our brothers and sisters. It said: “If your brother sins against you, correct him when it is just the two of you.
0.2	If he listens to you, you have won your brother back.
0.3	But if he refuses to listen to you, take two or three others with you. Then every word can be confirmed by two or three witnesses.
0.4	If he will not listen to them either, tell it to the Church.
0.5	And if he will not listen to the Church, treat him like a heathen and a tax collector.”
0.6	Today the passage that comes next, which we heard read, is about the same subject.
0.7	When the Lord Jesus had said this to Peter, Peter went on to ask his Master how often he should forgive a brother who had sinned against him. He asked if seven times would be enough.
0.8	“The Lord answered him: Not just seven times, but seventy times seven.”
0.9	Then He added a parable that is full of terror. “The kingdom of heaven is like the master of a house who settled accounts with his servants. Among them he found one who owed ten thousand talents.
0.10	The master ordered that everything the man had, his whole family, and the man himself be sold to pay the debt. Then the servant fell down at his master’s feet.” He begged for more time, and he was let off the whole debt.
0.11	As we have heard, “His master felt sorry for him and forgave him the whole debt.”
0.12	That man was now free from his debt, but he was a slave to evil. After he left his master, he found someone who owed him money. That man did not owe him ten thousand talents, the amount he himself had been forgiven. He owed him a hundred denarii. And “he grabbed him by the throat and began to drag him, saying, Pay me what you owe.”
0.13	Then the man begged his fellow servant, just as that servant had begged his master. But he did not find his fellow servant to be the kind of man that servant had found his master to be.
0.14	He would not forgive him the debt. He would not even give him more time.
0.15	He was very violent and dragged him off to make him pay. And he himself had only just been set free from his own debt to his master!
0.16	The other servants were upset. They “went and told their master what had happened.” The master called his servant in and said to him, “You wicked servant! You owed me such a huge debt, and I felt sorry for you and forgave you all of it.
0.17	Shouldn’t you have had mercy on your fellow servant, just as I had mercy on you?”
0.18	And he ordered that everything he had forgiven him must be paid.
0.19	2. So He told this parable to teach us. With this warning He wanted to keep us from being lost.
0.20	He said, “My heavenly Father will do the same to you if each of you does not forgive his brother his sins from your heart.”
0.21	You see, brothers and sisters, this is clear. The warning is useful. We must by all means obey it, for our own good, so that we do what we have been told.
0.22	Every person owes a debt to God. And every person also has some brother who owes a debt to him.
0.23	Who does not owe God a debt? Only someone in whom no sin can be found.
0.24	And who has no brother in debt to him? Only someone no one has ever sinned against.
0.25	Do you think you can find anyone in the human race who does not owe his brother something because of some sin?
0.26	So every person is a debtor, and yet he also has debtors of his own.
0.27	So the righteous God gives you a rule for how to treat the person who owes you. He will follow the same rule with those who owe Him.
0.28	There are two works of mercy that set us free. The Lord Himself stated them briefly in the Gospel: “Forgive, and you will be forgiven. Give, and it will be given to you.”
0.29	“Forgive, and you will be forgiven” is about pardoning.
0.30	“Give, and it will be given to you” is about doing good to others.
0.31	About pardoning: you want your own sin to be pardoned, and you have someone else you can pardon.
0.32	About doing good: a beggar asks you for something, and you yourself are God’s beggar.
0.33	When we pray, we are all God’s beggars. We stand, or rather we lie face down, at the door of the great Master of the house. We groan and plead because we want to receive something. And that something is God Himself.
0.34	What does the beggar ask you for?
0.35	Bread. And what do you ask God for, if not Christ? He says, “I am the living Bread that came down from heaven.”
0.36	Do you want to be forgiven?
0.37	Then forgive. “Forgive, and you will be forgiven.”
0.38	Do you want to receive? “Give, and it will be given to you.”
0.39	3. But now listen. Even in such a clear command, I may raise a difficulty.
0.40	When someone asks for pardon, the one who should grant it owes it to him. But this question of forgiveness may puzzle us, just as it puzzled Peter.
0.41	“How often should I forgive?
0.42	Is up to seven times enough?”
0.43	“It is not enough,” says the Lord. “I do not tell you up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven.”
0.44	Now count how many times your brother has sinned against you.
0.45	If you can reach the seventy-eighth wrong, and get past the seventy times seven, then go ahead and take revenge.
0.46	Is this really what He means? Is it really true that if he sins “seventy times seven,” you must forgive him, but if he sins seventy-eight times, you are then allowed not to forgive?
0.47	No, I dare to say that even if he sins seventy-eight times, you must forgive.
0.48	Yes, as I said, if he sins seventy-eight times, forgive.
0.49	And if he sins a hundred times, forgive.
0.50	But why should I name one number after another?
0.51	In a word: as often as he sins, forgive him.
0.52	Have I then taken it on myself to go beyond the limit my Lord set?
0.53	He set the limit of forgiveness at the number seventy-seven. Do I dare to jump over that limit?
0.54	No. I have not dared to go beyond it at all.
0.55	I have heard the Lord Himself speaking through His Apostle, and there no limit or number is set.
0.56	He says, “Forgive one another if anyone has a quarrel against someone, just as God in Christ has forgiven you.”
0.57	That is the rule.
0.58	Suppose Christ forgave your sins only “seventy times and seven.” Suppose He pardoned up to that point and refused to pardon any more. Then you may set the same limit and refuse to forgive beyond it.
0.59	But if Christ has found thousands upon thousands of sins and has still forgiven them all, then do not hold back your mercy. Instead, ask forgiveness for that great number.
0.60	The Lord did not say “seventy times seven” without a reason. There is no wrong at all that you should not forgive.
0.61	Look at this servant in the parable. He was a debtor who turned out to have a debtor of his own. He owed ten thousand talents.
0.62	I think ten thousand talents means at least ten thousand sins.
0.63	I will not even say how one single talent can include all sins.
0.64	But how much did the other servant owe him?
0.65	He owed a hundred denarii.
0.66	Isn’t that more than “seventy-seven”?
0.67	And yet the Lord was angry because he did not forgive him.
0.68	A hundred is more than “seventy-seven.” And besides, a hundred denarii may be worth a thousand coins called “asses.”
0.69	But what was that compared with ten thousand talents?
0.70	4. So if we want to be forgiven, let us be ready to forgive every wrong done to us.
0.71	Think about our sins. Count what we do by our actions, by our eyes, by our ears, by our thoughts, and by countless stirrings. I am not sure we even sleep without owing a talent.
0.72	That is why we beg every day. Every day we knock at God’s ears in prayer. Every day we bow down and say, “Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.”
0.73	Which of your debts?
0.74	All of them, or only some?
0.75	You will answer, “All of them.”
0.76	Then do the same with the person who owes you.
0.77	This is the rule you set. This is the condition you name. This is the promise and agreement you speak of when you pray, “Forgive us, as we forgive our debtors.”
0.78	5. So, brothers and sisters, what does “seventy times seven” mean?
0.79	Listen, because it is a great mystery, a wonderful sacred sign.
0.80	When the Lord was baptized, the Gospel writer Saint Luke recorded His family line at that point. He listed the generations in proper order, one after another, down to the generation in which Christ was born.
0.81	Matthew starts with Abraham and goes down to Joseph. But Luke counts upward.
0.82	Why does one count downward and the other upward?
0.83	Because Matthew set out the family line by which Christ came down to us. So he started his count at Christ’s birth and counted downward.
0.84	But Luke starts counting at Christ’s baptism, and that is where the going up begins. So he counts upward. And in his count he reaches seventy-seven generations.
0.85	With whom did he begin his count?
0.86	Notice with whom. He started counting from Christ and went all the way up to Adam. Adam was the first sinner, and he fathered us bound with the chain of sin.
0.87	He counted up to Adam, and that makes seventy-seven generations. From Christ to Adam, and from Adam to Christ, there are these seventy-seven generations.
0.88	So if no generation was left out, then no wrong is left out of what we must forgive.
0.89	That is why he counted seventy-seven generations, the same number the Lord used about forgiving sins. He began counting from the baptism, where all sins are forgiven.
0.90	6. Brothers and sisters, see an even greater mystery in this.
0.91	The number seventy-seven holds a mystery about the forgiveness of sins.
0.92	That is how many generations there are from Christ to Adam.
0.93	So look a little more carefully for the secret meaning of this number. Search out its hidden meaning. Knock more carefully, so that it will be opened to you.
0.94	Righteousness means keeping the Law of God. That is true.
0.95	The Law is given in ten commandments.
0.96	That is why the servant in the parable “owed ten thousand talents.”
0.97	These are the famous Ten Commandments, written by the finger of God and given to the people by Moses, the servant of God.
0.98	So he “owed ten thousand talents.” This stands for all sins, based on the number of the Law.
0.99	And the other servant “owed a hundred denarii.” This also comes from the same number.
0.100	A hundred times a hundred is ten thousand, and ten times ten is a hundred.
0.101	One “owed ten thousand talents,” and the other owed ten times ten denarii.
0.102	Neither number moves away from the number of the law. In both numbers you will find every kind of sin included.
0.103	Both men are debtors. Both plead and beg for pardon. But the wicked, ungrateful servant would not repay what he had received. He would not show the mercy that had been shown to him, though he did not deserve it.
0.104	7. So think about this, brothers and sisters. Everyone starts from Baptism. He goes out free, and the “ten thousand talents” are forgiven him. And when he goes out, he will soon find some fellow servant who owes him something.
0.105	So let him notice what sin really is. The number eleven stands for breaking the law.
0.106	The law is ten; sin is eleven.
0.107	The law is shown by ten, and sin by eleven.
0.108	Why is sin shown by eleven?
0.109	Because to get to eleven, you have to go past ten.
0.110	The law sets the proper limit, and going past it is sin.
0.111	When you go past ten, you come to eleven.
0.112	This deep mystery was pictured when the command was given to build the tabernacle.
0.113	Many things there are listed by number, and they hold a great mystery.
0.114	Among other things, the curtains made of haircloth were to be not ten but eleven. That is because haircloth stands for confessing our sins.
0.115	What more do you need?
0.116	Do you want to know how all sins are contained in the number “seventy-seven”?
0.117	Seven usually stands for something whole. In seven days the cycle of time is complete. When the seventh day is over, it goes back to the first, so that the same cycle can go on.
0.118	Whole ages pass by in cycles like this. Yet they never move away from the number seven.
0.119	So He was speaking of all sins when He said “seventy times seven.” Multiply that eleven by seven, and you get seventy-seven.
0.120	That is why He wants all sins forgiven. He marked them out by the number “seventy-seven.”
0.121	So let no one hold on to anything to his own harm by refusing to forgive. Otherwise it will be held against him when he prays.
0.122	For God says, “Forgive, and you will be forgiven.”
0.123	I have forgiven you first. You at least should forgive after that.
0.124	If you will not forgive, I will call you back. I will put on you again everything I had forgiven you.
0.125	The Truth does not lie. Christ does not deceive, and He is not deceived. At the end of the parable He said, “Your Father in heaven will do the same to you.”
0.126	You have found a Father. Imitate your Father.
0.127	If you will not imitate Him, you are working to lose your inheritance.
0.128	So “My heavenly Father will do the same to you, if each of you does not forgive his brother his sins from your heart.”
0.129	Do not say “I forgive” with your tongue while putting off forgiving in your heart. By His threat of vengeance, God shows you what your punishment will be.
0.130	God knows where you are speaking from.
0.131	People can hear your voice. God looks into your conscience.
0.132	If you say, “I forgive,” then forgive.
0.133	It is better to be harsh in your words and forgive in your heart than to be gentle in your words and unforgiving in your heart.
0.134	8. Now unruly boys will beg us. They hate being beaten, and they object like this when we want to punish them.
0.135	“I have sinned, but forgive me.”
0.136	All right, I forgave him, and he sins again.
0.137	“Forgive me,” he cries, and I forgave him.
0.138	He sins a third time.
0.139	“Forgive me,” he cries, and a third time I forgave him.
0.140	Now, the fourth time, let him be beaten.
0.141	And he will say, “What? Have I worn you out up to seventy-seven times?”
0.142	If objections like this put strict discipline to sleep, then once discipline is gone, wickedness will run wild without punishment.
0.143	So what should we do?
0.144	Let us correct with words, and if needed, with the lash. But let us also forgive the sin and throw the memory of it out of our hearts.
0.145	That is why the Lord added, “from your heart.” Even when we discipline out of love, gentleness should not leave our heart.
0.146	What is as kind and gentle as a surgeon with his knife?
0.147	The person who is to be cut cries out, but he is cut. The person who is to be cauterized cries out, but he is cauterized.
0.148	This is not cruelty. That surgeon’s treatment must never be called cruelty.
0.149	He is cruel to the wounded part so that the patient can be healed. If the wound is handled softly, the man is lost.
0.150	So this is my advice, my brothers and sisters. Let us love our brothers and sisters, no matter how they have sinned against us. Let us not let our love for them leave our hearts. And when it is needed, let us discipline them. Otherwise, if discipline becomes slack, wickedness will grow, and we will begin to be accused on God’s behalf. For we have heard it read: “Rebuke those who sin in front of everyone, so that others also may fear.”
0.151	Certainly, if you tell the different times apart, which is the only right way, and solve the question like that, then everything is true.
0.152	If the sin is secret, rebuke it in secret.
0.153	If the sin is public and open, rebuke it in public so that the sinner may be changed, and “so that others also may fear.”
