0.1	“He forgives all your sins. He heals all your diseases. He rescues your life from destruction. He crowns you with loving kindness and tender mercies. He fills your mouth with good things, so that your youth is made new like the eagle’s.”—Psalm 103:3–5
0.2	In this psalm that I read to you, we should notice especially how much the psalmist stresses praise. He says, “Praise the Lord (or Jehovah), my soul, and all that is within me.”
0.3	His whole soul is involved in this service.
0.4	All of it! The love of his heart and the powers of his mind—all of it!
0.5	This is what we should aim for.
0.6	It is not that we fail to thank the Lord. I trust that we do. But our thanks should be more spiritual. More and more, our hearts should be involved. All that is within us should be busy praising, adoring, and honoring the Lord.
0.7	Then the psalmist adds something remarkable: “Praise his holy name.”
0.8	Only believers can really do this.
0.9	By nature, we do not care about God’s holiness.
0.10	By nature, people like to please themselves. They would like God to be like them.
0.11	By nature, holiness is the last thing about God that we care about.
0.12	But when we are born again—when we are made new and have spiritual life—a longing for holiness is born in our hearts. And we are glad that God is a holy God.
0.13	Then we can look forward to being like him one day.
0.14	Only a child of God really cares about admiring God’s holiness and praising him for it.
0.15	Then in the second verse the psalmist adds: “Praise Jehovah, my soul, and do not forget all his benefits.”
0.16	We are in danger of forgetting God’s mercies.
0.17	We are quick to talk about our trials, our difficulties, the loved ones we have lost, and our crosses. But are we just as quick to talk about God’s goodness and admire it? Are we quick to speak of his mercies, shown to us in countless ways every day of our lives?
0.18	The psalmist cared about this very much.
0.19	Now let us turn to our text:
0.20	“He forgives all your sins.”
0.21	This is the first blessing the psalmist names.
0.22	It is the first mercy he especially wanted to remember.
0.23	Notice carefully that out of all the blessings, this one comes first.
0.24	And isn’t this the best blessing we have received?
0.25	Can any other blessing compare with this one?
0.26	Think of all the blessings we have in our business. We should be grateful for them. But what are they compared with the forgiveness of our sins?
0.27	Think of all the peace and quiet in our families. We should be grateful for these too. But what are they compared with the forgiveness of our sins?
0.28	We should be thankful for good health. But what is a healthy body compared with the forgiveness of our sins?
0.29	What is the strength and energy of mind God has blessed us with, compared with the forgiveness of our sins?
0.30	It is nothing compared with it.
0.31	The psalmist puts this blessing first because it is the best, the greatest, and the most precious blessing a person can have.
0.32	Let me ask you lovingly: Have all of us received forgiveness for our sins?
0.33	That is the point! That is the point!
0.34	Are all of us forgiven sinners?
0.35	We are all sinners, every one of us.
0.36	Are all of us forgiven sinners?
0.37	Have all of us received forgiveness for our sins?
0.38	That is the point!
0.39	I have no doubt that many hundreds of you here have. But at the same time, I cannot help feeling that many have not.
0.40	So this is the all-important question: Which of the two groups do we belong to?
0.41	Are we forgiven sinners, whose heavy load of guilt has been taken away? Or are we unforgiven sinners, who cannot look up to God with peace and calm through the atoning sacrifice of the Lord Jesus Christ?
0.42	Ask yourself before God: Which group do I belong to?
0.43	If I am not forgiven, I have no peace. I cannot look ahead to eternity with peace and calm. And I do not know where my life’s path will end.
0.44	It is a terrible thing to go on day after day without being forgiven.
0.45	This leads to the question: How can we receive forgiveness for our sins?
0.46	Simply by taking hold, through faith, of the atoning sacrifice of the Lord Jesus Christ.
0.47	We deserved to be sent to the place of eternal ruin. But God, in his rich grace, did not send us there. Instead, he laid all the punishment for our countless sins on his Son, the Lord Jesus Christ.
0.48	God sent him into the world to take our place. There he earned a righteousness for us by keeping the whole law of God. So when poor, guilty sinners trust in him, God sees them as if they had lived the holy and spotless life of the Lord Jesus Christ.
0.49	The sinner who believes stands before God in Christ’s righteousness. He hides himself in the righteousness that the Lord Jesus Christ earned for the poor sinner who trusts in him. And God accepts him.
0.50	In our place, the Lord Jesus Christ took every bit of the punishment that we wicked, guilty sinners should have suffered.
0.51	So God does not expect us to do something to finish the work of salvation. He wants us to accept what he so graciously provides for sinners in his Son. He accepts his Son’s righteousness for the sinner.
0.52	But when a sinner believes in the Lord Jesus Christ, a new kind of life begins. He wants to please Christ. He wants his life to make Christ’s teaching look beautiful. He wants to live the way Christ wants.
0.53	He does not do this to be saved by it, or to add something to the work of the Lord Jesus Christ.
0.54	He has already been saved through faith. He has been forgiven. He has been accepted because of the righteousness the Lord Jesus Christ earned for sinners. And so he wants to please God.
0.55	This is the way to be forgiven: simply trust in Jesus, and in this way believe in him.
0.56	Everyone who does this is forgiven.
0.57	Another important point is knowing that we are forgiven.
0.58	We do not have to wait until we die to know this. Much less do we have to wait until the day of judgment.
0.59	This blessing is for now. We can know it now. We can enjoy it now.
0.60	Someone who does not have it cannot be very happy for long.
0.61	This is what brings the peace and joy of the Holy Spirit into our hearts: knowing that our sins are forgiven, even though we are wicked and guilty.
0.62	My dear Christian friends, are all of us enjoying the forgiveness of our sins?
0.63	I hope no one says it is impossible to have it.
0.64	It certainly is possible. The first Christians enjoyed this blessing.
0.65	They knew whom they believed. They knew that in him they had “redemption through his blood—that is, the forgiveness of sins.”
0.66	They were told this about the Lord Jesus Christ: “All the prophets bear witness to him: through his name, everyone who believes in him will receive forgiveness of sins.”
0.67	So every poor sinner who believes in Jesus is forgiven.
0.68	This really is a blessing we can have now.
0.69	If any of you do not have this blessing, do not give the Lord any rest until you know that your sins are forgiven.
0.70	You really can have it and enjoy it now, because salvation brings blessing with it right now.
0.71	This is one of the blessings that come with believing in the Lord Jesus Christ: knowing that we are forgiven.
0.72	Notice something else in what the psalmist says. He does not say, “He forgives some of your sins.” He says ALL.
0.73	That is so precious.
0.74	It is not that five hundred of our sins are forgiven, or five thousand. Every single one is forgiven. Even though they cannot be counted, every one is forgiven.
0.75	Just think of it! We are vile and guilty. Yet every sin of everyone who believes in the Lord Jesus Christ is forgiven.
0.76	Are you enjoying this?
0.77	I really do enjoy the forgiveness of my sins.
0.78	It is not because I have very strong feelings.
0.79	I do not depend on feelings.
0.80	I take God at his word.
0.81	I depend on his word: “Whoever believes in him will not perish, but will have eternal life.”
0.82	I believe in Jesus, so I have been forgiven.
0.83	I have not had any dream or vision about it.
0.84	Some people think they must stay in doubt unless they see some strange vision—Jesus hanging on the cross in some corner of the room.
0.85	I have had no vision like that.
0.86	For fifty years I have never doubted for one minute that my sins are forgiven.
0.87	I have been a believer all these years. And all this time the word I mentioned, the word I depend on, has been written in the Book. By it I know that my sins are forgiven.
0.88	Every believer who is willing to take God at his word has the right to see himself as a pardoned sinner, a forgiven sinner.
0.89	It is a blessing, a great blessing, to know that all our sins are forgiven.
0.90	Now suppose we had exactly 9,090 sins. And suppose 9,089 of them were forgiven, with just one single sin not forgiven.
0.91	What then? That one single sin would bring us to the place of eternal ruin.
0.92	Sin is not something to play with.
0.93	We must be completely without sin—hidden in the merits of the Lord Jesus Christ and fully forgiven. Otherwise we are unclean, and we cannot come into his presence.
0.94	So see how wonderful this statement of the psalmist is: “He forgives ALL your sins.”
0.95	All gone! Oh, how wonderful this is!
0.96	Every one of them is gone! Sins in what we do, sins in what we say, sins in what we think, sins in what we feel, sins in what we desire, sins in what we intend, sins in what we lean toward—all gone. They are gone as surely as we trust the Lord Jesus Christ to save our souls.
0.97	Now I come to a point I especially want to mention. It is to teach Christians, and especially young believers.
0.98	We read, “He FORGIVES.”
0.99	This shows that forgiveness is something that is going on now.
0.100	Christians may say: Sometimes the New Testament speaks as if all our sins were forgiven. At other times it speaks as if we needed to receive forgiveness. And this verse, “He forgives,” sounds as if forgiveness were still going on.
0.101	Here is the explanation.
0.102	As sinners, we are by nature guilty criminals. In that position, the moment we believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, all our sins are forgiven. As far as our salvation is concerned, this is done once and for all.
0.103	But then we leave our old relationship, in which we were guilty criminals before the righteous Judge. We move into the position of children. In this relationship as children, our salvation is settled. Yet we still fail, more or less every day. And whenever we fail and the Holy Spirit makes us aware of it, we must admit it before God with the simple trust of a child. We must tell him that in this way we have not behaved as we should, or that we have left this thing undone, or done that thing wrongly.
0.104	With the simple trust of a child, we are to confess to the Lord. Then this word applies: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” And for this the Lord Jesus Christ is needed in his work as High Priest.
0.105	As far as our salvation is concerned, when he died he could say, “It is finished.” Then he went up to heaven as the great High Priest. There he still points to the power of his blood on the sinner’s behalf.
0.106	It is in this second sense—in our position as children—that we need to confess whenever we go wrong or fail. Then we receive our Father’s forgiveness. And we will receive it just as surely as we confess. I trust this will explain it to my dear Christian friends.
0.107	“He heals all your diseases.”
0.108	This is the next blessing the psalmist wanted to remember.
0.109	We do not mean that diseases of the body are left out. If anyone is cured of a bodily disease, it is not by the doctor’s skill or by the strength of the medicine. It is by God’s blessing on the doctor’s skill.
0.110	Still, I believe the main point here is spiritual disease.
0.111	You remember what Isaiah said at the beginning of his prophecies: “From the sole of the foot to the head there is nothing healthy—only wounds, bruises, and rotting sores.”
0.112	We all know that this was not about the bodies of the Israelites. Their bodies were not in that disgusting state. It was about their spiritual diseases.
0.113	This is true of all sinners, at all times, and in all situations.
0.114	If we are spiritually pure, it must come from the Lord.
0.115	Now here we must especially remember this. The moment a poor sinner trusts in the Lord Jesus Christ, he puts himself into the care of the infinitely wise and gracious Doctor. He does this almost without knowing it.
0.116	That blessed One takes him in and puts him into his own hospital, his own infirmary. And he will not let him go until he is completely cured.
0.117	And as you all know, no one is ever sent out of that hospital because their disease cannot be cured, as often happens among people. Instead, the patient is cared for until he is completely free from spiritual disease.
0.118	The moment we are there, the cure is going on.
0.119	This very meeting is meant, with the blessing of Jesus, to help with that cure—to help us out of our diseased state.
0.120	And this is what the weakest believer can look forward to. As surely as he believes in Jesus, and as surely as he is not living in sin on purpose, he will finally be brought to the state where he is completely free from sin.
0.121	God has taken hold of us so that we will be made like his Son. In the end we will be completely like him, completely holy, and completely free from spiritual disease.
0.122	Pride will be gone completely. Irritability, greed, and love for the world will all be gone.
0.123	We will be gentle and lovely, pure and holy—just like Jesus.
0.124	Oh, what a wonderful future! Jesus will not give up on us until we are completely free from our spiritual diseases.
0.125	Then these words will come true: “He heals all your diseases.”
0.126	He trains us and cares for us so well that every day something is done toward this complete cure. We should ask ourselves: Have I made some progress today?
0.127	Am I a little freer from disease today than I was yesterday?
0.128	Am I a little more like Jesus today?
0.129	At the new year we should ask: Have I made more progress this past year than the year before? For the Lord’s will for us is that in the end we will be like Jesus. God’s will should only have to be shown to us, and right away, deep in our souls, we should be ready to do it.
0.130	The psalmist wanted to be grateful to God for carrying on this cure.
0.131	“He rescues your life from destruction.”
0.132	This is true in many different ways.
0.133	Think about our natural life. If God did not watch over us, what would happen to us?
0.134	We are always in danger of losing our lives.
0.135	This is especially true of little children.
0.136	Look at the dozens of little children near the Tabernacle, with carts and carriages going by. Who can fail to see that God watches over them?
0.137	How true this is of our natural life: “He rescues your life from destruction.”
0.138	And when we travel, how much danger our lives are in!
0.139	Even if we do not travel but lie in our beds, we are always in danger of losing our lives.
0.140	How often a stack of chimneys has fallen and crushed people in an instant!
0.141	But there is more to it than this.
0.142	Our whole life was meant to be lived for God’s glory.
0.143	Suppose we had not believed in Jesus—either as the Messiah who was still to come, or as the one who has come. Then this life that was given to us would have been spent in hell.
0.144	Our life has truly been rescued from destruction.
0.145	But think of those who do not yet believe in the Lord Jesus Christ. Their life was given to them for Christ’s glory and to be enjoyed forever. Yet it could still be spent in hell.
0.146	Make sure that your life is rescued from destruction. Unless you believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, it is not yet fully true that your life has been rescued from destruction.
0.147	There is more. We believe in the Lord Jesus Christ and want to live for him. But suppose we had been left on our own since we believed. We would have fallen away.
0.148	It is only because of God’s grace, mercy, and faithfulness that we are on the Lord’s side today and have not gone back into the world.
0.149	So how important it is to be grateful that God has held us up! We have his word: “He who began a good work in you will carry it through until the day of Jesus Christ.” And he will never leave us or abandon us.
0.150	What a blessed and glorious future this is!
0.151	“He crowns you with loving kindness and tender mercies.”
0.152	This was the next mercy the psalmist especially wanted to be grateful for.
0.153	We should clearly understand the picture used here.
0.154	Whenever the Bible uses a picture, we should try to understand it clearly. Then we can know what spiritual truth is hidden in it.
0.155	The picture here is “crowning.”
0.156	We all know that in a country, not a dozen people are crowned, or even half a dozen. Just one person is chosen, and the crown is put on his head as a sign of honor and power.
0.157	This one person, chosen and taken out from all the rest, is given this place of honor and power by the crown put on his head.
0.158	That is exactly how our heavenly Father treats us who believe in the Lord Jesus Christ. We are not one bit better than the worst of people.
0.159	We are just as wicked as the poor prisoners on the Cut, or in the Bridewell, or at Lawford’s Gate.
0.160	In ourselves we are just as bad as they are—just as bad as the worst people in the city.
0.161	When we see drunkards or prisoners being taken away by the police, we should say: If it were not for God’s grace, I would be a drunkard now.
0.162	If it were not for God’s grace, I would be in the hands of those policemen.
0.163	We should never forget this, right to the end. Then our hearts will be filled more and more with love and gratitude to God, and to the Lord Jesus Christ—more and more, more and more. For if we had been left on our own, we would have been wallowing in sin and in a far worse state.
0.164	This is the very point the psalmist has in mind when he says, “He crowns you with loving kindness and tender mercies.” “Loving kindness” means grace.
0.165	In the Old Testament, in the Hebrew, it means grace.
0.166	We owe it to God’s grace and mercy.
0.167	If it were not for God’s grace and mercy, we would be like the worst of the worst.
0.168	We should remember all this. It will keep us from pride and from thinking we are better than others. And it will keep our hearts filled with love and gratitude to the Lord Jesus Christ.
0.169	Now there is one more mercy left that the psalmist wanted to be grateful for.
0.170	Precious Lord Jesus Christ, use your servant’s mouth now. By your Spirit, lead your poor servant to bring out the very points that these dear disciples especially need for their help.
0.171	Help your servant. Let the word come not only in words but in the power of the Holy Spirit. Then these dear disciples here will not forget it for the rest of their lives.
0.172	Your servant asks this for the sake of your dear name.
0.173	“He fills your mouth with good things, so that your youth is made new like the eagle’s.”
0.174	Notice the picture again: the old eagles shed their feathers. When this has happened, their strength is truly made new, and they become strong and powerful again.
0.175	This was true of those who were old.
0.176	So the psalmist looked at himself and marveled at what the Lord had done for him.
0.177	He is not referring to food, though food is included. We should be very grateful for every crumb of bread and every drop of water, because the food we are given makes our bodies strong and healthy.

0.179	But he meant more than this. He was talking about spiritual food, which renewed his spiritual strength.
0.180	Now, dear Christian friends, this is the all-important point.
0.181	Older believers do not need to become more and more lifeless, careless, and worldly.
0.182	As the psalmist grew old, he did not become very worldly. He did not become lifeless, cold, and unspiritual. Instead, his spiritual strength was renewed.

0.185	It can be very different. It should be very different. And if it is not, we are not living for the praise and glory of God.
0.186	In his old age the psalmist was happier in the Lord and more spiritually minded. He had more spiritual power and strength at the end than he had at the beginning.
0.187	Oh, my dear younger brothers and sisters, you do not have dull and miserable days ahead of you. You have brighter and happier days ahead.
0.188	And here, for the honor and glory of God, I will give my own testimony.
0.189	I have been a believer for almost fifty-one years, and I am happier now than I was fifty years ago. I am far happier than I was forty years ago, or thirty years ago, or twenty years ago, or ten years ago.

0.193	As you sometimes sing, “More and more, more and more”—there is still more to come.

0.195	It is the joy and delight of his heart to give more and more.
0.196	Why shouldn’t it be that way?

0.199	Has the Bible changed?
0.200	No! We have the same blessed word.
0.201	Is the power of the Holy Spirit any less?

0.203	The Lord Jesus Christ is always ready to bless.
0.204	The word we have now is the whole revelation.
0.205	And our heavenly Father has the same heart toward his children.
0.206	So nothing can stop us from becoming happier as time goes on.
0.207	If we are not happier, what is the reason?

0.232	May the Lord help each of us to do this!
0.233	But are there any dear friends here who have not yet been forgiven?
