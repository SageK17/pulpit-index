1.102	After he died, they pulled back the covers. His stiff hands were found clutching the key to his safe in a death grip.
1.103	Right to the end, his heart and his hand held on to his possessions. But he could not take them with him.
1.104	A person may be proud, and people may even call his sin a virtue.
1.105	Listen to what God’s Word says: “Proud eyes and an arrogant heart are sin”; and “The Lord hates everyone whose heart is proud.”
1.106	These are the mistakes people make.
1.107	They live respectable lives, and they think everything is fine.
1.108	They do not see that rot has spread into many of the things their hearts love most.
1.109	Most of all, people who call themselves Christians need to be careful that they are not being fooled.
H:1.33	Not Paying Attention
1.110	How carefully people should watch their thoughts, their habits, and their feelings!
1.111	Most of the time, people are fooled because they neglect things.
1.112	They do not stop to examine themselves. They do not open up their hearts and minds as if they stood before God, and judge themselves by His perfectly holy will.
1.113	A man does not have to shoot himself to kill himself. He only has to stop eating properly, and he will soon die.
1.114	When an enemy is strong and on the attack, an army is sure to be beaten and captured unless it keeps a sharp lookout, with every soldier wide awake at his post.
1.115	People have noticed that there are more accidents in Switzerland in good weather than in stormy weather.
1.116	In good weather, people tend to set out on trips they would not try in worse conditions, and they are less careful about what they do.
1.117	In the same way, moral and spiritual disaster usually hits people when they are off guard and careless about temptation.
1.118	When life is going well, they become proud and depend on themselves. But hard times drive them to the living God for guidance and comfort.
1.119	Dr. Johnson once said that there is so much falsehood in the world more because people are careless about the truth than because they tell lies on purpose.
1.120	That is why we must keep watch all the time.
1.121	The Persians had a festival once a year when they killed all the snakes and poisonous creatures they could find. But then they let them multiply as fast and freely as ever until the festival came around again.
1.122	That was a foolish plan.
1.123	Sins are like snakes. They multiply quickly, and we need to watch for them all the time.
1.124	And we should keep watch on every side.
1.125	Many people have fallen at the very place where they thought they were safest.
1.126	Moses was so gentle and humble that people still talk about it.
1.127	Yet he lost the Promised Land. He let the people of Israel provoke him, and “he spoke carelessly with his lips.”
1.128	Peter was the most eager and daring of the disciples, bold and outspoken. Yet for a short time he sank into being a lying, cursing, sneaking coward who was afraid of a servant girl.
1.129	There is an old fable about a deer with only one eye who used to graze near the sea. To stay safe, she kept her blind eye toward the water, since she did not expect any danger from that side. With her good eye she watched the land.
1.130	Some men noticed this. They took a boat, came at her from the sea, and shot her.
1.131	With her last breath she said:
1.132	“Oh, what a cruel fate! I got my death wound from the side where I expected no harm, and I was safe on the side where I looked for the most danger.”
1.133	Let danger and need drive you closer to God.
1.134	He never dozes or sleeps. When He is keeping you, you will be safe.
1.135	Hold on tight to Him in prayer.
1.136	“Watch and pray.”
H:1.42	Christianity Is Not to Blame
1.137	Christianity is not to blame for the deceit found among people who claim to follow it.
1.138	Someone has used this example before: blaming Christianity would make as much sense as blaming the Cunard shipping company when a passenger jumps off one of their ships at sea and kills himself.
1.139	If that person had stayed on the ship, he would have been safe. And if the disciple had stayed true to what he believed, he would never have turned out to be a hypocrite.
1.140	Was anyone ever harsher in speaking against hypocrisy than Christ?
1.141	Do you want to know why, every now and then, the church is shocked when some leading church member or Sunday school leader is exposed?
1.142	It is not his Christianity that caused it. It is his lack of it.
1.143	Some secret sin has been eating away at the heart of the tree. Then, at a critical moment, the wind blows it down, and everyone sees how rotten it was.
H:1.44	Deception Cannot Last Forever
1.144	The deception cannot possibly last forever.
1.145	Lincoln used to say that you may be able to fool all the people some of the time, and some of the people all the time, but you will not be able to fool all the people all the time.
1.146	If the deception has not been found out sooner, death will uncover it. And the poor victim will stand, no longer fooled, before a God who cannot be mocked.
T:2	When You Sow, You Expect to Reap
2.1	“Look at the farmer. He waits for the precious crop to come up from the ground. He is very patient with it, until the early and late rains come.”—James 5:7.
2.2	Notice these four things about sowing and reaping. When someone sows, he expects to reap. He expects to reap the same kind of seed that he sows. He expects to reap more than he sowed. And not knowing what kind of seed it is makes no difference.
2.3	First: When someone sows, he expects to reap.
2.4	Imagine a farmer who kept sowing, spring after spring, but never harvested anything in the fall. You would say he belonged in a mental hospital.
2.5	No, he is always looking ahead to the time when he will reap the reward for his hard work.
2.6	He never expects the seed he has sown to be lost.
2.7	A young man spends a long time as an apprentice, learning some trade or profession. But he expects that someday he will reap the fruit of all those years of patient hard work.
2.8	Ask an engineer why he works so hard for five, six, or seven years trying to learn his profession.
2.9	He answers that he is looking forward to the reaping time, when he will make his fortune and his name.
2.10	The lawyer studies long and hard. But he too is looking forward to the time when he will have many clients and be paid back for all his work.
2.11	Many medical students have a hard time supporting themselves while they are in college.
2.12	As soon as they get their diplomas and become doctors, they expect the reaping time to come. That is what they have been working for.
2.13	Some crops ripen almost right away. But as a rule in the natural world, it takes time before the seed is fully grown.
2.14	Still, it is growing the whole time. First the little green shoot breaks through the soil. Then comes the stalk, then the head of grain, and then the head full of ripe grain.
2.15	The farmer is not disappointed when his crops do not all pop up overnight like mushrooms.
2.16	He waits patiently, knowing that the reaping time will come at the right season.
2.17	It is the same with the harvest of what we do.
2.18	Few people, if any, would give in to sin unless they expected to get some pleasure from it.
2.19	A drunkard does not drink just for the sake of drinking. He drinks because he hopes to enjoy himself right now.
2.20	A thief does not steal just for the sake of stealing. He steals to get something out of it.
2.21	And it is the same with a good person.
2.22	He does not make sacrifices just for the sake of sacrificing. He makes them because he hopes and expects that by doing so he will do good and help others.
2.23	All these things are ways of reaching a goal. There is always the hope of a harvest.
H:2.7	The Harvest Is Certain
2.24	The text tells us to look ahead to the certainty of reaping: “Whatever a person sows, that is what he will also reap.”
