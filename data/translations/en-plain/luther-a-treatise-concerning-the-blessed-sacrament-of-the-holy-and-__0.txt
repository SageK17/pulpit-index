0.1	1. The holy sacrament of the altar is also called the sacrament of the holy and true body of Christ. Like the sacrament of holy baptism, it has three parts. We need to know all three.
0.2	The first part is the sacrament itself, which is the sign. The second is what the sacrament means. The third is the faith that both of these call for. These three parts must be found in every sacrament.
0.3	The sacrament must be outward and visible, with some physical form. Its meaning must be inward and spiritual, inside a person's spirit. And faith must take hold of both and put them to use.
0.4	2. The sacrament, or outward sign, comes in the form of bread and wine, just as the sign of baptism is water. But the sign is not just the bread and wine themselves. It is the use of them, when we eat and drink. In the same way, the water of baptism is used when a person is dipped into it or has it poured over them.
0.5	The sacrament, or sign, has to be received, or at least longed for, if it is going to bring a blessing.
0.6	These days the people are not given both kinds, the bread and the wine, every day, as they were long ago. That is not necessary anyway. The priests take it every day in front of the people. It is enough for the people to long for it every day and to receive one of the two kinds at the proper time, as the Christian Church orders and offers.
0.7	3. Still, I think it would be good for the Church, meeting in a general council, to decide once again that everyone, not just the priests, should be given both kinds.
0.8	This is not because one kind is not enough. In fact, the simple longing of faith is enough, as St. Augustine says: "Why are you getting your stomach and teeth ready?
0.9	Just believe, and you have already received the sacrament." No, the reason is that it would be fitting and right to give the form, or sign, of the sacrament completely, not just part of it. I said the same about baptism: it would be better to dip people fully into the water than to pour it on them, so that the sign is whole and perfect.
0.10	This sacrament stands for the complete union and undivided fellowship of the saints, as we will see. Using only one part of the sacrament shows this poorly and in a way that does not fit.
0.11	Also, using the cup is not as dangerous as people think. After all, people seldom go to this sacrament. And Christ knew very well about every danger that would come. Even so, He chose to set up both kinds for all His Christians to use.
0.12	4. The meaning, or purpose, of this sacrament is the fellowship of all saints. That is where its common name comes from: synaxis or communio, which means "fellowship." And communicare means to take part in this fellowship, or, as we say, to go to the sacrament. This is because Christ and all saints are one spiritual body. It is like the people of a city, who form one community and one body. Each citizen is a member of the others and a member of the whole city.
0.13	So all the saints are members of Christ and of the Church. The Church is God's spiritual and eternal city. Anyone who is taken into this city is said to be welcomed into the community of saints. That person is made part of Christ's spiritual body and becomes a member of Him.
0.14	On the other hand, excommunicare means to put someone out of the community and cut a member off from this body. In our language we call that "putting someone under the ban." Yet there is a difference, as I will show in the next treatise, which is about the ban.
0.15	So receiving the bread and wine of this sacrament means nothing other than receiving a sure sign. It is a sign that you share in this fellowship and are joined into one body with Christ and all saints.
0.16	It is as if a citizen were given a sign, a document, or some other token. It would prove that he is a citizen of the city and a member of the community.
0.17	That is just what St. Paul says: "We are all one bread and one body, because we all share in one bread and one cup."
0.18	[1 Corinthians 10:17]
0.19	5. This fellowship works like this. All the spiritual riches of Christ and His saints are given to and shared with the person who receives this sacrament. And in return, all that person's sufferings and sins are shared with them. In this way love produces love and unites everyone.
0.20	To take our everyday picture further: it is like a city where every citizen shares everything with all the others. They share the city's name, honor, freedom, trade, customs, ways of doing things, help, support, protection, and so on. On the other hand, they also share every danger: fire and flood, enemies and death, losses, taxes, and so on.
0.21	Whoever wants a share in the common gains must also share in the losses. And he must always answer love with love.
0.22	Here we see that whoever harms one citizen harms the whole city and all its citizens. And whoever does good to one citizen earns favor and thanks from all the others.
0.23	It is the same with our physical body. St. Paul says so in 1 Corinthians 12, where he explains this sacrament in a spiritual way. The parts of the body care for each other. If one part suffers, all the parts suffer with it. If one part is honored, all the parts are glad with it.
0.24	[1 Corinthians 12:25–26]
0.25	We can see this plainly. If someone's foot hurts, even just the smallest toe, the eye looks at it right away. The fingers take hold of it, the face frowns, and the whole body bends down to it. Every part is concerned about this one small part. And when it is taken care of, all the other parts are glad.
0.26	Anyone who wants to understand this sacrament must think this picture through carefully. The Scriptures use it for the sake of people who have not had much schooling.
0.27	6. So in this sacrament God Himself, through the priest, gives a person a sure sign. It shows that, in the same way, he will be united with Christ and His saints and will share everything with them. Christ's sufferings and life will be his own, along with the lives and sufferings of all the saints. So whoever hurts him hurts Christ and all the saints. As God says through the prophet, "Whoever touches you touches the apple of My eye" [Zechariah 2:8]. On the other hand, whoever is kind to him is kind to Christ and all His saints. As He says, "What you have done for one of the least of My brothers, you have done for Me."
0.28	[Matthew 25:40] In return, he must be willing to share all the burdens and troubles of Christ and His saints, both their sorrow and their joy.
0.29	We will look more closely at these two sides of the fellowship.
0.30	7. Now, trouble attacks us in more than one way.
0.31	First, there is the sin that stays in our flesh even after baptism. It is the pull toward anger, hatred, pride, sexual impurity, and so on. It attacks us as long as we live.
0.32	Against this sin we need the help of the church family and of Christ, so that they can fight it together with us. But we also need Christ and His saints to pray for us before God. Then our sin will not be counted against us by God's strict judgment.
0.33	So God gives us this sacrament to give us strength and courage against these sins. It is as if He were saying: "Look, many kinds of sin are attacking you. Take this sign. With it I give you My promise that sin is attacking not only you, but also My Son Christ and all His saints in heaven and on earth.
0.34	So be brave and confident. You are not fighting alone. Great help and support are all around you."
0.35	King David also says about this bread: "Bread makes a person's heart strong" [Psalm 104:15]. And in other places the Scriptures describe this sacrament as something that gives strength.
0.36	So in Acts 9 we read that St. Paul was baptized, and when he had eaten some food, he grew strong again.
0.37	[Acts 9:19] Second, the evil spirit attacks us without stopping, with many sins and troubles.
0.38	Third, the world is full of wickedness. It tempts us and persecutes us, and it is completely bad.
0.39	Finally, our own guilty conscience attacks us. It brings up our past sins, and it fills us with fear of death and of the pains of hell.
0.40	All these troubles wear us out and make us weak, unless we look for strength in this fellowship and find it there.
0.41	8. Maybe someone is in despair. Maybe his sinful conscience troubles him, or he is terrified of death, or some other burden weighs on his heart, and he wants to be free of all of it. Then let him go joyfully to the sacrament of the altar. There he can lay down his grief in the middle of the church family and ask the whole company of the spiritual body for help. It is just like a citizen whose property has been damaged or ruined by his enemies. He brings his complaint to the town council and his fellow citizens and asks them for help.
0.42	That is why God gives us His endless grace and mercy in this sacrament. There we can lay down all our misery and trouble and place it on the church family, and above all on Christ. Then we can joyfully strengthen and comfort ourselves and say: "I am a sinner and I have fallen. This or that trouble has come upon me. Even so, I will go to the sacrament to receive a sign from God. It shows that Christ's righteousness, life, and sufferings are on my side. So are all the holy angels, all the blessed in heaven, and all godly people on earth.
0.43	If I die, I am not alone in death. If I suffer, they suffer with me.
0.44	I have shared all my trouble with Christ and the saints, because I have a sure sign that they love me."
0.45	You see, this is the good that comes from this sacrament. This is how we should use it. Then the heart cannot help being glad and comforted.
0.46	9. So when you have received this sacrament, or want to receive it, you must in turn share the troubles of the church family too, as was said.
0.47	But what are these troubles?
0.48	Christ in heaven, the angels, and all the saints have no troubles of their own, except when harm is done to the truth and to God's Word. In fact, as we said, every harm and every blessing that comes to any of the saints on earth touches them.
0.49	That is where your heart must reach out in love and devotion. You must learn that this sacrament is a sacrament of love. Love and service are given to you, and in return you must give love and service to Christ and to His people in need.
0.50	You must feel sorrow over all the dishonor done to Christ in His holy Word. You must feel sorrow over all the misery of Christians everywhere and all the unfair suffering of innocent people. The whole world is overflowing with these things. You must fight, work, and pray. And if you cannot do more, you must feel deep sympathy from your heart.
0.51	That is how you carry, in your turn, the troubles and hardships of Christ and His saints.
0.52	This is where Paul's saying fits:
0.53	"Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ."
0.54	[Galatians 6:2] You see, in this way you hold them all up, and they all hold you up in return. Everything is shared, both good and bad.
0.55	Then everything becomes easy, and the evil spirit cannot win against a community like that.
0.56	When Christ set up the sacrament, He said: "This is My body, which is given for you. This is My blood, which is poured out for you. Whenever you do this, remember Me."
0.57	[Luke 22:19–20] It is as if He were saying: "I am the Head. I will be the first to give Myself for you. I will make your suffering and trouble My own and carry it for you. Then you, in your turn, can do the same for Me and for one another. You can share everything in Me and with Me. Let this sacrament be a sure token of all this for you, so that you do not forget Me. Every day, remember and encourage one another by what I have done for you and am still doing. Then you will be made strong by it, and you will also be patient with one another."
0.58	10. This is also a reason, in fact the main reason, why we receive this sacrament many times, while baptism is given only once.
0.59	Baptism is the beginning of a new life and the door into it. Along the way, endless troubles attack us through sins and suffering, both our own and other people's.
0.60	As was said, the devil, the world, and our own flesh and conscience never stop chasing us and pressing us down.
0.61	So we need the strength, support, and help of Christ and His saints. These are promised to us in this sacrament, as if by a sure token. Through it we are made one with them and joined into one body with them. And all our suffering is laid down in the middle of the church family.
0.62	That is why this holy sacrament does little or no good for people who have no troubles or worries, or who do not feel their hardships.
0.63	It is given only to those who need strength and comfort. It is for people with fearful hearts and terrified consciences, who are attacked by sin or have even fallen into sin.
0.64	What could it do for people who are untroubled and feel falsely safe? They do not need it, and they do not want it.
0.65	As the Mother of God says, "He fills only the hungry, and He comforts those who are in distress." [Luke 1:53]
0.66	11. So Christ wanted His disciples to be truly worthy and well prepared for this sacrament. That is why He first made them sad. He told them about His going away and His death, and this troubled them deeply.
0.67	Then He frightened them badly when He said that one of them would betray Him.
0.68	[Matthew 25:21 and following] Now they were full of sorrow and worry, and they were troubled by the sorrow and sin of betrayal. That is when they were worthy, and He gave them His holy body to make them strong.
0.69	In this way He teaches us that this sacrament gives strength and comfort to those who are troubled and distressed by sin and evil. St. Augustine says the same: "This food wants only hungry souls. No one avoids it more than a soul that is already full and does not need it."
0.70	In the same way, the Jews had to eat the Passover with bitter herbs, standing up and in a hurry. This also shows that this sacrament calls for souls that are longing, needy, and sad.
0.71	Now suppose someone makes the troubles of Christ and of all Christians his own. Suppose he defends the truth, stands against wrongdoing, and helps carry the needs of innocent people and the sufferings of all Christians. He will find plenty of trouble and hardship. And that is on top of what his own evil nature, the world, the devil, and sin put on him every day.
0.72	And God wants and plans to set so many hunting dogs on us, to chase us and to give us bitter herbs everywhere. He does this so that we will long for this strength and delight in the holy sacrament. That is how we become worthy of it, which means that we long for it.
0.73	12. So He wants us to receive it often. Then we will remember Him and practice this fellowship by following His example.
0.74	If His example were no longer kept in front of us, the fellowship would soon be forgotten too.
0.75	Sadly, that is what we see today. Many masses are held, yet the Christian fellowship has completely died out. This fellowship should be preached, practiced, and kept in front of us by Christ's example. Now we hardly know what this sacrament is for or how it should be used. In fact, with our masses we often destroy this fellowship and twist everything.
0.76	This is the fault of the preachers. They do not preach the Gospel or the sacraments. Instead they preach made-up human stories about all the works people should do and how to live the right way.
0.77	But long ago this sacrament was used properly. People were taught to understand this fellowship so well that they even gathered food and goods in the church. There they shared them out among those in need, as St. Paul writes [1 Corinthians 11:21].
0.78	A trace of this still remains in the word "collect," which is still used in the mass. It means a general collection, like when a common fund is gathered to give to the poor.
0.79	That was the time when so many people became martyrs and saints.
0.80	There were fewer masses, but those masses brought much strength and blessing. Christians took care of each other, helped each other, felt for each other, and carried each other's burdens and troubles.
0.81	All of this has disappeared. What is left is just the many masses, and the many people who receive this sacrament without understanding or living out what it means at all.
0.82	13. Some people, of course, want to share the benefits but not the cost. They are glad to hear that in this sacrament the help, fellowship, and support of all the saints are promised and given to them. But they are afraid of the world. So they are not willing to do their part for this fellowship in return. They will not help the poor, put up with sins, care for the sick, suffer with those who suffer, or pray for others. They will not defend the truth or work for the reform of the Church and of all Christians, if it puts their life, property, or honor at risk.
0.83	They do not want to suffer disfavor, harm, shame, or death. Yet God wants them to be driven, for the sake of the truth and their neighbors, to long for the great grace and strength of this sacrament.
0.84	They are selfish people, and this sacrament does them no good.
0.85	It is just like a citizen who wants the community to help him, protect him, and give him freedom, but who will do nothing for it in return and will not serve it. We could not stand such a person.
0.86	No, if we want Christ and His saints to make our troubles their own, we must make other people's troubles our own. Then the fellowship will be complete, and the sacrament will be given its due.
0.87	The sacrament brings no blessing and has no meaning unless love grows every day. That love must change a person so much that he becomes one with all others.
0.88	14. To picture this fellowship, God chose signs for the sacrament that fit this purpose in every way. Their very form urges us and moves us toward this fellowship.
0.89	Think of how bread is made from many grains that are ground and mixed together. Out of many separate grains comes one loaf of bread. Each grain loses its own shape and body and takes on the shared body of the bread. In the same way, drops of wine lose their own form and become one wine. That is how it should be with us. And that is how it really is, if we use this sacrament the right way.
0.90	Christ, together with all saints, takes on our form out of love. He fights alongside us against sin, death, and every evil [Philippians 2:7]. This lights such a love in us that we take on His form. We rely on His righteousness, His life, and His blessed joy. Through this exchange of His blessings and our troubles, we become one loaf, one bread, one body, one drink, and we share everything.
0.91	Paul says this is a great sacrament [Ephesians 5:32]: that Christ and the Church are one flesh and bone [Ephesians 5:31].
0.92	In the same way, this same love should change us. We should make the weaknesses of all other Christians our own. We should take on their form and their needs, and give them all the good we are able to give, so that they can enjoy it [Judges 9:2].
0.93	That is real fellowship, and that is the true meaning of this sacrament.
0.94	In this way love changes us into one another and brings us into fellowship with one another. Without love, no such change can happen.
0.95	15. He chose this double form, bread and wine, instead of anything else, to show even more clearly the union and fellowship in this sacrament.
0.96	There is no union closer, deeper, or harder to break than the union between food and the person who eats it. The food goes into him, becomes part of his very nature, and becomes one with his being.
0.97	Other ways of joining things, with nails, glue, ropes, and so on, do not turn the joined things into one substance that cannot be divided.
0.98	In the sacrament we become united with Christ and are made one body with all the saints. So He cares about us and acts for us as if He were what we are. What matters to us matters to Him as much as it does to us, and even more. On the other hand, we also care about Him as if we were what He is. And in the end we really will be, when we are made like Him. As St. John says, "We know that when He appears, we will be like Him" [1 John 3:2]. That is how complete the fellowship of Christ and all the saints with us is.
0.99	Our sins attack Him, and His righteousness protects us. The union makes everything shared, until at last, on the last day, He destroys sin in us completely and makes us like Himself.
0.100	In the same way, by the same love, we are to be united with our neighbors, we in them and they in us.
0.101	16. On top of this, He did not set up this double form by itself alone. He gave His true, natural flesh in the bread and His natural, true blood in the wine. He did this to give us a truly perfect sacrament, or sign.
0.102	The bread is changed into His true, natural body, and the wine into His true, natural blood. Just as truly, we are drawn and changed into the spiritual body, that is, into the fellowship of Christ and all saints. Through this sacrament we are given all the virtues and mercies of Christ and His saints. It is like what was said above about a citizen who is taken into the city and made part of it, sharing in the protection and freedom of the whole community.
0.103	That is why He did not set up just one form, but two separate forms: His flesh under the bread and His blood under the wine. This shows that all of it belongs to us. His life and good works belong to us; His flesh stands for them, and He did them in His flesh. His suffering and martyrdom also belong to us; His blood stands for them, and in them He shed His blood. And because we are drawn into this fellowship, we may use and enjoy all of it.
0.104	17. All this makes it clear that this holy sacrament is nothing but a sign from God. In it, Christ and all saints are promised, granted, and given to us, with all their works, sufferings, merits, mercies, and riches. This is to comfort and strengthen everyone who is worried and sad, and who is hunted by the devil, sin, the world, the flesh, and every evil. And receiving the sacrament means nothing other than longing for all this and firmly believing that it will happen.
0.105	Next comes the third part of the sacrament: faith. Everything depends on it.
0.106	It is not enough to know what the sacrament is and what it means.
0.107	It is not enough for you to know that it is a fellowship, a gracious exchange or mixing of our sin and suffering with the righteousness of Christ and His saints. You must also long for it and firmly believe that you have received it.
0.108	This is where the devil and our own nature fight their hardest battle, so that faith will not stand firm at all.
0.109	Some people push their clever tricks and fine arguments so far that they ask where the bread goes when it is changed into Christ's flesh, and where the wine goes when it is changed into His blood. They also ask how the whole Christ, His flesh and His blood, can fit into such a small piece of bread and such a little wine.
0.110	Why does that matter?
0.111	It is enough to know that it is a sign from God, in which Christ's flesh and blood are truly present. How and where, we leave to Him.
0.112	18. Make sure you practice and strengthen your faith. Then, when you are sad or your sins trouble you, and you go to the sacrament or hear mass, you will go with a heartfelt longing for this sacrament and for what it means. Do not doubt that you have what the sacrament stands for. Be certain that Christ and all His saints come to you. They bring all their virtues, sufferings, and mercies, to live, work, suffer, and die with you. They come to be completely yours and to share everything with you.
0.113	If you practice and strengthen this faith, you will find out what a rich and joyful wedding supper and celebration your God has prepared for you on the altar.
0.114	Then you will understand what the great feast of King Ahasuerus means [Esther 1:5]. You will see what that wedding is for which God has killed His oxen and fattened calves, as it says in the Gospel [Matthew 22:2 and following]. And your heart will become truly free and confident, strong and brave, against every enemy.
0.115	Who would be afraid of any disaster if he is sure that Christ and all His saints are with him and share everything with him, bad or good?
0.116	That is why we read that Christ's disciples broke this bread and ate it with great gladness of heart.
0.117	This work is so great that our small, weak souls do not dare to long for it, let alone hope for it or expect it. So it is necessary and helpful to go to the sacrament often, or at least to practice and strengthen this faith in the daily mass. Everything depends on this faith, and the sacrament was set up for its sake.
0.118	If you doubt, you dishonor God more than in any other way. You treat Him as someone who does not keep His word, as a liar.
0.119	If you cannot believe, pray for faith, as was said earlier in the other treatise.
0.120	19. Also make sure you become a companion to every person. Never shut anyone out because of hatred or anger. This sacrament of fellowship, love, and unity cannot put up with quarreling and division.
0.121	You must let other people's weaknesses and needs weigh on your heart, as if they were your own. And you must offer them your strength, as if it were theirs. That is what Christ does for you in the sacrament.
0.122	That is what we mean when we say we are changed into one another through love. Many little pieces become one bread and one drink. Each gives up its own form and takes on a form that belongs to everyone.
0.123	That is why people who slander others, and who wickedly judge and look down on others, cannot help receiving death in the sacrament, as St. Paul writes [1 Corinthians 11:29].
0.124	They do not do for their neighbors what they want from Christ and what the sacrament points to. They wish their neighbors nothing good. They have no sympathy for them. They do not welcome them the way they want Christ to welcome them. Then they become so blind that they do not know what else to do in this sacrament, except to fear and honor Christ in the sacrament with their prayers and devotion.
0.125	Once they have done this, they think they have done all they need to do. But Christ gave His body for this purpose: that the meaning of the sacrament, which is fellowship and love for one another, would be put into practice. His own natural body is meant to count for less than His spiritual body, which is the fellowship of His saints.
0.126	What matters most to Him, especially in this sacrament, is this. He wants our faith in the fellowship with Him and His saints to be practiced rightly and to grow strong in us. And He wants us, in keeping with that faith, to practice our fellowship with one another rightly.
0.127	They do not see this purpose of Christ. In their devotion they say and hear mass every day, yet they stay the same every day. In fact, they get worse every day and do not even notice it.
0.128	So pay attention. It is more important for you to recognize the spiritual body of Christ than His natural body. And faith in the spiritual body is more important than faith in the natural body.
0.129	In this sacrament, the natural body without the spiritual body does us no good. A change has to happen, and it has to show itself through love.
0.130	20. Many people pay no attention to this change of love and faith. Instead they rely on the idea that the mass or the sacrament is, as they say, opus gratum opere operato. That means a work that pleases God all by itself, even if the people who do it do not please Him.
0.131	From this they conclude that it is still a good thing to have many masses, no matter how unworthily they are said. They think the harm falls on those who say or use them unworthily.
0.132	I let everyone have his own opinion, but I do not like such made-up stories.
0.133	If you want to talk like that, then every creature and every work pleases God by itself. As it is written, "God saw all His works, and they pleased Him."
0.134	[Genesis 1:31] But what good comes of it if someone misuses bread, wine, gold, and every good created thing, even though in themselves they please God?
0.135	None at all. The result is condemnation.
0.136	It is the same here. The more precious the sacrament is, the greater the harm that comes on the whole church family when it is misused.
0.137	It was not set up for its own sake, just to please God. It was set up for our sake, so that we would use it rightly, practice our faith through it, and through it become pleasing to God.
0.138	If it is only an opus operatum, it does nothing but harm. It must become an opus operantis.
0.139	Bread and wine do nothing but harm if they are not used, no matter how much they please God in themselves. In the same way, it is not enough for the sacrament to be prepared (that is, opus operatum). It must also be used in faith (that is, opus operantis).
0.140	We must be careful that such dangerous explanations do not turn our minds away from the sacrament's power and strength. Otherwise faith will die out completely, because people feel falsely safe once the sacrament has been carried out on the outside.
0.141	All this happens because in this sacrament they pay more attention to Christ's natural body than to the fellowship, the spiritual body.
0.142	Christ on the cross was also a finished work, and it pleased God very much. But to this day the Jews have found it a stumbling block, because they did not make it a work to be used in faith.
0.143	So make sure the sacrament is an opus operantis for you, that is, a work that is put to use. And make sure it pleases God, not because of what it is in itself, but because of your faith and the right way you use it.
0.144	God's Word also pleases God by itself. But it harms me when it does not also please God inside me.
0.145	In short, phrases like opus operatum and opus operantis are nothing but useless human words. They get in the way more than they help.
0.146	And who could list all the terrible misuses and false beliefs about this blessed sacrament that grow in number every day? Some of them seem so spiritual and holy that they could almost lead an angel astray.
0.147	To put it briefly, anyone who wants to understand these misuses only needs to keep in mind the use and faith of this sacrament described above. There must be a sad, hungry soul. It must long with all its heart for the love, help, and support of the whole communion of Christ and all saints. It must not doubt that by faith it receives them. And then, on the other side, it must make itself one with everyone.
0.148	Whoever does not arrange his hearing or reading of masses and his receiving of the sacrament in this way is making a mistake. He is not using this sacrament for his salvation.
0.149	This is also why the world is flooded with deadly diseases, wars, and other horrible disasters. With our many masses we only bring even more disfavor on ourselves.
0.150	21. Now we see how much this sacrament is needed by those who have to face death or other dangers to body and soul. They are not left alone in these dangers. They are made strong in the communion of Christ and all saints.
0.151	That is also why Christ set it up and gave it to His disciples when they were in the greatest need and danger.
0.152	Every day all of us are surrounded by all kinds of danger, and in the end we must die. So we should thank the God of all mercy humbly, with all our heart and all our strength, for giving us a gracious sign. Through this sign, if we hold on to it by faith,
0.153	He leads us and draws us through death and every danger to Himself, to Christ, and to all saints.
0.154	So it is also useful and necessary that the love and fellowship of Christ and all saints stay hidden, invisible, and spiritual. Only a bodily, visible, outward sign of it is given to us.
0.155	Suppose this love, fellowship, and help could be seen by everyone, like the everyday fellowship people have in this world. 0.156	Everything that belongs to time and to our senses must fall away. We must learn to live without it if we are going to come to God.
0.157	So the mass and this sacrament are a sign that trains us and gets us used to letting go of all the love, help, and comfort we can see. It teaches us to trust in Christ and in the unseen love, help, and comfort of His saints.
0.158	Death takes away everything we can see. It separates us from people and from the things of this passing world. So to face death, we need the help of invisible and eternal things. The sacrament and sign point us to these things. We hold on to them by faith until we also reach them and see them with our eyes.
0.159	So the sacrament is, for us, a shallow place to cross a river, a bridge, a door, a ship, and a stretcher to carry us. In it and by it we pass from this world into eternal life.
0.160	That is why everything depends on faith.
0.161	A person who does not believe is like someone who has to cross the sea but is so fearful that he does not trust the ship. So he has to stay where he is and is never saved, because he never gets on board and crosses over.
0.162	This happens because we depend on our senses and our faith has not been tested. So it pulls back from crossing the Jordan of death. And the devil cruelly helps this along.

0.167	And the waters above us stand up high, as if they were about to crash down on us. These are the horrors and ghostly sights of the other world that terrify us at the hour of death.
0.168	But if we pay no attention to them and keep going with firm faith, we will enter eternal life with dry feet and unharmed.
0.169	So we have two main sacraments in the church: baptism and the bread.
0.170	Baptism leads us into a new life on earth. The bread guides us through death into eternal life.
0.171	These two are pictured by the Red Sea and the Jordan, and by the two lands, one on the far side of the Jordan and one on this side.
0.172	That is why our Lord said at the Last Supper: "I will not drink this fruit of the vine again until the day I drink it new with you in My Father's kingdom."
0.173	[Matthew 26:29] This sacrament is meant and set up completely to make us strong against death and to let us enter eternal life.
0.174	Finally, the blessing of this sacrament is fellowship and love. They make us strong against death and every evil.
0.175	This fellowship has two sides. On one side, we share in Christ and all saints. On the other side, we let all Christians share in us, in whatever ways they and we are able. Through this love for one another there is one bread, one drink, one body, one community. That is the true union of Christian brothers.
