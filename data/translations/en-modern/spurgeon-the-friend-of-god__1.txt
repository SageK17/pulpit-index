0.300	IV. I will have finished when I have said a word or two on the last point, which is THE TITLE TO BE UTILIZED for practical purposes.
0.301	The practical purposes are just these.
0.302	First, here is a great encouragement to the people of God.
0.303	See what possibilities lie before you.
0.304	The other Sabbath morning I tried to say something about the future possibilities of saints, since he who was faithful with his pound was made ruler over ten cities.
0.305	It does not yet appear what we shall be: we do not have even the beginning of an idea of what we shall be in the next world if we are found faithful to our Master here, nor of what the glory will be that shall transfigure us in the day of the coming of Christ, and during the thousand years of his glorious reign on the earth.
0.306	But I want you now to notice the prize of your high calling in this life.
0.307	You may become the friends of God, and may be so plainly in league with him that men may call you the friends of God.
0.308	How few attain to this!
0.309	Do you know one such person?
0.310	Let your eye travel over all the Christian people you know, and tell me how many might be called the friends of God.
0.311	I know one such man; I will not mention his name.
0.312	I fear he may not be on earth much longer, for he is well advanced in years.
0.313	He is a man who has trusted God, walked with God, and been faithful to God, and has as a result been greatly honored by God to carry on a vast work of usefulness.
0.314	I wish I might grow to be like him; but I feel like a mere baby in his presence.
0.315	He is a rare man.
0.316	Why are there not more such men?
0.317	Is it because God’s arm is shortened?
0.318	No, but because our iniquities hide him from us.
0.319	We might be, and we ought to be, such men and women that those who know us at home and in business would discover us to be the friends of Jesus.
0.320	As a preacher I would like it to be said of me that I maintained the glory of my Lord, and defended the doctrines of his cross, and was the friend of the old gospel while others were running after novelties.
0.321	In some form or other we should aspire to this heavenly friendship.
0.322	See the possibility that lies within your reach—make it a reality at once.
0.323	Next, here is a solemn thought for those who would be friends of God.
0.324	A man’s friend must show himself friendly, and behave with tender care for his friend.
0.325	A little word from a friend will pain you much more than a fierce slander from an enemy.
0.326	Remember how the Savior said, “It was not an enemy; then I could have borne it: but it was you, a man my equal, my acquaintance.”
0.327	“The Lord your God is a jealous God”; and if he brings any of us so near to him as to be his friends, then his jealousy burns like coals of juniper that have a most vehement flame.
0.328	He will save you, brother, despite a thousand imperfections; but he will not call you his friend unless you are exceedingly careful to please him in all things.
0.329	Shall we draw back from the honor because of the responsibility?
0.330	No, we delight in the responsibility: we thirst to be well-pleasing to God.
0.331	Though our God is a consuming fire, we aspire to dwell in him.
0.332	To our new nature this fire is its element.
0.333	Even now we pray that it may refine us, and consume all our dross and tin.
0.334	We would gladly be baptized with the baptism of fire.
0.335	We wish nothing to be spared which ought to be consumed, or which can be consumed.
0.336	We accept friendship with God on his own terms.
0.337	I tremble while I speak.
0.338	We are willing to bear anything which will make us one with God.
0.339	The Spirit of God is the Spirit of judgment and the Spirit of burning: do you know what you ask when you pray to be filled with him?
0.340	I trust you will reply, “Whatever it may be, I desire to feel that heavenly influence which can make me forever the friend of God.”
