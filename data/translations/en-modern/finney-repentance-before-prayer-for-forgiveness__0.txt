H:0.9	II. Therefore Peter urged him to repent. What is repentance?
0.23	Repentance should always be distinguished from conviction of sin.
0.24	The latter is an involuntary state of mind, and of course has no virtue in it.
0.25	There may be as much of it as there is in hell itself, and yet no virtue.
0.26	There is awful conviction in hell, but no genuine repentance.
0.27	Neither does repentance consist merely in sorrow.
0.28	Indeed, this is properly and strictly no part of it, for sorrow does not belong to the thinking faculty of the mind—much less does it belong to the will and to the faculty of voluntary action.
0.29	Nor again is repentance the same thing as remorse.
0.30	Remorse, though it amounts to the keenest and most galling self-condemnation, does not necessarily imply repentance.
0.31	There may be no change of mind in it whatever.
0.32	Repentance is simply and precisely a change of mind.
0.33	The original term means a thinking again—a turning of the mind—as when someone finds himself going wrong and turns around to pursue the opposite course.
0.34	The term, when applied to evangelical repentance, means not merely a turning of the mind, but a change of the entire purposes of action, a change in the entire attitude of the will.
0.35	Repentance, therefore, is not remorse, is not sorrow, is not anything of this kind; but it is the mind turning away from selfish attitudes to benevolent ones—from being selfish to being truly benevolent.

0.44	No; selfishness is the perfection of dishonesty.

0.46	He has not a particle of genuine honesty, any more than Satan has!
0.47	What is honesty? Respecting all other men’s rights, and especially God’s rights, according to their true value.
0.48	But the sinner sets himself up as an honest man while he utterly denies God’s rights.
0.49	How false their claims are!
0.50	They respect their neighbor’s rights only for their own interests—only because in some way they expect to reap good for themselves from the respect they hypocritically show to their neighbor’s interests.
0.51	The fact is, men are completely deceived if they think themselves honest toward men while they are not truly honest toward God.
0.52	The heart of honesty is not in them.

0.54	No man has a right to say in practice that his rights and interests are greater than his neighbor’s.

0.62	5. Repentance implies confession of sin to God and to all those whom you have injured.
0.63	Let no man think himself truly penitent until he has made confession in all things where it is due.
0.64	Some sins are known only to God, and some are committed only against God.
0.65	These should be confessed to God.

0.67	This, however, is the exception and not the rule.

0.70	You have deceived many about your neighbor, to his hurt, and this wrong which you have done him you must undo, or you cannot suppose yourself to be truly penitent.
0.71	6. But besides confession, restitution is always implied in repentance.
