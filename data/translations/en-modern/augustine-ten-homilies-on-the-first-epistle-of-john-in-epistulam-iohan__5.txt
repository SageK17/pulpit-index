6.76	5. Here a question meets us: for it is not this or that man, or you or I, who come into question—for if I have asked anything of God and do not receive it, anyone may easily say of me, “He does not have charity”; and of any man whatever of this present time this may easily be said; and let anyone think what he will, a man of a man—not we, but rather those come into question, those men of whom it is known on all sides that they were saints when they wrote, and that they are now with God.
6.77	Where is the man who has charity, if Paul did not have it, who said, “Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians, our heart is enlarged; you are not restricted by us”; who said, “I will myself be spent for your souls”; and so great was the grace in him that it was made plain that he had charity.
6.78	And yet we find that he asked and did not receive.
6.79	What do we say, brethren?
6.80	It is a question: look attentively to God; this too is a great question.
6.81	Just as, where it was said of sin, “He who is born of God does not sin,” we found this sin to be the violation of charity, and that this was the thing strictly meant in that place, so too we now ask what it is that he means to say.
6.82	For if you look only at the words, it seems plain; if you take the examples into account, it is obscure.
6.83	Nothing can be plainer than the words here.
6.84	“And whatever we ask, we shall receive from Him, because we keep His commandments and do those things that are pleasing in His sight.”
6.85	“Whatever we ask,” he says, “we shall receive from Him.”
6.86	He has pressed us sorely.
6.87	In the other place also he would have pressed us hard, if he meant all sin; but there we found room to explain it in this way, that he meant it of a certain sin, not of all sin; yet of a sin which “whoever is born of God does not commit”; and we found that this same sin is none other than the violation of charity.
6.88	We also have a clear example from the Gospel, when the Lord says, “If I had not come, they would not have had sin.”
6.89	How? Were the Jews innocent when He came to them, because He speaks this way?
6.90	Then if He had not come, would they have had no sin?
6.91	Then did the Physician’s presence make a man sick, and not take away the fever?
6.92	What madman would even say this?
6.93	He came for no other purpose than to cure and heal the sick.
6.94	Therefore when He said, “If I had not come, they would not have had sin,” what would He have us understand, but a certain sin in particular?
6.95	For there was a sin which the Jews would not have had.
6.96	What sin? That they did not believe in Him, that when He had come they despised Him.
6.97	As then He said “sin” there, and it does not follow that we are to understand all sin, but a certain sin, so here also not all sin, lest it contradict that place where he says, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us”; but a certain sin in particular, that is, the violation of charity.
6.98	But in this place he has bound us more tightly: “If we ask,” he has said, “if our heart does not accuse us, and answers us in the sight of God that true love is in us”; “Whatever we ask, we shall receive from Him.”
6.99	6. Well now: I have already told you, my beloved brethren, let no one turn toward us.
6.100	For what are we? Or what are you?
6.101	What, but the Church of God, which is known to all?
6.102	And, if it pleases Him, we are in that Church; and those of us who abide in it by love, let us persevere there, if we would show the love we have.
6.103	But then the apostle Paul—what evil are we to think of him?
6.104	He not love the brethren!
6.105	He not have within himself the testimony of his conscience in the sight of God!
6.106	Paul not have within him that root of charity from which all good fruits proceeded!
6.107	What madman would say this?
6.108	Well then: where do we find that the apostle asked and did not receive?
6.109	He says himself: “Lest I should be lifted up beyond measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, an angel of Satan to strike me.
6.110	For this thing I pleaded with the Lord three times, that He would take it from me.
6.111	And He said to me, My grace is sufficient for you: for strength is made perfect in weakness.”
6.112	See, he was not heard in his prayer that the “angel of Satan” should be taken from him.
6.113	But why? Because it was not good for him.
6.114	He was heard, then, for salvation, when he was not heard according to his wish.
6.115	Know, my beloved, a great mystery, which we urge on your consideration on purpose that it may not slip away from you in your temptations.
6.116	The saints are in all things heard for salvation: they are always heard in what concerns their eternal salvation; it is this that they desire, because in regard to this their prayers are always heard.
6.117	7. But let us distinguish God’s different ways of hearing prayer.
6.118	For we find some not heard for their wish, but heard for salvation; and again we find some heard for their wish, but not heard for salvation.
6.119	Mark this difference; hold fast this example of a man not heard for his wish but heard for salvation.
6.120	Hear the apostle Paul; for God Himself showed him what the hearing of prayer for salvation is: “Sufficient for you,” He says, “is My grace; for strength is perfected in weakness.”
6.121	You have pleaded, have cried out, have cried out three times: the very first cry you raised I heard; I did not turn My ears away from you; I know what I should do: you would have it taken away, the healing thing by which you are burned; I know the infirmity by which you are burdened.
6.122	Well then: here is a man who was heard for salvation, while as to his will he was not heard.
6.123	Where do we find persons heard for their will, but not heard for salvation?
6.124	Do we find, do you think, some wicked, some ungodly man, heard by God for his will, but not heard for salvation?
6.125	If I put before you the instance of some man, perhaps you will say to me, “It is you who call him wicked, for he was righteous; had he not been righteous, his prayer would not have been heard by God.”
6.126	The instance I am about to bring forward is of one whose iniquity and ungodliness no one can doubt.
6.127	The devil himself: he asked for Job, and received.
6.128	Have you not also heard here concerning the devil, that “he who commits sin is of the devil”?
6.129	Not that the devil created him, but that the sinner imitates him.
6.130	Is it not said of him, “He did not stand in the truth”?
6.131	Is not he even “that old serpent,” who through the woman offered the first man the drink of poison?
6.132	Who even in the case of Job kept his wife for him, so that by her the husband might be, not comforted, but tempted?
6.133	The devil asked for a holy man, to tempt him, and he received; the apostle asked that the thorn in the flesh might be taken from him, and he did not receive.
6.134	But the apostle was heard more than the devil.
6.135	For the apostle was heard for salvation, though not for his wish; the devil was heard for his wish, but for damnation.
6.136	For Job was yielded up to him to be tempted in order that, by Job’s standing the test, the devil should be tormented.
6.137	But this, my brethren, we find not only in the books of the Old Testament, but also in the Gospel.
6.138	The demons begged the Lord, when He drove them out of the man, that they might be permitted to go into the swine.
6.139	Did the Lord not have power to tell them not to approach even those creatures?
6.140	For had it not been His will to permit this, they were not going to rebel against the King of heaven and earth.
6.141	But with a view to a certain mystery, with a certain deeper meaning, He let the demons go into the swine: to show that the devil has dominion in those who lead the life of swine.
6.142	The demons, then, were heard in their request; was the apostle not heard?
6.143	Or rather (what is truer) shall we say, The apostle was heard, the demons were not heard?
6.144	Their will was carried out; his welfare was perfected.
6.145	8. In keeping with this, we ought to understand that God, though He does not give to our will, does give for our salvation.
6.146	For suppose the thing you have asked for is to your hurt, and the Physician knows that it is to your hurt; what then?
6.147	It is not to be said that the physician does not listen to you when, perhaps, you ask for cold water, and if it is good for you, he gives it immediately; if it is not good, he does not give it.
6.148	Had he no ears for your request, or rather, did he listen for your welfare, even when he opposed your will?
6.149	Then let there be charity in you, my brethren; let it be in you, and then set your minds at rest: even when the thing you ask for is not given you, your prayer is granted—only you do not know it.
6.150	Many have been given over into their own hands, to their own hurt; of whom the apostle says, “God gave them up to the lusts of their own hearts.”
6.151	Some man has asked for a great sum of money; he has received it, to his hurt.
6.152	When he did not have it, he had little to fear; no sooner had he come to have it than he became a prey to the more powerful.
6.153	Was not that man’s request granted to his own hurt, who insisted on having that for which he would be hunted by the robber, whereas, being poor, no one hunted him?
6.154	Learn to plead with God in such a way that you commit it to the Physician to do what He knows best.
6.155	You confess the disease; let Him apply the means of healing.
6.156	Only hold fast to charity.
6.157	For He will cut, He will burn; what if you cry out, and are not spared for your crying under the cutting, under the burning and the tribulation? Yet He knows how far the rottenness reaches.
6.158	You would have Him take off His hands even now, and He considers only the depth of the sore; He knows how far to go.
6.159	He does not attend to you for your will, but He does attend to you for your healing.
6.160	Be sure, then, my brethren, that what the apostle says is true: “For we do not know what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself intercedes for us with groanings that cannot be uttered: for He intercedes for the saints.”
6.161	How is it said, “The Spirit itself intercedes for the saints,” except as meaning the charity which is worked in you by the Spirit?
6.162	For this is why the same apostle says: “The charity of God is poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us.”
6.163	It is charity that groans, it is charity that prays: against it He who gave it cannot shut His ears.
6.164	Set your minds at rest: let charity ask, and the ears of God are there.
6.165	Not what you wish is done, but what is advantageous is done.
6.166	Therefore, “whatever we ask,” he says, “we shall receive from Him.” I have already said: if you understand it to mean “for salvation,” there is no question; if not for salvation, there is a question, and a great one—a question that makes you an accuser of the apostle Paul.
6.167	“Whatever we ask, we receive from Him, because we keep His commandments and do these things that are pleasing in His sight”: within, where He sees.
6.168	9. And what are those commandments?
6.169	“This,” he says, “is His commandment, that we should believe on the name of His Son Jesus Christ, and love one another.”
6.170	You see that this is the commandment; you see that whoever does anything against this commandment commits the sin from which “everyone who is born of God” is free.
6.171	“As He gave us commandment”: that we love one another.
6.172	“And he who keeps His commandment”—you see that nothing else is commanded us than that we love one another—“and he who keeps His commandment shall abide in Him, and He in him.”
6.173	“And by this we know that He abides in us, by the Spirit whom He has given us.
6.174	Is it not plain that this is what the Holy Spirit works in a man, that there should be love and charity in him?
6.175	Is it not plain, as the apostle Paul says, that “the love of God is poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us”?
6.176	For [our apostle] was speaking of charity, and was saying that we ought to question our own heart in the sight of God.
6.177	“But if our heart does not think ill of us”: that is, if it confesses that whatever is done in us in any good work is done from the love of our brother.
6.178	And then besides, in speaking of the commandment, he says this: “This is His commandment, that we should believe on the name of His Son Jesus Christ, and love one another, as He gave us commandment.”
6.179	“And he who does His commandment abides in Him, and He in him.
6.180	By this we know that He abides in us, by the Spirit whom He has given us.”
6.181	If in truth you find that you have charity, you have the Spirit of God in order to understand: for it is a very necessary thing.
6.182	10. In the earliest times, “the Holy Spirit fell upon those who believed, and they spoke with tongues,” which they had not learned, “as the Spirit gave them utterance.”
6.183	These were signs suited to the time.
6.184	For there had to be that sign of the Holy Spirit in all tongues, to show that the Gospel of God was to run through all tongues over the whole earth.
6.185	That thing was done as a sign, and it passed away.
6.186	In the laying on of hands now, that persons may receive the Holy Spirit, do we expect them to speak with tongues?
6.187	Or when we laid our hands on these infants, did each one of you watch to see whether they would speak with tongues, and, when he saw that they did not speak with tongues, was any of you so wrong-minded as to say, These have not received the Holy Spirit; for had they received Him, they would speak with tongues, as was the case in those times?
6.188	If then the witness of the presence of the Holy Spirit is not now given through these miracles, by what is it given? By what does one come to know that he has received the Holy Spirit?
6.189	Let him question his own heart.
6.190	If he loves his brother, the Spirit of God dwells in him.
6.191	Let him look, let him test himself before the eyes of God; let him see whether there is in him the love of peace and unity, the love of the Church that is spread over the whole earth.
6.192	Let him not rest only in loving the brother whom he has before his eyes, for we have many brethren whom we do not see, and in the unity of the Spirit we are joined to them.
6.193	What wonder that they are not with us?
6.194	We are in one body; we have one Head, in heaven.
6.195	Brethren, our two eyes do not see each other; as one might say, they do not know each other.
6.196	But in the charity of the bodily frame do they not know each other?
6.197	For, to show you that in the charity which knits them together they do know each other: when both eyes are open, the right cannot rest on any object on which the left will not rest likewise.
6.198	Direct the glance of the right eye without the other, if you can.
6.199	Together they meet in one object, together they are directed to one object: their aim is one, their places are different.
6.200	If then all who love God with you have one aim with you, do not heed that in the body you are separated in place; you have alike fixed the eyesight of the heart on the light of truth.
6.201	Then if you would know that you have received the Spirit, question your heart, lest perhaps you have the sacrament and do not have the virtue of the sacrament.
6.202	Question your heart. If love of your brethren is there, set your mind at rest.
6.203	There cannot be love without the Spirit of God, since Paul cries, “The love of God is poured out in your hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us.”
6.204	11. “Beloved, do not believe every spirit.”
6.205	Because he had said, “By this we know that He abides in us, by the Spirit whom He has given us.”
6.206	But how this same Spirit is known, mark this: “Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are from God.”
6.207	And who is he who tests the spirits?
6.208	A hard matter he has put before us, my brethren!
6.209	It is well for us that he should tell us himself how we are to discern them.
6.210	He is about to tell us; do not fear; but first see—mark—see that here is expressed the very thing that vain heretics taunt us with.
6.211	Mark, see what he says: “Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are from God.”
6.212	The Holy Spirit is spoken of in the Gospel by the name of water, where the Lord “cried out and said, If anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink.
6.213	He who believes in Me, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”
6.214	But the evangelist has explained what He said this about; for he goes on to say, “But this He spoke of the Spirit, whom those who believed in Him would receive.”
6.215	Why did the Lord not baptize many?
6.216	But what does he say?
6.217	“For the Holy Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified.”
6.218	Then, seeing that those had baptism, and had not yet received the Holy Spirit, whom the Lord sent from heaven on the day of Pentecost, the glorifying of the Lord was first waited for, so that the Spirit might be given.
6.219	Even before He was glorified, and before He sent the Spirit, He still invited men to prepare themselves to receive the water of which He said, “Whoever thirsts, let him come and drink”; and, “He who believes in Me, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”
6.220	What does “Rivers of living water” mean?
6.221	What is that water?
6.222	Let no one ask me; ask the Gospel.
6.223	“But this,” it says, “He said of the Spirit, whom those who should believe in Him would receive.”
6.224	Consequently, the water of the sacrament is one thing; the water which signifies the Spirit of God is another.
6.225	The water of the sacrament is visible; the water of the Spirit is invisible.
6.226	That washes the body, and signifies what is done in the soul.
6.227	By this Spirit the soul itself is cleansed and fed.
6.228	This is the Spirit of God, which heretics and all who cut themselves off from the Church cannot have.
6.229	And whoever do not openly cut themselves off, but are cut off by iniquity, and, being within, whirl about as chaff and are not grain—these do not have this Spirit.
6.230	This Spirit is denoted by the Lord under the name of water; and we have heard from this epistle, “Do not believe every spirit”; and those words of Solomon bear witness, “Keep yourself far from strange water.”
6.231	What does “water” mean? Spirit.
6.232	Does water always signify spirit?
6.233	Not always: but in some places it signifies the Spirit, in some places it signifies baptism, in some places it signifies peoples, in some places it signifies counsel; thus you find it said in a certain place, “Counsel is a fountain of life to those who possess it.”
6.234	So then, in different places of the Scriptures, the term “water” signifies different things.
6.235	Now, however, you have heard the Holy Spirit spoken of by the term water, not by an interpretation of ours but by the witness of the Gospel, where it says, “But this He said of the Spirit, whom those who should believe in Him would receive.”
6.236	If then the Holy Spirit is signified by the name of water, and this epistle says to us, “Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God,” let us understand that it is of this that it is said, “Keep yourself far from strange water, and do not drink from a strange fountain.”
6.237	What does “Do not drink from a strange fountain” mean?
6.238	Do not believe a strange spirit.
6.239	12. There remains, then, the test by which it is to be proved to be the Spirit of God.
6.240	He has indeed set down a sign, and this, perhaps, a difficult one; let us see, however.
6.241	We are to return to that charity; it is that which teaches us, because it is the anointing.
6.242	However, what does he say here?
6.243	“Test the spirits, whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into this world.”
6.244	Here now are all the heretics and all the schismatics.
6.245	How then am I to test the spirit?
6.246	He goes on: “By this the Spirit of God is known.”
6.247	Wake up the ears of your heart.
6.248	We were at a loss; we were saying, Who knows? Who discerns?
6.249	Behold, he is about to tell the sign.
6.250	“By this the Spirit of God is known: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God; and every spirit that does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not of God; and this is the antichrist, of whom you have heard that he would come; and even now he is already in this world.”
6.251	Our ears, so to speak, are on the alert for discerning the spirits; and we have been told something by which we discern not a bit the more.
6.252	For what does he say?
6.253	“Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ came in the flesh is of God.”
6.254	Then is the spirit that is among the heretics of God, seeing they “confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh”?
6.255	Yes, here perhaps they rise up against us and say: You do not have the Spirit from God; but we confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh”; and the apostle here has said that those do not have the Spirit of God who do not confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.”
6.256	Ask the Arians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh”; ask the Eunomians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh”; ask the Macedonians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh”; put the question to the Cataphryges: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh”; put it to the Novatians: they confess “that Jesus Christ came in the flesh.”
6.257	Then do all these heresies have the Spirit of God?
6.258	Are they then no false prophets?
6.259	Is there then no deception there, no seduction there?
6.260	Assuredly they are antichrists; for “they went out from us, but they were not of us.”
6.261	13. What are we to do then?
6.262	By what are we to discern them?
6.263	Be very attentive; let us go together in heart, and knock.
6.264	Charity herself keeps watch; for it is none other than she who shall knock, and she also who shall open: soon you shall understand, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
6.265	You have already heard that it was said above, “Whoever denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, the same is an antichrist.”
6.266	There also we asked, Who denies? For neither do we deny, nor do they deny.
6.267	And we found that some deny in their deeds; and we brought testimony from the apostle, who says, “For they profess that they know God, but in their deeds they deny Him.”
6.268	So then let us now also make the inquiry in the deeds, not in the tongue.
6.269	What is the spirit that is not from God?
6.270	That “which denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.”
6.271	And what is the spirit that is from God?
6.272	That “which confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.”
6.273	Who is he who confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh?
6.274	Now, brethren, to the point! Let us look to the works, and not stop at the noise of the tongue.
6.275	Let us ask why Christ came in the flesh; so we shall get at the persons who deny that He has come in the flesh.
6.276	If you stop at tongues, why, you will hear many a heresy confessing that Christ has come in the flesh; but the truth convicts those men.
6.277	Why did Christ come in the flesh?
6.278	Was He not God?
6.279	Is it not written of Him, “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God”?
6.280	Was it not He who fed the angels? Is it not He who feeds the angels?
6.281	Did He not come here in such a way that He did not depart from there?
6.282	Did He not ascend in such a way that He did not forsake us?
6.283	Why then did He come in the flesh?
6.284	Because it was necessary for us to have the hope of resurrection shown to us.
6.285	He was God, and He came in flesh; for God could not die, flesh could die; He came then in the flesh, that He might die for us.
6.286	But how did He die for us?
6.287	“Greater charity than this no man has, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”
6.288	Charity therefore brought Him to the flesh.
6.289	Whoever therefore does not have charity denies that Christ has come in the flesh.
6.290	Here, then, you now question all heretics.
6.291	Did Christ come in the flesh?
6.292	“He did come; this I believe, this I confess.”
6.293	No, this you deny.
6.294	“How do I deny it?
6.295	You hear me say it!”
6.296	No, I convict you of denying it.
6.297	You say it with the voice, deny it with the heart; say it in words, deny it in deeds.
6.298	“How,” you say, “do I deny it in deeds?”
6.299	Because the end for which Christ came in the flesh was that He might die for us.
6.300	He died for us, because in this He taught much charity.
6.301	“Greater charity than this no man has, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”
6.302	You do not have charity, seeing that for your own honor you divide unity.
6.303	Therefore by this understand the spirit that is from God.
6.304	Give the earthen vessels a tap; put them to the test, whether perhaps they are cracked and give a dull sound; see whether they ring full and clear, see whether charity is there.
6.305	You take yourself away from the unity of the whole earth, you divide the Church by schisms, you tear apart the Body of Christ.
6.306	He came in the flesh to gather into one; you raise an outcry to scatter abroad.
6.307	This, then, is the Spirit of God, which says that Jesus has come in the flesh; which says it, not in tongue but in deeds; which says it, not by making a noise but by loving.
6.308	And that spirit is not of God which denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh; denies, here also, not in tongue but in life; not in words but in deeds.
6.309	It is plain therefore by what we may know the brethren.
6.310	Many who are inside are inside only in a sense; but no one is outside unless he is truly outside.
6.311	14. Indeed, and so that you may know that he has referred the matter to deeds, he says, “And every spirit, qui solvit Christum, which does away with Christ that He came in the flesh, is not of God.”
6.312	A doing away in deeds is meant.
6.313	What has he shown you?
6.314	“That denies”: in that he says, “does away with” (or, “unmakes”).
6.315	He came to gather into one; you come to unmake.
6.316	You would pull Christ’s members apart.
6.317	How can it be said that you do not deny that Christ has come in the flesh, you who tear apart the Church of God which He has gathered together?
6.318	Therefore you go against Christ; you are an antichrist.
6.319	Whether you are inside or outside, you are an antichrist: only, when you are inside, you are hidden; when you are outside, you are made manifest.
6.320	You unmake Jesus and deny that He came in the flesh; you are not of God.
6.321	Therefore He says in the Gospel: “Whoever shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach so, shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven.”
6.322	What is this breaking?
6.323	What is this teaching? A breaking in the deeds, and a teaching, as it were, in words.
6.324	“You who preach that men should not steal, do you steal?”
6.325	Therefore he who steals breaks or undoes the commandment in his deed, and as it were teaches so: “he shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven,” that is, in the Church of this present time.
6.326	Of him it is said, “What they say, do; but what they do, do not do.
6.327	But he who shall do and shall teach so shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.”
6.328	From this, that He has said here fecerit, “shall do,” while in contrast to this He said there solverit, meaning non fecerit, “shall not do, and shall teach so”—to break, then, is not to do—what does He teach us, but that we should examine men’s deeds, and not take their words on trust?
6.329	The obscurity of the matters compels us to speak at great length, chiefly so that what the Lord deigns to reveal may be brought within reach even of the brethren of slower understanding, because all were bought by the blood of Christ.
6.330	And I am afraid the epistle itself will not be finished during these days, as I promised; but as the Lord wills, it is better to reserve the remainder than to overload your hearts with too much food.
T:7	Homily VII (1 John 4:4–12)
7.1	“Now you are of God, little children, and have overcome him, because greater is He who is in you than he who is in this world.
7.2	They are of the world; therefore they speak of the world, and the world hears them.
7.3	We are of God; he who knows God hears us; he who is not of God does not hear us.
7.4	From this we know the spirit of truth, and [the spirit] of error.
7.5	Dearly beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves is born of God, and knows God.
7.6	He who does not love does not know God, for God is love.
7.7	In this the love of God was made manifest in us, that God sent His only-begotten Son into this world, that we may live through Him.
7.8	In this is love, not that we loved, but that He loved us, and sent His Son to be the Atoner for our sins.
7.9	Dearly beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another.
7.10	No man has seen God at any time.”
7.11	1. This world is to all the faithful who are seeking their own country what the desert was to the people of Israel.
7.12	They were indeed still wandering, and were seeking their own country; but with God as their guide they could not wander astray.
7.13	Their way was God’s command.
7.14	For though they went about for forty years, the journey itself is made up of very few stages, and is known to all.
7.15	They were delayed because they were in training, not because they were forsaken.
7.16	That, therefore, which God promises us is an indescribable sweetness and a good, as the Scripture says, and as you have often heard us repeat, which “eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man.”
7.17	But we are exercised by temporal labors, and trained by the temptations of this present life.
7.18	However, if you would not die of thirst in this wilderness, drink charity.
7.19	It is the fountain which God has been pleased to place here, so that we do not faint on the way; and we shall drink of it more abundantly when we have come to our own land.
7.20	The Gospel has just been read; now, to speak of the very words with which the reading ended, what else did you hear but concerning charity?
7.21	For we have made an agreement with our God in prayer, that if we would have Him forgive us our sins, we also should forgive the sins which may have been committed against us.
7.22	Now that which forgives is none other than charity.
7.23	Take charity away from the heart; hatred possesses it; it does not know how to forgive.
7.24	Let charity be there, and she forgives fearlessly, not being cramped.
7.25	And this whole epistle which we have undertaken to explain to you—see whether it commends anything else than this one thing, charity.
7.26	Nor need we fear that by much speaking of it, it may come to be hateful.
7.27	For what is there to love, if charity comes to be hateful?
7.28	It is by charity that other things come to be rightly loved; then how must it itself be loved!
7.29	Therefore let not that which ought never to depart from the heart depart from the tongue.
7.30	2. “Now,” he says, “you are of God, little children, and have overcome him”: whom but Antichrist?
7.31	For above he had said, “Whoever unmakes Jesus Christ and denies that He has come in the flesh is not of God.”
7.32	Now we explained, if you remember, that all those who violate charity deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.
7.33	For Jesus had no need to come except because of charity; as indeed the charity we are commending is that which the Lord Himself commends in the Gospel: “Greater love than this no man can have, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”
7.34	How was it possible for the Son of God to lay down His life for us without putting on flesh in which He might die?
7.35	Whoever therefore violates charity, let him say what he will with his tongue, his life denies that Christ has come in the flesh; and this is an antichrist, wherever he may be, by whatever way he has come in.
7.36	But what does the apostle say to those who are citizens of that country for which we sigh?
7.37	“You have overcome him.”
7.38	And by what have they overcome?
7.39	“Because greater is He who is in you than he who is in this world.”
7.40	Lest they should attribute the victory to their own strength, and be overcome by the arrogance of pride (for whomever the devil makes proud, he overcomes), wishing them to keep humility, what does he say?
7.41	“You have overcome him.”
7.42	Every man now, on hearing this saying, “You have overcome,” lifts up his head, lifts up his neck, wishes himself to be praised.
7.43	Do not exalt yourself; see who it is that has overcome in you.
7.44	Why have you overcome?
7.45	“Because greater is He who is in you than he who is in the world.”
7.46	Be humble, carry your Lord; be the beast for Him to sit on.
7.47	It is good for you that He should rule, and He guide.
7.48	For if you do not have Him to sit on you, you may lift up your neck, you may kick out your heels; but woe to you without a ruler, for this liberty sends you among the wild beasts to be devoured!
7.49	3. “These are of the world.”
7.50	Who? The antichrists. You have already heard who they are.
7.51	And if you are not such, you know them; but whoever is such does not know.
7.52	“These are of the world; therefore they speak of the world, and the world hears them.”
7.53	Who are they who “speak of the world”?
7.54	Mark who are against charity.
7.55	Behold, you have heard the Lord saying, “If you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you your trespasses.
7.56	But if you do not forgive men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.”
7.57	It is the sentence of Truth; or if it is not Truth that speaks, contradict it.
7.58	If you are a Christian and believe Christ, He has said, “I am the truth.”
7.59	This sentence is true, is firm.
7.60	Now hear men who “speak of the world.”
7.61	“And will you not avenge yourself?
7.62	And will you let him say that he has done this to you?
7.63	No: let him feel that he has to deal with a man.”
7.64	Every day such things are said. Those who say such things, “of the world they speak, and the world hears them.”
7.65	None say such things but those who love the world, and such things are heard by none but those who love the world.
7.66	And you have heard that to love the world and neglect charity is to deny that Jesus came in the flesh.
7.67	Or say whether the Lord Himself in the flesh did that? Whether, when struck, He willed to be avenged? Whether, hanging on the cross, He did not say, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do”?
7.68	But if He did not threaten, who had the power, why do you threaten, why are you puffed up with anger, you who are under the power of another?
7.69	He died because it was His will to die, yet He did not threaten; you do not know when you will die, and do you threaten?
