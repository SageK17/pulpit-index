1.110	How watchful men should be of their thoughts, their practices, their feelings!
1.111	The cause of deception is, for the most part, neglect.
1.112	Men do not stop to examine themselves, to lay their hearts and minds bare as in the sight of God, and to judge themselves by His most holy will.
1.113	A man need not shoot himself in order to commit suicide: he need only neglect the proper means of nourishment, and he will soon die.
1.114	Where an enemy is strong and aggressive, an army is doomed to sure defeat and capture unless a sharp lookout is kept, every man wide awake at his post of duty.
1.115	It has been noticed that there are more accidents in Switzerland in fine seasons than in stormy ones.
1.116	People are apt to undertake expeditions that they would not undertake under less favorable conditions, and they are less careful in their conduct.
1.117	And so it is that moral and spiritual disaster usually overtakes men when they are off their guard, careless against temptation.
1.118	They become proud and self-reliant in seasons of prosperity, whereas adversity drives them to the living God for guidance and comfort.
1.119	Dr. Johnson once said that it is more from carelessness about the truth than from intentional lying that there is so much falsehood in the world.
1.120	Hence the necessity of continual watchfulness.
1.121	The Persians had an annual festival when they killed all the serpents and venomous creatures they could find; but they allowed them to swarm as fast and freely as ever until the festival came round once more.
1.122	It was poor policy.
1.123	Sins, like serpents, breed quickly, and need to be constantly watched.
1.124	And we ought to watch on every side.
1.125	Many a man has fallen at the very point where he thought he was safest.
1.126	The meekness of Moses has become proverbial.
1.127	Yet he lost the Promised Land, because he allowed the children of Israel to provoke him, and “he spoke unadvisedly with his lips.”
1.128	Peter was the most zealous and defiant of the disciples, bold and outspoken; yet for a short time he sank into a lying, swearing, sneaking coward, afraid of a maid.
1.129	There is an old fable that a doe that had only one eye used to graze near the sea; and in order to be safe, she kept her blind eye toward the water, from which side she expected no danger, while with her good eye she watched the land.
1.130	Some men, noticing this, took a boat and came upon her from the sea and shot her.
1.131	With her dying breath, she said:
1.132	“Oh, hard fate! that I should receive my death wound from the side from which I expected no harm, and be safe on the side where I looked for the most danger.”
1.133	Let danger and need drive you closer to God.
1.134	He never slumbers or sleeps, and in His keeping you will be safe.
1.135	Take hold of Him in prayer.
1.136	“Watch and pray.”
H:1.42	Christianity Not Responsible
1.137	Christianity is not responsible for the deception that exists among its professing disciples.
1.138	The illustration has been used before that you might just as reasonably hold the Cunard company responsible for the suicide of a passenger who jumps overboard from one of their vessels at sea.
1.139	Had the person remained on the vessel, he would have been safe; and had the disciple remained true to his principles, he would never have turned out a hypocrite.
1.140	Was anybody ever more severe in denouncing hypocrisy than Christ?
1.141	Do you want to know the reason why, every now and then, the church is scandalized by the exposure of some leading church member or Sabbath school superintendent?
1.142	It is not his Christianity, but his lack of it.
1.143	Some secret sin has been eating at the heart of the tree, and in a critical moment it is blown down and its rottenness revealed.
H:1.44	The Deception Cannot Last Forever
1.144	It is impossible for the deception to last forever.
1.145	Lincoln had a saying that you may be able to deceive all the people some of the time, and some of the people all of the time, but you will not be able to deceive all the people all of the time.
1.146	Death will uncover the deception, if it has not been detected sooner; and the unfortunate victim will stand, undeceived, in the presence of a God who cannot be mocked.
T:2	When a Man Sows, He Expects to Reap
2.1	“Behold, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, and has long patience for it, until he receives the early and latter rain.”—James 5:7.
2.2	Notice these four things about sowing and reaping: a man expects to reap when he sows; he expects to reap the same kind of seed that he sows; he expects to reap more; and ignorance of the kind of seed makes no difference.
2.3	First: When a man sows, he expects to reap.
2.4	If a farmer went on sowing, spring after spring, and never reaping in the autumn, you would say he was a fit subject for the lunatic asylum.
2.5	No; he is always looking forward to the time when he will reap the reward of his toil.
2.6	He never expects that the seed he has sown will be lost.
2.7	A young man serves a long apprenticeship in some trade or profession; but he expects by and by to reap the fruit of all those years of patient industry.
2.8	Ask an engineer why he works so hard for five, six, or seven years in the effort to learn his profession.
2.9	He replies that he is looking forward to the reaping time, when his fortune and reputation will be made.
2.10	The lawyer studies long and hard; but he, too, looks forward to the time when his clients will be numerous and he will be repaid for his toil.
2.11	A great many medical students have a hard time trying to support themselves while they are at college.
2.12	As soon as they get their diploma and become doctors, they expect that the reaping time is coming; that is what they have been working for.
2.13	Some harvests ripen almost immediately, but as a rule we find it true in the natural world that there is a delay before the seed comes to maturity.
2.14	It is growing all the time, however; first the little green shoot breaking through the soil, then the blade, then the ear, then the full grain in the ear.
2.15	The farmer is not disappointed because all his crops do not spring up overnight like mushrooms.
2.16	He looks forward with patience, knowing that the reaping time will come in due season.
2.17	So it is with the harvest of our actions.
2.18	Few men, if any, would indulge in sin unless they expected pleasure from it.
2.19	A drunkard does not drink merely for the sake of drinking, but in the hope of present enjoyment.
2.20	A thief does not steal merely for the sake of stealing, but for the sake of gain.
2.21	And it is the same with the good man.
2.22	He does not make sacrifices merely for the sake of sacrifice, but because by them he hopes and expects to do good and help others.
2.23	All these things are means to ends: there is always the expectation of a harvest.
H:2.7	The Certainty of the Reaping
2.24	The text bids us look forward to the certainty of the reaping: “Whatever a man sows, that is what he will also reap.”
