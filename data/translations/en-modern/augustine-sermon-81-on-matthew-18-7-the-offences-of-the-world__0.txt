0.1	1. The divine readings, which we have just heard as they were being read, warn us to gather a store of virtues, to fortify a Christian heart against the offenses which were predicted to come, and this from the mercy of the Lord.
0.2	“For what is man,” says Scripture, “except that You are mindful of him?”
0.3	“Woe to the world because of offenses,” says the Lord; the Truth says so; He alarms and warns us, He would not have us be off our guard; for surely He would not make us despair.
0.4	Against this “woe,” that is, against this evil which is to be feared, and dreaded, and guarded against, Scripture counsels, and exhorts, and instructs us in that place where it is said, “Great peace have they who love Your law, and nothing is an offense to them.”
0.5	He has shown us an enemy to be guarded against, but He has not failed to show us also a wall of defense.
0.6	You were thinking, as you heard, “Woe to the world because of offenses,” where you might go beyond the world, so that you might not be exposed to offenses.
0.7	Therefore, to avoid offenses, where will you go beyond the world, unless you flee to Him who made the world?
0.8	And how shall we be able to flee to Him who made the world, unless we give ear to His law, which is preached everywhere?
0.9	And to give ear to it is but a small matter, unless we love it.
0.10	For divine Scripture, in making you secure against offenses, does not say, “Great peace have they who hear Your law.”
0.11	For it is not the hearers of the law who are just before God.
0.12	But because “the doers of the law shall be justified,” and “faith works by love,” it says, “Great peace have they who love Your law, and nothing is an offense to them.”
0.13	With this sentiment the passage which we have sung in its turn also agrees: “But the meek shall inherit the earth, and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace.”
0.14	Because “great peace have they who love Your law.”
0.15	For these “meek” ones are those who “love the law of God.”
0.16	For, “Blessed is the man whom You chasten, O Lord, and teach out of Your law, that You may give him rest from the days of adversity, until the pit is dug for the sinner.”
0.17	How different those words of Scripture seem, yet they so flow and meet together into one meaning that, whatever you can hear out of that most rich fountain, so that you rest in it and are in loving harmony with the truth, you will at once be filled with peace, glowing with love, and fortified against offenses.
0.18	2. It is our part, then, to see, or seek, or learn how we must be “meek”; and we are guided by what I have just brought forward out of the Scriptures to find what we are seeking.
0.19	Be attentive, then, Beloved, for a little while; it is a weighty matter that is at hand, that we may be meek; a necessary thing in the adversities of life.
0.20	But it is not the adverse circumstances of this life that are called offenses; but note what “offenses” are.
0.21	A man, for instance, under some hard necessity, is weighed down by a pressure of trouble.
0.22	That he is weighed down by a pressure of trouble is no offense.
0.23	By such pressure even the Martyrs were pressed, but not oppressed.
0.24	Beware of an offense, but of a pressure of trouble not so much.
0.25	The latter presses you; an offense oppresses you.
0.26	What, then, is the difference between the two?
0.27	In the pressure of trouble you made ready to maintain patience, to hold fast to constancy, not to abandon faith, not to consent to sin.
0.28	If you maintain this, or shall have maintained it, the trouble that presses you shall not be your fall; but that pressure of trouble shall serve the same end as in the oil press: not to destroy the olive, but to extract the oil.
0.29	In a word, if in this trouble that presses you, you give praise to God, how useful the press will be to you, by which such oil is pressed out!
0.30	Under such a press the Apostles sat in chains, and in that press they sang a hymn to God.
0.31	What precious oil was this that was pressed and forced out!
0.32	Beneath a heavy press Job sat on the dunghill, without resources, without help, without substance, without children; full, but of worms only, as far, that is, as concerned the outward man; but because he too was full of God within, he praised God, and that press was no “offense” to him.
0.33	Where, then, was the “offense”?
0.34	When his wife came to him and said, “Speak a word against God, and die.”
0.35	When all had been taken from him by the devil, an Eve was kept back for the tried sufferer, not to console but to tempt her husband.
0.36	See, then, where the offense was.
0.37	She exaggerated his miseries, and her own miseries too along with his, and began to persuade him to blaspheme.
0.38	But he who was “meek,” because “God had taught him out of His law, and given him rest from the days of adversity,” had “great peace” in his heart, as one “loving the law of God, and nothing was an offense to him.”
0.39	She was an offense, but not to him.
0.40	In a word, behold the meek man, behold one taught in the law of God, the eternal law of God, I mean.
0.41	For that law on tablets was not yet given to the Jews in the time of Job, but in the hearts of the godly there still remained the eternal law, from which the law that was given to the people was copied.
0.42	Because, then, by the law of God he had “rest given him from the days of adversity,” and “had great peace as loving the law of God,” see how “meek” he is, and what he answers.
0.43	Learn from this what I propose to inquire into: who the meek are.
0.44	“You speak,” he says, “as one of the foolish women speaks.
0.45	If we have received good from the hand of the Lord, shall we not bear the evil?”
0.46	3. We have heard by an example who the meek are; let us, if we can, define them in words.
0.47	The meek are those to whom, in all their good deeds, in all the things they do well, nothing is pleasing but God; to whom, in all the evils they suffer, God is not displeasing.
0.48	Now, Brethren, pay attention to this rule, to this pattern; let us stretch ourselves out toward it, let us seek to grow, that we may fill it.
0.49	For what does it profit that we plant and water, unless God gives the increase?
0.50	“For neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase.”
0.51	Give ear, whoever you are who would be “meek,” who would have “rest from the days of adversity, who loves the law of God,” that there may be “no offense to you,” and that you may “have great peace,” that you may “possess the earth, and delight in the multitude of peace”; give ear, whoever you are who would be “meek.”
0.52	Whatever good you do, do not be pleased with yourself.
0.53	“For God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”
0.54	So then, whatever good you do, let nothing but God be pleasing to you; whatever evil you suffer, let not God be displeasing to you.
0.55	What more do you need?
0.56	Do this, and you shall live.
0.57	The days of adversity shall not overwhelm you; you shall escape what is said, “Woe to the world because of offenses.”
0.58	For to what world is there woe because of offenses, but to that of which it is said, “And the world did not know Him”?
0.59	Not to that world of which it is said, “God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself.”
0.60	There is an evil world, and there is a good world; the evil world is all the evil men in this world, and the good world is all the good in this world.
0.61	As we frequently observe with a field.
0.62	This field is full: of what?
0.63	Of wheat. Yet we also say, and say truly too, This field is full of chaff.
0.64	So with a tree: it is full of fruit.
0.65	Another says it is full of leaves.
0.66	And both he who says it is full of fruit speaks truly, and he who says it is full of leaves speaks truly.
0.67	Neither has the full display of leaves taken away the room for the fruit, nor has the full display of fruit driven off the abundance of leaves.
0.68	It is full of both; but the one the wind searches out, the other the farmer gathers in.
0.69	So therefore, when you hear, “Woe to the world because of offenses,” do not be afraid; “love the law of God, and nothing shall be an offense to you.”
0.70	4. But your wife comes to you advising you to some evil thing.
0.71	You love her as a wife should be loved; she is one of your members.
0.72	“But if your eye offends you, if your hand offends you, if your foot offends you,” you have just heard the Gospel, “cut them off, and cast them from you.”
0.73	Whoever he is that is dear to you, whoever he is that is held in high esteem by you, let him be held in high esteem by you only so long, and be your beloved member only so long, as he does not begin to offend you, that is, to advise you to any evil.
0.74	Hear now that this is the meaning of “offense.”
0.75	I have brought forward the example of Job and his wife; but there the word “offense” did not occur.
0.76	Hear the Gospel: when the Lord prophesied of His Passion, Peter began to persuade Him not to suffer.
0.77	“Get behind Me, Satan, you are an offense to Me.”
0.78	Here undoubtedly the Lord, who has given you an example of life, has taught you both what an “offense” is and how an offense is to be avoided.
0.79	Him to whom He had a little while before said, “Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah,” He had shown to be His member.
0.80	But when he begins to be an offense, He cuts off the member; only He restored the member, and put it back into its place again.
0.81	He, then, will be an “offense” to you who begins to persuade you to any evil thing.
0.82	And here, Beloved, take heed; this happens for the most part not through any evil will, but through a mistaken good will.
0.83	Your friend who loves you and is loved by you in return, your father, your brother, your child, your wife, sees you in an evil plight, and would have you do what is evil.
0.84	What do I mean by “sees you in an evil plight”?
0.85	Sees you in some pressure of trouble.
0.86	Perhaps you are suffering this pressure for righteousness’ sake; you are suffering it because you will not give false witness.
0.87	I would speak merely by way of illustration.
0.88	Examples abound; for “woe to the world because of offenses.”
0.89	See, for instance, some powerful person, to cover up his robbery and plunder, asks of you the service of a false witness.
0.90	You refuse: refuse the false oath, lest you should deny Him who is true.
0.91	Not to dwell long on this: he is angry, he is powerful, he oppresses you; a friend comes who would not have you in this pressure of trouble, in this evil plight: “I beg you, do what you are told; what great matter is it?”
0.92	And then perhaps, as Satan did with the Lord: “It is written of You, He shall give His Angels charge concerning You, so that You do not dash Your foot against a stone.”
0.93	Perhaps too this friend of yours, because he sees that you are a Christian, wishes to persuade you out of the Law to do what he thinks you ought to do.
0.94	“Do what the other tells you.”
0.95	“What? Do what the other wishes.”
0.96	“But it is a lie, it is false.”
0.97	“Well, have you not read, ‘All men are liars’?”
0.98	Now he is an “offense.”
0.99	He is a friend; what will you do?
0.100	He is an eye, he is a hand: “Cut it off, and cast it from you.”
0.101	What is “cut it off, and cast it from you”?
0.102	Do not consent to him.
0.103	For the members in our body make up a unity by consent; by consent they live, by consent they are joined together one with another.
0.104	Where there is dissent, there is disease, or a sore.
0.105	He is, then, one of your members; you will love him.
0.106	But he is an offense to you: “Cut him off, and cast him from you.”
0.107	Do not consent to him; drive him away from your ears; it may be he will return corrected.
0.108	5. And how will you do this that I say, “Cut him off, and cast him from you,” and so, it may be, correct him? Answer me: how are you going to do it?
0.109	He wished to persuade you out of the Law to tell a lie.
0.110	For he said, “Speak.”
0.111	And perhaps he did not dare to say, “Speak a lie”; but rather, “Speak what the other wishes.”
0.112	You say, “But it is a lie.”
0.113	And he, to excuse it, says, “All men are liars.”
0.114	Then you, my brother, say against this, “The mouth that lies slays the soul.”
0.115	Note, it is no light thing you have heard: “The mouth that lies slays the soul.”
0.116	What can that powerful enemy who oppresses me do to me, that you pity me and my condition, and would not have me be in this evil plight, while you would have me be evil?
0.117	What can that powerful man do to me, and what can he oppress?
0.118	The flesh. He can oppress your body, you will say: I grant he may oppress it even to destruction.
0.119	Still, how much more mildly he deals with me than I would deal with myself if I were to lie!
0.120	He kills my flesh; I kill my soul.
0.121	He, in his power and anger, slays the body; “the mouth that lies slays the soul.”
0.122	He slays the body; and die it must, even if it were not slain; but the soul which iniquity does not slay, the truth receives forever.
0.123	Preserve, then, what you can preserve; and let that perish which must perish sometime or other.
0.124	You have given an answer, then, but you have not resolved the “All men are liars.”
0.125	Answer him on this too, so that he may not imagine he has said anything to persuade you to lie by bringing a testimony out of the Law, thus urging you out of the Law against the Law.
0.126	For it is written in the Law, “You shall not bear false witness”; and it is written in the Law, “All men are liars.”
0.127	Go back, then, to what I suggested just now, when I defined in words, as best I could, the “meek” man.
0.128	He is “meek” to whom, in all things that he does well, nothing but God is pleasing, and in all the evils which he suffers, God is not displeasing.
0.129	Answer him, then, who says, Lie, for it is written, “All men are liars”: I will not lie, for it is written, “The mouth that lies slays the soul.”
0.130	I will not lie, because it is written, “You shall destroy those who speak lies.”
0.131	I will not lie, because it is written, “You shall not bear false witness.”
0.132	Though he whom I displease by the truth harass my body with oppressions, I will give ear to my Lord: “Do not fear those who kill the body.”
0.133	6. “How, then, are all men liars?
0.134	What! You are not a man, I suppose?”
0.135	Answer quickly and truly.
0.136	“And oh, that I may not be a man, so that I may not be a liar.”
0.137	For see: “God looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any who understood and sought after God.
0.138	They have all gone out of the way, they have all together become unprofitable; there is none who does good, no, not even one.”
0.139	Why? Because they wished to be sons of men.
0.140	But in order that He might deliver them from these iniquities, and cure, heal, and change the sons of men, “He gave them power to become the sons of God.”
0.141	What marvel, then! You were men, if we were the sons of men; you were all men, and were liars, for “All men are liars.”
0.142	The grace of God came to you, and “gave you power to become the sons of God.”
0.143	Hear the voice of My Father saying, “I have said, You are gods, and you are all children of the Most High.”
0.144	Since, then, they are men, and the sons of men, if they are not the children of the Most High, they are liars, for “all men are liars.”
0.145	If they are the sons of God, if they have been redeemed by the Savior’s grace, if purchased with His precious Blood, if born again of water and of the Spirit, if predestined to the inheritance of heaven, then indeed they are children of God.
0.146	And so, by this, they are gods.
0.147	What, then, would a lie have to do with you?
0.148	For Adam was a mere man; Christ, man and God; God, the Creator of all creation.
0.149	Adam a mere man; the Man Christ, the Mediator with God, the Only Son of the Father, the God-man.
0.150	Look, you, O man, are far from God, and God is far above man; between them the God-man placed Himself.
0.151	Acknowledge Christ, and through Him as Man ascend up to God.
0.152	7. Being now, then, reformed, and, if my words have been so blessed, “meek,” let us “hold fast our profession without wavering.”
0.153	Let us love the law of God, that we may escape what is written, “Woe to the world because of offenses.”
0.154	Now I would say a few words about “offenses,” of which the world is full, and how it is that offenses thicken and pressing troubles abound.
0.155	The world is laid waste; the winepress is trodden.
0.156	Ah, Christians, heavenly shoot, you strangers on the earth, who seek a city in heaven, who long to be joined with the holy Angels; understand that you have come here on this condition only, that you should soon depart.
0.157	You are passing on through the world, striving to reach Him who created it.
0.158	Let not the lovers of the world, who wish to remain in the world and yet, whether they will or not, are compelled to move from it—let them not disturb you, let them not deceive or seduce you.
0.159	These pressing troubles are not offenses.
0.160	Be righteous, and they will be only exercises.
0.161	Tribulation comes; it will be as you choose it, either an exercise or a condemnation.
0.162	Such as it finds you to be, such will it be.
0.163	Tribulation is a fire; does it find you gold? It takes away the filth. Does it find you chaff? It turns it into ashes.
0.164	The pressing troubles, then, which abound are not “offenses.”
0.165	But what are “offenses”?
0.166	Those expressions, those words in which we are addressed like this:
0.167	“See what Christian times bring about”; look, these are the true offenses.
0.168	For this is said to you to this end: that, if you love the world, you may blaspheme Christ.
0.169	And the one who says this to you is your friend and counselor; and so he is “your eye.”
0.170	The one who says this to you is he who serves you and shares your labors; and so he is “your hand.”
0.171	The one who says this to you is perhaps he who supports you, who lifts you up from a low earthly state; and so he is “your foot.”
0.172	Cast them all aside, cut them off, throw them all away from you; do not consent to them.
0.173	Answer such men as he answered who was advised to give false witness.
0.174	So you answer too; say to the man who says to you, “See, it is in Christian times that there are such pressing troubles, that the whole world is laid waste”; answer him, “And Christ foretold this to me before it came to pass.”
0.175	8. For why are you disturbed?
0.176	Your heart is disturbed by the pressing troubles of the world, as that ship was in which Christ was asleep.
0.177	Look, what is the reason, stout-hearted man, that your heart is disturbed?
0.178	That ship in which Christ is asleep is the heart in which faith is asleep.
0.179	For what new thing, what new thing, I ask, is told you, Christian?
0.180	“In Christian times the world is laid waste, the world is failing.”
0.181	Did not your Lord tell you the world shall be laid waste?
0.182	Did not your Lord tell you the world shall fail?
0.183	Why, when the promise was made, did you believe, and are disturbed now, when it is being fulfilled?
0.184	So then, the storm beats furiously against your heart; beware of shipwreck; wake up Christ.
0.185	The Apostle says, “that Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith.”
0.186	Christ dwells in you by faith.
0.187	Present faith is Christ present; waking faith is Christ awake; slumbering faith is Christ asleep.
0.188	Arise and stir yourself; say, “Lord, we perish.”
0.189	See what the Heathen say to us; and, what is worse, what evil Christians say!
0.190	Awake, O Lord, we perish.
0.191	Let your faith awake, and Christ begins to speak to you: “‘Why are you troubled?’
0.192	I told you beforehand of all these things.
0.193	I foretold them, so that when evils came, you might hope for good things, so that you might not faint in the evil.”
0.194	Do you wonder that the world is failing?
0.195	Wonder that the world has grown old.
0.196	It is like a man who is born, and grows up, and grows old.
0.197	There are many complaints in old age: the cough, the phlegm, the weakness of the eyes, fretfulness, and weariness.
0.198	So then, as when a man is old he is full of complaints, so the world is old and is full of troubles.
0.199	Is it a little thing that God has done for you, in that in the world’s old age He has sent Christ to you, that He may renew you then, when all is failing?
0.200	Do you not know that He signified this in the seed of Abraham?
0.201	“The seed of Abraham,” says the Apostle, “which is Christ.
0.202	He does not say, And to seeds, as of many; but as of One, And to your seed, which is Christ.”
0.203	Therefore a son was born to Abraham in his old age, because Christ was to come in the old age of this world.
0.204	He came when all things were growing old, and made them new.
0.205	As a made, created, perishing thing, the world was now declining to its fall.
0.206	It could not but abound in troubles; He came both to console you in the midst of present troubles and to promise you everlasting rest.
0.207	Do not choose, then, to cling to this aged world and to be unwilling to grow young in Christ, who tells you, “The world is perishing, the world is growing old, the world is failing; it is distressed by the heavy breathing of old age.
0.208	But do not fear: ‘Your youth shall be renewed like the eagle’s.’”
0.209	9. See, they say, it is in Christian times that Rome perishes.
0.210	Perhaps Rome is not perishing; perhaps she is only scourged, not utterly destroyed; perhaps she is chastened, not brought to nothing.
0.211	It may be so; Rome will not perish if the Romans do not perish.
0.212	And perish they will not if they praise God; perish they will if they blaspheme Him.
0.213	For what is Rome, but the Romans?
0.214	For the question is not of her wood and stones, of her lofty free-standing palaces, and all her spacious walls.
0.215	All this was made only on this condition: that it should fall some day.
0.216	When man built it, he laid stone on stone; and when man destroyed it, he removed stone from stone.
0.217	Man made it; man destroyed it.
0.218	Is any injury done to Rome because it is said, “She is falling”?
0.219	No, not to Rome, but to her builder perhaps.
0.220	Do we, then, do its builder any injury because we say that Rome is falling, which Romulus built?
0.221	This world itself, which God built, will be burned with fire.
0.222	But neither does what man has made fall to ruin except when God wills it, nor what God has made, except when He wills.
0.223	For if the work of man does not fall without God’s will, how can God’s work fall by the will of man?
0.224	Yet God made the world that was one day to fall for you; and therefore He made you as one who was one day to die.

0.226	And yet perhaps the city’s end has not come now; yet some time or other come it will.
0.227	But why does Rome perish amid the sacrifices of Christians?
0.228	Why was her mother Troy burned amid the sacrifices of Heathens?
0.229	The gods in whom the Romans have placed all their hope, yes, the Roman gods in whom the Heathen Romans placed their hope, moved from the flames of Troy to found Rome.
0.230	These very gods of Rome were originally the gods of Troy.
0.231	Troy was burned, and Aeneas took the fugitive gods; or rather, himself a fugitive, he carried away these senseless gods.
0.232	For they could be carried by the fugitive; but they could not flee away themselves.
0.233	And coming with these gods into Italy, with these false gods, he founded Rome.

0.235	An author of theirs well known to all speaks thus: “As I have received the account, the Trojans, who under the leadership of Aeneas were wandering about as fugitives without any settled home, originally built and inhabited Rome.”
0.236	So they had their gods with them; they built Rome in Latium, and there they placed the gods to be worshiped which had before been worshiped in Troy.
0.237	Juno is brought in by their poet, incensed against Aeneas and the fugitive Trojans, saying,
0.238	“A race of wandering slaves, abhorred by me, / With prosperous passage cuts the Tuscan sea; / To fruitful Italy their course they steer, / And for their vanquished gods design new temples there.”
0.239	Now when these vanquished gods were carried into Italy, was it as a protecting deity, or as an omen of their future fall?
0.240	“Love,” therefore, “the law of God, and nothing shall be an offense to you.”
0.241	We pray you, we beg you, we exhort you: be meek, sympathize with the suffering, bear with the weak; and on this occasion of the gathering of so many strangers, and needy, and suffering people, let your hospitality and your good works abound.
0.242	Only let Christians do what Christ commands, and then the Heathen will blaspheme only to their own hurt.
