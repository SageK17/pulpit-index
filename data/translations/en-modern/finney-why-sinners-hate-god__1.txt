0.246	6th. Again, the hatred of sinners is cruel.
0.247	It is as God says, “returning hatred for his love.”
0.248	He is love, and this is the reason, and the only reason, why they hate him.
0.249	Notice, it is not because they overlook the fact that he is infinitely benevolent.
0.250	It is not merely that, in the face of this fact, they hate him for other reasons; it is because of this fact.
0.251	It is literally and absolutely returning hatred for his love.
0.252	He is opposed to their injuring each other.
0.253	He desires their happiness and is infinitely opposed to their making themselves miserable.
0.254	He is infinitely more opposed to their doing anything that will prove harmful to themselves than any earthly parent ever was to that course of conduct in his beloved child which he foresaw would ruin him.
0.255	His heart yearns with infinitely more than a parent’s tenderness.
0.256	He pleads earnestly with sinners and says, “Oh, do not do that abominable thing that I hate.”
0.257	“How shall I give you up, Ephraim?
0.258	How shall I hand you over, Israel?
0.259	How shall I make you like Admah?
0.260	How shall I set you like Zeboim?
0.261	My heart is turned within me, and my compassions are kindled together.”
0.262	He feels all the outpourings of a father’s tenderness, and all of a father’s opposition to any course that will injure his offspring; and as children will sometimes hate and revile their parents for opposing their wayward courses that lead to destruction, so sinners hate God more than they hate all other beings, because he is infinitely more opposed to their destroying their own souls.
0.263	7th. The better God is, the more sinners hate him.
0.264	The better he is, the more he is opposed to their selfishness; and the more he opposes their selfishness, while they remain selfish, the more they are provoked with him.
0.265	In my second discourse on depravity, I showed that men hate God supremely.
0.266	The only reason is that his excellence is supreme excellence.
0.267	His goodness is unmixed goodness, and therefore their hatred is unmixed enmity.
0.268	If there were any defect in his character, men would not hate him so much.
0.269	If God were not perfectly — yes, infinitely — good, men might not be totally depraved; I mean, they might not be totally opposed to his character; but because his character has no blemish, therefore they sincerely, heartily, and perfectly hate him.
0.270	8th. Again, the more he tries to do them good while they remain impenitent, the more they will hate him.
0.271	While they retain their selfishness, all his efforts to restrain it, to hedge them in, to prevent the fulfillment of their selfish desires — the more he intervenes to tear away their idols, to wean them from the world, the more he embitters every cup of joy with which they try to satisfy themselves, the more means he uses to reclaim, and sanctify, and save — if their selfishness remains unbroken, the more deeply and eternally will they hate him.
0.272	9th. This conduct in sinners is infinitely blameworthy and deserves eternal death.
0.273	It is impossible to conceive of guilt deeper and more damning than that of sinners under the Gospel.
0.274	They sin under such special circumstances that their guilt is more aggravated than that of devils.
0.275	Devils have broken the law, and so have you, sinners.
0.276	But devils never rejected the Gospel.
0.277	They have been guilty of rebellion, and so have you.
0.278	But they have never rejected the offer of pardon and spurned, as it were with their feet, the offer of eternal life through the atoning blood of the Son of God.
0.279	If you, sinners, do not deserve eternal death, I cannot conceive that there is a devil in hell who deserves it.
0.280	And yet, strange to say, sinners often speak as if it were doubtful whether they deserve to be damned.
0.281	10th. It is easy to see from this subject that saints and angels will be entirely satisfied with the justice of God in the damnation of sinners.
0.282	They will never take delight in the misery of the damned; but in the display of justice, in the vindication of his insulted majesty and injured honor, in the respect which punishment will create for the law and character of God, they will take pleasure; they will see that the display of his justice is glorious, and will cry hallelujah, while “the smoke of their torment shall ascend forever and ever.”
