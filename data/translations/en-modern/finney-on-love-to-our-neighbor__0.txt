0.2	In speaking on this portion of our Lord’s summary of the divine law, I will first inquire what KIND of love is required here;
0.3	Second, state some of the things implied in this love;
0.4	Third, show that nothing short of this love is true humanity; and
0.5	Lastly, that nothing less is true morality.
0.6	I. The love required toward our neighbor is certainly not complacency in his character.
0.7	Complacency is approval of and delight in character; but our Lord makes no distinction between the good and the bad, and therefore he requires us to love them all.
0.8	But it cannot be that he requires us to approve of and delight in the character of bad men; and so we must conclude that the love of complacency is not in his mind, and is not the thing he requires.
0.9	To take complacency in the character of wicked men as wicked is to be as bad as they are.
0.10	No reasonable man can suppose this to be what the Savior requires, or what he interprets the law of God to require.
0.11	Again, it is not the love of fondness that we sometimes feel toward particular individuals.
0.12	Some persons are naturally pleasing to certain other persons; some are pleasing to all, being naturally amiable and suited to awaken pleasant emotions.
0.13	But this is not the love referred to in the text.
0.14	Again, this love cannot be involuntary.
0.15	As I said before of the love required toward God, that it must be a voluntary act and could not be involuntary, because if it were, it could not justly be demanded, so I say of this love to our neighbor.
0.16	It cannot be involuntary, for if it were, no just being could require it.
0.17	Positively, this love to our neighbor is and must be good will.
0.18	God’s love to man is good will—a pure and strong interest in his welfare, a desire for his happiness, and the positive willing of his happiness as the object dearest to his heart.
0.19	The way in which his great love has shown itself proves this.
0.20	Our very reason affirms that this is the love God has borne and still bears toward our race.
0.21	Consequently we must conclude that this is the love he requires men to exercise toward one another.
0.22	Note again that this love requires us to value our neighbor’s interests as our own.
0.23	This rule applies to all our neighbors—to our enemies as well as to our friends.
0.24	Again, it is a constant love, not fitful and fleeting, and not impulsive, but flowing from a fountain of good will that endures forever.
0.25	It is a state of mind—an established state that regards our neighbor’s interests as our own.

0.26	(1.) It does not imply the universal and equal distribution of our energies and means among all mankind, or even among all who may be near enough to be known to us.
0.27	It cannot mean to imply this, because with such a meaning it would be impossible to obey it.
0.28	There can be no doubt that the law of God demands good will toward all mankind, always and under all circumstances; but there are circumstances that forbid such ways of expressing it as would be proper at other times.

0.30	The relation that sinners come to hold toward God under the sentence of his law is such that it forbids him to bless them.

0.32	Therefore he cannot give them even so much good as a cup of cold water.
0.33	The same circumstances may sometimes demand of us the same withholding of positive efforts to do good to the wicked.
0.34	Again, since each one is required by this law to love his neighbor, it is plain that God intends these kind services to be mutual.
0.35	If God does us good, we should gratefully seek to do him good.
0.36	If he promotes our interests, we should strive to promote his.

0.39	When parents are spared in life long enough to become old and helpless, their children should rejoice in the opportunity to repay the favors shown to them when they too were helpless.

0.80	It is not true humanity to do good only to one’s own offspring.
