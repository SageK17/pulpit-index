0.1	1. Yesterday the holy Gospel warned us not to neglect the sins of our brothers: “But if your brother sins against you, rebuke him between him and you alone.
0.2	If he hears you, you have gained your brother.
0.3	But if he refuses to hear you, take with you two or three more, so that every word may be established in the mouth of two or three witnesses.
0.4	And if he neglects to hear them too, tell it to the Church.
0.5	But if he neglects to hear the Church, let him be to you as a heathen and a tax collector.”
0.6	Today also the passage that follows, which we heard when it was read, relates to the same subject.
0.7	For when the Lord Jesus had said this to Peter, he went on to ask his Master how often he should forgive a brother who had sinned against him; and he asked whether seven times would be enough.
0.8	“The Lord answered him: Not only seven times, but seventy times seven.”
0.9	Then He added a parable very full of terror: that “the kingdom of heaven is like a householder who settled accounts with his servants; among them he found one who owed ten thousand talents.
0.10	And when he commanded that all he had, and all his family, and he himself be sold, and the debt be paid, he fell down at his lord’s feet,” and begged for time, and obtained complete remission.
0.11	For as we have heard, “His lord was moved with compassion, and forgave him all the debt.”
0.12	Then that man, free from his debt but a slave of wickedness, after he had gone out from the presence of his lord, found in his turn a debtor of his own, who owed him not ten thousand talents, the sum that had been forgiven him, but a hundred denarii; and “he began to drag him by the throat, saying, Pay me what you owe.”
0.13	Then he pleaded with his fellow servant as he had done with his lord; but he did not find his fellow servant to be such a man as the other had found his lord to be.
0.14	He not only would not forgive him the debt; he did not even grant him time.
0.15	He hurried him along with great violence to make him pay, he who had only just been set free from his debt to his lord.
0.16	His fellow servants were displeased, and “went and told their lord what had been done”; and the lord summoned his servant into his presence and said to him, “You wicked servant, when you owed me so great a debt, out of pity for you I forgave you all.
0.17	Ought you not also to have had compassion on your fellow servant, even as I had pity on you?”
0.18	And he commanded that all he had forgiven him should be paid.
0.19	2. It is, then, for our instruction that He put forth this parable, and by this warning He would save us from perishing.
0.20	“So,” He said, “will My heavenly Father also do to you, if from your hearts you do not each forgive your brother his trespasses.”
0.21	Look, brothers, the matter is plain, the warning is useful, and a wholesome obedience is by all means due, so that what has been commanded may be fulfilled.
0.22	For every man is at once God’s debtor, and also has some brother who is a debtor to himself.
0.23	For who is there who is not God’s debtor, except the one in whom no sin can be found?
0.24	And who is there who does not have a brother as his debtor, except the one against whom no one has sinned?
0.25	Do you think anyone among mankind can be found who is not himself bound to his brother by some sin?
0.26	So then every man is a debtor, yet he also has debtors of his own.
0.27	The righteous God therefore sets a rule for you toward your debtor, which He also will observe with His.
0.28	For there are two works of mercy that set us free, which the Lord Himself has briefly laid down in the Gospel: “Forgive, and you shall be forgiven; give, and it shall be given to you.”
0.29	“Forgive, and you shall be forgiven” relates to pardoning.
0.30	“Give, and it shall be given to you” relates to doing kindnesses.
0.31	As to what He says about pardoning: you both want your sin to be pardoned, and you have another whom you may pardon.
0.32	Again, as to doing kindnesses: a beggar asks of you, and you are God’s beggar.
0.33	For when we pray we are all God’s beggars; we stand, or rather we fall prostrate, before the door of the Great Householder; we groan in supplication, wishing to receive something; and this something is God Himself.
0.34	What does the beggar ask of you?
0.35	Bread. And what do you ask of God, but Christ, who says, “I am the living Bread which came down from heaven”?
0.36	Do you want to be forgiven?
0.37	Forgive. “Forgive, and it shall be forgiven you.”
0.38	Do you want to receive? “Give, and it shall be given to you.”
0.39	3. But now hear what difficulty I may raise in so plain a precept.
0.40	In this question of forgiveness, when pardon is asked and it is due from the one who should grant it, it may be a difficulty to us as it was to Peter.
0.41	“How often ought I to forgive?
0.42	Is up to seven times enough?”
0.43	“It is not enough,” says the Lord; “I do not say to you, until seven times, but until seventy times seven.”
0.44	Now count up how often your brother has sinned against you.
0.45	If you can reach the seventy-eighth fault, so as to get beyond the seventy times seven, then set about revenge.
0.46	Is this then what He really means, and is it really so: that if he sins “seventy times seven,” you should forgive him, but if he sins seventy-eight times, it would then be lawful for you not to forgive?
0.47	No, I dare to say that even if he should sin seventy-eight times, you must forgive.
0.48	Yes, as I have said, if he sins seventy-eight times, forgive.
0.49	And if he sins a hundred times, forgive.
0.50	And why need I name this number or that?
0.51	In one word, as often as he sins, forgive him.
0.52	Have I then taken it upon myself to go beyond the measure of my Lord?
0.53	He fixed the limit of forgiveness at the number seventy-seven; shall I presume to leap over this limit?
0.54	It is not so; I have not presumed to go beyond it at all.
0.55	I have heard the Lord Himself speaking in His Apostle, where no measure or number is fixed.
0.56	For He says, “Forgiving one another, if anyone has a quarrel against anyone, just as God in Christ has forgiven you.”
0.57	Here you have the rule.
0.58	If Christ has forgiven you your sins only “seventy times and seven,” if He has pardoned up to this point and refused to pardon beyond it, then you also fix this limit and be unwilling to forgive beyond it.
0.59	But if Christ has found thousands of sins upon sins and has still forgiven all, then do not withdraw your mercy, but ask forgiveness for that great number.
0.60	For it was not without meaning that the Lord said “seventy times seven,” since there is no trespass whatever that you ought not to forgive.
0.61	See this servant in the parable, who, being a debtor, was found to have a debtor: he owed ten thousand talents.
0.62	And I suppose that ten thousand talents are at least ten thousand sins.
0.63	For I will not say how even one talent may include all sins.
0.64	But how much did the other servant owe him?
0.65	He owed a hundred denarii.
0.66	Now is this not more than “seventy and seven”?
0.67	And yet the Lord was angry, because he did not forgive him.
0.68	For not only is a hundred more than “seventy-seven,” but a hundred denarii are perhaps a thousand “asses.”
0.69	But what was this compared to ten thousand talents?
0.70	4. And so let us be ready to forgive all the trespasses that are committed against us, if we desire to be forgiven.
0.71	For if we consider our sins, and count up what we do in deed, what by the eye, what by the ear, what by thought, what by countless movements, I do not know whether we so much as sleep without a talent.
0.72	And therefore we beg daily, daily knock at the ears of God by prayer, daily prostrate ourselves and say, “Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.”
0.73	What debts of yours?
0.74	All, or a certain part?
0.75	You will answer, All.
0.76	So then do the same with your debtor.
0.77	This, then, is the rule you lay down, this the condition you speak of; this the covenant and agreement you mention when you pray, saying, “Forgive us, as we forgive our debtors.”
0.78	5. What then, brothers, is the meaning of “seventy times seven”?
0.79	Listen, for it is a great mystery, a wonderful sacrament.
0.80	When the Lord was baptized, the Evangelist St. Luke recorded in that place His generations in the regular order, series, and line in which they had come down to that generation in which Christ was born.
0.81	Matthew begins at Abraham and comes down to Joseph in descending order; but Luke begins to count in ascending order.
0.82	Why does the one count in descending order, and the other in ascending order?
0.83	Because Matthew set forth the generation of Christ by which He came down to us; and so he began to count from when Christ was born, in descending order.
0.84	But because Luke begins to count from when Christ was baptized, and in this is the beginning of ascent, he begins to count in ascending order, and in his count he has completed seventy-seven generations.
0.85	With whom did he begin his count?
0.86	Notice with whom. He began to count from Christ up to Adam himself, who was the first sinner, and who begot us with the bond of sin.
0.87	He counted up to Adam, and so seventy-seven generations are counted; that is, from Christ up to Adam and from Adam up to Christ are the aforementioned seventy-seven generations.
0.88	So then, if no generation was omitted, no trespass is exempted from those which ought to be forgiven.
0.89	For this is why he counted up his seventy-seven generations, the number the Lord mentioned concerning the forgiveness of sins: since he begins to count from the baptism, in which all sins are remitted.
0.90	6. And, brothers, observe in this a still greater mystery.
0.91	In the number seventy-seven is a mystery of the remission of sins.
0.92	That many generations are found from Christ to Adam.
0.93	Now then, ask with somewhat more careful diligence for the secret meaning of this number, and inquire into its hidden meaning; knock with more careful diligence, that it may be opened to you.
0.94	Righteousness consists in keeping the Law of God: true.
0.95	For the Law is set forth in ten commandments.
0.96	That is why the servant in the parable “owed ten thousand talents.”
0.97	This is that memorable Decalogue written by the finger of God, and delivered to the people by Moses, the servant of God.
0.98	He “owed,” then, “ten thousand talents,” which signifies all sins, with reference to the number of the Law.
0.99	And the other “owed a hundred denarii,” derived equally from the same number.
0.100	For a hundred times a hundred make ten thousand, and ten times ten make a hundred.
0.101	And the one “owed ten thousand talents,” and the other ten times ten denarii.
0.102	For there was no departure from the number of the law, and in both numbers you will find every kind of sin included.
0.103	Both are debtors, and both implore and beg for pardon; but the wicked, ungrateful servant would not repay what he had received, would not grant the mercy that had been shown him undeservedly.
0.104	7. Consider then, brothers: every man begins from Baptism; he goes out free, the “ten thousand talents” are forgiven him; and when he goes out, he will soon find some fellow servant who is his debtor.
0.105	Let him note, then, what sin itself is; for the number eleven is the transgression of the law.
0.106	For the law is ten, sin eleven.
0.107	For the law is denoted by ten, sin by eleven.
0.108	Why is sin denoted by eleven?
0.109	Because to get to eleven, there is the transgression of the ten.
0.110	But the due limit is fixed in the law; and the transgression of it is sin.
0.111	Now when you have passed beyond the ten, you come to eleven.
0.112	This high mystery was prefigured when the tabernacle was commanded to be built.
0.113	Many things are mentioned there by number, which are a great mystery.
0.114	Among the rest, curtains of haircloth were ordered to be made, not ten, but eleven; because haircloth signifies the confession of sins.
0.115	Now what more do you require?
0.116	Would you know how all sins are contained in this number “seventy-seven”?
0.117	Seven, then, is usually put for a whole; because in seven days the cycle of time is completed, and when the seventh is ended, it returns to the first again, so that the same cycle may be continued.
0.118	In such cycles whole ages pass away; yet there is no departure from the number seven.
0.119	For He spoke of all sins when He said “seventy times seven”; for multiply that eleven seven times, and it makes seventy-seven.
0.120	Therefore He would have all sins forgiven, for He marked them out by the number “seventy-seven.”
0.121	Let no one, then, retain anything to his own harm by refusing to forgive, lest it be retained against him when he prays.
0.122	For God says, “Forgive, and you shall be forgiven.”
0.123	For I have forgiven you first; you at least forgive after that.
0.124	For if you will not forgive, I will call you back and lay on you again all that I had forgiven you.
0.125	For the Truth does not speak falsely; Christ neither deceives nor is deceived, and He has said at the close of the parable, “So likewise will your Father who is in heaven do to you.”
0.126	You find a Father; imitate your Father.
0.127	For if you will not imitate Him, you are contriving to be disinherited.
0.128	“So likewise,” then, “will My heavenly Father also do to you, if from your hearts you do not each forgive your brother his trespasses.”
0.129	Do not say with the tongue, “I forgive,” while putting off forgiving in the heart; for by His threat of vengeance God shows you your punishment.
0.130	God knows from where you speak.
0.131	Man can hear your voice; God looks into your conscience.
0.132	If you say, I forgive, then forgive.
0.133	It is better that you should be violent in words and forgive in the heart, than be soft in words and relentless in the heart.
0.134	8. Now then, unruly boys will beg, and take it hard to be beaten, taking exception against us in this way when we want to punish them.
0.135	“I have sinned, but forgive me.”
0.136	Well, I have forgiven, and he sins again.
0.137	“Forgive me,” he cries, and I have forgiven him.
0.138	He sins a third time.
0.139	“Forgive me,” he cries, and a third time I have forgiven him.
0.140	Now then, the fourth time let him be beaten.
0.141	And he will say, “What! Have I worn you out up to seventy-seven times?”
0.142	Now if by such objections the severity of discipline falls asleep, then once discipline is suppressed, wickedness will rage unpunished.
0.143	What then is to be done?
0.144	Let us rebuke with words, and if need be with the lash; but let us at the same time forgive the sin, and cast the memory of it out of the heart.
0.145	For this is why the Lord added, “from your hearts”: that though discipline be exercised out of love, gentleness might not depart from the heart.
0.146	For what is so kind and gentle as the surgeon with his knife?
0.147	The one who is to be cut cries out, yet he is cut; the one who is to be cauterized cries out, yet he is cauterized.
0.148	This is not cruelty; on no account let that surgeon’s treatment be called cruelty.
0.149	He is cruel to the wounded part so that the patient may be cured; for if the wound is treated softly, the man is lost.
0.150	So this is my advice, my brothers: that we love our brothers, however they may have sinned against us; that we do not let affection toward them depart from our hearts; and that when there is need, we exercise discipline toward them; lest through the relaxing of discipline wickedness increase, and we begin to be accused on God’s behalf, for it has been read to us, “Rebuke those who sin in the presence of all, that others also may fear.”
0.151	Certainly, if one distinguishes the times, which is the only true way, and so solves the question, all is true.
0.152	If the sin is in secret, rebuke it in secret.
0.153	If the sin is public and open, rebuke it publicly, that the sinner may be reformed, and “that others also may fear.”
