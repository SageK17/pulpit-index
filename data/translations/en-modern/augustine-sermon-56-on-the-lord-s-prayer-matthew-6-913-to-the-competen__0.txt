0.1	1. The blessed Apostle, to show that the times when all the nations would believe in Christ had been foretold by the Prophets, brought forward this testimony where it is written, “And it shall be that whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.”
0.2	For in former times the name of the Lord who made heaven and earth was called upon among the Israelites only; the rest of the nations called upon mute and deaf idols, by whom they were not heard, or upon devils, by whom they were heard to their harm.
0.3	“But when the fullness of time came,” that was fulfilled which had been foretold, “And it shall be that whoever calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”
0.4	Moreover, because the Jews, even those who believed in Christ, begrudged the Gospel to the Gentiles, and said that the Gospel ought not to be preached to those who were not circumcised; because against these the Apostle Paul brought forward this testimony, “And it shall be that whoever calls upon the Name of the Lord shall be saved,” he immediately added, to convince those who were unwilling that the Gospel should be preached to the Gentiles, the words, “But how shall they call upon Him in whom they have not believed? Or how shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard? Or how shall they hear without a preacher? Or how shall they preach unless they are sent?”
0.5	Because, then, he said, “How shall they call upon Him in whom they have not believed?” you have not first learned the Lord’s Prayer and after that the Creed, but first the Creed, where you might know what to believe, and afterwards the Prayer, where you might know whom to call upon.
0.6	The Creed, then, has to do with faith, the Lord’s Prayer with prayer; because it is the one who believes who is heard when he calls.
0.7	2. But many ask for what they ought not to ask, not knowing what is good for them.
0.8	Therefore the one who prays must beware of two things: that he not ask what he ought not, and that he not ask from whom he ought not.
0.9	From the devil, from idols, from evil spirits, nothing must be asked.
0.10	From the Lord our God Jesus Christ, God the Father of Prophets and Apostles and Martyrs, from the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from God who made heaven and earth, the sea, and all things in them, from Him we must ask whatever we have to ask.
0.11	But we must beware that we do not ask of Him what we ought not to ask.
0.12	If, because we ought to ask for life, you ask it of mute and deaf idols, what does it profit you?
0.13	So if from God the Father, who is in heaven, you wish for the death of your enemies, what does it profit you?
0.14	Have you not heard or read in the Psalm in which the damnable end of the traitor Judas is foretold, how the prophecy spoke of him, “Let his prayer be turned into sin”?
0.15	If, then, you rise up and pray for evil on your enemies, your “prayer will be turned into sin.”
0.16	3. You have read in the Holy Psalms how the one who speaks in them calls down, as it would seem, many curses upon his enemies.
0.17	And surely, one may say, the one who speaks in the Psalms is a righteous man; why then does he so wish evil upon his enemies?
0.18	He does not wish it, but he foresees it; it is the prophecy of one who is telling things to come, not a vow of cursing; for the prophets knew by the Spirit to whom evil was appointed to happen, and to whom good; and by prophecy they spoke as if they wished for what they foresaw.
0.19	But how can you know whether the one for whom you are asking evil today may not tomorrow be a better man than yourself?
0.20	But you will say, I know him to be a wicked man.
0.21	Well, you must know that you are wicked too.
0.22	Although it may be that you take it upon yourself to judge another’s heart, which you do not know; but as for your own self, you know that you are wicked.
0.23	Do you not hear the Apostle saying, “I who was formerly a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent; but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief”?
0.24	Now when the Apostle Paul persecuted the Christians, binding them wherever he found them, and dragged them to the Chief Priests to be questioned and punished, what do you think, brethren, did the Church pray against him, or for him?
0.25	Surely the Church of God, which had learned instruction from her Lord, who said as He hung upon the Cross, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do,” prayed in this way for Paul (or rather, as yet, Saul), that what was worked in him might be worked.
0.26	For when he says, “But I was unknown by face to the churches of Judea which are in Christ; they only heard that he who persecuted us in times past now preaches the faith which he once destroyed; and they glorified God in me,” why did they glorify God, except because they asked this of God before it came to pass?
0.27	4. Our Lord, then, first of all cut off “much speaking,” so that you would not bring a multitude of words to God, as though by your many words you would teach Him.
0.28	Therefore, when you pray, you need piety, not wordiness.
0.29	“For your Father knows what you need before you ask Him.”
0.30	Be unwilling, then, to use many words, for He knows what you need.
0.31	But lest perhaps anyone should say here, If He knows what we need, why should we use even a few words? Why should we pray at all?
0.32	He knows Himself; let Him then give what He knows we need.
0.33	Yes, but it is His will that you should pray, so that He may give to your longings, so that His gifts may not be lightly esteemed; seeing that He Himself has formed this longing desire in us.
0.34	The words, therefore, which our Lord Jesus Christ has taught us in His prayer are the rule and standard of our desires.
0.35	You may not ask for anything but what is written there.
0.36	5. “Therefore say,” He says, “Our Father, who is in heaven.”
0.37	Here you see that you have begun to have God for your Father.
0.38	You will have Him when you are born anew.
0.39	Although even now, before you are born, you have been conceived of His seed, as being on the eve of being brought forth in the font, the womb, as it were, of the Church.
0.40	“Our Father, who is in heaven.”
0.41	Remember, then, that you have a Father in heaven.
0.42	Remember that you were born of your father Adam to death, and that you are to be born anew of God the Father to life.
0.43	And what you say, say in your hearts.
0.44	Only let there be the earnest affection of prayer, and there will be the effectual answer of Him who hears prayer.
0.45	“Hallowed be Your Name.”
0.46	Why do you ask that God’s Name may be hallowed?
0.47	It is holy. Why then do you ask for that which is already holy?
0.48	And then, when you ask that His Name may be hallowed, do you not, as it were, pray to Him for Him, and not for yourself?
0.49	No. Understand it rightly, and it is for your own self that you ask.
0.50	For this is what you ask: that what is always holy in itself may be hallowed in you.
0.51	What is “be hallowed”?
0.52	“Be regarded as holy,” not be despised.
0.53	So then you see that the good you wish, you wish for your own self.
0.54	For if you despise the Name of God, it will be bad for you, and not for God.
0.55	6. “Your kingdom come.”
0.56	To whom do we speak? And will God’s kingdom not come if we do not ask for it?
0.57	For we speak of that kingdom which will be after the end of the world.
0.58	For God always has a kingdom; nor is He ever without a kingdom, He whom the whole creation serves.
0.59	But what kingdom, then, do you wish for?
0.60	That of which it is written in the Gospel, “Come, you blessed of My Father, receive the kingdom which is prepared for you from the beginning of the world.”
0.61	See, here is the kingdom of which we say, “Your kingdom come.”
0.62	We pray that it may come in us; we pray that we may be found in it.
0.63	For come it certainly will; but what will it profit you if it finds you at the left hand?
0.64	Therefore, here again it is for your own self that you wish well; for yourself you pray.
0.65	This is what you long for; this is the desire in your prayer, that you may so live that you may have a part in the kingdom of God, which is to be given to all the saints.
0.66	Therefore, when you say, “Your kingdom come,” you pray for yourself, that you may live well.
0.67	Let us have part in Your kingdom: let that come even to us which is to come to Your saints and righteous ones.
0.68	7. “Your will be done.”
0.69	What! If you do not say this, will God not do His will?
0.70	Remember what you have repeated in the Creed, “I believe in God the Father Almighty.”
0.71	If He is Almighty, why do you pray that His will may be done?
0.72	What, then, is this, “Your will be done”?
0.73	May it be done in me, so that I may not resist Your will.
0.74	Therefore here again it is for yourself that you pray, and not for God.
0.75	For the will of God will be done in you, even if it is not done by you.
0.76	For both in those to whom He shall say, “Come, you blessed of My Father, receive the kingdom prepared for you from the beginning of the world,” shall the will of God be done, that the saints and righteous may receive the kingdom; and in those to whom He shall say, “Depart into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels,” shall the will of God be done, that the wicked may be condemned to everlasting fire.
0.77	That His will may be done by you is another thing.
0.78	It is not, then, without cause, but so that it may be well with you, that you pray that His will may be done in you.
0.79	But whether it be well or ill with you, it will still be done in you; but oh, that it may be done by you also!
0.80	Why, then, do I say, “Your will be done in heaven and on earth,” and do not say, “Your will be done by heaven and earth”?
0.81	Because what is done by you, He Himself does in you.
0.82	Never is anything done by you which He Himself does not do in you.
0.83	Sometimes, indeed, He does in you what is not done by you; but never is anything done by you if He does not do it in you.
0.84	8. But what is “in heaven and on earth,” or “as in heaven so on earth”?
0.85	The Angels do Your will; may we do it also.
0.86	“Your will be done as in heaven so on earth.”
0.87	The mind is heaven, the flesh is earth.
0.88	When you say (if indeed you do say it) with the Apostle, “With my mind I serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin,” the will of God is done in heaven, but not yet on earth.
0.89	But when the flesh shall be in harmony with the mind, and “death shall be swallowed up in victory,” so that no carnal desires shall remain for the mind to be in conflict with; when strife in the earth shall have passed away, the war of the heart be over, and that be gone of which it is said, “The flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh; for these are contrary to one another, so that you cannot do the things that you would”; when this war, I say, shall be over, and all sinful desire shall have been changed into love, nothing shall remain in the body to oppose the spirit, nothing to be tamed, nothing to be bridled, nothing to be trodden down; but the whole shall go on through concord to righteousness, and the will of God will be done in heaven and on earth.
0.90	“Your will be done in heaven and on earth.”
0.91	We wish for perfection when we pray for this.
0.92	“Your will be done as in heaven so on earth.”
0.93	In the Church the spiritual are heaven, the carnal are earth.
0.94	So then, “Your will be done as in heaven so on earth”; that as the spiritual serve You, so the carnal, being reformed, may serve You also.
0.95	“Your will be done as in heaven so on earth.”
0.96	There is yet another very spiritual meaning of it.
0.97	For we are admonished to pray for our enemies.
0.98	The Church is heaven, the enemies of the Church are earth.
0.99	What, then, is “Your will be done as in heaven so on earth”?
0.100	May our enemies believe, as we also believe in You! May they become friends, and end their hostilities!
0.101	They are earth, and therefore they are against us; may they become heaven, and they will be with us.
0.102	9. “Give us this day our daily bread.”
0.103	Now here it is evident that it is for ourselves we pray.
0.104	When you say, “Hallowed be Your Name,” it requires explanation how it is that you pray for yourself, not for God.
0.105	When you say, “Your will be done,” here again there is need of explanation, lest you think that you are wishing well to God in this prayer, that His will may be done, and not rather that you are praying for yourself.
0.106	When you say, “Your kingdom come,” this again must be explained, lest you think that you are wishing well to God in this prayer, that He may reign.
0.107	But from this point onward to the end of the Prayer, it is plain that we are praying to God for our own selves.
0.108	When you say, “Give us this day our daily bread,” you profess yourself to be God’s beggar.
0.109	But do not be ashamed of this; however rich any man may be on earth, he is still God’s beggar.
0.110	The beggar takes his stand before the rich man’s house; but the rich man himself stands before the door of the great Rich One.
0.111	Petition is made to him, and he makes his own petition.
0.112	If he were not in need, he would not knock at the ears of God in prayer.
0.113	And what does the rich man need?
0.114	I dare to say, the rich man needs even daily bread.
0.115	For how is it that he has an abundance of all things? From where, except because God has given it to him?
0.116	What would he have if God withdrew His hand?
0.117	Have not many lain down to sleep in wealth, and risen up in beggary?
0.118	And that he is not in want is due to God’s mercy, not to his own power.
0.119	10. But this bread, dearly beloved, by which our body is filled, by which the flesh is restored day by day—this bread, I say, God gives not only to those who praise Him, but also to those who blaspheme Him; “Who causes His sun to rise upon the evil and upon the good, and sends rain upon the just and upon the unjust.”
0.120	You praise Him, and He feeds you; you blaspheme Him, and He feeds you.
0.121	He waits for you to repent; but if you will not change yourself, He will condemn you.
0.122	Because, then, both good and bad receive this bread from God, do you think there is no other bread for which the children ask, of which the Lord said in the Gospel, “It is not fitting to take the children’s bread and to cast it to dogs”?
0.123	Yes, surely there is.
0.124	What, then, is that bread? And why is it called daily?
0.125	Because this is necessary, as the other is; for without it we cannot live; without bread we cannot live.
0.126	It is shamelessness to ask God for wealth; it is no shamelessness to ask for daily bread.
0.127	That which serves pride is one thing; that which serves life is another.
0.128	Nevertheless, because this bread which can be seen and handled is given both to the good and to the bad, there is a daily bread for which the children pray.
0.129	That is the word of God, which is dealt out to us day by day.
0.130	Our bread is daily bread; and by it live not our bodies, but our souls.
0.131	It is necessary for us who are even now laborers in the vineyard—it is our food, not our wages.
0.132	For he who hires the laborer into the vineyard owes him two things: food, so that he does not faint, and his wages, with which he may rejoice.
0.133	Our daily food, then, on this earth is the word of God, which is always dealt out in the Churches; our wages after labor are called eternal life.
0.134	Again, if by this our daily bread you understand what the faithful receive, what you shall receive when you have been baptized, it is with good reason that we ask and say, “Give us this day our daily bread,” that we may live in such a way that we are not separated from the Holy Altar.
0.135	11. “And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.”
0.136	Concerning this petition, again, we need no explanation to show that it is for ourselves that we pray.
0.137	For we beg that our debts may be forgiven us.
0.138	For we are debtors, not in money, but in sins.
0.139	Perhaps you are saying at this moment, And you too.
0.140	We answer, Yes, we too.
0.141	What, you holy Bishops, are you debtors?
0.142	Yes, we are debtors too.
0.143	What, you! My Lord.
0.144	Far be it from you; do not do yourself this wrong.
0.145	I do myself no wrong, but I speak the truth; we are debtors: “If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
0.146	We have been baptized, and yet we are debtors.
0.147	Not that anything then remained which was not remitted to us in Baptism, but because in our lives we are constantly contracting what needs daily forgiveness.
0.148	Those who are baptized and immediately depart out of this life come up from the font without any debt; without any debt they leave the world.
0.149	But those who are baptized and are still kept in this life contract defilements by reason of their mortal frailty, by which, though the ship is not sunk, they still need to have recourse to the pump.
0.150	For otherwise, little by little, that will enter in by which the whole ship will be sunk.
0.151	And to offer this prayer is to have recourse to the pump.
0.152	But we ought not only to pray, but also to give alms, because when the pump is used to keep the ship from sinking, both the voices and the hands are at work.
0.153	Now we are at work with our voices when we say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.154	And we are at work with our hands when we do this: “Break your bread to the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house.
0.155	Shut up alms in the heart of a poor man, and it shall intercede for you with the Lord.”
0.156	12. Although, therefore, all our sins were forgiven in the “washing of regeneration,” we would be driven into great straits if we were not given the daily cleansing of the Holy Prayer.
0.157	Alms and prayers purge away sins; only let such sins not be committed for which we must necessarily be separated from our daily Bread; let us avoid all such debts to which a severe and certain condemnation is due.
0.158	Do not call yourselves righteous, as though you had no cause to say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.159	Though you abstain from idolatry, from the consolations of astrologers, from the cures of enchanters; though you abstain from the seductions of heretics, from the divisions of schismatics; though you abstain from murders, from adulteries and fornications, from thefts and plunderings, from false witness, and from all such other sins which I do not name, which have ruinous consequences, for which it is necessary that the sinner be cut off from the altar, and be so bound on earth as to be bound in heaven, to his great and deadly danger, unless he is again so loosed on earth as to be loosed in heaven—yet after all these are excepted, there is still no lack of occasions by which a man may sin.
0.160	A man sins in seeing with pleasure what he ought not to see.
0.161	Yet who can hold in the quickness of the eye?
0.162	For from this the eye is said to have received its very name, from its quickness.
0.163	Who can restrain the ear or the eye?
0.164	The eyes may be shut when you wish, and are shut in a moment; but the ears you can close only with an effort: you must raise the hand and reach them, and if anyone holds your hand, they are kept open, and you cannot close them against reviling, impure, or flattering and seductive words.
0.165	And when you hear anything you ought not to hear, even though you do not do it, do you not sin with the ear? For do you not hear something that is bad with pleasure?
0.166	How great are the sins that the deadly tongue commits!
0.167	Yes, sometimes sins of such a nature that a man is separated from the altar for them.
0.168	To the tongue belongs the whole matter of blasphemies, and again many idle words are spoken which are not fitting.
0.169	But let the hand do nothing wrong, let the feet not run to any evil, nor the eye be directed to immodesty; let not the ear be open with pleasure to filthy talk, nor the tongue move to indecent speech; yet tell me, who can restrain the thoughts?
0.170	How often we pray, my brethren, and our thoughts are elsewhere, as though we forgot before whom we are standing, or before whom we are prostrating ourselves!
0.171	If all these things are collected together against us, will they therefore fail to overwhelm us because they are small faults?
0.172	What does it matter whether lead or sand overwhelms us?
0.173	The lead is all one mass, the sand is small grains, but by their great number they overwhelm you.
0.174	So your sins are small.
0.175	Do you not see how the rivers are filled and the lands are laid waste by small drops?
0.176	They are small, but they are many.
0.177	13. Let us therefore say every day—and say it in sincerity of heart, and do what we say—“Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.178	It is an engagement, a covenant, an agreement that we make with God.
0.179	The Lord your God says to you, Forgive, and I will forgive.
0.180	You have not forgiven; it is you who retain your sins against yourself, not I. I beg you, my dearly beloved children, since I know what is good for you in the Lord’s Prayer, and most of all in that sentence of it, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors”—hear me.
0.181	You are about to be baptized: forgive everything; whatever anyone has in his heart against anyone else, let him forgive it from his heart.
0.182	So enter in, and be sure that all your sins which you have contracted—whether from your birth of your parents after Adam with original sin, for the sake of which sins you run with infants to the Savior’s grace, or whatever sins you have contracted afterwards in your lives, by word, or deed, or thought—all are forgiven; and you will go out of the water as from the presence of your Lord, with the sure discharge of all debts.
0.183	14. Now because, by reason of those daily sins of which I have spoken, it is necessary for you to say in that daily prayer of cleansing, as it were, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors,” what will you do?
0.184	You have enemies. For who can live on this earth without them?
0.185	Take heed to yourselves: love them.
0.186	In no way can your enemy hurt you by his violence as much as you hurt yourself if you do not love him.
0.187	For he may injure your estate, or flocks, or house, or your manservant, or your maidservant, or your son, or your wife; or at most, if such power is given him, your body.
0.188	But can he injure your soul, as you can yourself?
0.189	Reach forward, dearly beloved, I beg you, to this perfection.
0.190	But have I given you this power?
0.191	He alone has given it to whom you say, “Your will be done as in heaven so on earth.”
0.192	Yet do not let it seem impossible to you.
0.193	I know, I have known by experience, that there are Christian men who do love their enemies.
0.194	If it seems impossible to you, you will not do it.
0.195	Believe, then, first that it can be done, and pray that the will of God may be done in you.
0.196	For what good can your neighbor’s evil do you?
0.197	If he had no evil, he would not even be your enemy.
0.198	Wish him well, then, that he may end his evil, and he will be your enemy no longer.
0.199	For it is not the human nature in him that is at enmity with you, but his sin.
0.200	Is he therefore your enemy because he has a soul and a body?
0.201	In this he is as you are: you have a soul, and so has he; you have a body, and so has he.
0.202	He is of the same substance as you are; you were both made out of the same earth, and given life by the same Lord.
0.203	In all this he is as you are. Acknowledge in him, then, your brother.
0.204	The first pair, Adam and Eve, were our parents—the one our father, the other our mother; and therefore we are brethren.
0.205	But let us leave aside the consideration of our first origin.
0.206	God is our Father, the Church our Mother, and therefore we are brethren.
0.207	But you will say, My enemy is a heathen, a Jew, a heretic, of whom I spoke some time ago on the words, “Your will be done as in heaven so on earth.”
0.208	O Church, your enemy is the heathen, the Jew, the heretic; he is the earth.
0.209	If you are heaven, call on your Father who is in heaven, and pray for your enemies; for so Saul was an enemy of the Church; thus prayer was made for him, and he became her friend.
0.210	He not only ceased to be her persecutor, but he labored to be her helper.
0.211	And yet, to tell the truth, prayer was made against him—but against his malice, not against his nature.
0.212	So let your prayer be against the malice of your enemy, that it may die and he may live.
0.213	For if your enemy were dead, you would have lost, it might seem, an enemy, yet you would not have found a friend.
0.214	But if his malice dies, you have at once lost an enemy and found a friend.
0.215	15. But still you are saying, Who can do this? Who has ever done it?
0.216	May God bring it about in your hearts!
0.217	I know as well as you that there are but few who do it; great and spiritual men are they who do so.
0.218	Are all the faithful in the Church who approach the altar and take the Body and Blood of Christ—are they all such?
0.219	And yet they all say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.220	What if God should answer them, “Why do you ask Me to do what I have promised, when you do not do what I have commanded?”
0.221	What have I promised?
0.222	“To forgive your debts.”
0.223	What have I commanded?
0.224	“That you also forgive your debtors.”
0.225	How can you do this if you do not love your enemies?
0.226	What then must we do, brethren?
0.227	Is the flock of Christ reduced to such a scanty number?
0.228	If only those who love their enemies ought to say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors,” I do not know what to do, I do not know what to say.
0.229	For must I say to you, If you do not love your enemies, do not pray? I dare not say so; rather, pray that you may love them.
0.230	But must I say to you, If you do not love your enemies, do not say in the Lord’s Prayer, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors”?
0.231	Suppose that I were to say, Do not use these words.
0.232	If you do not, your debts are not forgiven; and if you do use them and do not act accordingly, they are not forgiven.
0.233	In order, therefore, that they may be forgiven, you must both use the prayer and act accordingly.
0.234	16. I see some ground on which I may comfort not only a few, but the multitude of Christians; and I know that you are longing to hear it.
0.235	Christ has said, “Forgive, that you may be forgiven.”
0.236	And what do you say in the Prayer which we have now been discussing?
0.237	“Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.238	So, Lord, forgive, as we forgive.
0.239	This is what you say: “O Father, who is in heaven, so forgive our debts as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.240	For this you ought to do, and if you do not do it, you will perish.
0.241	When your enemy asks pardon, forgive him at once.
0.242	And is this much for you to do?
0.243	Though it were much for you to love your enemy when he is violent against you, is it much to love a man who is a suppliant before you?
0.244	What do you have to say?
0.245	He was violent before, and then you hated him.
0.246	I would rather you had not hated him even then; I would rather that then, when you were suffering from his violence, you had remembered the Lord saying, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.”
0.247	I would have wished very much that even at that time when your enemy was violent against you, you had paid regard to the Lord your God speaking thus.
0.248	But perhaps you will say, He did it, but He did it as being the Lord, as the Christ, as the Son of God, as the Only-Begotten, as the Word made flesh.
0.249	But what can I, a weak and sinful man, do?
0.250	If your Lord is too high an example for you, turn your thoughts to your fellow servant.
0.251	The holy Stephen was being stoned, and as they stoned him, on bended knees he prayed for his enemies and said, “Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge.”
0.252	They were casting stones, not asking pardon, yet he prayed for them.
0.253	I wish you were like him; reach forward.
0.254	Why are you forever trailing your heart along the earth?
0.255	Hear, “Lift up your heart”; reach forward, love your enemies.
0.256	If you cannot love him in his violence, love him at least when he asks pardon.
0.257	Love the man who says to you, “Brother, I have sinned, forgive me.”
0.258	If you do not forgive him then, I say not merely that you blot this prayer out of your heart, but that you yourself shall be blotted out of the book of God.
0.259	17. But if you then at least forgive him, or let go of hatred from your heart—it is hatred from the heart that I bid you give up, and not proper discipline.
0.260	What if one who asks my pardon is one who ought to be chastised by me!
0.261	Do what you will, for I suppose that you love your child even when you chastise him.
0.262	You do not regard his cries under the rod, because you are reserving his inheritance for him.
0.263	This, then, is what I say: that you give up all hatred from your heart when your enemy asks pardon of you.
0.264	But perhaps you will say, “He is playing false, he is pretending.”
0.265	O you judge of another’s heart, tell me your own father’s thoughts; tell me your own thoughts yesterday.
0.266	He asks and petitions for pardon; forgive, by all means forgive him.
0.267	If you will not forgive him, it is yourself you hurt, not him, for he knows what he has to do.
0.268	You are not willing to forgive your own fellow servant; he will go, then, to your Lord and say to Him, “Lord, I have begged my fellow servant to forgive me, and he would not; do You forgive me.”
0.269	Has not the Lord power to release His servant’s debts?
0.270	So he, having obtained pardon from his Lord, returns released, while you remain bound.
0.271	How bound? The time of prayer will come, the time must come for you to say, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors”; and the Lord will answer you, You wicked servant, when you owed Me so great a debt, you asked Me, and I forgave you; “ought you not also to have had compassion on your fellow servant, even as I had pity on you?”
0.272	These words are from the Gospel, not from my own heart.
0.273	But if, on being asked, you forgive him who begs for pardon, then you can say this prayer.
0.274	And if you do not yet have the strength to love him in his violence, you may still offer this prayer, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.275	Let us pass on to the rest.
0.276	18. “And lead us not into temptation.
0.277	Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors,” we say because of past sins, which we cannot undo so that they should not have been done.
0.278	You can labor not to do what you have done before, but how can you bring it about that what you have done should not have been done?
0.279	As regards those things which have been done already, that sentence of the prayer is your help, “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.”
0.280	As regards those into which you may fall, what will you do?
0.281	“Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil.”
0.282	“Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil”—that is, from temptation itself.
0.283	19. Now these three first petitions, “Hallowed be Your Name, Your kingdom come, Your will be done as in heaven so on earth”—these three concern the life eternal; for God’s Name ought to be hallowed in us always, we ought to be in His kingdom always, we ought to do His will always.
0.284	This will be to all eternity.
0.285	But “daily bread” is necessary now.
0.286	All the rest that we pray for from this petition onward concerns the necessities of the present life.
0.287	Daily bread is necessary in this life; the forgiveness of our debts is necessary in this life.
0.288	For when we arrive at the other life, there will be an end of all debts.
0.289	In this life there is temptation; in this life the sailing is dangerous; in this life something is always stealing its way in through the chinks of our frailties, which must be pumped out.
0.290	But when we are made equal to the Angels of God, there will be no more need to say and pray to God to forgive us our debts, when there will be none.
0.291	Here, then, is the “daily bread”; here the prayer that our “debts may be forgiven”; here that we “enter not into temptation,” for in that life temptation does not enter; here that we may be “delivered from evil,” for in that life there will be no evil, but eternal and abiding good.
