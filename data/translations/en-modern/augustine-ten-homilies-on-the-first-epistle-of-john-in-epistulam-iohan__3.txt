3.233	13. “And you have no need that anyone teach you, because His anointing teaches you concerning all things.”
3.234	Then to what purpose is it that “we,” my brethren, teach you?
3.235	If “His anointing teaches you concerning all things,” it seems we labor without cause.
3.236	And what do we mean by crying out as we do?
3.237	Let us leave you to His anointing, and let His anointing teach you.
3.238	But this is putting the question only to myself: I put it also to that same apostle: let him deign to hear a little child who asks of him: to John himself I say, Did those to whom you were speaking have the anointing?
3.239	You have said, “His anointing teaches you concerning all things.”
3.240	To what purpose have you written an epistle like this? What teaching did “you” give them? What instruction? What edification?
3.241	See here now, brethren, see a mighty mystery.
3.242	The sound of our words strikes the ears; the Master is within.
3.243	Do not suppose that any man learns anything from man.
3.244	We can admonish by the sound of our voice; if there is not One within who teaches, the noise we make is in vain.
3.245	Yes, brethren, do you wish to know it?
3.246	Have you not all heard this present discourse? And yet how many will go from this place untaught!
3.247	I, for my part, have spoken to all; but those to whom that Anointing within does not speak, those whom the Holy Spirit within does not teach, go back untaught.
3.248	The teachings of the master from without are a kind of aids and admonitions.
3.249	He who teaches the hearts has His chair in heaven.
3.250	Therefore He also says Himself in the Gospel: “Call no man your master upon earth; One is your Master, even Christ.”
3.251	Let Him therefore Himself speak to you within, when no one of mankind is there: for though there may be someone at your side, there is no one in your heart.
3.252	Yet let there not be no one in your heart: let Christ be in your heart: let His anointing be in the heart, lest it be a heart thirsting in the wilderness, and having no fountains with which to be watered.
3.253	There is then, I say, a Master within who teaches: Christ teaches; His inspiration teaches.
3.254	Where His inspiration and His anointing are not, words make a noise from without in vain.
3.255	So are the words, brethren, which we speak from without, as the farmer is to the tree: he works from without, applies water and diligent cultivation; let him apply from without whatever he will, does he form the apples? Does he clothe the nakedness of the wood with a shady covering of leaves? Does he do anything like this from within?
3.256	But whose doing is this?
3.257	Hear the farmer, the apostle: both see what we are, and hear the Master within: “I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase: neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but He who gives the increase, even God.”
3.258	This then we say to you: whether we plant, or whether we water, by speaking we are not anything; but He who gives the increase, even God: that is, “His anointing which teaches you concerning all things.”
T:4	Homily IV (1 John 2:27–3:8)
4.1	“And it is true, and does not lie.
4.2	Even as it has taught you, abide in it.
4.3	And now, little children, abide in Him; that, when He appears, we may have confidence, and not be put to shame by Him at His coming.
4.4	If you know that He is righteous, know that everyone who does righteousness is born of Him.
4.5	Behold, what manner of love the Father has bestowed upon us, that we should be called and should be the sons of God: therefore the world does not know us, because it did not know Him; us also the world does not know.
4.6	Beloved, now we are the sons of God, and it has not yet been revealed what we shall be.

4.8	And every man who has this hope in Him purifies himself, even as He is pure.
4.9	Whoever commits sin also commits iniquity.
4.10	Sin is iniquity. And you know that He was revealed to take away sin; and in Him there is no sin.
4.11	Whoever abides in Him does not sin: whoever sins has not seen Him, nor known Him.
4.12	Little children, let no one lead you astray.
4.13	He who does righteousness is righteous, even as He is righteous.
4.14	He who commits sin is of the devil; for the devil has sinned from the beginning.
4.15	For this purpose the Son of God was revealed, that He might destroy the works of the devil.”
4.16	1. You remember, brethren, that yesterday’s reading was brought to a close at this point, that “you have no need that anyone teach you, but the anointing itself teaches you concerning all things.”
4.17	Now this, as I am sure you remember, we so expounded to you, that we who speak to your ears from without are like workmen applying cultivation to a tree from without, but we cannot give the increase nor form the fruits: only He who created and redeemed and called you, He, dwelling in you by faith and the Spirit, must speak to you within, or else all our noise of words is in vain.
4.18	From what does this appear?
4.19	From this: that while many hear, not all are persuaded of what is said, but only those to whom God speaks within.
4.20	Now those to whom He speaks within are those who give place to Him: and those give place to God who “do not give place to the devil.”
4.21	For the devil wishes to inhabit the hearts of men, and to speak there the things that are able to lead astray.
4.22	But what does the Lord Jesus say?
4.23	“The ruler of this world is cast out.”
4.24	Cast out from where? Out of heaven and earth? Out of the fabric of the world?
4.25	No, but out of the hearts of those who believe.
4.26	The invader being cast out, let the Redeemer dwell within: because the same One who created has redeemed.
4.27	And the devil now assaults from without; he does not conquer Him who has possession within.
4.28	And he assaults from without, by casting in various temptations: but that person does not consent to them to whom God speaks within, and the anointing of which you have heard.
4.29	2. “And it is true,” namely, this same anointing; that is, the very Spirit of the Lord who teaches men cannot lie: “and is not false.
4.30	Even as it has taught you, abide in the same.
4.31	And now, little children, abide in Him, that when He is revealed, we may have boldness in His sight, that we may not be put to shame by Him at His coming.”
4.32	You see, brethren: we believe in Jesus whom we have not seen: those announced Him who saw, who handled, who heard the word from His own mouth; and that they might persuade all mankind of its truth, they were sent by Him; they did not dare to go of themselves.
4.33	And where were they sent?
4.34	You heard when the Gospel was read, “Go, preach the Gospel to the whole creation which is under heaven.”
4.35	Consequently, the disciples were sent “everywhere,” with signs and wonders to attest that what they spoke, they had seen.
4.36	And we believe in Him whom we have not seen, and we look for Him to come.
4.37	Those who look for Him by faith shall rejoice when He comes: those who are without faith, when that which they do not now see has come, shall be ashamed.
4.38	And that confusion of face shall not be for a single day and so pass away, in the way that those are usually confounded who are found out in some fault and are scoffed at by their fellow men.
4.39	That confusion shall carry those who are confounded to the left hand, so that it may be said to them, “Go into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels.”
4.40	Let us abide then in His words, that we may not be confounded when He comes.
4.41	For He Himself says in the Gospel to those who had believed in Him: “If you abide in My word, then you are truly My disciples.”
4.42	And, as if they had asked, With what fruit?
4.43	“And,” He says, “you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.”
4.44	For as yet our salvation is in hope, not in fact: for we do not already possess what is promised, but we hope for it to come.
4.45	And “He who promised is faithful”; He does not deceive you: only do not faint, but wait for the promise.
4.46	For He, the Truth, cannot deceive.
4.47	Do not be a liar, professing one thing and doing another; keep the faith, and He keeps His promise.
4.48	But if you do not keep the faith, it is your own self, not He who promised, who has defrauded you.
4.49	3. “If you know that He is righteous, know that everyone who does righteousness is born of Him.”
4.50	The righteousness which is ours at present is of faith.
4.51	Perfect righteousness does not exist, except only in the angels: and scarcely in angels, if they are compared with God: yet if there is any perfect righteousness of souls and spirits which God has created, it is in the angels, holy, just, good, turned aside by no lapse, falling by no pride, but remaining ever in the contemplation of the Word of God, and having nothing else sweet to them except Him by whom they were created; in them is perfect righteousness: but in us it has begun to be, of faith, by the Spirit.
4.52	You heard when the Psalm was read, “Begin to the Lord in confession.”
4.53	“Begin,” it says; the beginning of our righteousness is the confession of sins.
4.54	You have begun not to defend your sin; now you have made a beginning of righteousness: but it shall be perfected in you when to do nothing else shall delight you, when “death shall be swallowed up in victory,” when there shall be no itching of lust, when there shall be no struggling with flesh and blood, when there shall be the palm of victory, the triumph over the enemy; then there shall be perfect righteousness.
4.55	At present we are still fighting: if we fight, we are in the arena; we strike and are struck; but who shall conquer remains to be seen.
4.56	And that man conquers who, even when he strikes, does not presume on his own strength, but relies upon God who cheers him on.
4.57	The devil is alone when he fights against us.
4.58	If we are with God, we overcome the devil: for if you fight alone with the devil, you will be overcome.
4.59	He is a skillful enemy: how many palms he has won!
4.60	Consider to what he has cast us down!
4.61	That we are born mortal comes from this, that he in the first place cast our very origin down from Paradise.
4.62	What then is to be done, seeing he is so well practiced?
4.63	Let the Almighty be called upon to your aid against the devices of the devil.
4.64	Let Him dwell in you who cannot be overcome, and you shall securely overcome him who is accustomed to overcome.
4.65	But to overcome whom?
4.66	Those in whom God does not dwell.
4.67	For, that you may know it, brethren: Adam, being in Paradise, despised the commandment of God, and lifted up his neck, as if he desired to be his own master and were unwilling to be subject to the will of God: so he fell from that immortality, from that blessedness.
4.68	But there was a certain man, a man now well skilled, though born a mortal, who even as he sat on the dunghill, putrefying with worms, overcame the devil: yes, Adam himself then overcame: even he, in Job; because Job was of his race.
4.69	So then Adam, overcome in Paradise, overcame on the dunghill.
4.70	Being in Paradise, he listened to the persuasion of the woman which the devil had put into her: but being on the dunghill he said to Eve, “You have spoken as one of the foolish women.”
4.71	There he lent an ear, here he gave an answer: when he was glad, he listened; when he was scourged, he overcame.
4.72	Therefore see what follows, my brethren, in the Epistle: because this is what it would have us take to heart, that we may overcome the devil indeed, but not of ourselves.
4.73	“If you know that He is righteous,” it says, “know that everyone who does righteousness is born of Him”: of God, of Christ.
4.74	And in saying, “Is born of Him,” he cheers us on.
4.75	Already therefore, in that we are born of Him, we are perfect.
4.76	4. Hear. “Behold what manner of love the Father has given us, that we should be called sons of God, and be (such).”
4.77	For those who are called sons and are not sons, what does the name profit them where the thing is not?
4.78	How many are called physicians who do not know how to heal! How many are called watchmen who sleep all night long!
4.79	So many are called Christians, and yet in deeds are not found to be such; because they are not what they are called, that is, in life, in conduct, in faith, in hope, in charity.
4.80	But what have you heard here, brethren?
4.81	“Behold, what manner of love the Father has bestowed upon us, that we should be called, and should be, the sons of God: therefore the world does not know us, because it has not known Him; us also the world does not know.”
4.82	There is a whole world Christian, and a whole world ungodly; because throughout the whole world there are ungodly, and throughout the whole world there are godly: those do not know these.
4.83	In what sense, do we think, do they not know them?
4.84	They deride those who live good lives.
4.85	Mark well and see: for perhaps there are such also among you.
4.86	Each one of you who now lives godly, who despises worldly things, who does not choose to go to the shows, who does not choose to make himself drunk as if by solemn custom, yes, what is worse, to make himself unclean under the cover of holy days: the man who does not choose to do these things, how he is derided by those who do them!
4.87	Would he be scoffed at if he were known?
4.88	But why is he not known?
4.89	“The world does not know Him.”
4.90	Who is “the world”?
4.91	Those inhabitants of the world.
4.92	Just as we say, “a house,” meaning its inhabitants.
4.93	These things have been said to you again and again, and we refrain from repeating them, lest they weary you.
4.94	By this time, when you hear the word “world” in a bad sense, you know that you must understand it to mean only lovers of the world, because through love they inhabit it, and by inhabiting have come to be entitled to the name.
4.95	Therefore the world has not known us, because it has not known Him.
4.96	He walked here Himself, the Lord Jesus Christ in the flesh; He was God, He was hidden in weakness.
4.97	And why was He not known?
4.98	Because He reproved all sins in men.
4.99	They, through loving the delights of sins, did not acknowledge the God: through loving what the fever prompted, they did wrong to the Physician.
4.100	5. For us then, what are we?
4.101	Already we are begotten of Him; but because we are such in hope, he says, “Beloved, now we are sons of God.”
4.102	Now already? Then what is it we look for, if already we are sons of God?
4.103	“And not yet,” he says, “has it been revealed what we shall be.”
4.104	But what else shall we be than sons of God?
4.105	Hear what follows: “We know that, when He appears, we shall be like Him, because we shall see Him as He is.”
4.106	Understand, my beloved. It is a great matter: “We know that, when He appears, we shall be like Him; for we shall see Him as He is.”
4.107	In the first place mark what is called “Is.”
4.108	You know what it is that is so called.
4.109	That which is called “Is,” and is not only called so but is so, is unchangeable: It ever remains, It cannot be changed, It is in no part corruptible: It has neither progress, for It is perfect; nor decline, for It is eternal.
4.110	And what is this?
4.111	“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”
4.112	And what is this?
4.113	“Who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God.”
4.114	To see Christ in this way, Christ in the form of God, the Word of God, the Only-Begotten of the Father, equal with the Father, is impossible for the wicked.
4.115	But inasmuch as the Word was made flesh, the wicked also shall be able to see Him: because in the day of judgment the wicked also will see Him; for He shall so come to judge, as He came to be judged.
4.116	In the selfsame form, a man, but yet God: for “cursed is everyone who puts his trust in man.”
4.117	As a man He came to be judged; as a man He will come to judge.
4.118	And if He shall not be seen, what is this that is written, “They shall look on Him whom they pierced”?
4.119	For of the ungodly it is said that they shall see and be confounded.
4.120	How shall the ungodly not see, when He shall set some on the right hand, others on the left?
4.121	To those on the right hand He will say, “Come, you blessed of My Father, receive the kingdom”: to those on the left He will say, “Go into everlasting fire.”
4.122	They will see only the form of a servant; the form of God they will not see.
4.123	Why? Because they were ungodly; and the Lord Himself says, “Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.”
4.124	Therefore we are to see a certain vision, my brethren, “which neither eye has seen, nor ear has heard, nor has entered into the heart of man”: a certain vision, a vision surpassing all earthly beauty, of gold, of silver, of groves and fields; the beauty of sea and air, the beauty of sun and moon, the beauty of the stars, the beauty of angels: surpassing all things, because from it all things are beautiful.
4.125	6. What then shall “we” be, when we see this?
4.126	What is promised to us?
4.127	“We shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is.”
4.128	The tongue has done what it could, it has sounded the words: let the rest be thought out by the heart.
4.129	For what has even John himself said in comparison with That which Is, or what can be said by us men, who are so far from being equal to his merits?
4.130	Let us return therefore to that anointing of His, let us return to that anointing which inwardly teaches what we cannot speak: and because you cannot see at present, let your part and duty be in desire.
4.131	The whole life of a good Christian is a holy desire.
4.132	Now what you long for, you do not yet see: however, by longing you are made capable, so that when that has come which you may see, you shall be filled.
4.133	For just as, if you would fill a bag, and know how great the thing is that shall be given, you stretch the opening of the sack or the skin, or whatever else it may be; you know how much you would put in, and see that the bag is narrow; by stretching you make it capable of holding more: so God, by deferring our hope, stretches our desire; by the desiring, stretches the mind; by stretching, makes it more capacious.
4.134	Let us desire therefore, my brethren, for we shall be filled.
4.135	See Paul widening, as it were, his bosom, that it may be able to receive what is to come.
4.136	He says, namely, “Not that I have already received, or am already perfect: brethren, I do not consider myself to have apprehended.”
4.137	Then what are you doing in this life, if you have not yet apprehended?
4.138	“But this one thing [I do]: forgetting the things that are behind, reaching forth to the things that are before, under strain I follow on toward the prize of the high calling.”
4.139	He says he reaches forth, or stretches himself, and says that he follows “under strain.”
4.140	He felt himself too small to take in that “which eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has entered into the heart of man.”
4.141	This is our life, that we should be exercised by longing.
4.142	But holy longing exercises us just so much as we prune off our longings from the love of the world.
4.143	We have already said, “Empty out what is to be filled.”
4.144	You are to be filled with good: pour out the bad.
4.145	Suppose that God would fill you with honey: if you are full of vinegar, where will you put the honey?
4.146	What the vessel bore in it must be poured out: the vessel itself must be cleansed; it must be cleansed, even with labor, even with hard rubbing, that it may become fit for that thing, whatever it may be.
4.147	Let us say honey, say gold, say wine; whatever we say it is, being that which cannot be said, whatever we would gladly say, It is called—God.
4.148	And when we say “God,” what have we said?
4.149	Is that one syllable the whole of what we look for?
4.150	So then, whatever we have been able to say is beneath Him: let us stretch ourselves toward Him, that when He comes, He may fill us.
4.151	For “we shall be like Him, because we shall see Him as He is.”
4.152	7. “And everyone who has this hope in Him.”
4.153	You see how he has set our place for us, in “hope.”
4.154	You see how the Apostle Paul agrees with his fellow apostle: “By hope we are saved.
4.155	But hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man sees, why does he hope for it?
4.156	For if we hope for what we do not see, by patience we wait for it.”
4.157	This very patience exercises desire.
4.158	Continue, for He continues: and persevere in walking, that you may reach the goal: for that toward which you are heading will not move away.
4.159	See: “And everyone who has this hope in Him purifies himself, even as He is pure.”
4.160	See how he has not taken away free will, in that he says, “purifies himself.”
4.161	Who purifies us but God?
4.162	Yes, but God does not purify you if you are unwilling.
4.163	Therefore, in that you join your will to God, in that you purify yourself.
4.164	You purify yourself, not by yourself, but by Him who comes to dwell in you.
4.165	Still, because you do something in it by the will, therefore something is attributed to you.
4.166	But it is attributed to you only to the end that you should say, as in the Psalm, “Be my helper; do not forsake me.”
4.167	If you say, “Be my helper,” you are doing something: for if you are doing nothing, how could He be said to “help” you?
4.168	8. “Everyone who does sin also does iniquity.”
4.169	Let no one say, Sin is one thing, iniquity another: let no one say, I am a sinful man, but not a doer of iniquity.
4.170	For, “Everyone who does sin also does iniquity.
4.171	Sin is iniquity.” Well then, what are we to do concerning sins and iniquities?
4.172	Hear what He says: “And you know that He was revealed to take away sin; and sin is not in Him.”
4.173	He in whom sin is not, the same has come to take away sin.
4.174	For if there were sin in Him, it would have to be taken away from Him, not He take it away Himself.
4.175	“Whoever abides in Him does not sin.”
4.176	Insofar as he abides in Him, so far he does not sin.
4.177	“Whoever sins has not seen Him, nor known Him.”
4.178	A great question this: “Whoever sins has not seen Him, nor known Him.”
4.179	No wonder. We have not seen Him, but are going to see; we have not known Him, but are going to know: we believe in One we have not known.
4.180	Or perhaps by faith we have known, and by actual beholding have not yet known?
4.181	But then in faith we have both seen and known.
4.182	For if faith does not yet see, why are we said to have been enlightened?
4.183	There is an enlightening by faith, and an enlightening by sight.
4.184	At present, while we are on pilgrimage, “we walk by faith, not by sight,” or actual beholding.
4.185	Therefore our righteousness also is “by faith, not by sight.”
4.186	Our righteousness shall be perfect when we see by actual beholding.
4.187	Only, in the meantime, let us not forsake that righteousness which is of faith, since “the just lives by faith,” as the apostle says.
4.188	“Whoever abides in Him does not sin.”
4.189	For, “whoever sins has not seen Him, nor known Him.”
4.190	That man who sins does not believe: but if a man believes, so far as pertains to his faith, he does not sin.
4.191	9. “Little children, let no one lead you astray.
4.192	He who does righteousness is righteous, as He is righteous.”
4.193	What, on hearing that we are “righteous as He is righteous,” are we to think ourselves equal with God?
4.194	You must know what that “as” means: thus he said a while ago, “Purifies himself even as He is pure.”
4.195	Then is our purity like and equal to the purity of God, and our righteousness to God’s righteousness?
4.196	Who can say this?
4.197	But the word “as” is not always used in the sense of equality.
4.198	As, for example, if, having seen this large church, a person should wish to build a smaller church, but with the same proportions: as, for example, if this is one measure in width and two measures in length, he too should build his church one measure in width and two measures in length: in that case one sees that he has built it “as” this is built.
4.199	But this church has, say, a hundred cubits in length, the other thirty: it is at once “as” this, and yet unequal.
4.200	You see that this “as” does not always refer to parity and equality.
4.201	For example, see what a difference there is between the face of a man and its image in a mirror: there is a face in the image, a face in the body: the image exists in imitation, the body in reality.
4.202	And what do we say?
4.203	Why, “as” there are eyes here, so also there; “as” ears here, so ears also there.
4.204	The thing is different, but the “as” is said of the resemblance.
4.205	Well then, we also have in us the image of God; but not that which the Son, equal with the Father, has: yet unless we also, according to our measure, were “as” He, we should in no respect be said to be like Him.
4.206	“He purifies us,” then, “even as He is pure”: but He is pure from eternity, we pure by faith.
4.207	We are “righteous even as He is righteous”; but He is so in His unchangeable perpetuity, we righteous by believing in One we do not see, so that we may one day see Him.
4.208	Even when our righteousness is perfect, when we are equal to the angels, not even then shall it be made equal to Him.
4.209	How far then is it from Him now, when not even then shall it be equal!
4.210	10. “He who does sin is of the devil, because the devil sins from the beginning.”
4.211	“Is of the devil”: you know what he means: by imitating the devil.
4.212	For the devil made no man, begot no man, created no man: but whoever imitates the devil, that person, as if begotten of him, becomes a child of the devil; by imitating him, not literally by being begotten of him.
4.213	In what sense are you a child of Abraham, since Abraham did not beget you?
4.214	In the same sense as the Jews, the children of Abraham, by not imitating the faith of Abraham, have become children of the devil: of the flesh of Abraham they were begotten, and the faith of Abraham they have not imitated.
4.215	If then those who were begotten from him were put out of the inheritance because they did not imitate, you, who are not begotten of him, are made a child, and in this way shall be a child of his by imitating him.
4.216	And if you imitate the devil, in the way that he became proud and impious against God, you will be a child of the devil: by imitating, not because he created you or begot you.
4.217	11. “For this end the Son of God was revealed.”
4.218	Now then, brethren, mark!
4.219	All sinners are begotten of the devil, as sinners.
4.220	Adam was made by God: but when he consented to the devil, he was begotten of the devil; and he begot all men such as he was himself.
4.221	With lust itself we were born; even before we add our own sins, we have our birth from that condemnation.
4.222	For if we are born without any sin, why this running with infants to baptism that they may be released?
4.223	Then mark well, brethren, the two birth-stocks, Adam and Christ: two men there are; but one of them a man who is man, the other a Man who is God.
4.224	By the man who is man we are sinners; by the Man who is God we are justified.
4.225	That birth has cast down to death; this birth has raised up to life: that birth brings sin with it; this birth sets free from sin.
4.226	For to this end Christ came as Man, to undo the sins of men.
4.227	“For this end the Son of God was revealed, that He might undo the works of the devil.”
4.228	12. The rest I commend to your thoughts, my beloved, that I may not burden you.
4.229	For the question we labor to solve is this—that we call ourselves sinners: for if any man shall say that he is without sin, he is a liar.
4.230	And in the Epistle of this same John we have found it written, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves.”
4.231	For you should remember what went before: “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
4.232	And yet, on the other hand, in what follows you are told, “He who is begotten of God does not sin: he who does sin has not seen Him, nor known Him.—Everyone who does sin is of the devil”: sin is not of God: this frightens us again.
4.233	In what sense are we begotten of God, and in what sense do we confess ourselves sinners?
4.234	Shall we say it is because we are not begotten of God?
4.235	And what then do these Sacraments do in regard to infants?
4.236	What has John said?

T:5	Homily V (1 John 3:9–18)
5.1	“Whoever is born of God does not commit sin; for his seed remains in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God.
5.2	In this the children of God are revealed, and the children of the devil: whoever is not righteous is not of God, nor is he who does not love his brother.
5.3	For this is the message that you heard from the beginning, that we should love one another.
5.4	Not as Cain, who was of the wicked one, and killed his brother.
5.5	And why did he kill him?
5.6	Because his own works were evil, and his brother’s righteous.
5.7	Do not marvel, my brethren, if the world hates us.
5.8	We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren.
5.9	He who does not love abides in death.
5.10	Whoever hates his brother is a murderer: and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him.
5.11	By this we know love, that He laid down His life for us: and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.
5.12	But whoever has this world’s goods, and sees his brother in need, and shuts up his heart of compassion from him, how can the love of God dwell in him?
5.13	My little children, let us not love only in word and in tongue, but in deed and in truth.”
5.14	1. Listen intently, I beg you, because it is no small matter that we have to deal with: and I do not doubt, because you were intent upon it yesterday, that you have come together today with even greater intentness of purpose.
5.15	For it is no slight question, how he says in this Epistle, “Whoever is born of God does not sin,” and how in the same Epistle he has said above, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.16	What shall the man do who is pressed by both sayings from the same Epistle?
5.17	If he confesses himself a sinner, he fears lest it be said to him, Then you are not born of God; because it is written, “Whoever is born of God does not sin.”
5.18	But if he says that he is righteous and that he has no sin, he receives on the other side a blow from the same Epistle, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.19	Placed then as he is in the middle, he cannot find what he can say and what confess, or what profess.
5.20	To profess himself to be without sin is full of peril; and not only full of peril, but also full of error: “We deceive ourselves,” he says, “and the truth is not in us, if we say that we have no sin.”
5.21	But oh, that you had none, and said this! For then you would speak truly, and in uttering the truth would have not so much as a trace of wrong to be afraid of.
5.22	But the reason you do wrong if you say so is that what you say is a lie.
5.23	“The truth,” he says, “is not in us, if we say that we have no sin.”
5.24	He does not say, “Have not had,” lest perhaps it should seem to be spoken of the past life.
5.25	For the man here has had sins: but from the time that he was born of God, he has begun not to have sins.
5.26	If it were so, there would be no question to perplex us.
5.27	For we should say, We have been sinners, but now we are justified: we have had sin, but now we have none.
5.28	He does not say this: but what does he say?
5.29	“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
5.30	And then after a while he says on the other hand, “Whoever is born of God does not sin.”
5.31	Was John himself not born of God?
5.32	If John was not born of God—John, of whom you have heard that he lay in the Lord’s bosom—does any man dare to vouch for himself that in him has taken place that regeneration which it was not granted to that man to have, to whom it was granted to lie in the bosom of the Lord?
5.33	The man whom the Lord loved more than the rest, him alone had He not begotten of the Spirit?
