0.1	1. The holy Gospel, which we heard just now as it was being read, has admonished us concerning the remission of sins.
0.2	And on this subject you must now be admonished by my sermon.
0.3	For we are ministers of the word, not our own word, but the word of our God and Lord, whom no one serves without glory, whom no one despises without punishment.
0.4	He, then, the Lord our God, who, abiding with the Father, made us, and, having been made for us, re-made us, He, the Lord our God, Jesus Christ Himself, says to us what we have heard just now in the Gospel.
0.5	“If,” He says, “your brother sins against you, rebuke him; and if he repents, forgive him; and if he sins against you seven times in a day, and comes and says, I repent, forgive him.”
0.6	He would not have “seven times in a day” understood otherwise than “as often as may be,” lest perhaps he sin eight times, and you be unwilling to forgive.
0.7	What then is “seven times”?
0.8	Always, as often as he sins and repents.
0.9	For this, “Seven times in a day I will praise You,” is the same as what another Psalm says, “His praise shall always be in my mouth.”
0.10	And there is the strongest reason why seven times should be put for that which is always: for the whole course of time revolves in a circle of seven days that come and return.
0.11	2. Whoever you are, then, who have your thoughts on Christ and desire to receive what He has promised, do not be slow to do what He has commanded.
0.12	Now what has He promised?
0.13	“Eternal life.” And what has He commanded?
0.14	That pardon be given to your brother.
0.15	As if He had said to you, “You, O man, give pardon to a man, so that I, who am God, may come to you.”
0.16	But to pass over, or rather to pass by for a while, those more exalted divine promises in which our Creator pledges to make us equal to His angels, so that we may live without end with Him, and in Him, and by Him; not to speak of this just now, do you not wish to receive from your God this very thing which you are commanded to give your brother?
0.17	This very thing, I say, which you are commanded to give your brother, do you not wish to receive from your Lord?
0.18	Tell me if you do not wish it; and then do not give it.
0.19	What is this, but that you should forgive the one who asks you, if you need to be forgiven?
0.20	But if you have nothing to be forgiven, I dare to say, be unwilling to forgive.
0.21	Though I ought not even to say this.
0.22	Even if you have nothing to be forgiven, forgive.
0.23	3. You are just on the point of saying to me, “But I am not God; I am a man, a sinner.”
0.24	Thank God that you confess that you have sins.
0.25	Forgive, then, so that they may be forgiven you.
0.26	Yet the Lord our God Himself exhorts us to imitate Him.
0.27	In the first place God Himself, Christ, exhorts us, of whom the Apostle Peter said, “Christ has suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow His steps, who did no sin, nor was deceit found in His mouth.”
0.28	He, then, truly had no sin, and yet He died for our sins, and shed His Blood for the remission of sins.
0.29	For our sakes He took upon Himself what was not His due, so that He might deliver us from what was due to us.
0.30	Death was not due to Him, nor life to us.
0.31	Why? Because we were sinners.
0.32	Death was not due to Him, nor life to us; He received what was not due to Him, He gave what was not due to us.
0.33	But since we are speaking of the remission of sins, lest you should think it too high a thing to imitate Christ, hear the Apostle saying, “Forgiving one another, even as God in Christ has forgiven you.”
0.34	“Therefore be imitators of God.”
0.35	These are the Apostle’s words, not mine.
0.36	Is it really a proud thing to imitate God?
0.37	Hear the Apostle: “Be imitators of God as dearly beloved children.”
0.38	You are called a child: if you refuse to imitate Him, why do you seek His inheritance?
0.39	4. I would say this even if you had no sin which you might desire to have forgiven.
0.40	But as it is, whoever you are, you are a man; though you are righteous, you are a man; whether you are a layman, or a monk, or a cleric, or a bishop, or an apostle, you are a man.
0.41	Hear the Apostle’s voice: “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves.”
0.42	He, that famous John, and an Evangelist, he whom the Lord Christ loved beyond all the rest, who lay on His breast, he says, “If we say.”
0.43	He did not say, “If you say that you have no sin,” but “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
0.44	He joined himself in the guilt, so that he might be joined in the pardon also.
0.45	“If we say.”
0.46	Consider who it is that says, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.
0.47	But if we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity.”
0.48	How does He cleanse?
0.49	By forgiving; not as though He found nothing to punish, but as finding something to forgive.
0.50	So then, brothers, if we have sins, let us forgive those who ask us.
0.51	Let us not retain enmities in our heart against another.
0.52	For retaining enmities corrupts this heart of ours more than anything.
0.53	5. I want you, then, to forgive, since I find you asking for forgiveness.
0.54	You are asked: forgive. You are asked, and you yourself will ask; you are asked: forgive; you will ask to be forgiven; for, look, the time of prayer will come: I hold you fast by the words you will have to speak.
0.55	You will say, “Our Father, who is in heaven.”
0.56	For you will not be among the number of children if you do not say, “Our Father.”
0.57	So then you will say, “Our Father, who is in heaven.”
0.58	Go on: “Hallowed be Your Name.”
0.59	Say on: “Your kingdom come.”
0.60	Go on still: “Your will be done, as in heaven, so on earth.”
0.61	See what you add next: “Give us this day our daily bread.”
0.62	Where are your riches?
0.63	So you are a beggar.
0.64	Nevertheless, in the meantime (it is the point I am speaking of), say what comes next after “Give us this day our daily bread.”
0.65	Say what follows this: “Forgive us our debts.”
0.66	Now you have come to my words: “Forgive us our debts.”
0.67	By what right? By what covenant? On what condition? On what express stipulation?
0.68	“As we also forgive our debtors.”
0.69	It is only a small thing that you do not forgive; indeed you do more: you lie to God.
0.70	The condition is laid down, the law fixed.
0.71	“Forgive as I forgive.”
0.72	Therefore He does not forgive unless you forgive.
0.73	“Forgive as I forgive.”
0.74	You wish to be forgiven when you ask; forgive the one who asks of you.
0.75	He who is skilled in heaven’s laws has dictated these prayers: He does not deceive you; ask according to the wording of His heavenly voice: say, “Forgive us, as we also forgive,” and do what you say.
0.76	He who lies in his prayers loses the benefit he seeks; he who lies in his prayers both loses his case and finds his punishment.
0.77	And if anyone lies to the emperor, he is convicted of his lie at his coming; but when you lie in prayer, you are convicted by your very prayer.
0.78	For God does not look for a witness concerning you in order to convict you.
0.79	He who dictated the prayers to you is your Advocate; if you lie, He is a witness against you; if you do not amend yourself, He will be your Judge.
0.80	So then, both say it and do it.
0.81	For if you do not say it, you will not obtain what you ask, since you make your requests contrary to the law; but if you say it and do not do it, you will be guilty of lying as well.
0.82	There is no way of evading that verse except by fulfilling what we say.
0.83	Can we blot this verse out of our prayer?
0.84	Would you have that clause, “Forgive us our debts,” remain there, and have us blot out what follows, “As we also forgive our debtors”?
0.85	You shall not blot it out, lest you yourself be blotted out first.
0.86	So then in this prayer you say, “Give,” and you say, “Forgive,” so that you may receive what you do not have, and be forgiven what you have done wrong.
0.87	So then, you wish to receive: give; you wish to be forgiven: forgive.
0.88	It is a brief summary.
0.89	Hear Christ Himself in another place: “Forgive, and you shall be forgiven.”
0.90	What will you forgive?
0.91	What others have sinned against you.
0.92	What will you be forgiven?
0.93	What you yourselves have sinned.
0.94	“Forgive.” “Give, and there shall be given you what you desire”: eternal life.
0.95	Support the temporal life of the poor man, sustain the poor man’s present life, and for this so small and earthly seed you shall receive as your harvest life eternal. Amen.
