0.1	Text: “Love works no ill to its neighbor; therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.” — Romans 13:10
0.2	In speaking from these words, I intend:
0.3	I. To make some remarks about the nature of love.
0.4	II. To show that love is the whole of religion.
0.5	III. Some things that are not essential to perfect love.
0.6	IV. Some things that are essential to it.
0.7	V. Some of the effects that perfect love produces.
H:0.7	I. I will make some remarks on the nature of love.
0.8	1. The first remark I have to make is that love may exist in various forms.
0.9	The two principal forms, as far as religion is concerned, are benevolence and delight.
0.10	Benevolence is an affection of the mind, that is, an act of the will.
0.11	It is willing good, or a desire to promote the happiness of its object.
0.12	Delight is esteem, or approval of the character of its object.
0.13	Benevolence should be exercised toward all beings, regardless of their moral character.
0.14	Delight is due only to the good and holy.
0.15	2. Love may exist either as an affection or as an emotion.
0.16	When love is an affection, it is voluntary; it consists in the act of the will.
0.17	When it is an emotion, it is involuntary.
0.18	What we call feelings, or emotions, are involuntary.
0.19	They are not directly dependent on the will, nor controlled by a direct act of the will.
0.20	The virtue of love is found mostly when it takes the form of an affection.
0.21	The happiness of love is found mostly when it takes the form of an emotion.
0.22	If the affection of love is very strong, it produces a high degree of happiness; but the emotion of holy love is happiness itself.
0.23	I said that the emotion of love is involuntary.
0.24	I do not mean that the will has nothing to do with it, but that it is not the result of a mere or direct act of the will.
0.25	No one can exercise the emotion of love merely by willing it.
0.26	And the emotion may often exist in spite of the will.
0.27	People often feel emotions rising in their minds which they know to be improper, and they try by direct efforts of the will to banish them from their minds; and finding that impossible, they conclude that they have no control over these emotions.
0.28	But they may always be controlled by the will in an indirect way.
0.29	The mind can call up any class of emotions it chooses by directing its attention sufficiently to the proper object.
0.30	They are certain to rise in proportion as the attention is fixed, provided the will is right in regard to the object of attention.
0.31	So it is with those emotions which are improper or unpleasant: the mind may be rid of them by turning its attention entirely away from the object and not allowing the thoughts to dwell on it.
0.32	3. Ordinarily, the emotions of love toward God are experienced when we exercise love toward Him in the form of affection.
0.33	But this is not always so.
0.34	We may exercise goodwill toward any object and yet at times feel no perceptible emotions of love.
0.35	It is not certain that even the Lord Jesus Christ exercised love toward God in the form of emotion at all times.
0.36	As far as our acquaintance with the nature of the mind goes, we know that a person may exercise affection, and be guided and governed by it constantly in all his actions, without any felt emotion of love toward its object at the time.
0.37	Thus a husband and father may be occupied in laboring for the benefit of his family, with his very life controlled by affection for them, while his thoughts are not so taken up with them as to make him feel any perceptible emotions of love toward them at the time.
0.38	The things with which he is occupied may take up his mind so much that he scarcely has a thought of them, and so he may have no felt emotion toward them; and yet all the while he is guided and governed by affection for them.
0.39	Observe here that I use the term affection in the sense of President Edwards, as he explains it in his celebrated Treatise on the Will.
0.40	An affection, in his treatise, is an act of the will, or a volition.
0.41	4. Love to our neighbor naturally implies the existence of love to God, and love to God naturally implies love to our neighbor.
0.42	The same thing is declared in the 8th verse: “Owe no man anything, but to love one another; for he that loves another has fulfilled the law.
0.43	For this—You shall not commit adultery, You shall not kill, You shall not steal, You shall not bear false witness, You shall not covet—and if there is any other commandment, it is briefly contained in this saying, namely, You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
0.44	Here it is taken for granted that love to our neighbor implies the existence of love to God; otherwise it could not be said that “he that loves another has fulfilled the law.”
0.45	The apostle James recognizes the same principle when he says, “If you fulfill the royal law according to the scripture, You shall love your neighbor as yourself, you do well.”
0.46	Here love to our neighbor is spoken of as constituting obedience to the whole law.
0.47	Benevolence, that is, goodwill to our neighbor, naturally implies love to God.
0.48	It is love for the happiness of being.
0.49	So the love of delight toward holy beings naturally implies love to God, as a being of infinite holiness.
H:0.17	II. I will show that love is the whole of religion.
0.50	In other words, all that God requires of man consists in love, in its various modifications and results.
0.51	Love is the sum total of all.
0.52	1. The first proof I shall offer is that this truth is taught in the text and in many other passages of Scripture.
0.53	The Scriptures fully teach that love is the sum total of all the requirements, both of the law and of the gospel.
0.54	Our Savior declares that the great commandment—You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind and strength, and your neighbor as yourself—is the sum total of all the law and the prophets; that is, it implies and includes all that the whole Scriptures, the law and the gospel, require.
0.55	2. God is love; and to love is to be like God, and to be perfect in love is to be perfect as God is perfect.
0.56	All God’s moral attributes consist in love, acting under certain circumstances and for certain ends.
0.57	God’s justice in punishing the wicked, His anger at sin, and the like, are only exercises of His love for the general happiness of His kingdom.
0.58	So it is in man.
0.59	All that is good in man is some modification of love.
0.60	Hatred of sin is only love for virtue, acting itself out in opposing whatever is opposed to virtue.
0.61	So true faith implies and includes love; and faith which has no love in it, or which does not work by love, is no part of religion.
0.62	The faith that belongs to religion is an affectionate confidence in God.
0.63	There is a kind of faith in God which has no love in it.
0.64	The devil has that kind of faith.
0.65	The sinner under conviction has it.
0.66	But there is no religion in it.
0.67	Faith might rise even to the faith of miracles, and yet if there is no love in it, it amounts to nothing.
0.68	The apostle Paul, in the 13th chapter of 1 Corinthians, says, “Though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and do not have charity, I am nothing.”
0.69	It is just so with repentance.
0.70	The repentance that does not include love is not “repentance toward God.”
0.71	True repentance implies obedience to the law of love, and consequent opposition to sin.
H:0.24	III. I will mention some things that are not essential to perfect love.
0.72	1. The highest degree of emotion is not essential to perfect love.
0.73	It is evident that the Lord Jesus Christ very seldom had the highest degree of the emotion of love, and yet He always had perfect love.
0.74	He generally showed very little emotion, or excitement.
0.75	Excitement is always proportioned to the strength of the emotions, since it consists in them.
0.76	The Savior generally seemed remarkably calm.
0.77	Sometimes His indignation was strong, or His grief at the hardness of men’s hearts; and sometimes we read that He rejoiced in spirit.
0.78	But He was commonly calm, and showed no high degree of emotion.
0.79	And it is plainly not essential to perfect love that the emotion of love should exist in a high degree.
0.80	2. Perfect love does not exclude the idea of increase in love, or growth in grace.
0.81	I suppose that the growth of the mind in knowledge to all eternity naturally implies growth in love to all eternity.
0.82	The Lord Jesus Christ, in His human nature, grew in stature and in favor with God and man.
0.83	No doubt, as a child, He grew in knowledge; and as He grew in knowledge, He grew in love toward God, as well as in favor with God.
0.84	His love was perfect when He was a child, but it was greater when He became a man.
0.85	As a human being, He probably continued to increase in love to God as long as He lived.
0.86	From the nature of the mind, we see that it may be so with all the saints in glory: their love will increase to all eternity, and yet it is always perfect love.
0.87	3. It is not essential to perfect love that love should always be exercised toward all individuals alike.
0.88	We cannot think of all individuals at once.
0.89	You cannot even think of every individual among your acquaintances at once.
0.90	The degree of love toward an individual depends on whether that individual is present to our thoughts.
0.91	4. It is not essential to perfect love that there should be the same degree of the spirit of prayer for every individual, or for the same individual at all times.
0.92	The spirit of prayer is not always essential to pure and perfect love.
0.93	The saints in heaven have pure and perfect love for all beings, yet we do not know that they have the spirit of prayer for any.
0.94	You may love an individual with a very strong degree of love, and yet not have the spirit of prayer for that individual.
0.95	That is, the Spirit of God may not lead you to pray for the salvation of that individual.
0.96	You do not pray for the wicked in hell.
0.97	The spirit of prayer depends on the influences of the Holy Spirit, leading the mind to pray for things agreeable to the will of God.
0.98	You cannot pray in the Spirit with the same degree of fervor and faith for all mankind.
0.99	Jesus Christ said expressly that He did not pray for all mankind: “It is not for the world that I pray.”
0.100	Here there has been a great mistake in regard to the spirit of prayer.
0.101	Some suppose that Christians have not done all their duty if they have not prayed in faith for every individual, as long as there is a sinner on earth.
0.102	Then Jesus Christ never did all His duty, for He never did this.
0.103	God has never told us that He will save all mankind, and has never given us any reason to believe He will do it.
0.104	How then can we pray in faith for the salvation of all?
0.105	What does that faith have to rest on?
0.106	5. Perfect love is not inconsistent with those feelings of languor or bodily weakness which are the necessary consequence of exhaustion or ill health.
0.107	We are so made that excitement naturally and necessarily exhausts our powers.
0.108	But love may be perfect nevertheless.
0.109	Though a person may feel more like lying down and sleeping than like praying, yet his love may be perfect.
0.110	The Lord Jesus Christ often felt this weariness and exhaustion, when the spirit was still willing but the flesh was weak.
H:0.35	IV. What is essential to perfect love.
0.111	1. It implies that there is nothing in the mind inconsistent with love.
0.112	No hatred, malice, wrath, envy, or any other malignant emotions that are inconsistent with pure and perfect love.
0.113	2. That there is nothing in the life inconsistent with love.
0.114	All the actions, words, and thoughts continually under the entire and perfect control of love.
0.115	3. That love to God is supreme.
0.116	Love to God is completely supreme, and so entirely above all other objects that nothing else is loved in comparison with God.
0.117	4. That love to God is disinterested.
0.118	God is loved for what He is; not for His relation to us, but for the excellence of His character.
0.119	5. That love to our neighbor should be equal; that is, that his interest and happiness should be regarded by us as of equal value with our own, and that he and his interests are to be treated by us accordingly.
H:0.45	V. I will mention some of the effects of perfect love.
0.120	1. One effect of perfect love to God and man will certainly be delight in self-denial for the sake of promoting the interests of God’s kingdom and the salvation of sinners.
0.121	See affectionate parents, how they delight in self-denial for the sake of promoting the happiness of their children.
0.122	There is a father: he gives himself up to exhausting labor, day after day and year after year, through the whole of a long life, rising early and continually eating the bread of anxious care, to promote the welfare of his family.
0.123	And he counts all this self-denial and toil not a grief or a burden, but a delight, because of the love he bears his family.
0.124	See that mother: she wishes to educate her son at college.
0.125	And now, instead of finding it painful, it is a joy to her to sit up late and labor incessantly to help him.
0.126	That is because she truly loves her son.
0.127	Such parents rejoice more in bestowing gifts on their children than they would in enjoying the same things themselves.
0.128	What parent does not enjoy a piece of fruit more in giving it to his little child than in eating it himself?
0.129	The Lord Jesus Christ enjoyed more solid satisfaction in working out salvation for mankind than any of His saints can ever enjoy in receiving favors at His hands.
0.130	He testified that it is more blessed to give than to receive.
0.131	This was the joy set before Him, for which He endured the cross and despised the shame.
0.132	His love for mankind was so great that it compelled Him to undertake this work, and sustained Him triumphantly through it.
0.133	The apostle Paul did not count it a grief and a hardship to be hunted from place to place, imprisoned, scourged, stoned, and counted the refuse of all things, for the sake of spreading the gospel and saving souls.
0.134	It was his joy.
0.135	The love of Christ so compelled him, and he had such a desire to do good, that it was his highest delight to lay himself on that altar as a sacrifice to the cause.
0.136	Other individuals have had the same mind as the apostle.
0.137	People have been known who would be willing to live a thousand years, or to the end of time, if they could be employed in doing good, in promoting the kingdom of God, and in saving the souls of men, and who were willing to forgo even sleep and food to benefit the objects they so greatly love.
0.138	2. It delivers the soul from the power of legal motives.
0.139	Perfect love leads a person to obey God, not because he fears the wrath of God, or hopes to be rewarded for doing this or that, but because he loves God and loves to do the will of God.
0.140	There are two extremes on this subject.
0.141	One class makes virtue consist in doing right simply because it is right, without any reference to the will of God or any influence from love to God.
0.142	Another class makes virtue consist in acting from love of the activity itself, but without reference to God’s authority as Ruler and Lawgiver.
0.143	Both of these are in error.
0.144	To do a thing simply because one thinks it right, and not out of love to God, is not virtue.
0.145	Neither is it virtue to do a thing because one loves to do it, with no regard to God’s will.
0.146	A woman might do certain things because she knew it would please her husband; but if she did the same thing merely because she loved to do it, and with no regard to her husband, it would be no virtue as far as her husband is concerned.
0.147	If a person loves God, as soon as he knows what God’s will is, he will do it because it is God’s will.
0.148	Perfect love will lead to universal obedience—to doing God’s will in all things, because it is the will of God.
0.149	3. The individual who exercises perfect love will be dead to the world.
0.150	By this I mean that he will be cut loose from the influence of worldly considerations.
0.151	Perfect love will so annihilate selfishness that he will have no will but the will of God, and no interest but God’s glory.
0.152	He will not be influenced by public opinion, or by what this or that man will say or think.
0.153	See that woman, what she will do out of natural affection for her husband.
0.154	She is willing to cut loose from all her friends, as much as if she were dead to them, and to pay not the least regard to what they say, and to leave all the riches and honors and delights they can offer, in order to join the one she loves and live with him in poverty, in disgrace, and in exile.
0.155	Her affection is so great that she does it joyfully, and is ready to go from a palace to any cottage or cave on earth and be perfectly happy.
0.156	And all that her friends can say against the man of her affection has not the least influence on her mind, except to make her cling the more closely to him.
0.157	This one ALL-ABSORBING affection has actually killed all the influences that used to act on her.
0.158	To attempt to influence her by such things is in vain.
0.159	There is only one avenue of approach to her mind; only one class of motives moves her, and that is through the object of her affection.
0.160	As far as the philosophy of the mind is concerned, the perfect love of God operates in the same way.
0.161	It is impossible to divert from God the mind that is filled with perfect love, while that love continues in exercise.
0.162	Take away his worldly possessions, his friends, his good name, his children; send him to prison, beat him with stripes, bind him to the stake, fill his flesh full of pine knots and set them on fire; and then leave him his God, and he is happy.
0.163	His strong affection can make him insensible to everything else.
0.164	He is as if he were dead to all the world but his God.
0.165	Cases have been known of martyrs who, while their bodies were frying at the stake, were so perfectly happy in God as to lose the sense of pain.
0.166	Put such a one in hell, in the lake of fire and brimstone, and as long as he enjoys God, and the love of God fills his soul, he is happy.
0.167	Who has not witnessed or heard of cases of affection approaching in degree what I have described, where a person is in fact dead to all other things and lives only for the loved one?
0.168	How often do you see fond parents who live for an only child, and when that child dies, wish themselves dead!
0.169	Sometimes a husband and wife have such an absorbing affection for each other that they live for nothing else; and if the husband dies, the wife pines away and dies also.
0.170	The soul-absorbing object for which she lived is gone, and why should she live any longer?
0.171	So, when an individual is filled with the perfect love of God, he wishes to live only to love and serve God; he is dead to the world, dead to his own reputation, and has no desire to live for any other reason—here, or in heaven, or anywhere else in the universe—but to glorify God.
0.172	He is willing to live, here or anywhere else, and to suffer and labor a thousand years, or to all eternity, if it will glorify God.
0.173	I recall often hearing a friend say, “I don’t know that I have one thought of living a single moment for any other purpose than to glorify God, any more than I would think of leaping right into hell.”
0.174	This was said soberly and deliberately, and the whole life of that individual corresponded with the declaration.
0.175	He was intelligent, sober-minded, and honest, and I have no doubt he expressed what had been the fullest conviction of his mind for years.
0.176	What was this but perfect love?
0.177	What more does any angel in heaven do than this?
0.178	His love may be greater in degree, because his strength is greater.
0.179	But the highest angel could not love more perfectly than to be able to say in sincerity, “I would as soon think of leaping into hell as of living one moment for any other object but to glorify God.”
0.180	What more could Jesus Christ Himself say than that?
0.181	4. It is hardly necessary to say that perfect joy and peace are the natural results of perfect love.

0.183	You will observe that the word here translated charity is the same one that is rendered love in other places.
0.184	It means love. “Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and do not have charity, I have become like sounding brass or a tinkling cymbal.

0.186	He might have even the faith of miracles, so strong that he could move mountains from their everlasting foundations, and yet have no love.

0.189	“Charity suffers long.” Long-suffering is meekness under opposition or injury.
0.190	This is one of the effects of love: to bear great provocations and not retaliate or revile in return.
0.191	Love “is kind,” or affectionate in all its dealings with others, never harsh or rude, never needlessly giving pain to anyone.
0.192	Love “is not envious”; it never dislikes others because they are more thought of or noticed, more honored or useful, or make greater attainments in knowledge, happiness or piety.

0.197	It “is not easily provoked.”
0.198	This is always the effect of love.
0.199	See that mother, how long she bears with her children, because she loves them.
0.200	If you see an individual who is testy or crusty, easily flying into a passion when anything goes wrong, he is by no means perfect in love, if he has any love.
0.201	To be easily provoked is always a sign of pride.
0.202	If a person is full of love, it is impossible to make him exercise sinful anger while love continues.
0.203	He exercises such indignation as God exercises, and as holy angels feel, at what is base and wrong, but he will not be provoked by it.

0.205	He has that in his own mind which makes him think evil of others.

0.209	They do not suspect evil where the outward appearance is fair, nor without the strongest proof.

0.215	Where you see an individual who does not have this spirit, rest assured, he is by no means perfect in love.

0.275	Religion may be revived in some individuals, but in the main, in such cases, it is a revival of irreligion.
0.276	VII. When persons profess to be converted, if love is not the ruling feature in their character, they are not truly converted.
0.277	However well they may appear in other respects, no matter how clear their views or how deep their feelings, if they do not have the spirit of love to God and love to man, they are deceived.
0.278	Let no such converts be trusted.
0.279	VIII. See what the world will be when mankind are universally moved by a spirit of love.
0.280	We learn that the time will come when there shall be nothing to hurt or destroy, and when the Spirit of love will universally prevail.
0.281	What a change in society!
0.282	What a change in all the methods of doing business, and in all the dealings of mankind, when each shall love his neighbor as himself, and seek the good of others as his own!
0.283	Could one of the saints who live now revisit the earth in that day, he would not know the world in which he had lived; everything will be so altered.
0.284	“Is it possible,” he would exclaim, “that this is the earth—the same earth that used to be so full of wrangling, and oppression, and fraud?”
0.285	IX. The thing on which the Lord Jesus Christ is bent is to bring all mankind under the influence of love.
0.286	Is it not a worthy object?
0.287	He came to destroy the works of the devil.
0.288	And this is the way to accomplish it.
0.289	Suppose the world were full of such men as Jesus Christ was in His human nature.
0.290	Compare it with what it is now.
0.291	Would not such a change be worthy of the Son of God?
0.292	What a glorious end—to fill the earth with love!
0.293	X. It is easy to see what makes heaven.
0.294	It is love—perfect love.
0.295	And it is easy to see what makes heaven begun on earth in those who are full of love.
0.296	How sweet their temper, what delightful companions, how blessed to live near them and to associate with them—so full of candor, so kind, so gentle, so careful to avoid offense, so divinely amiable in all things!
0.297	And can this be attained by men?
0.298	Can we love God, here in this world, with all the heart, and soul, and strength, and mind?
0.299	Is it our privilege and our duty to have the Spirit of Christ, and shall we display the spirit of the devil?
0.300	Beloved, let our hearts be set on perfect love, and let us give God no rest until we feel our hearts full of love, and until all our thoughts and all our lives are full of love to God and love to man.
0.301	Oh, when will the church come up to this ground?
0.302	Only let the church be full of love, and she will be fair as the moon, clear as the sun, and as formidable to all wickedness, in high places and low, as an army with banners.
