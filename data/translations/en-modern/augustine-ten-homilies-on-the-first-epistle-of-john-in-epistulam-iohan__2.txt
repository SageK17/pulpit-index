2.233	12. For “the world” is the name given not only to this fabric which God made—heaven and earth, the sea, things visible and invisible—but the inhabitants of the world are also called the world, just as we call a “house” both the walls and those who dwell in it.
2.234	And sometimes we praise a house, and find fault with those who live in it.
2.235	For we say, “A good house,” because it is laid with marble and has beautiful paneled ceilings; and in another sense we say, “A good house”: no one there suffers wrong, no acts of plunder, no acts of oppression are done there.
2.236	Now we are praising not the building, but those who dwell within the building; yet we call both the one and the other a “house.”
2.237	For all lovers of the world—because by love they inhabit the world, just as those inhabit heaven whose heart is on high while in the flesh they walk on earth—all lovers of the world, I say, are called the world.
2.238	These have only these three things: “lust of the flesh, lust of the eyes, vainglory of life.”
2.239	For they lust to eat, to drink, to lie together: to use these pleasures.
2.240	Surely it is not that there is no permitted measure in these things, or that when it is said, “Do not love these things,” it means that you are not to eat, or not to drink, or not to beget children?
2.241	This is not what is being said.
2.242	Only let there be measure, for the Creator’s sake, so that these things may not bind you through your love of them; lest you love for enjoyment what you ought to have for use.
2.243	But you are not put to the test except when two things are set before you, this or that: “Will you have righteousness, or gain?”
2.244	“I have nothing to live on, nothing to eat, nothing to drink.”
2.245	But what if you cannot have these except by wickedness?
2.246	Is it not better to love what you cannot lose than to lose yourself by wickedness?
2.247	You see the gain of gold; the loss of faith you do not see.
2.248	This, then, he tells us, is “the lust of the flesh,” that is, the lusting after those things which pertain to the flesh, such as food and bodily union, and all other things of that kind.
2.249	13. “And the lust of the eyes”: by “the lust of the eyes” he means all curiosity.
2.250	Now how wide is the scope of curiosity!
2.251	It is this that is at work in spectacles, in theaters, in the sacraments of the devil, in magical arts, in dealings with darkness: none other than curiosity.
2.252	Sometimes it tempts even the servants of God, so that they wish, as it were, to work a miracle, to test God whether He will hear their prayers by the working of miracles; it is curiosity: this is “the lust of the eyes”; it “is not of the Father.”
2.253	If God has given the power, do the miracle, for He has put it in your way to do it; for do not think that those who have not done miracles will not belong to the kingdom of God.
2.254	When the apostles were rejoicing that the demons were subject to them, what did the Lord say to them?
2.255	“Do not rejoice in this, but rejoice because your names are written in heaven.”
2.256	He wanted the apostles to rejoice in that very thing in which you also rejoice.
2.257	Woe to you indeed if your name is not written in heaven!
2.258	Is it woe to you if you do not raise the dead? Is it woe to you if you do not walk on the sea? Is it woe to you if you do not cast out demons?
2.259	If you have received power to do them, use it humbly, not proudly.
2.260	For even of certain false prophets the Lord has said that “they shall do signs and wonders.”
2.261	Therefore let there be no “ambition of the world”: ambitio saeculi is pride.
2.262	A man wishes to make much of himself in his honors; he thinks himself great, whether because of riches or because of some power.
2.263	14. These three there are, and you can find nothing by which human craving can be tempted except either by the lust of the flesh, or the lust of the eyes, or the pride of life.
2.264	By these three the Lord was tempted by the devil.
2.265	By the lust of the flesh He was tempted when it was said to Him, “If You are the Son of God, speak to these stones that they may become bread,” when He was hungry after His fast.
2.266	But in what way did He repel the tempter, and teach His soldier how to fight?
2.267	Notice what He said to him: “Not by bread alone does man live, but by every word of God.”
2.268	He was tempted also by the lust of the eyes concerning a miracle, when he said to Him, “Throw Yourself down; for it is written, He shall give His angels charge concerning You, and in their hands they shall bear You up, lest at any time You dash Your foot against a stone.”
2.269	He resisted the tempter, for to do the miracle would only have been to seem either to have yielded or to have done it out of curiosity; for He worked miracles when He willed, as God, yet as healing the weak.
2.270	For if He had done it then, He might have been thought to wish only to do a miracle.
2.271	But lest men should think this, notice what He answered; and when a similar temptation happens to you, you also say the same: “Get behind Me, Satan; for it is written, You shall not tempt the Lord your God”: that is, if I do this I shall tempt God.
2.272	He said what He would have you say.
2.273	When the enemy suggests to you, “What sort of man, what sort of Christian, are you?
2.274	Have you yet done a single miracle, or by your prayers have the dead been raised, or have you healed the feverish? If you were truly of any account, you would do some miracle”—answer and say: “It is written, You shall not tempt the Lord your God”; therefore I will not tempt God, as if I should belong to God if I do a miracle, and not belong to Him if I do none: and what then becomes of His words, “Rejoice, because your names are written in heaven”?
2.275	By “the pride of life,” how was the Lord tempted?
2.276	When he carried Him up to a high place and said to Him, “All these I will give You, if You will fall down and worship me.”
2.277	By the loftiness of an earthly kingdom he wished to tempt the King of all worlds; but the Lord who made heaven and earth trod the devil underfoot.
2.278	What great thing is it for the devil to be conquered by the Lord?
2.279	Then what did He do in the answer He made to the devil but teach you the answer He would have you make?
2.280	“It is written, You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve.”
2.281	Holding these things fast, you will not have the lust of the world; and by not having the lust of the world, neither the lust of the flesh, nor the lust of the eyes, nor the pride of life will subdue you; and you will make room for Charity when she comes, that you may love God.
2.282	Because if love of the world is there, love of God will not be there.
2.283	Rather hold fast the love of God, that as God is forever and ever, so you also may remain forever and ever: because each one is such as his love is.
2.284	Do you love earth? You will be earth.
2.285	Do you love God? What shall I say? You will be a god?
2.286	I dare not say it on my own authority; let us hear the Scriptures: “I have said, You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High.”
2.287	If then you would be gods and sons of the Most High, “Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world.
2.288	If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.
2.289	For all that is in the world is the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, which is not of the Father, but is of the world”: that is, of men, the lovers of the world.
2.290	“And the world passes away, and its lusts; but he who does the will of God abides forever, even as God also abides forever.”
T:3	Homily III (1 John 2:18–27)
3.1	“Children, it is the last hour; and as you have heard that antichrist shall come, even now there are many antichrists; by which we know that it is the last hour.
3.2	They went out from us, but they were not of us; if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us; but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.
3.3	But you have an anointing from the Holy One, and you know all things.
3.4	I write to you, not because you do not know the truth, but because you know it, and that no lie is of the truth.
3.5	Who is a liar but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ?
3.6	[He is antichrist who denies the Father and the Son.]
3.7	Whoever denies the Son, the same has neither the Father nor the Son; and he who acknowledges the Son has both the Father and the Son.
3.8	Therefore let that abide in you which you have heard from the beginning.
3.9	If what you have heard from the beginning remains in you, you also will continue in the Son and in the Father.
3.10	And this is the promise that He has promised us, even eternal life.
3.11	These things I have written to you concerning those who lead you astray; that you may know that you have an anointing, and that the anointing which you have received from Him may abide in you.
3.12	And you do not need anyone to teach you, because His anointing teaches you concerning all things.”
3.13	1. “Children, it is the last hour.”
3.14	In this reading he addresses the children, that they may make haste to grow, because “it is the last hour.”
3.15	The age or stature of the body is not at one’s own will.
3.16	A man does not grow in respect of the flesh when he wills, any more than he is born when he wills; but where being born rests with the will, growth also rests with the will.
3.17	No one is “born of water and the Spirit” unless he is willing.
3.18	Consequently, if he wills, he grows or increases; if he wills, he decreases.
3.19	What is it to grow?
3.20	To go forward by making progress.
3.21	What is it to decrease?
3.22	To go backward by falling away.
3.23	Whoever knows that he is born, let him hear that he is an infant; let him cling eagerly to his mother’s breasts, and he grows quickly.
3.24	Now his mother is the Church, and her breasts are the two Testaments of the Divine Scriptures.
3.25	From these, then, let him suck the milk of all the things that were done in time as signs of spiritual truths for our eternal salvation, so that, being nourished and strengthened, he may attain to the eating of solid food, which is, “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”
3.26	Our milk is Christ in His humility; our solid food, the very same Christ equal with the Father.
3.27	With milk He nourishes you, that He may feed you with bread; for to touch Christ spiritually with the heart is to know that He is equal with the Father.
3.28	2. That is why He forbade Mary to touch Him, and said to her, “Do not touch Me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father.”
3.29	What is this? He gave Himself to be handled by the disciples, and did He shun Mary’s touch?
3.30	Is He not the same who said to the doubting disciple, “Reach here your fingers, and feel the scars”?
3.31	Had He at that time ascended to the Father?
3.32	Then why does He forbid Mary, and say, “Do not touch Me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father”?
3.33	Or are we to say that He was not afraid to be touched by men, but was afraid to be touched by women?
3.34	The touch of Him cleanses all flesh.
3.35	Those to whom He willed first to be made manifest—was He afraid to be handled by them?
3.36	Was not His resurrection announced by women to the men, so that the serpent might be overcome by a kind of counterplot?
3.37	For because he first announced death to man through the woman, therefore life also was announced to men through a woman.
3.38	Then why was He unwilling to be touched, unless it was because He wanted that spiritual touch to be understood?
3.39	The spiritual touch takes place from a pure heart.
3.40	That person reaches Christ with his touch from a pure heart who understands Him as coequal with the Father.
3.41	But whoever does not yet understand Christ’s Godhead reaches only as far as the flesh; he does not reach the Godhead.
3.42	Now what great thing is it to reach only as far as the persecutors reached, who crucified Him?
3.43	But this is the great thing: to understand the Word, God with God, in the beginning, by whom all things were made—as He wanted Himself to be known when He said to Philip, “Have I been with you so long a time, and have you not known Me, Philip?
3.44	He who sees Me sees also the Father.”
3.45	3. But lest anyone be sluggish in going forward, let him hear: “Children, it is the last hour.”
3.46	Go forward, run, grow; “it is the last hour.”
3.47	This same last hour is long; yet it is the last.
3.48	For he has put “hour” for “the last time,” because it is in the last times that our Lord Jesus Christ is to come.
3.49	But some will say, How the last times? How the last hour?
3.50	Certainly antichrist will come first, and then the day of judgment will come.
3.51	John perceived these thoughts: lest people should in some way become secure, and think it was not the last hour because antichrist was still to come, he said to them, “And as you have heard that antichrist is to come, now many antichrists have come.”
3.52	Could it have many antichrists unless it were “the last hour”?
3.53	4. Whom has he called antichrists?
3.54	He goes on and explains.
3.55	“By which we know that it is the last hour.”
3.56	By what? Because “many antichrists have come.
3.57	They went out from us”; see the antichrists!
3.58	“They went out from us”: therefore we mourn the loss.
3.59	Hear the consolation: “But they were not of us.”
3.60	All heretics, all schismatics went out from us, that is, they go out from the Church; but they would not go out if they were of us.
3.61	Therefore, before they went out they were not of us.
3.62	If before they went out they were not of us, many are within, have not gone out, and yet are antichrists.
3.63	We dare to say this: and why, except so that no one, while he is within, may be an antichrist?
3.64	For he is about to describe and mark out the antichrists, and we shall see them now.
3.65	And each person ought to question his own conscience, whether he is an antichrist.
3.66	For antichrist in our language means “contrary to Christ.”
3.67	Not, as some take it, that antichrist is to be so called because he is to come ante Christum, before Christ, that is,
3.68	with Christ to come after him: it does not mean this, nor is it so written, but Antichristus, that is, contrary to Christ.
3.69	Now who is contrary to Christ you already perceive from the apostle’s own explanation, and you understand that none can go out except antichrists; whereas those who are not contrary to Christ can in no way go out.
3.70	For he who is not contrary to Christ holds fast in His body, and is counted with it as a member.
3.71	The members are never contrary to one another.
3.72	The entire body consists of all the members.
3.73	And what does the apostle say concerning the harmony of the members?
3.74	“If one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice with it.”
3.75	If, then, when a member is glorified the other members rejoice with it, and when it suffers all the members suffer, the harmony of the members has no antichrist.
3.76	And there are those who are inwardly in the body of our Lord Jesus Christ in such a way—seeing that His body is still under treatment, and its soundness will not be perfect except in the resurrection of the dead—they are in the body of Christ in such a way as bad humors are.
3.77	When these are vomited up, the body is relieved; so too when bad men go out, then the Church is relieved.
3.78	And when the body vomits and casts them out, one says, “These humors went out of me, but they were not of me.”
3.79	How were they not of me?
3.80	They were not cut out of my flesh, but they oppressed my chest while they were in me.
3.81	5. “They went out from us; but”—do not be sad—“they were not of us.”
3.82	How do you prove this?
3.83	If they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us.
3.84	From this, therefore, you may see that many who are not of us receive the Sacraments with us, receive baptism with us, receive with us what the faithful know they receive—the Blessing, the Eucharist, and whatever there is in the Holy Sacraments: the communion of the very altar they receive with us, and yet they are not of us.
3.85	Temptation proves that they are not of us.
3.86	When temptation comes to them, as if blown by a wind they fly off, because they were not grain.
3.87	But all of them will fly off, as we must often tell you, once the winnowing of the Lord’s threshing floor begins on the day of judgment.
3.88	“They went out from us, but they were not of us; if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us.”
3.89	For would you know, beloved, how very certain this saying is—that those who perhaps have gone out and return are not antichrists, are not contrary to Christ?
3.90	Those who are not antichrists cannot possibly continue outside.
3.91	But it is by his own will that each one is either an antichrist or in Christ.
3.92	Either we are among the members, or among the bad humors.
3.93	He who changes himself for the better is in the body, a member; but he who continues in his badness is a bad humor; and when he has gone out, then those who were oppressed will be relieved.
3.94	“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us; but (they went out) that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.”
3.95	He has added, “that they might be made manifest,” because even when they are within they are not of us; yet they are not manifest, but by going out they are made manifest.
3.96	“And you have an anointing from the Holy One, that you may be manifest to your own selves.”
3.97	The spiritual anointing is the Holy Spirit Himself, of whom the Sacrament is in the visible anointing.
3.98	Of this anointing of Christ he says that all who have it know the bad and the good; and they do not need to be taught, because the anointing itself teaches them.
3.99	6. “I write to you not because you do not know the truth, but because you know it, and that no lie is of the truth.”
3.100	Behold, we are admonished how we may know antichrist.
3.101	What is Christ? Truth.
3.102	He Himself has said, “I am the Truth.”
3.103	But “no lie is of the truth.”
3.104	Consequently, all who lie are not yet of Christ.
3.105	He has not said that some lie is of the truth and some lie is not of the truth.
3.106	Mark the sentence. Do not pamper yourselves, do not flatter yourselves, do not deceive yourselves, do not cheat yourselves: “No lie is of the truth.”
3.107	Let us see, then, how antichrists lie, because there is more than one kind of lying.
3.108	“Who is a liar, but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ?”
3.109	The meaning of the word “Jesus” is one thing, the meaning of the word “Christ” another: though it is one Jesus Christ our Savior, yet “Jesus” is His proper name.
3.110	Just as Moses was so called by his proper name, as Elijah, as Abraham, so our Lord has the name “Jesus” as His proper name; but “Christ” is the name of His sacred office.
3.111	As when we say “Prophet,” as when we say “Priest,” so by the name Christ we are given to understand the Anointed, in whom the redemption of the whole people was to be.
3.112	The coming of this Christ was hoped for by the people of the Jews; and because He came in lowliness, He was not acknowledged; because the stone was small, they stumbled at it and were broken.
3.113	But “the stone grew and became a great mountain”; and what does the Scripture say?
3.114	“Whoever stumbles at this stone shall be broken; and on whomever this stone comes, it will grind him to powder.”
3.115	We must note the difference in the words: it says, he who stumbles shall be broken; but he on whom it comes shall be ground to powder.
3.116	At first, because He came lowly, men stumbled at Him; because He shall come lofty to judgment, on whomever He comes, He will grind him to powder.
3.117	But He will not grind to powder at His future coming that man whom He did not break when He came.
3.118	He who did not stumble at the lowly One shall not dread the lofty One.
3.119	You have heard it briefly, brethren: he who did not stumble at the lowly One shall not dread the lofty One.
3.120	For to all bad men Christ is a stone of stumbling; whatever Christ says is bitter to them.
3.121	7. For hear and see.
3.122	Certainly all who go out from the Church and are cut off from the unity of the Church are antichrists; let no one doubt it; for the apostle himself has marked them: “They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us.”
3.123	Therefore, whoever do not continue with us but go out from us, it is manifest that they are antichrists.
3.124	And how are they proved to be antichrists?
3.125	By lying. “And who is a liar, but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ?”
3.126	Let us ask the heretics: where do you find a heretic who denies that Jesus is the Christ?
3.127	See now, my beloved, a great mystery.
3.128	Notice what the Lord God may have inspired us with, and what I would gladly work into your minds.
3.129	Behold, they went out from us and became Donatists; we ask them whether Jesus is the Christ; they at once confess that Jesus is the Christ.
3.130	If, then, the one who denies that Jesus is the Christ is an antichrist, neither can they call us antichrists, nor we them; therefore neither did they go out from us, nor we from them.
3.131	If, then, we have not gone out from one another, we are in unity; if we are in unity, what does it mean that there are two altars in this city? What does it mean that there are divided houses, divided marriages? That there is a common bed, and a divided Christ?
3.132	He admonishes us; he would have us confess what the truth is: either they went out from us, or we from them.
3.133	But let it not be imagined that we have gone out from them.
3.134	For we have the testament of the Lord’s inheritance; we recite it, and there we find, “I will give You the nations for Your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for Your possession.”
3.135	We hold fast Christ’s inheritance; they do not hold it, for they are not in communion with the whole earth, are not in communion with the universal body redeemed by the blood of the Lord.
3.136	We have the Lord Himself rising from the dead, who presented Himself to be felt by the hands of the doubting disciples; and while they still doubted, He said to them, “It was necessary for Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in His name”—Where? In what direction? To what persons?—“among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.”
3.137	Our minds are set at rest concerning the unity of the inheritance!
3.138	Whoever is not in communion with this inheritance has gone out.
3.139	8. But let us not be made sad: “They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us.”
3.140	If, then, they went out from us, they are antichrists; if they are antichrists, they are liars; if they are liars, they deny that Jesus is the Christ.
3.141	Once more we come back to the difficulty of the question.
3.142	Ask them one by one: they confess that Jesus is the Christ.
3.143	The difficulty that hampers us comes from our taking what is said in the Epistle in too narrow a sense.
3.144	At any rate you see the question; this question brings both us and them to a standstill, if it is not understood.
3.145	Either we are antichrists, or they are antichrists; they call us antichrists and say that we went out from them; we say the same of them.
3.146	But now this epistle has marked out the antichrists by this badge: “Whoever denies that Jesus is the Christ,” that same one “is an antichrist.”
3.147	Now therefore let us inquire who denies; and let us observe not the tongue, but the deeds.
3.148	For if all are asked, all with one mouth confess that Jesus is the Christ.
3.149	Let the tongue keep still for a little while; question the life.
3.150	If we find this—if the Scripture itself tells us that denial is a thing done not only with the tongue but also with deeds—then assuredly we find many antichrists, who with the mouth profess Christ and in their conduct dissent from Christ.
3.151	Where do we find this in Scripture?
3.152	Hear Paul the Apostle; speaking of such people, he says, “For they confess that they know God, but in their deeds they deny Him.”
3.153	We find these also to be antichrists: whoever denies Christ in his deeds is an antichrist.
3.154	I do not listen to what he says, but I look at what life he leads.
3.155	Works speak, and do we require words?
3.156	For where is the bad man who does not wish to talk well?
3.157	But what does the Lord say to such people?
3.158	“You hypocrites, how can you speak good things, while you are evil?”
3.159	Your voices you bring to My ears; I look into your thoughts.
3.160	I see an evil will there, and you make a show of false fruits.
3.161	I know what I must gather, and from where; I do not “gather figs from thistles,” I do not gather “grapes from thorns”; for “every tree is known by its fruit.”
3.162	A more lying antichrist is he who with his mouth professes that Jesus is the Christ and with his deeds denies Him.
3.163	A liar in this, that he says one thing and does another.
3.164	9. Now therefore, brethren, if deeds are to be questioned, we find not only many antichrists who have gone out, but many not yet manifest, who have not gone out at all.
3.165	For as many as the Church has within it who are perjurers, defrauders, given to black arts, consulters of fortune-tellers, adulterers, drunkards, usurers, kidnappers of boys, and all the other vices that we are not able to list—these things are contrary to the teaching of Christ, are contrary to the word of God.
3.166	Now the Word of God is Christ: whatever is contrary to the Word of God is in Antichrist.
3.167	For Antichrist means “contrary to Christ.”
3.168	And would you know how openly these people resist Christ?
3.169	Sometimes it happens that they do some evil, and someone begins to rebuke them; because they dare not blaspheme Christ, they blaspheme His ministers by whom they are rebuked; but if you show them that you are speaking Christ’s words, not your own, they try all they can to convict you of speaking your own words, not Christ’s; if, however, it is manifest that you are speaking Christ’s words, they go even against Christ, they begin to find fault with Christ: “How,” they say, “and why did He make us such as we are?”
3.170	Do not people say this every day, when they are convicted of their deeds?
3.171	Perverted by a depraved will, they accuse their Maker.
3.172	Their Maker cries to them from heaven (for the One who made us is the same who made us new): What did I make you?
3.173	I made man, not avarice; I made man, not robbery; I made man, not adultery.
3.174	You have heard that My works praise Me.
3.175	Out of the mouth of the Three Children—it was the hymn itself that kept them from the fires.
3.176	The works of the Lord praise the Lord; the heaven, the earth, the sea praise Him; all things that are in the heaven praise Him; the angels praise Him, the stars praise Him, the lights praise Him; whatever swims, whatever flies, whatever walks, whatever creeps praises Him; all these praise the Lord.
3.177	Have you heard there that avarice praises the Lord?
3.178	Have you heard that drunkenness praises the Lord?
3.179	That self-indulgence praises Him, that frivolity praises Him?
3.180	Whatever you do not hear giving praise to the Lord in that hymn, the Lord did not make that thing.
3.181	Correct what you have made, that what God made in you may be saved.
3.182	But if you will not, and you love and embrace your sins, you are contrary to Christ.
3.183	Whether you are within or without, you are an antichrist; whether you are within or without, you are chaff.
3.184	But why are you not without?
3.185	Because you have not met with a wind to carry you away.
3.186	10. These things are now manifest, my brethren.
3.187	Let no one say, “I do not worship Christ, but I worship God His Father.”
3.188	“Everyone who denies the Son has neither the Son nor the Father; and he who confesses the Son has both the Son and the Father.”
3.189	He speaks to you who are grain; and let those who were chaff hear, and become grain.
3.190	Let each one, looking well to his own conscience, if he is a lover of the world, be changed; let him become a lover of Christ, that he may not be an antichrist.
3.191	If someone tells him that he is an antichrist, he is angry, he thinks it a wrong done to him; perhaps, if he is told by one who quarrels with him that he is an antichrist, he threatens a lawsuit.
3.192	Christ says to him: Be patient; if you have been falsely spoken of, rejoice with Me, because I also am falsely spoken of by the antichrists; but if you are truly spoken of, come to terms with your own conscience; and if you fear to be called this, fear more to be it.
3.193	11. “Therefore let that abide in you which you have heard from the beginning.
3.194	If what you have heard from the beginning abides in you, you also will abide in the Son and in the Father.
3.195	And this is the promise that He has promised us.”
3.196	For perhaps you might ask about the wages, and say, Behold, “What I have heard from the beginning I keep safe in me, I comply with it; perils, labors, temptations, for the sake of this continuing, I bear up against them all: with what fruit? What wages? What will He give me hereafter, since in this world I see that I labor among temptations?
3.197	I see no rest here: mere mortality weighs down the soul, and the corruptible body presses it down to lower things; but I bear all things, that ‘what I have heard from the beginning’ may ‘remain’ in me, and that I may say to my God, ‘Because of the words of Your lips I have kept hard ways.’”
3.198	To what wages, then?
3.199	Hear, and do not faint.
3.200	If you were fainting in the labors, be strong upon the promised wages.
3.201	Where is the man who will work in a vineyard and let slip from his heart the reward he is to receive?
3.202	Suppose him to have forgotten it: his hands fail.
3.203	The remembrance of the promised wages makes him persevere in the work; and yet the one who promised it is a man who can disappoint your expectation.
3.204	How much stronger ought you to be in God’s field, when He who promised is the Truth, who can neither have any successor, nor die, nor deceive the one to whom the promise was made!
3.205	And what is the promise?
3.206	Let us see what He has promised.
3.207	Is it gold, which men here love so much, or silver?
3.208	Or possessions, on which men lavish gold, however much they love gold?
3.209	Or pleasant lands, spacious houses, many slaves, numerous cattle?
3.210	These are not the wages, so to speak, for which he exhorts us to endure in labor.
3.211	What are these wages called? “Eternal life.”
3.212	You have heard, and in your joy you have cried out: love what you have heard, and you are delivered from your labors into the rest of eternal life.
3.213	Behold, this is what God promises: “eternal life.”
3.214	Behold, this is what God threatens: eternal fire.
3.215	What to those set on the right hand?
3.216	“Come, you blessed of My Father, receive the kingdom prepared for you from the beginning of the world.”
3.217	To those on the left, what?
3.218	“Go into eternal fire, prepared for the devil and his angels.”
3.219	You do not yet love that: at least fear this.
3.220	12. Remember, then, my brethren, that Christ has promised us eternal life: “This,” he says, “is the promise which He has promised us, even eternal life.
3.221	These things I have written to you concerning those who lead you astray.”
3.222	Let no one lead you astray to death: desire the promise of eternal life.
3.223	What can the world promise?
3.224	Let it promise what you will, it makes the promise perhaps to one who will die tomorrow.
3.225	And with what face will you go from here to Him who abides forever?
3.226	“But a powerful man threatens me, so that I must do some evil.”
3.227	What does he threaten?
3.228	Prisons, chains, fires, torments, wild beasts: yes, but not eternal fire?
3.229	Dread what the One Almighty threatens; love what the One Almighty promises; and the whole world becomes worthless in our eyes, whether it promises or terrifies.
3.230	“These things I have written to you concerning those who lead you astray; that you may know that you have an anointing, and that the anointing which we have received from Him may abide in you.”
3.231	In the anointing we have the sacramental sign [of a thing unseen]; the power itself is invisible; the invisible anointing is the Holy Spirit; the invisible anointing is that charity which, in whomever it is, shall be like a root to him: however burning the sun, he cannot wither.
3.232	All that is rooted is nourished by the sun’s warmth, not withered.
