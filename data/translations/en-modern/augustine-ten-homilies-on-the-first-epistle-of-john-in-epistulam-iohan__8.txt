9.167	7. Let us speak first, then, to those who fear God just in the manner of that woman who delights in wickedness; that is, she fears her husband lest he condemn her. To such let us speak first.
9.168	O soul, you who fear God lest He condemn you, just as the woman fears who delights in wickedness—she fears her husband, lest she be condemned by her husband—as you are displeased with this woman, so be displeased with yourself.
9.169	If perhaps you have a wife, would you want your wife to fear you in this way, so that she may not be condemned by you? That, delighting in wickedness, she should be restrained only by the weight of her fear of you, not by condemning her own iniquity?
9.170	You would want her to be chaste so that she may love you, not so that she may fear you.
9.171	Show yourself to God such as you would want your wife to be to you.
9.172	And if you do not yet have a wife, and wish to have one, you would want her to be such.
9.173	And yet what are we saying, brethren?
9.174	That woman whose fear of her husband is the fear of being condemned by her husband perhaps does not commit adultery lest by some means or other it come to her husband’s knowledge, and he deprive her of this temporal light of life; yet the husband can be deceived and kept in ignorance, for he is only human, as she is who can deceive him.
9.175	She fears him from whose eyes she can be hidden: and do you not fear the face of your Husband, which is always upon you?
9.176	“The countenance of the Lord is against those who do evil.”
9.177	She seizes on her husband’s absence, and perhaps is stirred by the delight of adultery; and yet she says to herself, I will not do it: he is indeed absent, but it is hard to keep it from somehow coming to his knowledge.
9.178	She restrains herself lest it come to the knowledge of a mortal man—one who, it is also possible, may never know it; who, it is also possible, may be deceived, so that he will consider a bad woman good, and consider her chaste who is an adulteress: and do you not fear the eyes of Him whom no one can deceive? Do you not fear the presence of Him who cannot be turned away from you?
9.179	Pray God to look upon you, and to turn His face away from your sins: “Turn away Your face from my sins.”
9.180	But how do you deserve that He should turn His face away from your sins, if you do not turn your own face away from your sins?
9.181	For the same voice says in the Psalm: “For I acknowledge my iniquity, and my sin is always before me.”
9.182	Acknowledge it, and He forgives.
9.183	8. We have addressed that soul which still has the fear that does not endure forever, but which love shuts out and casts out: let us also address the soul which now has the fear that is chaste, enduring forever.
9.184	Shall we find that soul, do you think, so that we may address it? Is it here, do you think, in this congregation? Is it, do you think, here in this chancel? Is it here on earth, do you think?
9.185	It cannot but be here; only it is hidden.
9.186	Now it is winter: within, the greenness is in the root.
9.187	Perhaps we may reach the ears of that soul.
9.188	But wherever that soul is, oh, that I could find it, and instead of its listening to me, might myself listen to it!
9.189	It should teach me something, rather than learn from me!
9.190	A holy soul, a soul on fire, and longing for the kingdom of God: that soul it is not I who address, but God Himself addresses it, and thus consoles it while it patiently endures to live here on earth: “You would like Me to come even now, and I know that you wish I would come even now; I know what you are, such that you may wait for My coming without fear; I know that this is a trouble to you: but wait still longer, endure; I am coming, and coming quickly.”
9.191	But to the loving soul time moves slowly.
9.192	Hear her singing, like the lily she is, from among the thorns; hear her sighing and saying, “I will sing, and will understand in a blameless way: when will You come to me?”
9.193	But in a blameless way she may well not fear, because “perfect love casts out fear.”
9.194	And when He has come to her embrace, she still fears, but in the manner of one who feels secure.
9.195	What does she fear?
9.196	She will beware and guard herself against her own iniquity, so that she does not sin again: not lest she be cast into the fire, but lest she be forsaken by Him.
9.197	And there shall be in her—what? The “chaste fear, enduring forever.”
9.198	We have heard the two flutes sounding in unison.
9.199	That one speaks of fear, and this one speaks of fear: but that one, of the fear with which the soul fears lest she be condemned; this one, of the fear with which the soul fears lest she be forsaken.
9.200	That is the fear which charity casts out; this, the fear that endures forever.
9.201	9. “Let us love, because He first loved us.”
9.202	For how could we love, unless He had first loved us?
9.203	By loving we became friends: but He loved us as enemies, so that we might be made friends.
9.204	He first loved us, and gave us the gift of loving Him.
9.205	We did not yet love Him: by loving we are made beautiful.
9.206	If a deformed and ill-featured man loves a beautiful woman, what shall he do?
9.207	Or what shall a woman do if, being deformed and ill-featured and dark-complexioned, she loves a beautiful man?
9.208	Can she become beautiful by loving?
9.209	Can he become handsome by loving?
9.210	He loves a beautiful woman, and when he sees himself in a mirror, he is ashamed to lift his face to her, his lovely one, with whom he is in love.
9.211	What shall he do to become beautiful?
9.212	Does he wait for good looks to come?
9.213	No, rather, by waiting, old age is added to him and makes him uglier.
9.214	There is nothing, then, to be done, no way to advise him, except that he should restrain himself and not presume to love unequally; or if perhaps he does love her and wishes to take her as his wife, let him love chastity in her, not the face of the flesh.
9.215	But our soul, my brethren, is unlovely because of iniquity: by loving God it becomes lovely.
9.216	What a love that must be, which makes the lover beautiful!
9.217	But God is always lovely, never unlovely, never changeable.
9.218	He who is always lovely first loved us; and what were we when He loved us but foul and unlovely?
9.219	But not to leave us foul; no, but to change us, and to make us lovely instead of unlovely.
9.220	How shall we become lovely?
9.221	By loving Him who is always lovely.
9.222	As love increases in you, so loveliness increases: for love itself is the beauty of the soul.
9.223	“Let us love, because He first loved us.”
9.224	Hear the apostle Paul: “But God showed His love in us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us”: the just for the unjust, the beautiful for the foul.
9.225	How do we find Jesus beautiful?
9.226	“You are beautiful in loveliness beyond the sons of men; grace is poured upon Your lips.”
9.227	Why so? See again why it is that He is fair: “Beautiful in loveliness beyond the sons of men”: because “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”
9.228	But in taking flesh, He took upon Himself, as it were, your foulness, that is, your mortality, so that He might adapt Himself to you and become suited to you, and stir you up to love the beauty within.
9.229	Where then in Scripture do we find Jesus uncomely and deformed, as we have found Him comely and “beautiful in loveliness beyond the sons of men”? Where do we also find Him deformed?
9.230	Ask Isaiah: “And we saw Him, and He had no form nor comeliness.”
9.231	Here now are two flutes which seem to make discordant sounds: yet one Spirit breathes into both.
9.232	By this one it is said, “Beautiful in loveliness beyond the sons of men”; by that one it is said in Isaiah, “We saw Him, and He had no form nor comeliness.”
9.233	Both flutes are filled by one Spirit; they make no dissonance.
9.234	Do not turn away your ears; apply your understanding.
9.235	Let us ask the apostle Paul, and let him explain to us the harmony of the two flutes.
9.236	Let him sound for us the note, “Beautiful in loveliness beyond the sons of men.—Who, being in the form of God, did not think it robbery to be equal with God.”
9.237	Let him sound for us also the note, “We saw Him, and He had no form nor comeliness.—He made Himself of no reputation, taking upon Himself the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man.
9.238	He had no form nor comeliness,” so that He might give you form and comeliness.
9.239	What form? What comeliness?
9.240	The love which is in charity: so that, loving, you may run; running, you may love.
9.241	You are fair now: but do not rest your gaze upon yourself, lest you lose what you have received; let your gaze end in Him by whom you were made fair.
9.242	Be fair only to the end that He may love you.
9.243	But direct your whole aim to Him, run to Him, seek His embraces, fear to depart from Him, so that there may be in you the chaste fear which endures forever.
9.244	“Let us love, because He first loved us.”
9.245	10. “If anyone says, I love God.”
9.246	What God? Why do we love?
9.247	“Because He first loved us,” and gave us the power to love.
9.248	He loved us ungodly, to make us godly; loved us unrighteous, to make us righteous; loved us sick, to make us whole.
9.249	Ask each individual; let him tell you whether he loves God.
9.250	He cries out, he confesses: I love; God knows.
9.251	There is another question to be asked.
9.252	“If anyone says, I love God, and hates his brother, he is a liar.”
9.253	How do you prove that he is a liar?
9.254	Listen. “For he who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see?”
9.255	What then? Does he who loves a brother love God also?
9.256	He must of necessity love God; he must of necessity love Him who is Love itself.
9.257	Can anyone love his brother and not love Love?
9.258	Of necessity he must love Love.
9.259	What then? Because he loves Love, does it follow that he loves God?
9.260	Certainly it follows.
9.261	In loving Love, he loves God.
9.262	Or have you forgotten what you said a little while ago, “Love is God”?
9.263	If “Love is God,” whoever loves Love loves God.
9.264	Love your brother, then, and feel assured.
9.265	You cannot say, “I love my brother, but I do not love God.”
9.266	Just as you lie if you say “I love God” when you do not love your brother, so you are deceived when you say, I love my brother, if you think that you do not love God.
9.267	You who love your brother must of necessity love Love itself: but “Love is God”; therefore whoever loves his brother must of necessity love God.
9.268	But if you do not love the brother whom you see, how can you love God whom you do not see?
9.269	Why does he not see God?
9.270	Because he does not have Love itself.
9.271	That he does not see God is because he does not have love; that he does not have love is because he does not love his brother.
9.272	The reason, then, why he does not see God is that he does not have Love.
9.273	For if he has Love, he sees God, for “Love is God”: and that eye is being more and more purified by love, to see that Unchangeable Substance in whose presence he shall always rejoice, which he shall enjoy forever, when he is joined with the angels.
9.274	Only let him run now, so that he may at last have gladness in his own country.
9.275	Let him not love his pilgrimage, not love the road: let everything be bitter except Him who calls us, until we hold Him fast and say what is said in the Psalm: “You have destroyed all who go whoring away from You”—and who are they that go whoring? Those who go away and love the world. But what will you do? He goes on and says: “But for me it is good to cling to God.”
9.276	All my good is to cling to God, freely.
9.277	For if you question him and say, For what do you cling to Him? and he should say, That He may give me—Give you what?
9.278	It is He who made the heaven, He who made the earth: what shall He give you?
9.279	You are already clinging to Him: find something better, and He will give it to you.
9.280	11. “For he who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see?
9.281	And this commandment we have from Him, that he who loves God should love his brother also.”
9.282	Marvelously fine talk it was, that you said, “I love God,” and you hate your brother!
9.283	O murderer, how do you love God?
9.284	Have you not heard above in this very epistle, “He who hates his brother is a murderer”?
9.285	Yes, but I truly do love God, however much I hate my brother.
9.286	You truly do not love God, if you hate your brother.
9.287	And now I will make it good by another proof.
9.288	This same apostle has said, “He gave us a commandment that we should love one another.”
9.289	How can you be said to love Him whose commandment you hate?
9.290	Who will say, I love the emperor, but I hate his laws?
9.291	The emperor recognizes whether you love him by this: that his laws are observed throughout the provinces.
9.292	Our Emperor’s law—what is it?
9.293	“A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.”
9.294	You say, then, that you love Christ: keep His commandment, and love your brother.
9.295	But if you do not love your brother, how can you be said to love Him whose commandment you despise?
9.296	Brethren, I am never satisfied with speaking of charity in the name of the Lord.
9.297	In the measure that you have an insatiable desire for this thing, in that measure we hope the thing itself is growing in you and casting out fear, so that there may remain that chaste fear which is permanent forever.
9.298	Let us endure the world, endure tribulations, endure the stumbling blocks of temptations.
9.299	Let us not depart from the way; let us hold to the unity of the Church, hold to Christ, hold to charity.
9.300	Let us not be torn away from the members of His Bride, not be torn away from faith, so that we may glory in His coming; and we shall securely abide in Him, now by faith, then by sight, of whom we have so great a pledge, even the gift of the Holy Spirit.
T:10	Homily X (1 John 5:1–3)
10.1	“Whoever believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God; and everyone that loves Him that begot Him loves Him also that is begotten of Him.
10.2	By this we know that we love the children of God, because we love God and do His commandments.
10.3	For this is the love of God: that we keep His commandments.”
10.4	1. I suppose you remember, those of you who were present yesterday, what point in the course of this epistle our exposition has reached: namely, “He who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see?
10.5	And this commandment we have from Him, that he who loves God should love his brother also.”
10.6	Thus far we have discoursed.
10.7	Let us see, then, what comes next in order.
10.8	“Whoever believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God.”
10.9	Who is he that does not believe that Jesus is the Christ?
10.10	He who does not live as Christ commanded.
10.11	For many say, “I believe”: but faith without works does not save.
10.12	Now the work of faith is love, as Paul the apostle says, “And faith which works by love.”
10.13	Your past works, indeed, before you believed, were either none, or if they seemed good, were worth nothing.
10.14	For if they were none, you were like a man without feet, or with sore feet, unable to walk; but if they seemed good, before you believed, you did indeed run, but by running aside from the way you went astray instead of coming to the goal.
10.15	It is for us, then, both to run, and to run in the way.
10.16	He who runs aside from the way runs to no purpose, or rather runs only to toil.
10.17	He goes the more astray, the more he runs aside from the way.
10.18	What is the way by which we run?
10.19	Christ has told us, “I am the Way.”
10.20	What is the home to which we run?
10.21	“I am the Truth.”
10.22	By Him you run, to Him you run, in Him you rest.
10.23	But so that we might run by Him, He reached even to us: for we were far off, foreigners in a far country.
10.24	It was not enough that we were in a far country; we were also so feeble that we could not stir.
10.25	As a Physician, He came to the sick; as a Way, He extended Himself to those who were in a far country.
10.26	Let us be saved by Him; let us walk in Him.
10.27	This is what it is to “believe that Jesus is the Christ,” as Christians believe, who are Christians not in name only, but in deeds and in life—not as the devils believe.
10.28	For “the devils also believe and tremble,” as the Scripture tells us.
10.29	What more could the devils believe than to say, “We know who You are, the Son of God”?
10.30	What the devils said, Peter also said.
10.31	When the Lord asked them who He was, and whom men said that He was, the disciples answered Him, “Some say that You are John the Baptist; some, Elijah; and others, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets.
10.32	He says to them, But whom do you say that I am?
10.33	And Peter answered and said, You are the Christ, the Son of the Living God.”
10.34	And this he heard from the Lord: “Blessed are you, Simon Barjona; for flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but My Father in heaven.”
10.35	See what praises follow this faith.
10.36	“You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build My Church.”
10.37	What does “Upon this rock I will build My Church” mean?
10.38	Upon this faith; upon what has been said, “You are the Christ, the Son of the Living God.
10.39	Upon this rock,” He says, “I will build My Church.”
10.40	Mighty praise! So then, Peter says, “You are the Christ, the Son of the Living God”; the devils also say, “We know who You are, the Son of God, the Holy One of God.”
10.41	Peter said this, and the devils said this too: the words the same, the mind not the same.
10.42	And how is it clear that Peter said this with love?
10.43	Because a Christian’s faith is with love, but a devil’s is without love.
10.44	How without love? Peter said this so that he might embrace Christ; the devils said it so that Christ might depart from them.
10.45	For before they said, “We know who You are, the Son of God,” they said, “What have we to do with You?
10.46	Why have You come to destroy us before the time?”
10.47	It is one thing, then, to confess Christ so that you may hold Christ, another thing to confess Christ so that you may drive Christ away from you.
10.48	So then you see that in the sense in which he says here, “Whoever believes,” it is a faith of one’s own, not a faith such as one has in common with many.
10.49	Therefore, brethren, let none of the heretics say to you, “We also believe.”
10.50	For this is why I have given you an example from the case of the devils: so that you may not rejoice in the words of believing, but examine well the deeds of the life.
10.51	2. Let us see, then, what it is to believe in Christ; what it is to believe that Jesus is the Christ.
10.52	He proceeds: “Whoever believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God.”
10.53	But what is it to believe that?
10.54	“And everyone that loves Him that begot Him loves Him also that is begotten of Him.”
10.55	To faith he has immediately joined love, because faith without love is worth nothing.
10.56	With love, it is the faith of a Christian; without love, the faith of a devil: but those who do not believe are worse than devils, more stupid than devils.
10.57	Some man will not believe in Christ: so far, he is not even on a par with the devils.
10.58	A person does now believe in Christ, but hates Christ: he has the confession of faith in fear of punishment, not in love of the crown; so the devils also feared to be punished.
10.59	Add love to this faith, so that it may become a faith such as the apostle Paul speaks of, a “faith which works by love”: you have found a Christian, found a citizen of Jerusalem, found a fellow citizen of the angels, found a pilgrim sighing on the way: join yourself to him, he is your fellow traveler; run with him, if indeed you also are this.
10.60	“Everyone that loves Him that begot Him loves Him also that is begotten of Him.”
10.61	Who “begot”? The Father.
10.62	Who “is begotten”? The Son.
10.63	What then does he say?
10.64	“Everyone that loves the Father loves the Son.”
10.65	3. “In this we know that we love the sons of God.”
10.66	What is this, brethren?
10.67	Just now he was speaking of the Son of God, not of sons of God: see, here one Christ was set before us to contemplate, and we were told, “Whoever believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God: and everyone that loves Him that begot,” that is, the Father, “loves Him also that is begotten of Him,” that is, the Son, our Lord Jesus Christ.
10.68	And he goes on: “In this we know that we love the sons of God,” as if he had been about to say, “In this we know that we love the Son of God.”
10.69	He has said “the sons of God,” whereas just before he was speaking of the Son of God—because the sons of God are the Body of the only Son of God, and since He is the Head and we the members, it is one Son of God.
10.70	Therefore he who loves the sons of God loves the Son of God, and he who loves the Son of God loves the Father; nor can anyone love the Father unless he loves the Son, and he who loves the sons also loves the Son of God.
10.71	What sons of God?
10.72	The members of the Son of God.
10.73	And by loving he himself becomes a member, and through love comes to be in the frame of the body of Christ, so that there shall be one Christ, loving Himself.
10.74	For when the members love one another, the body loves itself.
10.75	“And whether one member suffers, all the members suffer along with it; or one member is honored, all the members rejoice along with it.”
10.76	And then he goes on to say, “Now you are the body of Christ, and members.”
10.77	John was speaking just before of brotherly love, and said, “He who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see?”
10.78	But if you love your brother, perhaps you love your brother and do not love Christ?
10.79	How could that be, when you love members of Christ?
10.80	When therefore you love members of Christ, you love Christ; when you love Christ, you love the Son of God; when you love the Son of God, you love the Father also.
10.81	Love, therefore, cannot be separated into parts.
10.82	Choose what you will love; the rest follow you.
10.83	Suppose you say, I love God alone, God the Father.
10.84	You lie: if you love, you do not love Him alone; but if you love the Father, you love the Son also.
10.85	Look, you say, I love the Father, and I love the Son: but only this—God the Father and God the Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, who ascended into heaven and sits at the right hand of the Father, that Word by whom all things were made, and “the Word was made flesh, and dwelt in us”: this alone I love.
10.86	You lie; for if you love the Head, you love the members also; but if you do not love the members, neither do you love the Head.
10.87	Do you not tremble at the voice uttered by the Head from heaven on behalf of His members, “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting ME?”
10.88	The persecutor of His members He called His persecutor; His lover, the lover of His members.
10.89	Now what His members are, you know, brethren: none other than the Church of God.
10.90	“In this we know that we love the sons of God, in that we love God.”
10.91	And how? Are not the sons of God one thing, and God Himself another?
10.92	But he who loves God loves His precepts.
10.93	And what are the precepts of God?
10.94	“A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.”
10.95	Let no one excuse himself by another love, for another love; so, and only so, is it with this love: as the love itself is bound together in one, so it makes one all that hang upon it, and like fire it melts them down into one.
10.96	It is gold: the lump is melted and becomes some one thing.
10.97	But unless the heat of charity is applied, there can be no melting down of many into one.
10.98	“That we love God”—by this “we know that we love the sons of God.”
10.99	4. And by what do we know that we love the sons of God?
10.100	By this: “that we love God, and do His commandments.”
10.101	We sigh here, because of the hardness of doing the commandments of God.
10.102	Hear what follows. O man, at what do you toil in loving?
10.103	In loving avarice. What you love is loved with toil: there is no toil in loving God.
10.104	Avarice will command you to labors, perils, severe hardships and tribulations; and you will do its bidding.
10.105	To what end? So that you may have that with which you will fill your chest, and lose your peace of mind.
10.106	Perhaps you felt more secure before you had it than since you began to have it.
10.107	See what avarice has commanded you.
10.108	You have filled your house, and are in dread of robbers; you have gotten gold, and lost your sleep.
10.109	See what avarice has commanded you.
10.110	Do it—and you did.
10.111	What does God command you?
10.112	Love Me. You love gold; you will seek gold, and perhaps not find it: whoever seeks Me, I am with him.
10.113	You will love honor, and perhaps not attain it: who ever loved Me and did not attain?
10.114	God says to you: You would make yourself a patron, or a powerful friend; you seek a way to his favor by means of some other person of lower rank.
10.115	Love Me, God says to you: favor with Me is not had by working through someone else; your love itself makes Me present to you.
10.116	What is sweeter than this love, brethren?
10.117	It is not without reason that you heard just now in the Psalm, “The unrighteous told me of delights, but not as Your law is, O Lord.”
10.118	What is the Law of God?
10.119	The commandment of God.
10.120	What is the commandment of God?
10.121	That “new commandment,” which is called new because it makes new: “A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.”
10.122	Hear that this is the law of God.
10.123	The apostle says, “Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ.”
10.124	This, even this, is the consummation of all our works: love.
10.125	In it is the end: for this we run; to it we run; when we have come to it, we shall rest.
10.126	5. You have heard in the Psalm, “I have seen the end of all perfection.”
10.127	He has said, I have seen the end of all perfection: what had he seen?
10.128	Do we think that he had climbed to the peak of some very high and pointed mountain, and looked out from there and seen the compass of the earth and the circles of the round world, and therefore said, “I have seen the end of all perfection”?
10.129	If this is a thing to be praised, let us ask the Lord for eyes of flesh so sharp-sighted that we shall need only some exceedingly high mountain on earth, so that from its summit we may see the end of all perfection.
10.130	Do not go far: look, I tell you, it is here; climb the mountain, and see the end.
10.131	Christ is the Mountain; come to Christ: from there you see the end of all perfection.
10.132	What is this end?
10.133	Ask Paul: “But the end of the commandment is charity, from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith without pretense”; and in another place, “Charity is the fullness,” or fulfillment, “of the law.”
10.134	What is so finished and complete as “fullness”?
10.135	For, brethren, the apostle here uses “end” in a way of praise.
10.136	Think not of consumption, but of consummation.
10.137	For it is in one sense that one says, I have finished my bread, and in another, I have finished my coat.
10.138	I have finished the bread by eating it; the coat, by making it.
10.139	In both cases the word is “end,” “finish”: but the bread is finished by being consumed, the coat is finished by being made; the bread, so as to be no more; the coat, so as to be complete.
10.140	In this sense, therefore, take this word “end” also, when the Psalm is read and you hear it said, “On the end, a Psalm of David.”
10.141	You are forever hearing this in the Psalms, and you should know what you hear.
10.142	What does “On the end” mean?—“For Christ is the end of the law to everyone who believes.”
10.143	And what does “Christ is the end” mean?
10.144	Because Christ is God, and “the end of the commandment is charity,” and “Charity is God”: because Father and Son and Holy Spirit are One.
10.145	There He is the End for you; elsewhere He is the Way.
10.146	Do not get stuck in the way, and so never come to the end.
10.147	Whatever else you come to, pass beyond it, until you come to the end.
10.148	What is the end?
10.149	It is good for me to “hold fast to God.”
10.150	Have you laid fast hold on God? You have finished the way: you shall abide in your own country.
10.151	Mark well! Someone seeks money: let it not be the end for you; pass on, as a traveler in a strange land.
10.152	But if you love it, you are entangled by avarice; avarice will be shackles on your feet: you can make no more progress.
10.153	Pass this also, therefore: seek the end.
10.154	You seek health of body: still, do not stop there.
10.155	For what is it, this health of the body, which death brings to an end, which sickness weakens—a feeble, mortal, fleeting thing?
10.156	Seek it, indeed, lest perhaps ill health hinder your good works: but for that very reason the end is not there, for it is sought for the sake of something else.
10.157	Whatever is sought for the sake of something else, the end is not there: whatever is loved for its own sake, and freely, the end is there.
10.158	You seek honors; perhaps you seek them in order to do something, so that you may accomplish something and so please God: do not love the honor itself, lest you stop there.
10.159	Do you seek praise? If you seek God’s, you do well; if you seek your own, you do ill; you stop short on the way.
10.160	But look, you are loved, you are praised: do not count it joy when you are praised in yourself; be praised in the Lord, so that you may sing, “In the Lord shall my soul be praised.”
10.161	You deliver some good discourse, and your discourse is praised.
10.162	Let it not be praised as yours; the end is not there.
10.163	If you set the end there, there is an end of you: but an end, not so that you are perfected, but so that you are consumed.
10.164	Then let your discourse not be praised as coming from you, as being yours.
10.165	But how should it be praised? As the Psalm says, “In God will I praise the discourse, in God will I praise the word.”
10.166	By this, what follows there will come to pass in you: “In God have I hoped; I will not fear what man can do to me.”
10.167	For when all that is yours is praised in God, there is no fear that your praise will be lost, since God does not fail.
10.168	Pass this also, therefore.
10.169	6. See, brethren, how many things we pass in which the end is not.
10.170	These we use as by the way; we take, as it were, our refreshment at the resting places on our journey, and pass on.
10.171	Where, then, is the end?
10.172	“Beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be”; this is said here, in this epistle.
10.173	As yet, then, we are on the way; as yet, wherever we come, we must pass on, until we attain to some end.
10.174	“We know that when He shall appear, we shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is.
10.175	That is the end; there, perpetual praising; there, Alleluia always without fail.
10.176	This, then, is the end he has spoken of in the Psalm: “I have seen the end of all perfection”; and as though it were said to him, What is the end you have seen?
10.177	“Your commandment, exceedingly broad.”
10.178	This is the end: the breadth of the commandment.
10.179	The breadth of the commandment is charity, because where charity is, there is no confinement.
10.180	In this breadth, this wide room, was the apostle when he said, “Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians, our heart is enlarged: you are not confined in us.”
10.181	In this, then, is “Your commandment exceedingly broad.”
10.182	What is the broad commandment?
10.183	“A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.”
10.184	Charity, then, is not confined.
10.185	Would you rather not be confined here on earth?
10.186	Dwell in the broad room.
10.187	For whatever man may do to you, he shall not confine you, because you love what man cannot harm: you love God, love the brotherhood, love the law of God, love the Church of God: it shall be forever.
10.188	You labor here on earth, but you shall come to the promised enjoyment.
10.189	Who can take from you what you love?
10.190	If no one can take from you what you love, you sleep secure: or rather, you keep watch secure, lest by sleeping you lose what you love.
10.191	For not without reason is it said, “Enlighten my eyes, lest at any time I sleep in death.”
10.192	Those who shut their eyes against charity fall asleep in the lusts of carnal delights.
10.193	Be wakeful, therefore. For these are the delights: to eat, to drink, to revel in luxury, to play, to hunt; all evils follow these vain pomps.
10.194	Are we ignorant that they are delights? Who can deny that they delight?
10.195	But the law of God is more beloved.
10.196	Cry out against such persuaders: “The unrighteous have told me of delights: but not such as Your law, O Lord.”
10.197	This delight remains. It not only remains as the goal to which you may come, but also calls you back when you flee.
