10.198	7. “For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments.”
10.199	You have already heard, “On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.”
10.200	See how He would not have you divide yourself over a multitude of pages: “On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.”
10.201	On what two commandments?
10.202	“You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind.
10.203	And, you shall love your neighbor as yourself.
10.204	On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.”
10.205	See here what commandments this whole epistle speaks of.
10.206	Therefore hold fast to love, and set your minds at rest.
10.207	Why are you afraid that you may do evil to someone?
10.208	Who does evil to the man he loves?
10.209	Love: it is impossible to do this without doing good.
10.210	But perhaps you rebuke him?
10.211	It is kindness that does it, not fierceness.
10.212	But perhaps you beat him?
10.213	You do this for discipline’s sake, because the kindness of your love will not let you leave him undisciplined.
10.214	And indeed these different and contrary results somehow come about: sometimes hatred uses winning ways, and charity shows itself fierce.
10.215	A person hates his enemy and feigns friendship for him: he sees him doing some evil, and he praises him; he wants him to go headlong, wants him to go blindly over the precipice of his lusts, perhaps never to return; he praises him, “For the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul”; he applies to him the ointment of flattery; see, he hates, and he praises.
10.216	Another sees his friend doing something of the same sort; he calls him back; if he will not listen, he uses even words of reproof, he scolds, he quarrels: there are times when it comes to this, that one must even quarrel!
10.217	See, hatred shows itself winningly gentle, and charity quarrels!
10.218	Do not fix your attention on the words of seeming kindness, or on the seeming cruelty of the rebuke; look into the vein they come from; seek the root from which they proceed.
10.219	The one is gentle and smooth in order to deceive; the other quarrels in order to correct.
10.220	Well then, brethren, it is not for us to enlarge your heart: obtain from God the gift of loving one another.
10.221	Love all men, even your enemies, not because they are your brethren, but so that they may be your brethren; so that you may at all times be on fire with brotherly love, whether toward him who has become your brother, or toward your enemy, so that by being loved he may become your brother.
10.222	Wherever you love a brother, you love a friend.
10.223	Now he is with you, now he is bound to you in unity, yes, catholic unity.
10.224	If you are living rightly, you love a brother made out of an enemy.
10.225	But you love some man who has not yet believed in Christ, or, if he has believed, believes as the devils do: you rebuke his vanity.
10.226	Love him, and that with a brotherly love: he is not yet a brother, but you love him so that he may be a brother.
10.227	Well then, all our love is a brotherly love, toward Christians, toward all His members.
10.228	The discipline of charity, my brethren—its strength, flowers, fruit, beauty, pleasantness, food, drink, nourishment, embracing—has no satiety in it.
10.229	If it delights us so while we are in a foreign land, how we shall rejoice in our own country!
10.230	8. Let us run then, my brethren, let us run, and love Christ.
10.231	What Christ? Jesus Christ.
10.232	Who is He? The Word of God.
10.233	And how did He come to the sick?
10.234	“The Word was made flesh, and dwelt in us.”
10.235	What the Scripture foretold is fulfilled, then: “Christ must suffer, and rise again from the dead on the third day.”
10.236	His body, where is it?
10.237	His members, where do they toil?
10.238	Where must you be, so that you may be under your Head?
10.239	“And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in His name through all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.”
10.240	There let your charity be spread abroad.
10.241	Christ says, and the Psalm—that is, the Spirit of God—says, “Your commandment is exceedingly broad”; and yet, if you please, some man would have charity confined to Africa!
10.242	Extend your charity over the whole earth if you would love Christ, for Christ’s members are throughout all the earth.
10.243	If you love only a part, you are divided; if you are divided, you are not in the body; if you are not in the body, you are not under the Head.
10.244	What does it profit you that you believe and blaspheme?
10.245	You adore Him in the Head, and blaspheme Him in the Body.
10.246	He loves His Body.
10.247	If you have cut yourself off from His Body, the Head has not cut itself off from its Body.
10.248	To no purpose do you honor Me, your Head cries to you from on high, to no purpose do you honor Me.
10.249	It is just as if a man wanted to kiss your head and tread on your feet: perhaps with hobnailed boots he would crush your feet while he clasps your head and kisses it: would you not cry out in the middle of the words with which he honors you, and say, What are you doing, man? You are treading on me.
10.250	You would not say, You are treading on my head; for the head he honored; but the head would cry out more for the members trodden on than for itself because it was honored.
10.251	Does not the head itself cry out, I want none of your honor; do not tread on me?
10.252	Now say, if you can, How have I trodden on you? Say that to the head: I wanted to kiss you, I wanted to embrace you.
10.253	But do you not see, you fool, that what you would embrace reaches, by virtue of a certain unity which binds the whole frame together, to that which you tread on?
10.254	Above you honor me, below you tread on me.
10.255	What you tread on feels more pain than what you honor feels joy.
10.256	In what way does the tongue cry out?
10.257	“It hurts me.” It does not say, “It hurts my foot,” but, “It hurts me,” it says.
10.258	O tongue, who has touched you? Who has struck? Who has goaded? Who has pricked?
10.259	No one; but I am bound together with the parts that are trodden on.
10.260	How would you have me not be pained, when I am not separate?
10.261	9. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, ascending into heaven on the fortieth day, commended to us His Body, where it would continue to lie, for this reason: because He saw that many would honor Him because He has ascended into heaven, and He saw that their honoring Him is useless if they trample on His members here on earth.
10.262	And so that no one would err and, while adoring the Head in heaven, trample on the feet on earth, He told us where His members would be.
10.263	For when He was about to ascend, He spoke His last words on earth: after those same words He spoke no more on earth.
10.264	The Head, about to ascend into heaven, commended His members on earth to us and departed.
10.265	From then on you do not find Christ speaking on earth; you find Him speaking, but from heaven.
10.266	And even from heaven, why?
10.267	Because His members on earth were being trodden on.
10.268	For to Saul the persecutor He said from on high, “Saul, Saul, why do you persecute Me?”
10.269	I have ascended into heaven, but I still lie on earth: here I sit at the right hand of the Father, but there I still hunger, thirst, and am a stranger.
10.270	In what way, then, did He commend His Body to us when He was about to ascend into heaven?
10.271	When the disciples asked Him, saying, “Lord, will You at this time present Yourself, and when will the kingdom of Israel be?”
10.272	He answered, now at the point of departing, “It is not for you to know the time which the Father has put in His own power; but you shall receive strength from the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you shall be witnesses to Me.”
10.273	See where His Body is spread abroad; see where He will not be trodden on: “You shall be witnesses to Me, to Jerusalem, and to Judea, and even to all the earth.”
10.274	See where I lie, I who am ascending!
10.275	For I ascend, because I am the Head: My Body still lies below.
10.276	Where does it lie? Throughout the whole earth.
10.277	Beware that you do not strike, beware that you do not hurt, beware that you do not trample: these are the last words of Christ as He was about to go into heaven.
10.278	Look at a sick man languishing on his bed, lying in his house, worn out with sickness, at death’s door, his soul as it were already between his teeth: anxious, it may be, about something dear to him, which he greatly loves, when it comes into his mind he calls his heirs and says to them, I beg you, do this.
10.279	He, as it were, holds back his soul by a violent effort, so that it may not depart before those words are made sure.
10.280	When he has dictated those last words, he breathes out his soul, and he is carried as a corpse to the tomb.
10.281	His heirs—how do they remember the last words of the dying man?
10.282	What if someone should stand up and say to them, Do not do it: what would they say?
10.283	“What? Shall I not do what my father, in the act of breathing out his soul, commanded me with his last breath, the last word of his that sounded in my ears as my father was departing this life?
10.284	Whatever other words of his I may disregard, his last ones have a stronger hold on me: since then I never saw him again, never again heard him speak.”
10.285	Brethren, think with Christian hearts: if to a man’s heirs his words, spoken when he was about to go to the tomb, are so sweet, so welcome, so weighty, what must we think of the last words of Christ, spoken not when He was about to go back to the tomb, but to ascend into heaven!
10.286	As for the man who lived and is dead, his soul is hurried off to other places, his body is laid in the earth, and whether these words of his are carried out or not makes no difference to him: he now has something else to do, or something else to suffer: either he rejoices in Abraham’s bosom, or in eternal fire he longs for a drop of water, while his corpse lies there without feeling in the tomb; and yet the last words of the dying man are kept.
10.287	What can those expect who do not keep the last words of Him who sits in heaven, who sees from on high whether they are despised or not despised?
10.288	The words of Him who said, “Saul, Saul, why do you persecute Me?”—who keeps account, until the judgment, of all that He sees His members suffer?
10.289	10. And what have we done? they say.
10.290	We are the persecuted, not the persecutors.
10.291	You are the persecutors, you wretched men.
10.292	In the first place, in that you have divided the Church.
10.293	The sword of the tongue is mightier than the sword of steel.
10.294	Hagar, Sarah’s maid, was proud, and she was afflicted by her mistress for her pride.
10.295	That was discipline, not punishment.
10.296	Accordingly, when she had gone away from her mistress, what did the angel say to her?
10.297	“Return to your mistress.”
10.298	Then, O carnal soul, like a proud bondwoman, suppose you have suffered some trouble for discipline’s sake, why do you rage?
10.299	“Return to your mistress”: hold fast the peace of the Church.
10.300	See, the Gospels are brought out, we read where the Church is spread abroad: men argue against us and say to us, “Betrayers!”
10.301	Betrayers of what? Christ commends His Church to us, and you do not believe: shall I believe you when you revile my parents?
10.302	Would you have me believe you about the “betrayers”?
10.303	First believe Christ yourself.
10.304	What is worth believing?
10.305	Christ is God, you are man: which ought to be believed first?
10.306	Christ has spread His Church abroad over all the earth: I say it—despise me; the gospel speaks—beware.
10.307	What does the gospel say?
10.308	“It was necessary for Christ to suffer, and to rise again from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in His name.”
10.309	Where there is remission of sins, there the Church is.
10.310	How the Church? Why, to her it was said, “To you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven, and whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven.”
10.311	Where is this remission of sins spread abroad?
10.312	“Through all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.”
10.313	See, believe Christ! But because you are well aware that if you believe Christ you will have nothing to say about “betrayers,” you insist on having me believe you when you speak evil against my parents, rather than believing for yourself what Christ foretold!
