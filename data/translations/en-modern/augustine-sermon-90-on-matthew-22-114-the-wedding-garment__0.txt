0.1	1. All the faithful know the marriage of the king’s son and his feast, and the Lord’s Table is spread and open to all of them who are willing.
0.2	But it matters for each one to see how he approaches, even when he is not forbidden to approach It.
0.3	For the Holy Scriptures teach us that there are two feasts of the Lord: one to which the good and the evil come, the other to which the evil do not come.
0.4	So then the feast of which we have just now heard when the Gospel was being read has both good and evil guests.
0.5	All who excused themselves from this feast are evil; but not all those who entered in are good.
0.6	You therefore who are the good guests at this feast I address, you who have in your minds the words, “He who eats and drinks unworthily eats and drinks judgment to himself.”
0.7	All of you who are such I address, that you do not look for the good outside, and that you bear with the evil within.
0.8	2. I do not doubt that you wish to hear, beloved, who they are of whom I have spoken in my address, saying that they should not look for the good outside and should bear with the evil within.
0.9	If all within are evil, whom do I address?
0.10	If all within are good, whom did I advise them to bear with as evil?
0.11	Let me first, then, with the Lord’s help, get out of this difficulty as best I can.
0.12	If you consider “good” perfectly and strictly speaking, no one is good but God alone.
0.13	You have the Lord saying most plainly, “Why do you call Me good? No one is good but One, that is, God.”
0.14	How then can that marriage feast have good and bad guests, if “no one is good but God alone”?
0.15	In the first place you ought to know that in a certain sense we are all evil.
0.16	Yes, doubtless in a certain sense we are all evil; but in no sense are we all good.
0.17	For can we compare ourselves with the Apostles, to whom the Lord Himself said, “If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children”?
0.18	If we consider the Scriptures, there was only one evil man among the twelve Apostles, with reference to whom the Lord said in a certain place, “And you are clean, but not all.”
0.19	But yet in addressing them all together, He said, “If you, being evil.”
0.20	Peter heard this, John heard this, Andrew heard this, all the rest of the eleven Apostles heard it.
0.21	What did they hear?
0.22	“If you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask Him?”
0.23	When they heard that they were evil, they were in despair; but when they heard that God in heaven was their Father, they revived.
0.24	“You, being evil”—what then is due to the evil but punishment?
0.25	“How much more will your Father who is in heaven?”
0.26	What is due to children but reward?
0.27	In the name “evil” is the dread of punishment; in the name “children” is the hope of heirs.
0.28	3. In a certain respect, then, they were evil who in another respect were good.
0.29	For to those to whom it is said, “You, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children,” is added immediately, “How much more will your Father who is in heaven?”
0.30	He is then the Father of the evil, but not of those who are to be left so; because He is the Physician of those who are to be cured.
0.31	In a certain sense, then, they were evil.
0.32	And yet those guests of the Householder at the King’s marriage were not, I suppose, of that number of whom it was said, “they invited good and bad,” so as to be reckoned among the number of the bad, who, as we have heard, were shut out in the person of the one who was found not to have a wedding garment.
0.33	In a certain respect, I repeat, they were bad who yet were good; and in a certain respect they were good who yet were bad.
0.34	Hear John on the respect in which they were bad: “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”
0.35	See in what respect they were bad: because they had sin.
0.36	In what respect were they good?
0.37	“If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”
0.38	If then we should say, on the principle of this interpretation which you have now heard me bring, as I think, out of the sacred Scriptures—namely, that the same men are in a certain way good and in a certain way bad—if we should wish to take in this sense the words, “they invited good and bad,” meaning the same persons as at once good and bad; if we should wish to take them so, we are not permitted to do so, because of that one who was found “not having a wedding garment,” and who was not merely “cast out” so as to be deprived of that feast, but so as to be condemned to the punishment of everlasting darkness.
0.39	4. But someone will say, What of one man? What strange, what great matter is it, if one among the crowd “not having a wedding garment” crept in unnoticed by the servants of the Householder?
0.40	Could it be said because of that one, “they invited good and bad”?
0.41	Pay attention, therefore, my brethren, and understand.
0.42	That one man represented one class; for they were many.
0.43	Here some attentive hearer may answer me and say, “I have no wish for you to tell me your guesses; I want to have it proved to me that that one represented many.”
0.44	With the Lord’s present help, I will prove it clearly; nor will I search far in order to be able to prove it.
0.45	God will help me with His own words in this place, and through my ministry He will furnish you with a plain proof of it.
0.46	“The Master of the house came in to see the guests.”
0.47	See, my brethren, the servants’ business was only to invite and bring in the good and bad; see that it is not said that the servants took notice of the guests, found among them a man who did not have on a wedding garment, and spoke to him.
0.48	This is not written.
0.49	The Master of the house saw him, the Master of the house discovered him, the Master of the house inspected him, the Master of the house separated him out.
0.50	It was not right to pass over this.
0.51	But I have undertaken to establish another point: how that one signifies many.
0.52	“The Master of the house” then “came in to see the guests, and He found there a man who did not have on a wedding garment.
0.53	And He said to him, Friend, how did you come in here without a wedding garment?
0.54	And he was speechless.”
0.55	For He who questioned him was One to whom he could give no false reply.
0.56	The garment that was looked for is in the heart, not on the body; for had it been put on outwardly, it could not have been hidden even from the servants.
0.57	Where that wedding garment must be put on, hear in the words, “Let Your priests be clothed with righteousness.”
0.58	Of that garment the Apostle speaks: “If indeed we shall be found clothed, and not naked.”
0.59	Therefore he who escaped the notice of the servants was discovered by the Lord.
0.60	Being questioned, he is speechless: he is bound, cast out, and condemned, one for many.
0.61	I have said, Lord, that You teach us that in this You give warning to all.

0.63	True, it was one man whom the Lord questioned; to one He said, “Friend, how did you come in here?”
0.64	It was one who was speechless, and of that same one it was said, “Bind him hand and foot, and cast him into outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.”
0.65	Why? “For many are called, but few chosen.”
0.66	How can anyone contradict this plain showing of the truth?
0.67	“Cast him,” He says, “into outer darkness.”
0.68	“Him”—that one man, assuredly—of whom the Lord says, “for many are called, but few chosen.”
0.69	So then it is the few who are not cast out.
0.70	It is true he was only one man “who did not have the wedding garment.
0.71	Cast him out.” But why is he cast out?
0.72	“For many are called, but few chosen.”
0.73	Leave the few alone; cast out the many.
0.74	It is true that man was only one.
0.75	Yet undoubtedly that one not only was many, but those many far surpassed in number the number of the good.
0.76	For the good are also many; but in comparison with the bad, they are few.
0.77	In the crop there is much wheat; compare it with the chaff, and the grains are few.
0.78	The same persons, considered in themselves, are many; in comparison with the bad, they are few.
0.79	How do we prove that in themselves they are many?
0.80	“Many will come from the east and from the west.”
0.81	Where will they come?
0.82	To that feast into which both good and bad enter.
0.83	But speaking of another feast, He added, “and will sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven.”
0.84	That is the feast which the bad will not approach.
0.85	Let the feast which now is be received worthily, that we may attain to the other.
0.86	The same, then, are many who are also few: in themselves many, in comparison with the bad few.
0.87	Therefore what does the Lord say?
0.88	He found one, and said, “Let the many be cast out, let the few remain.”
0.89	For to say, “many are called, but few chosen,” is nothing else than to show plainly who in this present feast are counted such as to be brought to that other feast, where no bad men will come.
0.90	5. What is it, then?
0.91	I would not have all of you who approach the Lord’s Table, which is in this life, be with the many who are to be shut out, but with the few who are to be kept.
0.92	And how will you be able to attain to this?
0.93	Take “the wedding garment.”
0.94	You will say, “Explain this ‘wedding garment’ to us.”
0.95	Without a doubt, that is the garment which none have but the good, who are to be left at the feast, kept for that other feast to which no bad man approaches, who are to be brought safely there by the grace of the Lord; these have “the wedding garment.”
0.96	Let us then, my brethren, seek among the faithful for those who have something which bad men do not have, and this will be “the wedding garment.”
0.97	If we speak of sacraments, you see how these are common to the bad and the good.
0.98	Is it Baptism? Without Baptism, it is true, no one attains to God; but not everyone who has Baptism attains to Him.
0.99	I cannot therefore understand Baptism—the Sacrament itself, that is—to be “the wedding garment”; for this garment I see in the good, I see in the bad.
0.100	Perhaps it is the Altar, or That which is received at the Altar.
0.101	But no; we see that many eat, and “eat and drink judgment to themselves.”
0.102	What is it then?
0.103	Is it fasting? The wicked fast also.
0.104	Is it running together to the church?
0.105	The wicked run there also.
0.106	Lastly, is it miracles?
0.107	Not only do both the good and the bad perform them, but sometimes the good do not perform them.
0.108	See, among the ancient people Pharaoh’s magicians worked miracles, the Israelites did not; among the Israelites, only Moses and Aaron worked them; the rest did not, but saw, and feared, and believed.
0.109	Were the magicians of Pharaoh who did miracles better men than the people of Israel who could not do them? And yet that people was the people of God.
0.110	In the Church itself, hear the Apostle: “Are all prophets?
0.111	Do all have the gifts of healing?
0.112	Do all speak with tongues?”
0.113	6. What then is that “wedding garment”?
0.114	This is the wedding garment: “Now the end of the commandment,” says the Apostle, “is love out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of genuine faith.”
0.115	This is “the wedding garment.”
0.116	Not love of any kind whatever; for very often those who share together in an evil conscience seem to love one another.
0.117	Those who commit robberies together, who love together the harmful arts of sorcery and the theater, who join together in the shouting at the chariot race or the wild beast fight—these very often love one another; but in these there is no “love out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of genuine faith.
0.118	The wedding garment” is love such as this.
0.119	“Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not love, I have become as sounding brass and a tinkling cymbal.”
0.120	Tongues have come in alone, and it is said to them, “How did you come in here without a wedding garment?”
0.121	“Though,” he said, “I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not love, I am nothing.”
0.122	See, these are the miracles of men who very often do not have “the wedding garment.”
0.123	“Though,” he says, “I have all these, and have not Christ, I am nothing.”
0.124	Is then “the gift of prophecy” nothing? Is then “the knowledge of mysteries” nothing?
0.125	It is not that these are nothing; but “I,” if I have them, “and have not love, am nothing.”
0.126	How many good things profit nothing without this one good thing!
0.127	If then I have not love, though I give alms freely to the poor, though I have come to the confession of Christ’s Name even to blood and fire, these things may be done even out of the love of glory, and so are empty.
0.128	Because, then, they may be done even out of the love of glory, and so be empty, and not through the rich love of a godly affection, he names them all expressly as well, and you must give ear to them: “Though I distribute all my goods for the use of the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, and have not love, it profits me nothing.”
0.129	This then is “the wedding garment.”
0.130	Question yourselves; if you have it, you may be without fear at the Feast of the Lord.
0.131	In one and the same man there exist two things, love and desire.
0.132	Let love be born in you, if it is not yet born; and if it is born, let it be nourished, fostered, increased.
0.133	But as to that desire—though in this life it cannot be utterly extinguished, “for if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us,” and in so far as desire is in us, so far we are not without sin—let love increase and desire decrease, so that the one, that is, love, may one day be perfected, and desire be consumed.
0.134	Put on “the wedding garment”: I address you who do not yet have it.
0.135	You are already within, already you approach the Feast, and still you do not yet have the garment to do honor to the Bridegroom; “You are still seeking your own things, not the things which are Jesus Christ’s.”
0.136	For “the wedding garment” is put on in honor of the union—the union, that is, of the Bridegroom to the Bride.
0.137	You know the Bridegroom; it is Christ.
0.138	You know the Bride; it is the Church.
0.139	Pay honor to the Bride, pay honor to the Bridegroom.
0.140	If you pay due honor to them both, you will be their children.
0.141	Therefore make progress in this.
0.142	Love the Lord, and so learn to love yourselves; so that when by loving the Lord you have loved yourselves, you may safely love your neighbor as yourselves.
0.143	For when I find a man who does not love himself, how shall I entrust to him his neighbor, whom he should love as himself?
0.144	And who is there, you will say, who does not love himself?
0.145	Who is there? See, “He who loves iniquity hates his own soul.”
0.146	Does he love himself who loves his body and hates his soul, to his own hurt, to the hurt of both his body and soul?
0.147	And who loves his own soul?
0.148	He who loves God with all his heart and with all his mind.
0.149	To such a one I would at once entrust his neighbor.
0.150	“Love your neighbor as yourselves.”

0.152	Every man is your neighbor.
0.153	Did we not all have the same two parents?

0.159	Did all doves come from one dove?
0.160	Did all snakes come from one snake? Or all gilt-heads from one gilt-head? Or all sheep from one sheep?
