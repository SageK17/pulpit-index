1.272	12. “He who loves his brother abides (manet) in the light, and there is no occasion of stumbling in him.”—I beg you by Christ: God is feeding us; we are about to refresh our bodies in the name of Christ; both are in some good measure refreshed, and are yet to be refreshed: let the mind be fed.
1.273	I do not say this because I am going to speak for a long time; for see, the reading is now coming to an end: but lest perhaps out of weariness we should hear less attentively than we ought that which is most necessary.—“He who loves his brother abides in the light, and there is no scandal,” or “no occasion of stumbling, in him.”
1.274	Who are they that take scandal or make scandal?
1.275	Those who are offended in Christ, and in the Church.
1.276	Those who are offended in Christ are as if burned by the sun; those in the Church, as if by the moon.
1.277	But the Psalm says, “The sun shall not burn you by day, nor the moon by night”: that is, if you hold fast to charity, you will have no occasion of falling either in Christ or in the Church; you will forsake neither Christ nor the Church.
1.278	For he who forsakes the Church, how is he in Christ, when he is not in the members of Christ?
1.279	How is he in Christ who is not in the body of Christ?
1.280	Those therefore take scandal, or occasion of falling, who forsake Christ or the Church.
1.281	How do we understand that the Psalm, in saying, “By day the sun shall not burn you, nor the moon by night,” says it of this, that the burning means scandal, or occasion of stumbling?
1.282	In the first place, notice the comparison itself.
1.283	Just as the person whom something is burning says, I cannot bear it, I cannot endure it, and draws back; so those persons who cannot bear some things in the Church, and withdraw themselves either from the name of Christ or from the Church, are taking scandal.
1.284	For see how those took scandal as from the sun, those carnal ones to whom Christ preached of His flesh, saying, “He who does not eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood shall have no life in him.”
1.285	Some seventy persons said, “This is a hard saying,” and went back from Him, and the twelve remained.
1.286	All those the sun burned, and they went back, not being able to bear the force of the Word.
1.287	So the twelve remained.
1.288	And lest perhaps men should imagine that they confer a benefit on Christ by believing in Christ, and not that the benefit is conferred by Him on them, when the twelve were left, the Lord said to them, “Will you also go?”
1.289	That you may know that I am necessary to you, not you to Me.
1.290	But those whom the sun had not burned answered by the voice of Peter: “Lord, You have the word of eternal life; where shall we go?”
1.291	But who are they whom the Church, as the moon, burns by night?
1.292	Those who have made schisms.
1.293	Hear the very word used in the apostle: “Who is offended, and I do not burn?”
1.294	In what sense then is it that there is no scandal or occasion of stumbling in him who loves his brother?
1.295	Because he who loves his brother bears all things for unity’s sake; because it is in the unity of charity that brotherly love exists.
1.296	Someone, I know not who, offends you: whether he is a bad man, or a bad man as you suppose, or a bad man as you pretend: and do you desert so many good men?
1.297	What sort of brotherly love is this that has appeared in these persons?
1.298	While they accuse the Africans, they have deserted the whole world!
1.299	What, were there no saints in the whole world?
1.300	Or was it possible that they should be condemned by you unheard?
1.301	But oh! if you loved your brethren, there would be no occasion of stumbling in you.
1.302	Hear the Psalm, what it says: “Great peace have those who love Your law, and to them there is no occasion of stumbling.”
1.303	Great peace, it says, there is for those who love the law of God, and that is why there is no occasion of stumbling for them.
1.304	Those then who take scandal, or occasion of stumbling, destroy peace.
1.305	And of whom does he say that they neither take nor make occasion of stumbling?
1.306	Those who love God’s law.
1.307	Consequently they are in charity.
1.308	But someone will say, “He said it of those who love God’s law, not of the brethren.”
1.309	Hear what the Lord says: “A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.”
1.310	What is the Law but commandment?
1.311	Moreover, how is it that they do not take occasion of stumbling, except that they bear with one another?
1.312	As Paul says, “Bearing with one another in love, striving to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.”
1.313	And to show that this is the law of Christ, hear the same apostle commending this very law.
1.314	“Bear one another’s burdens,” he says, “and so you will fulfill the law of Christ.”
1.315	13. “For he who hates his brother is in darkness, and walks in darkness, and does not know where he is going.”
1.316	A great thing, my brethren: mark it, we beg you.
1.317	“He who hates his brother walks in darkness, and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.”
1.318	What is so blind as those who hate their brethren?
1.319	For that you may know that they are blind, they have stumbled at a Mountain.
1.320	I say the same things often, so that they may not slip out of your memory.
1.321	The Stone which was “cut out of the Mountain without hands,” is it not Christ, who came from the kingdom of the Jews without the work of man?
1.322	Has not that Stone broken in pieces all the kingdoms of the earth, that is, all the dominions of idols and demons?
1.323	Has not that Stone grown, and become a great mountain, and filled the whole earth?
1.324	Do we point with the finger to this Mountain in the same way that the moon on its third day is pointed out to people?
1.325	For example, when they want people to see the new moon, they say, Look, the moon! Look, there it is! And if there are some there who are not sharp-sighted, and say, Where? then the finger is put out so that they may see it.
1.326	Sometimes, when they are ashamed to be thought blind, they say they have seen what they have not seen.
1.327	Do we point out the Church in this way, my brethren?
1.328	Is it not open?
1.329	Is it not manifest?
1.330	Has it not taken possession of all nations?
1.331	Is not that fulfilled which was promised to Abraham so many years before, that in his seed all nations should be blessed?
1.332	It was promised to one believer, and the world is filled with thousands of believers.
1.333	Behold here the mountain filling the whole face of the earth!
1.334	Behold the city of which it is said, “A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden!”
1.335	But those men stumble at the mountain, and when it is said to them, Go up, “There is no mountain,” they say, and they would sooner dash their heads against it than seek a dwelling there.
1.336	Isaiah was read yesterday; whoever of you was awake, not with his eyes only but with his ear, and not the ear of the body but the ear of the heart, noted this: “In the last days the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be manifest, prepared on the top of the mountains.”
1.337	What is so manifest as a mountain?
1.338	But there are even mountains that are unknown, because they are situated in one part of the earth.
1.339	Which of you knows Mount Olympus?
1.340	Just as the people who live there do not know our Giddaba.
1.341	These mountains are in different parts of the earth.
1.342	But not so that Mountain, for it has filled the whole face of the earth, and of it is said, “Prepared on the top of the mountains.”
1.343	It is a Mountain above the tops of all mountains.
1.344	“And,” he says, “to it all nations shall be gathered.”
1.345	Who can fail to be aware of this Mountain?
1.346	Who breaks his head by stumbling against it?
1.347	Who is ignorant of the city set on a mountain?
1.348	But do not marvel that it is unknown to those who hate the brethren, because they walk in darkness and do not know where they are going, because the darkness has blinded their eyes.
1.349	They do not see the Mountain: I would not have you marvel; they have no eyes.
1.350	How is it that they have no eyes?
1.351	Because the darkness has blinded them.
1.352	How do we prove this?
1.353	Because they hate the brethren, in that, while they are offended at Africans, they separate themselves from the whole earth: in that they do not tolerate, for the peace of Christ, those whom they defame, and yet do tolerate, for the sake of Donatus, those whom they condemn.
T:2	Homily II (1 John 2:12–17)
2.1	“I write to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven through His name.
2.2	I write to you, fathers, because you have known Him who is from the beginning.
2.3	I write to you, young men, because you have overcome the wicked one.
2.4	I write to you, children, because you have known the Father.
2.5	I write to you, fathers, because you have known Him who is from the beginning.
2.6	I write to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have overcome the wicked one.
2.7	Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world.
2.8	If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.
2.9	For all that is in the world is the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, which is not of the Father, but is of the world.
2.10	And the world passes away, and the lust of it: but he who does the will of God abides forever (even as God also abides forever).”
2.11	1. All things that are read from the Holy Scriptures for our instruction and salvation, we ought to hear with earnest attention.
2.12	Yet most of all those things must be committed to our memory which have the most force against heretics, whose treacherous designs never cease to ensnare all who are weaker and more negligent.
2.13	Remember that our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ both died for us and rose again: died, namely, for our offenses, and rose again for our justification.
2.14	Even as you have just heard concerning the two disciples whom He met on the way, how “their eyes were restrained, so that they should not know Him”: and He found them despairing of the redemption that was in Christ, and supposing that He had now suffered and was dead as a man, not reckoning that as Son of God He lives forever; and supposing too that He was so dead in the flesh as not to come to life again, but just as one of the prophets: as those of you who were attentive have just now heard their own words.
2.15	Then “He opened to them the Scriptures, beginning at Moses,” and going through all the prophets, showing them that all He had suffered had been foretold, lest they should be more staggered if the Lord should rise again, and fail all the more to believe Him, if these things had not been told beforehand concerning Him.
2.16	For the firmness of faith lies in this, that all things which came to pass in Christ were foretold.
2.17	The disciples, then, did not know Him, except “in the breaking of bread.”
2.18	And truly, he who does not eat and drink judgment to himself in the breaking of bread does know Christ.
2.19	Afterward also those eleven “thought they saw a spirit.”
2.20	He gave Himself to be handled by them, He who also gave Himself to be crucified; to be crucified by enemies, to be handled by friends: yet the Physician of all, both of the ungodliness of the former and of the unbelief of the latter.
2.21	For you heard, when the Acts of the Apostles were read, how many thousands of Christ’s slayers believed.
2.22	If those who had killed believed afterward, should not those believe who doubted for a little while?
2.23	And yet even in regard to them (a thing which you ought especially to observe and to commit to your memory, because God has been pleased to put in the Scriptures that which shall make us strong against treacherous errors, Scriptures against which no one dares to speak who in any way wishes to seem a Christian), when He had given Himself to be handled by them, that did not suffice Him, but He would also confirm by means of the Scriptures the heart of those who believe: for He looked forward to us who would come afterward; seeing that in Him we have nothing that we can handle, but have that which we may read.
2.24	For if those believed only because they held and handled Him, what shall we do?
2.25	Now, Christ has ascended into heaven; He is not to come except at the end, to judge the living and the dead.
2.26	By what shall we believe, but by that by which it was His will that even those who handled Him should be confirmed?
2.27	For He opened to them the Scriptures and showed them that it was needful for Christ to suffer, and that all things must be fulfilled which were written of Him in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms.
2.28	He embraced in His discourse the whole ancient text of the Scriptures.
2.29	All that there is of those former Scriptures tells of Christ; but only if it finds ears.
2.30	He also “opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures.”
2.31	Therefore we also must pray for this, that He would open our understanding.
2.32	2. But what did the Lord show to be written of Him in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms?
2.33	What did He show?
2.34	Let Him say it Himself. The evangelist has put this briefly, that we might know what, in all that great compass of the Scriptures, we ought to believe and to understand.
2.35	Certainly there are many pages, and many books; the content of them all is this which the Lord spoke briefly to His disciples.
2.36	What is this? That “it was needful for Christ to suffer, and to rise again the third day.”
2.37	You have it now concerning the Bridegroom, that “it was needful for Christ to suffer, and to rise again”: the Bridegroom has been set before us.
2.38	Concerning the Bride, let us see what He says; so that you, when you know the Bridegroom and the Bride, may not come to the marriage without reason.
2.39	For every celebration is a celebration of marriage: the Church’s nuptials are being celebrated.
2.40	The King’s Son is about to take a wife, and that King’s Son is Himself a King: and the guests who throng to the marriage are themselves the Bride.
2.41	It is not as in a carnal marriage, where some are guests and another is the one who is married; in the Church, those who come as guests, if they come to good purpose, become the Bride.
2.42	For the whole Church is Christ’s Bride, of which the beginning and firstfruits is the flesh of Christ: there the Bride was joined to the Bridegroom in the flesh.
2.43	With good reason, when He would signify that same flesh, He broke bread; and with good reason “in the breaking of bread” the eyes “of the disciples were opened, and they knew Him.”
2.44	Well then, what did the Lord say was written of Him in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms?
2.45	That “it was needful for Christ to suffer.”
2.46	Had He not added, “and to rise again,” those whose eyes were restrained might well mourn; but “to rise again” is also foretold.
2.47	And why this? Why was it needful for Christ to suffer and to rise again?
2.48	Because of that Psalm which we especially commended to your attention on the fourth day, the first station, of last week.
2.49	Why was it needful for Christ to suffer and to rise again?
2.50	For this reason: “All the ends of the earth shall be reminded and turned to the Lord, and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before Him.”
2.51	For, so that you may know that it was needful for Christ to suffer and to rise again, what has He added in this place also, so that after setting forth the Bridegroom He might also set forth the Bride?
2.52	“And that there be preached,” He says, “in His name, repentance and remission of sins throughout all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.”
2.53	You have heard, brethren; hold it fast.
2.54	Let no one doubt concerning the Church, that it is “throughout all nations”: let no one doubt that it began at Jerusalem and has filled all nations.
2.55	We know the field where the Vine is planted: but when it has grown we do not know it, because it has taken up the whole.
2.56	Where did it begin?
2.57	“At Jerusalem.” How far has it come?
2.58	To “all nations.” A few remain: it shall possess all.
2.59	In the meantime, while it is taking possession of all, it has seemed good to the Vinedresser to cut off some unprofitable branches, and they have made heresies and schisms.
2.60	Do not let the branches that are cut off induce you to be cut off: rather urge those who are cut off to be grafted in again.
2.61	It is manifest that Christ has suffered, has risen again, and has ascended into heaven: manifest also is the Church, because “repentance and remission of sins is preached in His name throughout all nations.”
2.62	Where did it begin?
2.63	“Beginning at Jerusalem.” The man hears this; foolish and vain, and (how shall I express it?) worse than blind! So great a mountain, and he does not see it; a lamp set on a lampstand, and he shuts his eyes against it!
2.64	3. When we say to them, If you are Catholic Christians, be in communion with that Church from which the Gospel is spread abroad over the whole earth: be in communion with that Jerusalem; when we say this to them, they answer us, We do not hold communion with that city where our King was slain, where our Lord was slain: as though they hate the city where our Lord was slain.
2.65	The Jews slew Him whom they found on earth; these scorn Him who sits in heaven!
2.66	Which are the worse: those who despised Him because they thought Him a man, or those who scorn the sacraments of Him whom they now confess to be God?
2.67	But they hate, so they say, the city in which their Lord was slain!
2.68	Pious men, and merciful! They grieve greatly that Christ was slain, and in men they slay Christ!
2.69	But He loved that city and pitied it: from it He commanded the preaching of Him to begin, “beginning at Jerusalem.”
2.70	He made there the beginning of the preaching of His name: and you shrink back in horror from having communion with that city!
2.71	No wonder that, being cut off, you hate the root.
2.72	What did He say to His disciples?
2.73	“Sit still in the city, because I send My promise upon you.”
2.74	Behold what the city is that they hate!
2.75	Perhaps they would love it, if Christ’s murderers lived in it.
2.76	For it is manifest that all Christ’s murderers, that is, the Jews, are expelled from that city.
2.77	That which once had in it those who were fierce against Christ, now has those who adore Christ.
2.78	Therefore these men hate it, because Christians are in it.
2.79	There it was His will that His disciples should remain, and there that He should send the Holy Spirit to them.
2.80	Where did the Church have its beginning, but where the Holy Spirit came from heaven and filled the hundred and twenty sitting in one place?
2.81	That number twelve was made tenfold.
2.82	They sat, a hundred and twenty persons, and the Holy Spirit came, “and filled the whole place, and there came a sound, as it were the rushing of a mighty wind, and there were cloven tongues like fire.”
2.83	You have heard the Acts of the Apostles: this was the reading today: “They began to speak with tongues, as the Spirit gave them to speak.”
2.84	And all who were on the spot, Jews who had come from various nations, each recognized his own tongue, and marveled that those unlearned and ignorant men had suddenly learned not one or two tongues, but the tongues of all nations whatever.
2.85	There, then, where all tongues sounded, it was signified that all tongues should believe.
2.86	But these men, who love Christ so much, and therefore refuse to hold communion with the city which killed Christ, so honor Christ as to affirm that He is left with two tongues, the Latin and the Punic, that is,
2.87	African. Christ possessing only two tongues!
2.88	For there are but these two tongues on the side of Donatus; more they do not have.
2.89	Let us awake, my brethren; let us rather see the gift of the Spirit of God, and let us believe the things spoken beforehand concerning Him, and let us see fulfilled the things foretold in the Psalm: “There are neither speeches nor discourses, but their voices are heard among them.”
2.90	And lest perhaps the case be that the tongues themselves came to one place, and not rather that the gift of Christ came to all tongues, hear what follows: “Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the world their words.”
2.91	Why this? Because “in the sun He has set His tabernacle,” that is, in the open light.
2.92	His tabernacle, His flesh: His tabernacle, His Church: it is set “in the sun”; not in the night, but in the day.
2.93	But why do those men not acknowledge it?
2.94	Return to the reading at the place where it ended yesterday, and see why they do not acknowledge it: “He who hates his brother walks in darkness, and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.”
2.95	As for us, then, let us see what follows, and not be in darkness.
2.96	How shall we not be in darkness?
2.97	If we love the brethren.
2.98	How is it proved that we love the brotherhood?
2.99	By this, that we do not tear apart unity, that we hold fast to charity.
2.100	4. “I write to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven you through His name.”
2.101	Therefore, “little children,” because in the forgiveness of sins you have your birth.
2.102	But through whose name are sins forgiven?
2.103	Through Augustine’s? No; therefore neither through the name of Donatus.
2.104	Let it be your concern to see who Augustine is, or who Donatus is: no, not through the name of Paul, not through the name of Peter.
2.105	For to those who divided the Church among themselves, and out of unity tried to make parties, mother charity in the apostle, laboring in birth with her little ones, lays bare her own inmost being, with words as it were tears open her breasts, bewails her children whom she sees carried out dead, calls back to the one Name those who insisted on making for themselves many names, repels them from love of herself so that Christ may be loved, and says, “Was Paul crucified for you?
2.106	Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?”
2.107	What does he say? “I would not have you be mine, so that you may be with me: be with me; we all are His who died for us, who was crucified for us”: hence here also it is said, “Your sins are forgiven you through His name,” not through the name of any man.
2.108	5. “I write to you, fathers.”
2.109	Why sons first? “Because your sins are forgiven you through His name,” and you are regenerated into a new life; therefore sons.
2.110	Why fathers? “Because you have known Him who is from the beginning”: for the beginning has relation to fatherhood.
2.111	Christ is new in the flesh, but ancient in Godhead.
2.112	How ancient, do we think? How many years old?
2.113	Of greater age, do we think, than His mother?
2.114	Assuredly of greater age than His mother, for “all things were made by Him.”
2.115	If all things, then the Ancient made the very mother of whom the New was to be born.
2.116	Was He, do we think, before His mother only?
2.117	Yes, and His antiquity is before His mother’s ancestors.
2.118	The ancestor of His mother was Abraham; and the Lord says, “Before Abraham I am.”
2.119	Before Abraham, do we say?
2.120	Heaven and earth were made before man was.
2.121	Before these was the Lord, or rather also is.
2.122	For rightly He says, not, Before Abraham I was, but, “Before Abraham I Am.”
2.123	For that of which one says “was,” is not; and that of which one says “will be,” is not yet: He knows nothing other than to be.
2.124	As God, He knows “to be”: “was” and “will be” He does not know.
2.125	It is one day there, but a day that is forever and ever.
2.126	That day is not set in the midst between a yesterday and a tomorrow: for when the “yesterday” is ended, the “today” begins, to be finished by the coming “tomorrow.”
2.127	That one day there is a day without darkness, without night, without spaces, without measure, without hours.
2.128	Call it what you will: if you will, it is a day; if you will, a year; if you will, years.
2.129	For it is said of this same, “And Your years shall not fail.”
2.130	But when is it called a day?
2.131	When it is said to the Lord, “Today I have begotten You.”
2.132	Begotten of the eternal Father, begotten from eternity, begotten in eternity: with no beginning, no bound, no space of breadth; because He is what is, because He Himself is “He who Is.”
2.133	This name of His He told to Moses: “You shall say to them, He who Is has sent me to you.”
2.134	Why speak then of “before Abraham”? Why, before Noah? Why, before Adam?
2.135	Hear the Scripture: “Before the day-star I have begotten You.”
2.136	In short, before heaven and earth.
2.137	Why? Because “all things were made by Him, and without Him nothing was made.”
2.138	By this know the “fathers”: for they become fathers by acknowledging “That which is from the beginning.”
2.139	6. “I write to you, young men.”
2.140	There are sons, there are fathers, there are young men: sons, because begotten; fathers, because they acknowledge the Beginning; why young men?
2.141	“Because you have overcome the wicked one.”
2.142	In the sons, birth; in the fathers, antiquity; in the young men, strength.
2.143	If the wicked one is “overcome” by the young men, he fights with us.
2.144	He fights, but does not conquer.
2.145	Why? Because we are strong, or because He is strong in us who in the hands of the persecutors was found weak?
2.146	He who did not resist His persecutors has made us strong.
2.147	“For He was crucified out of weakness, but He lives by the power of God.”
2.148	7. “I write to you, children.”
2.149	Why children? “Because you have known the Father.
2.150	I write to you, fathers”: he presses this home and repeats, “Because you have known Him who is from the beginning.”
2.151	Remember that you are fathers: if you forget “Him who is from the beginning,” you have lost your fatherhood.
2.152	“I write to you, young men.”
2.153	Consider again and again that you are young men: fight, that you may overcome; overcome, that you may be crowned; be humble, that you may not fall in the fight.
2.154	“I write to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have overcome the wicked one.”
2.155	8. All these things, my brethren (“because we have known That which is from the beginning, because we are strong, because we have known the Father”), do they not all, while in a manner they commend knowledge, commend charity?
2.156	If we have known, let us love: for knowledge without charity does not save.
2.157	“Knowledge puffs up, charity builds up.”
2.158	If you have a mind to confess and not to love, you begin to be like the demons.
2.159	The demons confessed the Son of God, and said, “What have we to do with You?” and were driven back.
2.160	Confess and embrace. For those feared because of their iniquities; you, love Him who forgives your iniquities.
2.161	But how can we love God, if we love the world?
2.162	He prepares us, therefore, to be inhabited by charity.
2.163	There are two loves: of the world, and of God: if the love of the world dwells within, there is no way for the love of God to enter: let the love of the world make way, and the love of God dwell within; let the better have place.
2.164	You loved the world: do not love the world: when you have emptied your heart of earthly love, you will drink in love divine: and from then on charity begins to dwell in you, from which nothing of evil can proceed.
2.165	Hear therefore his words, how he goes to work in the manner of one who makes a clearing.
2.166	He comes upon the hearts of men as upon a field that he would occupy: but in what state does he find it?
2.167	If he finds a wood, he roots it up; if he finds the field cleared, he plants it.
2.168	He would plant a tree there: charity.
2.169	And what is the wood he would root up?
2.170	Love of the world.
2.171	Hear him, the uprooter of the wood!
2.172	“Do not love the world,” (for this comes next,) “nor the things that are in the world; if anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.”
2.173	9. You have heard that “if anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.”
2.174	Let no one say in his heart that this is false, brethren: God says it; the Holy Spirit has spoken by the Apostle; nothing is more true: “If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.”
2.175	Would you have the Father’s love, so that you may be a joint heir with the Son?
2.176	Do not love the world.
2.177	Shut out the evil love of the world, that you may be filled with the love of God.
2.178	You are a vessel; but as yet you are full.
2.179	Pour out what you have, that you may receive what you do not have.
2.180	Certainly our brethren are now born again of water and of the Spirit: we also, some years ago, were born again of water and of the Spirit.
2.181	It is good for us that we do not love the world, lest the sacraments remain in us to our damnation, rather than as means of strengthening to salvation.
2.182	That which strengthens to salvation is to have the root of charity, to have the “power of godliness,” not “the form” only.
2.183	Good is the form, holy the form: but what does the form avail, if it does not hold the root?
2.184	The branch that is cut off, is it not cast into the fire?
2.185	Have the form, but in the root.
2.186	But in what way are you rooted so that you may not be rooted up?
2.187	By holding charity, as the Apostle Paul says, “rooted and grounded in charity.”
2.188	How shall charity be rooted there, amid the overgrown wilderness of the love of the world?
2.189	Make a clean clearing of the woods.
2.190	A mighty seed you are about to put in: let there be nothing in the field that will choke the seed.
2.191	These are the uprooting words which he has spoken: “Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world.
2.192	If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.”
2.193	10. “For all that is in the world is the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life”: three things he has named, which are not of the Father, but are of the world.
2.194	“And the world passes away, and the lust of it: but he who does the will of God abides forever, even as He abides forever.”
2.195	Why am I not to love what God made?
2.196	What will you have? Will you love the things of time, and pass away with time; or not love the world, and live to eternity with God?
2.197	The river of temporal things sweeps one along: but like a tree sprung up beside the river is our Lord Jesus Christ.
2.198	He took on flesh, died, rose again, ascended into heaven.
2.199	It was His will, in a manner, to plant Himself beside the river of the things of time.
2.200	Are you rushing down the stream to the headlong deep?
2.201	Hold fast to the tree.
2.202	Is love of the world whirling you along?
2.203	Hold fast to Christ. For you He became temporal, that you might become eternal; because He also became temporal in such a way that He still remained eternal.
2.204	Something was added to Him from time; nothing went from His eternity.
2.205	But you were born temporal, and by sin were made temporal: you were made temporal by sin; He was made temporal by mercy, in remitting sins.
2.206	How great the difference, when two are in a prison, between the criminal and the one who visits him!
2.207	For at times a person comes to his friend and goes in to visit him, and both seem to be in prison; but they differ by a wide distinction.
2.208	The one, his case presses down; the other, humanity has brought there.
2.209	So in this our mortal state, we were held fast by our guilt; He came down in mercy: He came in to the captive, a Redeemer, not an oppressor.
2.210	The Lord shed His blood for us, redeemed us, changed our hope.
2.211	As yet we bear the mortality of the flesh, and take the future immortality on trust: and on the sea we are tossed by the waves, but we have the anchor of hope already fixed on the land.
2.212	11. But let us “not love the world, nor the things that are in the world.
2.213	For the things that are in the world are the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life.”
2.214	These are the three: lest perhaps someone say, “The things that are in the world God made: that is, heaven and earth, the sea; the sun, the moon, the stars, all the adornment of the heavens.
2.215	What is the adornment of the sea? All creeping things.
2.216	What of the earth? Animals, trees, flying creatures.
2.217	These are ‘in the world’; God made them.
2.218	Why then am I not to love what God has made?”
2.219	Let the Spirit of God be in you, that you may see that all these things are good: but woe to you if you love the things made and forsake their Maker!
2.220	Fair are they to you: but how much fairer is He who formed them!
2.221	Mark this well, beloved. For by comparisons you may be instructed: lest Satan steal upon you, saying what he is accustomed to say, Take your enjoyment in the creature of God; why did He make those things except for your enjoyment?
2.222	And men drink themselves drunk, and perish, and forget their own Creator: while they use the things created not temperately but lustfully, the Creator is despised.
2.223	Of such the apostle says: “They worshiped and served the creature in place of the Creator, who is blessed forever.”
2.224	God does not forbid you to love these things, yet not to set your affections on them for blessedness, but to approve and praise them to this end, that you may love your Creator.
2.225	In the same way, my brethren, as if a bridegroom should make a ring for his bride, and she, having received the ring, should love it more than she loves the bridegroom who made the ring for her: would not her soul be found guilty of adultery in the very gift of the bridegroom, even though she loved only what the bridegroom gave her?
2.226	By all means let her love what the bridegroom gave: yet if she should say, “This ring is enough for me; I do not wish to see his face now,” what sort of woman would she be?
2.227	Who would not detest such folly? Who would not pronounce her guilty of an adulterous mind?
2.228	You love gold in place of the man, you love a ring in place of the bridegroom: if this is in you, that you love a ring in place of your bridegroom and have no wish to see your bridegroom, then the pledge he has given you serves not to bind you to him, but to turn your heart away from him!
2.229	For this is why the bridegroom gives a pledge: that in his pledge he himself may be loved.
2.230	Well then, God gave you all these things: love Him who made them.
2.231	There is more that He would gladly give you, that is, His very Self who made these things.
2.232	But if you love these (even though God made them) and neglect the Creator and love the world, shall not your love be counted adulterous?
